\section{Symbol table and stated extensions}\label{si:symbols}

With continuous model time measured in years, $B,E,Y_f,Y_c,S$ have units gha; their time derivatives have units gha\,yr$^{-1}$. \paragraph{Stated extensions (off in every reported run).} (i) A timber-liquidation channel $L_c=\pos{H_c-\sigma_cY_c}/b_{G,c}$ removes capital area when an annual timber-account target $H_c$ exceeds the harvestable capital account amount; $\gamma=1/b_{G,c}$ (ha\,gha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$) applies only to this channel. (ii) A direct fast-to-capital restoration $R_{fc}=\chi A_f\pos{B-E}$ (surplus-gated) can replace or supplement $R_{rc}$. (iii) A reservation $\sigma_f,\sigma_c<1$ withholds a share of each flow from demand (Section~\ref{si:policy}). (iv) The technology wave $T_b$ of Eq.~(14) is available but is off in all reported runs; applying it non-contingently in the recovery experiments produces artefacts (Section~\ref{si:gates}). (v) An endogenous per-capita footprint $e(t)$ is an offered extension not implemented here.

\paragraph{Conventions.} The positive part is exact; conversion is a rate through $\tconv$; land is conserved one-to-one across $A_f,A_c,A_r$ while $q$ is a separate capacity book; annual NFA account amounts $B,E,Y_f,Y_c,S$ are in gha, while the leaky time-integral $D$ is in gha\,yr and $\dot D,\eta D$ are in gha; a sustainable fast book exists because of the maintenance term $\mu A_r$. The reserve floor $A_r^{\min}$ and arable ceiling $A_f^{\max}$ are declared domain constraints that are checked after each run (never violated in any reported run: the minimum reserve in the drawdown sweeps is $0.80$\,ha) rather than enforced by clamps; $\Acmin$, $A_f\ge0$, $q\in[0,q_{\max}]$ and $K_{\min}$ are enforced.

\section{Derivations and proofs}\label{si:proofs}

\subsection{The composition identity with varying debt and quality}\label{si:identity}

Differentiating $B=b_fA_f+\Ups(q)A_c$ with $b_f=(b_{f0}+T_b)e^{-\alpha D}$ and substituting Eqs.~(6)--(7) gives the general identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:si_identity}
\dot B=\dconv\,u_c+b_f\big(\mu A_r-\eta_fA_f\big)+b_{G,c}G_c'(q)A_c\,\dot q+\Ups R_{rc}-\alpha\dot D\,b_fA_f+\frac{\dot T_b}{b_{f0}+T_b}\,b_fA_f .
\end{equation}
The conversion term has the sign of $\dconv$; the quality term has the sign of $G_c'(q)$ (non-negative for $q\le q_{\max}/2$, non-positive above it, since $\dot q\ge0$) and is bounded in magnitude by $b_{G,c}\rho_c\dot qA_c$, which is small at the baseline; the debt term is non-positive while debt accrues; the technology term is non-negative while the wave rises; the restoration term is non-negative. Proposition~1 of the paper is the case $T_b\equiv0$, $R_{rc}\equiv0$, $D$ fixed. In the demonstration run $D$ is not fixed (it rises to $0.20$\,gha\,yr and decays), so the debt term is present, and the mask holds nevertheless: the conversion term dominates by two orders of magnitude at the start ($\dconv u_c\approx0.78\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}\times0.27\,\mathrm{ha\,yr^{-1}}=0.21\,\mathrm{gha\,yr^{-1}}$ against $b_f(\mu A_r-\eta_fA_f)=-0.005\,\mathrm{gha\,yr^{-1}}$ and $\alpha\dot D b_fA_f\approx0.004\,\mathrm{gha\,yr^{-1}}$).

\subsection{The deficit-side affine system and its spectrum}\label{si:affine}

On the deficit side with $q=q_{\max}$, $D$ frozen, $R_{rc}=0$ and $S/b_f<A_c-\Acmin$, Eq.~(5) gives $u_c=(E-b_fA_f-b_cA_c)/(b_f\tconv)$, and with $A_r=A_{tot}-A_f-A_c$ Eqs.~(6)--(7) become
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}A_f\\A_c\end{pmatrix}=
\begin{pmatrix}-\tconv^{-1}-\mu-\eta_f & -\mu-\dfrac{b_c}{b_f\tconv}\\[8pt]\tconv^{-1} & \dfrac{b_c}{b_f\tconv}\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}A_f\\A_c\end{pmatrix}+
\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{E}{b_f\tconv}+\mu A_{tot}\\[8pt]-\dfrac{E}{b_f\tconv}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Hence $\operatorname{tr}J=-\tconv^{-1}(1-b_c/b_f)-\mu-\eta_f$ and $\det J=(\mu+\eta_f)(k-b_c/b_f)/\tconv$ with $k=\mu/(\mu+\eta_f)$. At the baseline $\operatorname{tr}J=-1.0512\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ and $\det J=0.05353\,\mathrm{yr}^{-2}$, and the eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{1,2}=\tfrac12\Big(\operatorname{tr}J\pm\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}^2J-4\det J}\Big)=-0.0537\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1},\ -0.9975\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Because $J$ contains neither $E$ nor the state, the spectrum is constant along the frozen-demand branch (verified at $E/\Eceil=0.50,\,0.90,\,0.99,\,0.999$: $\lambda_1=-0.05366\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ in every case). $J$ contains no $\rho_c$, so $\lambda_1$ is exactly independent of the regeneration rate ($-0.0537\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ at $\rho_c=0.04,\,0.08,\,0.16$\,yr$^{-1}$). It depends mildly on $\tconv$ ($-0.0553,\,-0.0537,\,-0.0505,\,-0.0417$\,yr$^{-1}$ at $\tconv=0.5,\,1,\,2,\,5$\,yr) and more strongly on the maintenance rate ($-0.0254,\,-0.0537,\,-0.1099$\,yr$^{-1}$ at $\mu=0.03,\,0.06,\,0.12$\,yr$^{-1}$); its value should therefore be reported as an order-of-magnitude negative number that never crosses zero, while the qualitative statements (no positive eigenvalue, no fold, no critical slowing) hold across all sweeps. The fixed point of the affine system is $A_c^*(E)$ of Eq.~(19) with $A_f^*=k(A_{tot}-A_c^*)$; for $E>\Eceil$ it lies below $\Acmin$ and the trajectory is captured by the face $A_c=\Acmin$, on which $u_c=0$ and the shortfall $S^*=E-\Eceil$ persists. Two verified members of the no-conversion family at the baseline are $(A_f^*,A_c^*,A_r^*,P^*)=(0.914\,\mathrm{ha},0.824\,\mathrm{ha},0.762\,\mathrm{ha},1.488\,\mathrm{cap})$ and $(1.091\,\mathrm{ha},0.500\,\mathrm{ha},0.909\,\mathrm{ha},1.731\,\mathrm{cap})$, both with $A_r^*/A_f^*=\eta_f/\mu=0.8333$ exactly; the second is a sustainable cropland equilibrium supported by the maintenance term. The slow mode is the equilibration of the reserve share $A_r/A_f\to\eta_f/\mu$, which is why the strict-inequality window of the demonstration run extends to $\approx150$\,yr although most of the conversion is complete within $25$\,yr.

In the other branch of the minimum ($S/b_f\ge A_c-\Acmin$), $u_c=(A_c-\Acmin)/\tconv$ and the distance to the floor obeys $\frac{d}{dt}(A_c-\Acmin)=-(A_c-\Acmin)/\tconv$: it decays exponentially and never reaches zero in finite time. This is the reason the floor is approached asymptotically in Fig.~1c of the paper and the ``floor-contact'' thresholds depend on the tolerance used to declare contact (Section~\ref{si:ladder}).

\subsection{Characteristic structure of the full delay system}\label{si:char}

With state $z=(A_f,A_c,q,P,D)$ the linearised system about any point of a given active set is $\dot z(t)=J_0z(t)+J_gz(t-\tau_g)+J_pz(t-\tau_p)$ with characteristic equation $\det\Delta(\lambda)=0$, $\Delta(\lambda)=\lambda I-J_0-J_ge^{-\lambda\tau_g}-J_pe^{-\lambda\tau_p}$. $J_0$ carries the conversion and debt feedbacks on the current state (the $2\times2$ block above, plus the $D$ row $\partial\dot D/\partial z=(-b_f,-\Ups,-b_{G,c}G_c'A_c,e,-\eta)$ on the deficit side and the $\partial b_f/\partial D=-\alpha b_f$ couplings); $J_g$ has the single entry $G_c'(q^*)$ in the $q$ row; $J_p$ has the row $(rP^{*2}/K^{*2})\,e^{-1}\,\partial B/\partial z$ in the $P$ position (from $\partial\dot P/\partial K=rP^2/K^2$ and $K=B/e$). Two scalar delayed modes decouple at the baseline. The quality mode linearised at $q_{\max}$ is $\dot\xi=-\rho_c\xi(t-\tau_g)$ (rate $-0.08\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ without the delay); its characteristic root satisfies $\lambda=-\rho_ce^{-\lambda\tau_g}$, and since $\rho_c\tau_g=1.6>\pi/2$ the linear delayed mode would be oscillatory. In the nonlinear model this cannot occur because $\dot q=G_c(q(t-\tau_g))\ge0$ for $q(t-\tau_g)\in[0,q_{\max}]$ and $q$ is clamped at $q_{\max}$, so quality is monotone non-decreasing and converges to $q_{\max}$; the linear oscillation is an artefact of linearising a one-sided law at its boundary. The population mode on the no-conversion face is the delayed logistic law with $r\tau_p=0.5<\pi/2$, below the Hutchinson threshold, so it is non-oscillatory at the baseline. No entry, determinant sign or eigenvalue is inherited from the one-stock characteristic equation of Section~\ref{si:onestock}; each is derived per active set (no conversion; active conversion; floor face $A_c=\Acmin$; $K=K_{\min}$).

\subsection{Debt fixed points beyond the ceiling}\label{si:debt}

For $E>\Eceil$ the capital book sits on the floor and the shortfall feeds the debt. With $B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)=b_{f0}e^{-\alpha D}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)+b_c\Acmin$, the debt equation $\dot D=E-B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)-\eta D$ has the fixed point $D^*(E)$ solving $\eta D=E-B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)$; the right-hand side is increasing in $D$, so the fixed point is unique and attracting whenever $\eta>\alpha b_{f0}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)$ ($0.034\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}<0.05\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ at the baseline). Solving numerically, $D^*=1.186$\,gha\,yr at $E=1.158$\,gha and $D^*=5.375$\,gha\,yr at $E=1.238$\,gha, with $B(D^*)=1.099$ and $0.969$\,gha and persistent shortfalls $S^*=0.059$ and $0.269$\,gha; the integrated debt values quoted in earlier versions ($1.21$ and $5.39$\,gha\,yr) agree to within $2\%$ (finite horizon). Under frozen demand the debt therefore saturates; it is unbounded only if the shortfall is.

\subsection{Boundedness asymmetry and the crossover shortfall}\label{si:bounded}

From Eq.~(12), $\ln b_f=\ln(b_{f0}+T_b)-\alpha D$. The first term lies in $[\ln b_{f0},\ \ln(b_{f0}+\Delta b)]$, so the technology channel contributes at most $\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})=\ln(1.85/0.85)=0.778$ to $\ln b_f$ at the baseline. From Eq.~(13), $\dot D\le\bar S-\eta D$ with $\bar S$ the running supremum of the shortfall, so $D(t)\le\max\{D(0),\bar S/\eta\}$ and the degradation channel contributes at least $-\alpha\bar S/\eta$. The two are equal when $\bar S=S^\dagger=(\eta/\alpha)\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})=1.296\,\mathrm{gha}$ at the baseline. For the fast-book yield itself, $Y_f=b_fA_f$ shares this structure; the capital term $\Ups A_c$ is unaffected by either channel. Under the additive law $b_f=b_{f0}+T_b-\alpha D$, both channels are bounded quantities of the same kind ($\Delta b$ against $\alpha D^*$) and the logarithmic asymmetry disappears, which is why the paper states the result for the multiplicative form.

\subsection{Macro-ratio identities}\label{si:ratios}

Define $R_B=E/B$, $R_A=E/Y_f$ and the fast-land share $\psi_f=Y_f/B$; then $R_A=R_B/\psi_f$ is an identity. The direct-yield share inside the capital book, $\psi_c=b_cA_c/Y_c$, is a different quantity and $R_A=R_B/\psi_c$ is false. $R_B=1$ is aggregate balance; $R_A=1$ (demand equals the fast-book account yield) is necessary but not sufficient for capital decline because the capital direct yield and regeneration flow are untouched. The capital book declines iff $u_c>R_{rc}$, with $u_c$ given by Eq.~(5), which is a statement about the shortfall $S$ and not about $R_B$: the capital book can fall while $R_B\le1$ (the composition mask does not require overshoot), and ``$R_B=1$ is the fold'' is a statement about the one-stock frozen-demand reducer (Section~\ref{si:onestock}), not a two-land property. At the regeneration maximum $q^*=q_{\max}/2$ the direct-yield share of capital value is $b_c/(b_c+b_{G,c}\rho_cq_{\max}/4)=0.758$ at the baseline; this is a property of that point, not a structural constant.
