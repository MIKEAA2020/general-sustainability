# External prior-art and literature grounding
## Whole empirical follow-up and proposed enhancements
**Assessment date: 29 October 2026**  
**Decision:** proceed only with a narrowed, observation-led contribution. The broad masking argument and the proposed conceptual enhancements are already established. A potentially defensible contribution is an independently measured, boundary-matched empirical comparison, coupled to validated conversion evidence and calibrated diagnostic performance—not a new general theory of ecological accounting.

This review addresses the implementation plan, companion-paper transfer recommendations and V18 enhancement recommendations together. It does not revise those documents or any manuscript. Source IDs below correspond to the accompanying 40-record evidence matrix; those records distinguish selected full text, abstracts, official documentation and discovery-only leads.

## 1. Executive findings

1. **The stock–flow/account–asset distinction is not new.** Mancini et al. explicitly proposed investigating stocks and flows by land type within Ecological Footprint accounting. Earlier criticism, methodology and responses already discuss omitted ecological damage, accounting constraints and the intended meaning of regenerative-capacity accounts. Three-dimensional Footprint and improved non-compensatory 3D Footprint approaches are additional close conceptual precedents. [R01](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2017.01.033), [R02](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2012.08.005), [R03](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001700), [R04](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001701), [R06](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2011.04.020), [R07](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2020.106315).
2. **Within-asset compensation has especially close empirical precedents.** Xu et al. quantify planted-forest gains offsetting natural-forest losses; Maes et al. report aggregate European forest-condition improvement alongside deterioration across substantial areas. These are not identical to the proposed account comparison, but they preclude a broad first demonstration of forest masking. [R08](https://doi.org/10.34133/remotesensing.0119), [R09](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-39434-0).
3. **A biocapacity–biomass connection is not itself new.** Karim et al. combine field biomass, stand structure and locally modified biocapacity estimates in the Sundarbans. Their cross-sectional, partly shared-input construction differs from an independent temporal account–stock comparison. That distinction must be demonstrated, not assumed. [R39](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2025.127815).
4. **Conversion, trajectory analysis and forest demography are established fields.** Their inclusion can improve this study but is not conceptual novelty. The useful questions concern verified transitions, additional measured information and the limits of inference. [R12](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abm9267), [R16](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/54160), [R21](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaz9463), [R40](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43016-021-00429-z).
5. **Do not design around a predetermined global biomass decline.** FAO FRA 2025 reports that global growing stock, biomass and forest carbon increased after 2000, with boreal/temperate gains and tropical declines. Naturally regenerating and primary-forest area can decline while global biomass increases. The proposed sample must admit these outcomes and reconcile definitions rather than cherry-pick the expected sign. [R34](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2dee6e93-1988-4659-aa89-30dd20b43b15/content/FRA-2025/key-findings.html).
6. **Two 2026 releases improve feasibility without removing validation requirements.** NEFBA 2026 has full UN-based results through 2023; ESA Biomass CCI v7 has 2015–2024 biomass epochs. A provisional 2015–2023 core window remains reasonable. HU-SoilCarbonGrids 2026 makes a Hungarian soil benchmark worth piloting, but its annual surfaces are model predictions, not independent annual observations. [R27](https://catalogue.ceda.ac.uk/uuid/6429d1aafe1e43b9b414e4a5a7f8b903/), [R30](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejss.70292), [R35](https://footprint.info.yorku.ca/files/2026/04/NEFBA_ReleaseNotes.pdf?x92789), [R36](https://overshoot.footprintnetwork.org/content/uploads/2026/05/2026-Nowcast-explained.pdf).

**What remains potentially publishable:** quantify how often, where and under which measurement conditions an aggregate or component account and a separately measured physical asset give different directional descriptions; distinguish conversion-related and within-asset changes; and test whether predeclared spatial/path reporting packages add reliable information beyond simpler baselines. This is a proposed contribution, not a finding or a novelty certificate.

## 2. Scope, method and evidentiary limits

This was purposeful external screening, not a systematic review. The session record contains 38 web-search calls, followed by targeted retrieval of publisher pages, repository abstracts, official technical notes and data documentation. Forty retained evidence records include research articles, methodological work, accounting standards/discussion papers, product documentation and publication-ethics guidance. **Forty is not a count of fully reviewed research papers.**

The searches covered accounting stock/flow limits; compensation and forest condition; conversion/cropland mapping; biomass measurement; map accuracy and calibration; trajectories/references; demography; and conditional soil, wetland and fish data. Recent sources were checked against the assessment date, not treated as future publications.

Important limits:
- Selected passages were reviewed for many accessible full texts; abstracts support only their stated scope. Code, supplements and raw-data validity were not generally audited.
- The official SEEA PDF exposed only its first 30 pages. Detailed conversion discussion additionally comes from Vardon's discussion paper; that paper is **not** the adopted standard.
- The Aldwaik–Pontius methodological lead and unsuccessful SoilGrids FAQ retrieval remain discovery-level records. They do not establish detailed methods or exact novelty overlap.
- The first 33 search strings are no longer retained verbatim; the search log labels their themes as reconstructions. No complete result corpus, formal deduplication process or PRISMA flow exists.
- DOI/title metadata were checked for key recent studies. Some dataset/guidance entries retain descriptive labels or a discovery locator; resolve complete author, venue, date and locator metadata before importing the registry into a manuscript bibliography.
- No empirical extraction, prevalence estimation, calibration experiment, manuscript edit or ethics clearance was performed.

These limits still permit strong negative conclusions about broad conceptual-priority claims because direct primary precedents exist. They do **not** justify “no previous study has…” for the exact proposed combination.

## 3. Account–asset divergence: established concern, narrower empirical opportunity

### 3.1 The closest accounting literature

Mancini et al. (2017), *Stocks and flows of natural capital: Implications for Ecological Footprint*, is central, not an incidental citation. It examines how stock and flow concepts enter the framework, discusses cropland-account limitations and soil depletion, and proposes land-type-specific research into stock versus flow use. The proposed follow-up should explicitly position itself as an observational implementation and test of this established agenda. [R01](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2017.01.033).

Borucke et al. set out biocapacity as area × yield factor × equivalence factor, summed across relevant types. Their cropland discussion explains accounting constraints on production footprint relative to biocapacity and omissions concerning soil depletion. Those identities are not measurements of a separately observed soil or biomass stock. Nor does a yield-factor increase demonstrate that all ecological functions improved. The 2013 methodology is a conceptual foundation, not a substitute for checking the selected 2026 tables. [R02](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2012.08.005).

Blomqvist et al. already argue that non-carbon account balances can coexist with ecological harm omitted from the measure, and distinguish national ecological deficits/trade dependence from global overshoot. Rees and Wackernagel defend the intended demand-on-regenerative-capacity interpretation. Galli et al. further discuss what productivity accounting can and cannot establish about ecological limits. A fair study should test a stated account interpretation, not refute an unstated claim that a global hectare measures every ecological stock or certifies safety. [R03](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001700), [R04](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001701), [R05](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2016.04.014).

Niccolucci et al.'s 3D framework and Chen et al.'s improved 3D Footprint application to Belt and Road Initiative countries already address stock/flow dependence and constraints on cross-category compensation. Chen et al. explicitly prohibit cross-category deficit compensation and allocate the carbon footprint using NPP, following Fang et al. (2018). Their account-derived stock-depletion quantities are not independent physical-stock observations. Acknowledge these approaches and, where the inputs are available, consider them as reporting comparators; review the underlying Fang methods before implementation. [R06](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2011.04.020), [R07](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2020.106315).

### 3.2 Independent observation is the crucial distinction

Karim et al. (2025) used 54 Sundarbans plots and studied biomass, stand structure, environmental gradients and a local biocapacity calculation. The reviewed methods construct the local yield factor using biomass-related inputs. This is an important empirical linkage, but it is not evidence that an externally constructed national account and independently measured stocks diverge through time. Reusing their approach would undermine the proposed independence claim; its published formula also needs explicit unit checking before any adaptation. [R39](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2025.127815).

**Recommended primary estimand:** directional agreement/disagreement between a specified territorial account/component and an independently sourced asset trend over the same declared period and boundary, allowing uncertainty-based abstention. Report all directional combinations and the eligible/assessed denominator. A national consumption footprint should not be treated as the pressure on domestic forest stocks without a trade/production bridge.

**Claim boundary:** disagreement is a difference in descriptions of different measured objects. It is not automatically an accounting error, ecological failure, causal effect of accounting, or evidence that one unit should be subtracted from the other. Never subtract global hectares from tonnes, nor interpret an account ratio as a stock-depletion horizon.

## 4. Conversion pathways and within-asset cancellation

### 4.1 Conversion is not a novel mechanism—and agriculture is not synonymous with cropland

SEEA distinguishes extent, condition and services. Vardon's discussion exposes difficult extent/condition/conversion boundaries, including temporary harvesting and persistence. The study therefore needs an operational persistence criterion, explicit source/destination classes and uncertain event dates. Tree-cover loss alone is not validated permanent ecosystem conversion. [R10](https://seea.un.org/sites/default/files/files/documents/2021/Oct/seea_ea_white_cover_final.pdf), [R11](https://seea.un.org/sites/default/files/vardon_theoretical-and-practical-issues-with-measurement-of-capacity-condition-and-conversion-in-the-seea-ecosystem_final-paper.pdf).

Potapov et al. already combined observed cropland expansion and productivity change with replacement of natural vegetation/tree cover. Their estimate that 49% of new cropland replaced that broader class is not a forest-only conversion estimate. A linked 2023 *Matters Arising* was not reviewed here; inspect it before relying on exact estimates. Pendrill et al. distinguish agriculture-driven deforestation from land subsequently used in productive agriculture and emphasize pasture. These precedents rule out novelty claims based merely on juxtaposing productive-area gains and natural-cover loss. [R40](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43016-021-00429-z), [R12](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abm9267).

The defensible increment would link **validated persistent transitions** to the chosen account components and separately measured stock changes, with uncertainty. At minimum distinguish forest→cropland, forest→pasture/other agriculture, forest→other/unknown, temporary disturbance/recovery and stable classes. Overlay association can locate co-occurrence; it does not establish the counterfactual causal contribution of conversion.

### 4.2 The closest forest-compensation comparator

Xu et al. (2024), *Changes in the Fine Composition of Global Forests from 2001 to 2020*, distinguish six management classes. They report planted-forest gains offsetting 59.56% of natural-forest area losses and 77.13% of biomass losses. This is a close empirical predecessor to “aggregate gains conceal losses in a different forest stratum.” Critically, their biomass input is 10 km and resampled to 250 m: the latter is not independent biomass information at 250 m. A proposed finer-scale study must demonstrate real effective support, not claim an improvement from pixel size alone. [R08](https://doi.org/10.34133/remotesensing.0119).

Maes et al. likewise report European forest-condition improvement on average alongside declining condition across roughly one-third of the area. Their condition/reference construction is not identical to biomass, but the aggregation issue is already empirical. Reference levels, indicators, weights and forest-type composition belong in the comparison. [R09](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-39434-0).

FAO's latest assessment makes a valuable competing benchmark: increasing global biomass after 2000, heterogeneous regional trends, and declining naturally regenerating/primary-forest area. Those findings need not contradict a satellite product with different definitions or pools. They prohibit assuming that the follow-up must find net global biomass decline. [R34](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/2dee6e93-1988-4659-aa89-30dd20b43b15/content/FRA-2025/key-findings.html).

### 4.3 Retain cancellation as an estimand, not a new concept

For local mass changes on a fixed common footprint and declared spatial units, define:

- `G = Σ max(δm_r, 0)`;
- `L = Σ max(−δm_r, 0)`;
- `ΔM = G − L`;
- optional cancellation description `1 − |ΔM|/(G + L)`, undefined when `G + L = 0`.

These are sums of **positive and negative local net changes**, not gross biological growth, recruitment, harvest or mortality. Within-unit turnover is not recovered by this decomposition. The ratio is an optional descriptive summary, not an externally validated ecological threshold or a claim to mathematical priority.

Noise can make both G and L positive even under no real change. Therefore compare against correlated-error nulls, aggregate to defensible support, test spatial scale, report uncertainty and stratify by observation quality. Do not infer loss of all ecological functions from biomass loss, or functional replacement of natural forests from biomass gains elsewhere. The claim worth testing is the robustness and account relevance of the decomposition, not the existence of gross/net differences.

## 5. Calibration, paths, references and demography

### 5.1 Whole-pipeline calibration is a strong design requirement, not conceptual novelty

Morris et al.'s ADEMP framework supplies the right discipline: specify aims, data-generating mechanisms, estimands, methods and performance measures. Their discussion explicitly distinguishes proof-of-concept, stress testing and realistic head-to-head comparison. The proposed increment is a transparent application to the **whole selected reporting pipeline**, not the invention of simulation calibration. [R18](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086).

Include eligibility screening, crosswalk construction, masking, temporal smoothing, change classification, uncertainty propagation and any outcome-dependent selection. Simulate null/concordant improvement, concordant decline, between-category compensation, within-category cancellation, disturbance/recovery and biased/shared-input measurements. Avoid generating truth only from the same mechanism that favors the proposed diagnostic. Vary spatial/temporal covariance, systematic bias, missingness, effective resolution and transition misclassification, including interactions where feasible.

Report false-positive rates, sensitivity, interval coverage, uncertainty-based abstention, assessed coverage and Monte Carlo standard errors. A report with low false alarms because it abstains on almost everything is not automatically useful. Simulated operating characteristics are conditional on the scenarios; independent reference data are still necessary for empirical validation. No calibration result here is a future ecological forecast or a sustainability certificate.

Olofsson et al. support probability-sample, area-adjusted change-map accuracy and uncertainty. Ploton et al. warn about optimistic spatial model evaluation; Wadoux et al. explain why spatial cross-validation is not the same target as design-based map accuracy. Use each for its proper purpose: reference-sample accuracy/area inference; spatial or temporal holdouts for predictive generalization; and appropriate clustered/correlated uncertainty for the final estimand. “Use spatial cross-validation” alone does not solve all three. [R17](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2014.02.015), [R19](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-18321-y), [R20](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2021.109692).

### 5.2 Paths and endpoints: demonstrate incremental information

LandTrendr already segments annual forest trajectories into disturbance and recovery. The abstract-level precedent is sufficient to reject priority for the general path idea; a full algorithm comparison would require deeper methods review. The study can instead test whether a measured path summary changes interpretation beyond endpoints, ordinary trends and a simple disturbance/recovery baseline. [R16](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/54160).

Keep three objects distinct:
1. **Observed endpoint change:** difference between supported measurements.
2. **Observed path information:** disturbance/recovery timing or duration supported by repeated observations.
3. **Reference deficit:** departure from a declared reference or counterfactual state.

Erb et al.'s actual-versus-potential biomass comparison concerns a counterfactual reference, not an observed earlier stock. It cannot be substituted for a historical trend or used to infer the date of loss. [R26](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature25138).

Do not call every annual predicted value an independent observation. CCI annual averages are not event-specific before/after snapshots. Khan et al.'s annual crop labels use multi-year features, gap filling and removal of isolated-year changes; those operations matter for apparent timing and persistence. [R28](https://ceos.org/gst/cci-biomass.html), [R14](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2026.115438).

### 5.3 Demographic replenishment stays conditional

McDowell et al. establish forest demographic change as an existing ecological field. Poorter et al. demonstrate attribute-specific recovery times: the 2016 abstract reports median 90% biomass recovery of 66 years across 45 Neotropical sites; the 2021 study covers 77 tropical sites and 12 attributes, with biomass/species-composition recovery exceeding 12 decades. These are different populations and attributes, not interchangeable global thresholds or a contradiction. [R21](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaz9463), [R22](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature16512), [R23](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/multidimensional-tropical-forest-recovery/).

A worthwhile module requires repeated recruitment, mortality, cohort/size structure or age observations and a tested increment beyond total biomass. Aggregate stock trajectories alone do not identify recruitment suppression versus harvest, mortality or growth change. Do not transfer the V18 common-logistic variance identity, illustrative recruitment function or universal regeneration penalty into an empirical claim. Without suitable observations, report this module as deferred rather than populate it with unvalidated proxies.

## 6. Data grounding: what the latest sources change

### 6.1 Account vintage and the provisional window

The NEFBA 2026 release was public on 22 April 2026 and reports a series through 2025. The accompanying GFN methodology distinguishes full UN-based results for **1961–2023** from estimated years **2024–2026** used in nowcasting. Therefore the proposed **2015–2023** comparison is consistent with the latest full-data account window. Do not exclude 2023 because an older 2025 edition ended its full-data coverage earlier. [R35](https://footprint.info.yorku.ca/files/2026/04/NEFBA_ReleaseNotes.pdf?x92789), [R36](https://overshoot.footprintnetwork.org/content/uploads/2026/05/2026-Nowcast-explained.pdf).

Freeze the exact downloadable account/OWID vintage, land category, total/per-capita definition and production/consumption boundary. The 2026 release changes historical estimates: GHSL-SMOD built-up-area treatment, fish trade/discards, forest commodity/recovered-paper trade, updated ocean carbon uptake and country areas affect different components. These are data/method revisions, not newly measured ecological changes. Never construct a temporal trend by splicing years from different editions without a harmonization analysis. Earlier internal numerical results remain vintage-specific until rerun; this review has not recomputed them.

### 6.2 Biomass and cropland

ESA Biomass CCI v7 was published on 21 May 2026, with 100 m maps for 18 epochs: 2005–2012 and 2015–2024, plus uncertainty/change information. CEOS guidance warns of regional bias, differing observation quality and the need for independent forest masks and local change validation. Map standard deviations do not, by themselves, supply the full temporal/spatial covariance needed for reliable change inference. Start with a small validated pilot, not an immediate global cancellation map. [R27](https://catalogue.ceda.ac.uk/uuid/6429d1aafe1e43b9b414e4a5a7f8b903/), [R28](https://ceos.org/gst/cci-biomass.html).

Ma et al.'s CONUS mature-forest result is a warning about underdetected growth, not a global correction factor. Kowalski et al.'s European gross biomass losses are not net stock change. Neither licenses selecting the direction of the final result. [R24](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-026-03151-w), [R25](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-026-02032-y).

Khan et al.'s 30 m cropland product is a strong candidate but its high extent accuracy must not be presented as transition accuracy. The reviewed table reports overall extent accuracy of 97.8%/97.3% for 2015/2023, whereas gain user/producer accuracy is 64.8%/47.3% and loss 62.8%/40.7%. These are product-study results, not validation of the proposed forest→crop sample. Check the crop definition, independently validate transitions and preserve temporal uncertainty. [R14](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2026.115438), [R15](https://glad.umd.edu/dataset/annual-croplands).

### 6.3 Conditional supporting modules

**Soil:** HU-SoilCarbonGrids 2026 provides annual modelled SOC at 100 m for 1992–2023, 0–30 cm, with uncertainty. Its underlying SIMS evidence comprises 1,048 sites and 9,385 observations over 11 epochs during 1992–2022. Bulk density/coarse fragments use fixed 1992 measurements; Landsat/climate covariates create potential shared-input dependence with other layers. This improves Hungarian benchmark feasibility but does not provide annual independent observations or eliminate bulk-density/equivalent-soil-mass issues. Pilot validation, predictor-lineage review and uncertainty checks are conditions of use. Do not automatically expand the core into a soil paper. [R30](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejss.70292).

**Wetlands:** GIEMS-MC supplies monthly inundation for 1992–2020 at 0.25°. It can contextualize hydrology but is not a fine-scale persistent conversion map or a measure of all wetland functions. Keep the module conditional on fit-for-purpose extent/condition evidence. [R31](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/2985/2025/).

**Fish:** RAM v4.66 has assessment-only and extended versions. The extended archive explicitly adds conversions and post-hoc modelled reference points and recommends assessment-only for typical users. Extract source assessments, biological units, vintage and coverage before selection. Landings are not spawning stock biomass; a historical limit reference point is not automatically current in 2026. No fish series or current stock assessment was analysed in this review. [R32](https://zenodo.org/records/14043031), [R33](https://zenodo.org/records/14043038).

## 7. Recommended contribution and decision gates

### A defensible working formulation

> We evaluate the agreement between selected regenerative-capacity accounts and independently measured ecological-asset trajectories on matched spatial and temporal supports. We assess validated conversion pathways and spatially offsetting local net changes, and test whether predeclared spatial and trajectory reporting packages improve calibrated identification of the specified physical-change states beyond simpler account and endpoint baselines.

This wording states a research objective. It neither anticipates disagreement nor claims global novelty, causality, ecological safety or complete asset coverage.

### Retain
- **Core:** forest–cropland account/asset comparison, independently measured forest-stock change, validated transitions, explicit account/extent/density decomposition and all eligible outcomes.
- **Core refinement:** within-asset spatial cancellation, only after effective-support and noisy-null checks.
- **Core methods:** whole-pipeline operating-characteristic calibration and independent empirical validation where feasible.
- **Path module:** retain only where actual observation support can distinguish temporal patterns, and benchmark against endpoints/trends/simple disturbance descriptions.

### Conditional
- Predeclared nested reporting packages: aggregate → component → spatial → path → demography. Compare incremental performance at matched false-positive rate and assessed coverage; include uncertainty and collection burden. More fields are not automatically better decisions.
- Demographic replenishment with repeated independent observations.
- A Hungarian soil benchmark and suitably supported wetland/fish cases. Do not force four complete global annual asset panels.

### Reject as contribution claims
- First discovery of stock/flow ambiguity, compensation, trajectory relevance, demography, or separation of extent/condition/services.
- A new index or vector architecture as the central empirical contribution.
- Causal conversion effects from overlays alone; physiological interpretation from biomass alone; safety thresholds from account balances; suppressed recruitment from aggregate stocks.
- Claimed fine-resolution biomass information created by resampling, or annual independence created by model prediction/interpolation.

### Gates before scaling

| Gate | Required evidence | If not met |
|---|---|---|
| G1: independence/alignment | Typed units, boundary/period agreement, outcome-input lineage, eligible population fixed before signs are inspected | Redesign comparison; no prevalence claim |
| G2: reliable change | Reference-sample transitions, validated biomass change, covariance/bias scenarios and effective-support checks | Restrict to a pilot or descriptive case; abstain where uncertain |
| G3: incremental information | Held-out/calibrated comparison with simple account, endpoint and trend baselines at comparable error and coverage | Drop performance/diagnostic novelty claim |
| G4: scoped inference | Concordant and divergent outcomes retained; explicit coverage, uncertainty and non-causal interpretation | No extrapolation to global ecological sustainability |
| G5: publication positioning | Central comparator methods and relevant debates checked; exact citations resolved; related-paper overlap disclosed | No first-ever or publication-independence assertion |

The accompanying design-action table turns these gates into 15 concrete tasks. They are recommendations, not amendments already made to the implementation plan.

## 8. Publication positioning and final verdict

The closest required comparator set is **Mancini** for the stock–flow research agenda; **Xu** and **Maes** for empirical compensation/aggregate masking; **Potapov/Pendrill** for conversion/productivity interpretation; **Karim** for biocapacity–biomass linkage; and **LandTrendr plus established map-accuracy/calibration literature** for the methodological additions. A manuscript should explain its difference in estimand, independence, boundary matching, effective resolution and validation—not merely list these papers in an introduction.

COPE distinguishes text recycling from redundant publication. Transparent citation/disclosure of related work, shared methods and reused material is necessary; acceptable reuse and publication independence remain journal-specific judgments. This review does not clear the proposed paper ethically or certify that internal companion papers have no overlapping publishable result. [R38](https://members.publicationethics.org/files/Web_A29298_COPE_Text_Recycling.pdf).

**Final verdict:** a narrowed empirical study is worth piloting. Its strongest possible contribution is the independently measured and uncertainty-qualified relationship between a specific account and specified asset changes, with demonstrably useful reporting diagnostics. Broad conceptual novelty is not defensible on the reviewed evidence. Exact-combination novelty remains unresolved and should be framed cautiously; even if the combination is uncommon, publication value must come from trustworthy data, validated inference and a substantive result—including a null or concordant result.
