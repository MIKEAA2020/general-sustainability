# Response to Reviewer

**Manuscript title:** A rising biocapacity total can mask ecological-capital land drawdown: a two-land model

**Manuscript number:** ECOMOD-26-1191

**Version of record for this response:** ECOMOD C20 A01, 31 October 2026 — main text 38 pages, Supplementary Information 31 pages
**Preprint deposit:** https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202611.0852.v1

**Note on versioning.** This response supersedes the earlier response letter in full. The reviewer's comments were made on an earlier single-stock draft. The manuscript has since been rebuilt, and several statements in the previous reply are now themselves out of date — most importantly the units convention, which we have reversed to the convention the reviewer asked for (point b), and the empirical window, which is now 1961–2023 rather than 1961–2022. Reviewer quotations are reproduced verbatim and unedited below. Section locators refer to the submitted version.

## Summary of substantive change since the reviewed draft

1. The model is two-land, not single-stock: conserved total area is divided among fast-provisioning land `A_f`, ecological-capital land `A_c` and reserve `A_r`, with an uncalibrated per-hectare quality state `q`, delayed regeneration, delayed demographic response and an internal leaky deficit `D`. The single-stock model survives only as an explicit comparator (SI S7, Figs. S1–S4).
2. The "fold" language is gone. The drawdown result is a minimum-area boundary reached by the reduced fixed-demand equality relation, with no fold and no critical slowing in that relation; this is explicitly scoped and is not asserted for every trajectory of the delayed model (§3.2, SI S2).
3. New analytic result: sharp static identification bounds, with attainment/sharpness proof and feasible endpoint witnesses (§3.5, SI S9).
4. New accounting result: signed integrated contribution budgets with timestep refinement and closure checks (§3.6, SI S10).
5. New empirical basis: NFA Edition 2026, 1961–2023, 63 complete annual world observations, plus component decomposition, a Divisia sensitivity, an OWID cropland-area/cereal-yield proxy with explicit residual and a crop calibration (§3.7, SI S5).
6. New operational layer: five conditional predictions P1–P5 with observables, independent calibration inputs, estimands and rejection criteria (§3.8, SI S11).
7. Units convention corrected to the reviewer's suggestion (point b).

## (a) The orchard analogy

> The orchard analogy — number of trees × average productivity rate = total productivity rate. If your needs exceed the total productivity rate such that all the apples in the orchard cannot fully meet them, then you will have to start killing trees to cover your liabilities, which leads to fewer trees and a vicious cycle with a positive feedback loop; your future liabilities will increase and your problems will get worse over time. Another analogy is hens that produce eggs: if you start killing hens to cover your liabilities, a vicious cycle unleashes.

The analogy now matches the equations rather than preceding them loosely. It is stated in §1 with its terms mapped explicitly: standing trees and living soil are the asset; the annual harvest is the flow; transferring ground from ecological-capital use to fast-provisioning use is the conversion that raises the annual account while reducing the capital-land base. The equation set implements exactly this: `B = b_f A_f + Υ(q) A_c` (§2.1, Eq. 3), with `Υ(q) = b_c + b_{G,c} G_c(q)`.

Two qualifications are stated with the analogy, because they are what the model can and cannot support. First, the conversion term raises `B` only conditionally — when the receiving class has the higher per-hectare contribution — and other flows can offset that increment. Second, the analogy is not evidence that NFA accounts measure physical stocks, and it does not assert that the model's classes map onto NFA categories. A second analogy (an elevator operating above its rated load) separates observed service from structural condition. Replanting is explicitly **not** treated as a one-for-one restoration of the distinct assets the capital-land class represents; restoration in the model requires an explicit surplus-gated flow (§3.4).

**Where this now lives:** §1 (analogies), §2.1 (equations), §3.4 (recovery asymmetry), §4.1 (what the model shows and does not show).

## (b) Units

> The units are inconsistent. Accumulation should be expressed in global hectare–years (gha·years), whereas Footprint is measured in global hectares (gha) — not gha per year. This needs correction for clarity and consistency.

We agree, and the manuscript now adopts the reviewer's convention. In the submitted version:

- **`B`, `E`, `Y_f`, `Y_c` and the shortfall `S` are annual account amounts reported in gha, with the annual basis implicit** — this is how the Global Footprint Network reports footprint and biocapacity for a given year. They are **not** written `gha·yr⁻¹`.
- **The accumulated deficit `D` is the only stock-like quantity** and is written in `gha·yr`. It enters only the degradation factor `exp(−αD)` in the fast-yield law (Eq. 7); `Ḋ` and `ηD` are in gha.
- **Per-capita footprint `e` is `gha·cap⁻¹`**, so `E = eP` is an annual account amount in gha.
- `q` is dimensionless; the quality-growth conversion coefficient `b_{G,c}` carries the units that make `Υ` a per-hectare annual contribution; areas are in ha.

The earlier reply proposed the alternative convention (`B`, `E` as `gha·yr⁻¹` flows). That was internally consistent but diverged from GFN practice and from the reviewer's reading, and it has been withdrawn. The correction is applied throughout: the parameter table, the SI symbol table, derivations, captions and the exported CSV headers. No functional form, parameter value or simulated trajectory was changed by this correction.

**Where this now lives:** §2.1 (units paragraph following the equations), Table 1, SI S1 (complete symbol table with units), SI S2 (derivations).

## (c) Assumptions about parameters

> Per-capita requirements are treated as fixed, but in reality they are not. Similarly, the productivity factor for biocapacity may change over time. These parameters should be treated as dynamic rather than static.

Partially agreed, and the two cases are different.

- **Per-hectare productivity is already dynamic.** The fast-land yield is `b_f = (b_{f0} + T_b(t)) exp(−αD)`: a bounded logistic technology term `T_b(t)` plus a debt-driven erosion term. The capital-land contribution moves with the quality state through `Υ(q)`. The technology term is off in the baseline, figures and tables and is active only in the matched wave/no-wave experiment of SI S8, where it is switched on and off with all states and parameters held identical, so its effect is attributable. That experiment also corrected an earlier bookkeeping error: the v43 recovery outcome that drove capital area to about 2.44 ha and reserve below its floor was a defect in propagating `T_b`, and the corrected run gives `max A_c = 1.731 ha`, `min A_r = 0.321 ha`, final `B = 0.899 gha`, with an area-conservation residual of 4.44e-16 ha. The erroneous values are withdrawn, not reported.
- **Per-capita demand is deliberately held fixed in the baseline, and is stated as an assumption.** Endogenising `e` is a scope extension, not a repair, and it is named as such in §4.5.

The reviewer's underlying concern is that a co-evolving per-capita requirement would change the conclusions. The manuscript now answers this empirically rather than by assertion: the NFA decomposition (§3.7) reports biocapacity per person falling 52.71% while the aggregate rises 24.86% and production footprint per person rises 16.73%, so the aggregate-versus-per-person divergence is measured, not assumed away.

**Where this now lives:** §2.1 (Eq. 7, Eqs. 8–9), §2.2 (parameter status), §3.3 (finite technology gains, debt), §3.7 (per-person empirical series), SI S8 (controlled wave experiment), §4.5 (limitations).

## (d) Missing references and data limitations

> The paper discusses national Footprint accounts without citing the relevant literature. Including these references is important, as it would clarify that such accounts are approximations constrained by available UN datasets.

The National Footprint Accounts literature is cited where the accounts are introduced and where they are used: methodology and standards (Wackernagel & Rees 1996; Global Footprint Network 2009, 2019), the peer-reviewed account description (Borucke et al. 2013), the compensation/aggregation critique (Nardo 2008; Becker et al. 2017; Fischer et al. 2022), and the methodological objections (Blomqvist et al. 2013; Giampietro & Saltelli 2014; van den Bergh & Grazi 2015; Galli et al. 2016). The composite-indicator literature is used to frame the aggregation problem, not merely listed.

> In those National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts: Biocapacity is likely overstated because environmental degradation is poorly captured; because of data limitations, the fragility of biocapacity is not reflected, leading to an underestimation of ecological overshoot.

This is now a stated limitation with the correct direction of bias, not a caveat buried in prose. §3.7 and §4.2 state that NFA accounts are annual account values built from measured yields and equivalence factors, that they carry no soil erosion, deforestation or groundwater depletion, and that the accounts therefore most likely **overstate** biocapacity and **understate** overshoot. §4.2 also states the consequence for our own empirical reading: the observed account composition change is a change in account values, not a measurement of forest biomass, soil condition, wetland extent, fish biomass or the model's `A_c`. SI S5.7 adds a component-level physical-stock measurement specification (extent versus condition, stock versus flow, overlap cautions, valuation requirements) so that the gap between account values and asset condition is explicit rather than rhetorical.

**Where this now lives:** §1 (account description and citations), §3.7, §4.2, §4.5, SI S5.7 (physical-stock specification).

> For this reason, the statement (line 328) that "global data from 1961–2022 are consistent with this interpretation: biocapacity grew modestly while ecological overshoot persisted, suggesting that technology has been outpacing degradation" is misleading and should be reconsidered.

The sentence has been removed. It has been replaced with the actual decomposition, reported with endpoints, an exact log identity and a component attribution, and with an explicit statement that these are annual account changes rather than physical-stock observations:

- world biocapacity 9.7254 → 12.1428 ×10^9 gha (+24.86%; Δln B = +0.221997);
- population 3.06487 → 8.09173 billion; biocapacity per person 3.17319 → 1.50065 gha cap⁻¹ (−52.71%);
- production footprint per person 2.19700 → 2.56462 gha cap⁻¹ (+16.73%); `E_prod/B` 0.69236 → 1.70901, first exceeding one in 1973;
- component contributions to the +24.86% aggregate change: cropland +24.06, built-up +7.44, forest −5.95, fishing grounds −0.42, grazing −0.27 percentage points.

No claim is made that technology has been outpacing degradation. The 2024–2025 estimated rows are excluded from the analysis and retained only for provenance; 2022 appears as a same-edition sensitivity, not as the primary window.

**Where this now lives:** §3.7 and Table 5, SI S5 (data and vintages, Divisia sensitivity, level fallback).

## (e) Missing empirical grounding

> A key challenge is demonstrating how overuse leads to declining biocapacity; the relationship, while intuitive, is not universally accepted, particularly by those who argue technological progress offsets degradation. To strengthen the paper: provide empirical evidence supporting this link; estimate parameters rather than asserting them; justify modelling choices. At present the model assumes dynamics rather than demonstrating them empirically.

We respond to the four sub-points in turn, and we are explicit about what remains unachieved.

1. **Empirical evidence supporting the link.** The paper now separates two evidence streams and refuses to let one stand for the other. The NFA decomposition measures account composition (1961–2023, 63 annual world observations). The two-land model is a conditional mechanism analysis with representative, not fitted, parameters. The manuscript states in §4.1 and §4.5 that no field dataset, causal identification or parameter fit is claimed, and that NFA accounts do not measure physical ecological capital. What the empirical work does establish is the accounting fact the paper is about: the aggregate rose while the per-person quantity fell by half and while the cropland contribution supplied essentially the whole net increase.
2. **Parameter estimation.** Parameters remain representative rather than estimated, and this is stated in §2.2 rather than implied. What has been added is that the *consequences* of the parameter choices are now bounded: sharp static intervals under independent observations (§3.5), a yield sweep and delay/quality-law sensitivity ladders (SI S3–S4), a timestep refinement and closure study (SI S10), and the demonstration that the readout `Υ(q)` is non-monotone, so `q = 0.1` and `q = 0.9` give the same per-hectare contribution and quality is not identifiable from yield alone (§3.5, SI S9).
3. **Justification of modelling choices.** Each structural choice is stated as a numbered assumption with its scope: conserved area with one-to-one transfers (§2.1), conversion only when the shortfall is positive and only up to a lower area bound (Eq. 4), reserve return only under positive surplus (Eq. 5), a projected quality equation with an *imposed* admissibility cap `q_max = 1` that is not an estimated ecological threshold (§2.1), and an internal deficit state that is not an area, a physical stock or an NFA quantity.
4. **Self-referentiality — "assumes the dynamics and then reproduces them".** The response is that the paper's central results are not trajectory shapes. They are (i) a conditional algebraic condition for a rising aggregate with a falling capital-land base, (ii) a static identification result with attainment proofs, and (iii) an accounting decomposition whose closure error is tested and shown to shrink under refinement. The trajectory is an illustration whose parameters are declared representative.
5. **Falsifiability, which the earlier draft lacked.** §3.8 and SI S11 now specify five conditional predictions with the observable required, the independent calibration inputs, the estimand and the rejection outcome for each, including the instruction that regime assumptions are to be checked independently of whether the fitted relaxation favours the prediction. The manuscript states plainly that no row of that map is passed by the present account decomposition.

**Where this now lives:** §2.2, §3.5, §3.6, §3.7, §3.8, §4.1, §4.2, §4.5, SI S3–S5, S9–S11.

## (f) Clarity of the model

> The equations and their underlying assumptions are not clearly presented. Key parameters — such as ρ, which represents regeneration rate, and γ, which appears to be defined but unclear — are not sufficiently explained. The model treats e (per-capita requirement) as fixed without clear justification. This lack of clarity extends to the definition of symbols, meaning of terms, and justification for key assumptions, making it difficult for readers to assess the validity of the modelling approach.

The presentation has been restructured around exactly these items:

- **A complete symbol table with units** in SI S1, and a parameter table in the main text (§2.2, Table 1) giving each parameter's role, baseline value, and whether it is fixed by convention, imposed for admissibility, or representative.
- **The single-stock `γ` parameter no longer appears in the two-land model.** The one-stock comparator, which used `γ` and a different `q` meaning, is isolated in SI S7 with its own notation block; the main text warns that comparator `q` (a rate coefficient), comparator `κ`, and two-land `κ_w` are distinct symbols. This removes the symbol collision the comment identified.
- **Each assumption is numbered and justified in place** (§2.1), including why per-capita demand is held fixed in the baseline, why the quality cap is imposed rather than estimated, and why restoration is asymmetric.
- **Every equation is dimensionally annotated** under the reviewer's units convention (point b), so that annual account amounts, the deficit stock and per-capita quantities cannot be confused.
- **Model scope is stated in §4.5** with the list of what is representative versus empirically anchored (the capital-land regeneration anchors in SI S5 and the provisioning-book yield and area in SI S5).

**Where this now lives:** §2.1–§2.4, Table 1, §4.5, SI S1, SI S7.

## (g) Didactic clarity and overall assessment

> The overall idea is understandable and the message is compelling, but the paper remains overly speculative and theoretical in its current form. Significant revision is needed to improve clarity, empirical grounding, and methodological rigor.

The revision responds on all three axes, and it also narrows the claims where the earlier draft overreached:

- **Clarity:** the paper is now organised as Introduction → Methods (model, parameters, data, numerics) → Results (masking, minimum-area boundary, technology and debt, recovery, identification bounds, contribution budgets, NFA composition, predictions) → Discussion (what the model shows, measurement implications, policy controls, relation to prior work, limitations) → Conclusions. The single-stock material, the long robustness ladders and the full derivations have moved to the SI (S1–S12), where every float is cited from the main text.
- **Empirical grounding:** the NFA Edition 2026 decomposition, per-capita series, component attribution, proxy construction and sensitivity analysis described under (d) and (e).
- **Methodological rigour:** a proof of the composition identity and of the deficit-side affine structure, sharp static identification bounds with sharpness and attainment, contribution budgets with a monotone closure-error study, and an operational falsification map.
- **Narrowing:** the earlier draft's general "no critical slowing / floor-collision" framing is now restricted to the reduced fixed-demand equality relation; the terminal behaviour is described as an asymptotic approach to balance rather than floor contact; floor-proximity thresholds are labelled as proximity measures; the 20/40-year equality is an imposed-cap consistency test, not an ecological prediction; and the paper states explicitly that it is not a forecast and that it does not show a measured decline in physical ecological capital.

Residual gaps we do not claim to have closed are listed in §4.5: parameters are representative rather than fitted, the account-to-asset mapping is not identified, and the predictions are stated to be testable rather than tested.
