## 2. Sperner — decision unchanged; `P3_Binomial` scoped

**The bound stays cited, not formalized, by decision.** That is settled
and was not reopened.

The separate question was whether `P3_Binomial` is **half-built** or
**complete machinery**. Scoped against the module and the v31 record:

**It is complete machinery. Nothing is half-built.** `choose` is defined
by Pascal recursion and every law the module claims is proved:
`choose_succ_succ` (by `rfl`), `choose_zero_right`, `choose_zero_succ`,
`choose_eq_zero_of_lt`, `choose_self`, `choose_pos`,
`choose_succ_self`, plus `choose_six_three` as a `native_decide` check
that the recursion computes what it should.

The module does exactly the job it was built for: `prop:antichain` (iii)
could not previously be **stated**, because the layer had no binomials at
all (`Nat.choose`, `Nat.factorial`, `List.permutations` are all unknown
identifiers). It is **machinery only**.

The three steps to a Sperner proof were never claimed:

1. unimodality `C(n,k) ≤ C(n, n/2)` — attempted and **failed
   informatively**: the two-step recursion needs `2(k+2) = n+1`, i.e.
   binomial *symmetry*, so symmetry must precede unimodality;
2. LYM by maximal-chain counting — needs factorials, a permutation
   enumerator, and `C(n,k)·k!·(n−k)! = n!`;
3. the cardinality bound.

Step 1's failure is a **recorded finding about a route not taken**, not
an unfinished proof. The heading said "(v31, INCOMPLETE)", which could
mislead a reader into thinking the module is broken; relabelled
"(v31, complete machinery)" with a scoping note.
