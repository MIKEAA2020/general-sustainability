## Paper 1 → Environmental Modelling & Software

The paper introduces a typed, domain-agnostic architecture that makes sustainability claims auditable — every object a type, every claim an explicit status, every cross-model statement a declared map — and proves a separation theorem for assessment doctrines: endpoint-only accounting, scalarized aggregate assessment (weak sustainability), and noncompensatory typed assessment (strong sustainability) form a strictly nested hierarchy, and the gap between the last two is exactly a quantifier non-commutativity (∃π∀d versus ∀d∃π over assessment weights). We believe this suits EM&S's methods and open-science scope directly: the paper is a formal framework plus a reproducible machine witness, and its central methodological contribution — negative results retained at first-class status with declared certification levels — is an open-science practice as much as a mathematical one. The work is standalone; four companion empirical studies and four companion theory papers are under separate review elsewhere and are not cited as evidence here. All data and code underlying the machine witness are publicly available, and the authors declare no conflicts of interest.

## E1 → Fisheries Research

Dear Editor,

We submit "Does a surplus-production ladder improve forecasts of Northern cod? A scored test on NAFO 2J3KL" for consideration as a research article in Fisheries Research.

The paper is a preregistered, scored model-ablation test on the NAFO 2J3KL Northern cod stock: nested surplus-production modules against last-value persistence and an autonomous Schaefer baseline, on the 1983–2015 NCAM M-shift SSB series (DFO SAR 2016/026) and an unpooled 1954–2024 extended specification. The finding is a clean negative result: no structural module beats persistence on out-of-sample RMSE at either horizon (one-year 98 kt versus 115–196 kt; five-year 265 kt versus 289–488 kt); the collapse window is missed by every model (694–819 kt), showing a constant-productivity surplus model with a 1992 catch drop cannot produce the observed crash; and the recovery-window Allee fit is unidentified. We report these as negative certificates — machine-verified findings of non-retention — rather than as a failure to reject baselines.

We believe Fisheries Research is the right home for honest forecast-evaluation work with assessment-adjacent methods: the retention rule is preregistered, the scoring is deterministic and byte-reproducible (independent rerun verified), and the result bears directly on what structure assessment pipelines are entitled to add. A companion short communication (submitted separately) reports the corresponding intervention-selection analysis. The authors declare no conflicts of interest.

Sincerely,
The authors

## E3 → Groundwater

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Does a one-pool water-balance model improve forecasts of Edwards Aquifer head? A scored test at J-17," for consideration as a research article in Groundwater.

The paper is a preregistered, scored forecast evaluation on the 90-year daily head record of the Edwards (Balcones Fault Zone) Aquifer J-17 index well: output-only AR(1), stock-and-flow recharge modules, climate-informed (ENSO-conditioned) recharge, and a residual module, all scored against last-value persistence on fixed and rolling-origin windows. The findings are negative certificates with one constructive retention: persistence (13.23 ft one-year RMSE) is not beaten by any causal module (the best causal module scores 14.70 ft; an output-only AR(1) is retained at 12.84 ft, a 0.39-ft margin; an oracle bounds the information-layer rent at 7.55 ft); at five years climatology beats persistence (16.80 versus 21.11 ft); and climate-informed recharge modules are not retained on the primary score. The karst system's description engages the regional modelling literature (Scanlon et al. 2003), and the benchmarking design engages the groundwater-forecasting and forecasting-competition literature (Daliakopoulos et al. 2005; Adamowski & Chan 2011; Makridakis et al. 2020; Ropelewski & Halpert 1986).

We believe Groundwater's applied forecasting readership will value a benchmark study that says precisely when simple baselines suffice. The protocols were frozen before computation (dated 2026-08-25/26), and the scoring is deterministic with a byte-identical independent rerun. The authors declare no conflicts of interest.

Sincerely,

## E4 → Journal of Water Resources Planning and Management

Dear Editor,

We submit "Governance operators and viability kernels of the Edwards Aquifer: an intervention-selection test at J-17" for consideration as an applied management-analysis article in the Journal of Water Resources Planning and Management.

The paper scores pumping-rule families — flat caps, staged cuts, and reactive critical-period management (CPM) rules — on the J-17 index-well record against robust and certified protection criteria. The constructive result: reactive rules deliver 3.3–16.2% more permitted pumping supply than flat caps at matched robust protection, and the CPM cascade matches the deepest cut's protection at 50.6% more supply. The boundary results are equally concrete: business-as-usual is not robustly viable beyond approximately 13 years under a perpetual-drought floor (the 692.6-ft boundary at year 12), a 7.2% mean cut restores invariance, and at the institutional 660-ft trigger every tested policy is equivalent to business-as-usual — protection there comes from wet years, not from the pumping family. The certified layer is kept strictly distinct (defect-bound to three years; the 15.41-ft training maximum is exceeded out of sample at 21.81 ft).

We believe this fits JWRPM's drought-trigger and policy-scoring register: the deliverable is a scored robustness comparison with explicit trigger thresholds, engaging the drought-indicator literature (Steinemann 2003) and the Edwards regional context (Scanlon et al. 2003; Watkins & McKinney 1997). The intervention protocol was frozen before computation (2026-08-26) and the analysis is deterministic with verified reruns. The authors declare no conflicts of interest.

Sincerely,
The authors
