### 1. Direct Contradiction and Redundancy with Paper 1

* **The Paper's Core Premise (Abstract & Introduction):**
  > *"The obstruction calculus to date states its certificates for one constraint at a time. This paper, the D4 item of the programme’s generalization catalogue, states the common-action obstruction for vector floors $V = \{x : q_j(x) \ge 0, j = 1, \dots, m\}$..."* (Abstract, lines 3–6)
  > *"The programme’s completed items... share one working simplification: the safe set is presented through a single scalar margin."* (Introduction, lines 4–8)
* **The Contradiction:**
  This claim mischaracterizes Paper 1 (*An Obstruction Calculus for Viability under Incomplete Observation*):
  1. **Paper 1, Section 2.2** explicitly defines the constraint set as a vector of $m$ constraints from the start:
     $$V = \{x : q_j(x) \ge 0, \; j = 1, \dots, m\}.$$
  2. **Paper 1, Section 2.2** defines the safe-control correspondence across all active constraints:
     $$\mathcal{R}_V(x) = \{u \in U(x) : \forall d \in D(x), \forall j \text{ with } q_j(x) = 0, \; \nabla q_j(x) \cdot f(x, u, d) \ge 0\}.$$
  3. **Paper 1, Section 3.2** already gives the exact stacked polyhedral Farkas certificate:
     > *"For polyhedral data the emptiness certificate is a Farkas pair: with safe actions given by finitely many affine inequalities $A_i u \le b_i$ across compatible states, stacked as $Au \le b$, the intersection is empty if and only if there exists $\mu \ge 0$ with $\mu^\top A = 0$ and $\mu^\top b < 0$ (Farkas, 1902; Gale, 1960)..."* (Paper 1, page 6, col 1).
  4. **Paper 1, Section 3.4** is titled **"A worked certificate (two floors, one scalar action)"**, working through $u \le 0.4$ and $u \ge 0.6$ with Farkas multiplier $\lambda = (1/2, 1/2)$.
* **Consequence:** Proposition 2 and the polyhedral formulation in this paper do not constitute an extension (item D4); they are a verbatim duplication of what was already derived and published in Sections 3.2 and 3.4 of Paper 1.

### 2. Conceptual Error: "Complementary Slackness" in Infeasible Systems

* **Remark 1 & Example E6:**
  > *"Remark 1 (Complementarity reading). In the stacked system of a three-floor instance below, the certified multiplier assigns weight 0 to a redundant row: inactive constraints receive zero multipliers, the standard complementary pairing of primal activity and dual weight, here read as 'the obstruction is carried by exactly the floors that bind.'"* (Page 2, col 1)
  > *"E6 (three floors, complementarity). The stacked system $\{u \le 2/5\} \wedge \{u \ge 3/5\} \wedge \{u \le 9/10\}$ is infeasible with certificate $\lambda = (1, 1, 0)$: the inactive third row carries multiplier zero, exhibiting the complementarity reading of Remark 1."* (Page 2, col 1)
* **The Flaw:**
  * In optimization and duality theory, **complementary slackness** ($\lambda_i^* (b_i - A_i u^*) = 0$) is a property of a **primal feasible** optimal solution $u^*$ paired with a dual optimal solution $\lambda^*$.
  * In Farkas' Lemma, the primal system $Au \le b$ is **infeasible**—it has **no** solution. Because no $u$ exists, no constraint can be evaluated as "active" ($A_i u = b_i$) or "inactive/slack" ($A_i u < b_i$).
  * The fact that $\lambda_3 = 0$ for the constraint $u \le 9/10$ simply means that the first two constraints ($u \le 2/5$ and $-u \le -3/5$) already form an infeasible sub-system. In linear programming, this is known as a **Minimal Infeasible Subsystem (MIS)** or the support of the dual infeasibility ray.
  * Framing the sparsity of a Farkas dual certificate as *"the standard complementary pairing of primal activity and dual weight"* is mathematically incorrect.

### 3. Misattribution in Proposition 1: What is the "Vector-Floor Gap"?

* **Proposition 1 Statement:**
  > *"Proposition 1 (Branchwise/joint gap for vector floors). If for each state $x \in B$ separately $\mathcal{R}_V(x) \ne \emptyset$ but $\bigcap_{x \in B} \mathcal{R}_V(x) = \emptyset$, then every branch of $B$ is individually floor-servable while the belief is vector-obstructed: the information constraint, not any single floor, is the cause of nonviability."*
* **The Flaw:**
  1. The condition $\mathcal{R}_V(x) \ne \emptyset \; \forall x \in B$ and $\bigcap_{x \in B} \mathcal{R}_V(x) = \emptyset$ is **identical to the scalar common-action obstruction** (Theorem 2 of Paper 1). For example, with a single scalar floor $z \ge 0$, state $x_1$ may require $u = +1$ and state $x_2$ may require $u = -1$. Proposition 1 fires identically there.
  2. The proposition has **nothing specifically to do with vector floors**. 
  3. A genuine "vector-floor gap" under partial observation would analyze the failure of joint multi-floor satisfiability across states where each floor *individually* is compatible across the entire belief:
     $$\bigcap_{x \in B} \mathcal{R}_{\{q_j \ge 0\}}(x) \ne \emptyset \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, m\}, \quad \text{yet} \quad \bigcap_{j=1}^m \bigcap_{x \in B} \mathcal{R}_{\{q_j \ge 0\}}(x) = \emptyset.$$
     Proposition 1 does not address this interaction; it merely re-states Theorem 2 of Paper 1.
  4. In the proof, the author writes: *"hence no floor fails in isolation... The cause cannot be a floor: each is satisfiable alone."* 
     This is a non sequitur. $\mathcal{R}_V(x) \ne \emptyset$ means there is an action satisfying all floors simultaneously at state $x$. It does not evaluate floors in isolation.

### 5. Coherence and Notation Breakdown in Example E4

* **Example E4 Statement:**
  > *"E4 (floor monotonicity). Dropping the second floor ($q_1$ only) enlarges the viable pair family from 28 to 36 of 36 under full information, setwise, with strict separation witnessed (e.g. $\{(1, 1), (1, 2)\}$: viable on one floor, nonviable on two)."* (Page 2, col 1)
* **The Inconsistencies:**
  1. What is a **"viable pair"** under **"full information"**?
     * Under full information, the controller observes the exact state $x$. Viability is a property of individual states $x \in X$, not pairs of states.
     * If the author means ordered pairs of state coordinates $(z_1, z_2) \in \{1, \dots, 6\}^2$, why does the text write `{(1, 1), (1, 2)}` with set brackets, denoting a set of two distinct states?
     * If `{(1, 1), (1, 2)}` is an information set (a belief of two states), it belongs to **partial information**, which contradicts the explicit phrase *"under full information"*.
  2. In the Abstract (lines 10–11), the author writes: *"adding a floor strictly shrinks the viable pair family (28 $\to$ 36 reversed: one floor admits 36, two floors 28)"*. 
     This phrasing is confusing: dropping a floor enlarges the set ($28 \to 36$), while adding a floor shrinks it ($36 \to 28$). Calling it "28 $\to$ 36 reversed" introduces unnecessary ambiguity.

### 7. Triviality of the Numerical Examples

* In **E1**, the paper solves the scalar system:
  $$u \le \frac{2}{5}, \quad -u \le -\frac{3}{5}.$$
  This is literally the statement that $(-\infty, 0.4] \cap [0.6, \infty) = \emptyset$.

### 8. What a True "Vector Floors" (D4) Generalization Actually Demands

In the Section "What is not claimed", the author concedes:
> *"The dynamic vector-floor theory is not developed beyond the per-cell re-reading of the audited systems: no vector-valued comparison functions, no multi-floor dynamic programming, no Pareto readings of trade-offs between floors. The Farkas layer is static..."*

By this admission, the paper bypasses all the substantive mathematical challenges of vector constraints in viability theory under partial observation:
1. **Timing Obstruction for Vector Constraints:**
   In Paper 1 (Theorem 3), the timing bound is $T_{\text{obs}} > \frac{\inf q}{\varepsilon}$ for a single floor $q$. With vector floors $q_1, \dots, q_m$, each floor has its own margin $\varepsilon_j$. Exit occurs when the first floor is breached:
   $$T_{\text{exit}} \le \min_{j=1, \dots, m} \frac{\inf_{x \in B_0} q_j(x)}{\varepsilon_j}.$$
   This basic and necessary extension is entirely absent.
2. **Isaacs Condition for Multiple Boundary Faces:**
   When multiple floors are simultaneously active (corners and edges of $V$), the normal cone contains non-trivial convex combinations of constraint gradients $\sum_{j \in J(x)} \mu_j \nabla q_j(x)$. The dynamic exit certificate (Theorem 4 of Paper 1) must be formulated across these cones. The paper completely avoids this.
3. **Cross-Floor vs. Cross-State Infeasibility:**
   The paper fails to distinguish between *infeasibility caused by competing floors at a single state* versus *infeasibility caused by merging states with conflicting single floors*.

### Summary Verdict
This draft does not meet the standards of a standalone mathematical contribution or a valid execution of Roadmap Item D4. It duplicates existing results from Paper 1 under the guise of an extension, misapplies the concept of complementary slackness to infeasible systems, relies on an undefined dynamic model ("two-patch system"), and explicitly disclaims all dynamic aspects of vector-floor viability.

**Technical points that check**

**Judgement.** The Farkas certificate for a stacked polyhedral common-action set is correct and checkable, and the complementarity and monotonicity remarks are valid. The note nevertheless cannot serve as a D4 generalization paper: the original calculus already contained vector floors, the identical two-floor Farkas example, and the Helly \(m+1\) witness. The abstract’s garbled 28/36 sentence, the undefined “viable pair family”, the full-information/pair mismatch, and the recycled examples are the items that must be repaired (or the note withdrawn in favour of a one-paragraph addendum to the original polyhedral paragraph). The same unshown-script evidence standard and production artefacts (empty-ish paths, declarations boilerplate) appear as in the stochastic-selector and viable-selector notes. No mathematical contradiction inside the Farkas layer itself; the contradiction is with the source paper the note claims to extend.
