### Issue ID: A1 — robust tangency versus strong invariance

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct.

**Mathematical status:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` if a Nagumo/viability tangency condition on a disturbance inclusion is used to conclude that one causal policy keeps every admissible disturbance in \(K\). `PROOF_INCOMPLETE` if the intended claim was only existential viability of the inclusion \(\dot z\in F(z)\).

**Corrected statement.**
Let \(X\) be a finite-dimensional normed space, \(K\subset X\) closed, \(U\) a compact metric control set, \(W\) a compact metric disturbance set, \(f:X\times U\times W\to X\) continuous. Write \(F(z,u):=\{f(z,u,w):w\in W\}\) and \(F(z):=\{f(z,u,w):u\in U,\,w\in W\}\). Let \(T_K(z)\) be the Bouligand contingent cone and \(T_K^C(z)\) the Clarke tangent cone.

1. **Existential viability** (Nagumo/Aubin). If \(F\) is Marchaud, \(K\) is viable under \(\dot z\in F(z)\) iff
   \[
   \forall z\in K,\quad F(z)\cap T_K(z)\neq\emptyset.
   \]
   Quantifiers: \(\forall z\in K\ \exists u\ \exists w\) (or \(\exists\) measurable open-loop \(u(\cdot)\) and some \(w(\cdot)\)). This is *not* Operator I.

2. **Strong invariance of a differential inclusion.** If \(F(\cdot)\) is Lipschitz (or one-sided Lipschitz) with compact convex values, \(K\) is strongly invariant under \(\dot z\in F(z)\) iff
   \[
   \forall z\in K,\quad F(z)\subset T_K^C(z)
   \]
   (equivalently the proximal-normal condition \(\langle p,v\rangle\le 0\) for all proximal normals \(p\in N_K^P(z)\) and all \(v\in F(z)\)). Quantifiers: \(\forall z\ \forall v\in F(z)\).

3. **Robust / discriminating viability (Operator I).**
   \[
   \operatorname{RViab}(K,T)
   :=
   \bigl\{
   z_0\in K:\
   \exists\pi\in\mathbb P^{\mathrm{impl}}\
   \forall\delta\in\Delta,\
   z^{\pi,\delta}(t)\in K\ \forall t\in T
   \bigr\}.
   \]
   A sufficient first-order condition for a Lipschitz feedback \(\pi\) is
   \[
   \forall z\in K,\quad F\bigl(z,\pi(z)\bigr)\subset T_K^C(z).
   \]
   The *largest* set on which the controller has a (generally nonanticipative, not necessarily Markov) strategy against every disturbance is the discriminating kernel \(\operatorname{Disc}_F(K)\) (Cardaliaguet–Quincampoix–Saint-Pierre). That is the correct official object for Operator I, not the viability kernel of the union inclusion.

**Minimal assumptions.** Closed \(K\); \(f\) continuous; \(U,W\) compact; linear growth; for strong invariance, Lipschitz-in-\(z\) uniformly in \((u,w)\) and convex values, or a Filippov/Krasovskii regularization plus a proximal-normal test; for discriminating kernels, a nonanticipative (Elliott–Kalton) strategy space, or sample-and-hold / π-strategies if one wants implementability.

**Proof sketch.** Viability produces one tangent velocity, hence one pair \((u,w)\), hence one trajectory of the *union* inclusion. Interchanging quantifiers from \(\forall z\,\exists u\,\exists w\) to \(\exists\pi\,\forall w\) is illicit. The missing step is a *uniform* tangency over \(W\) for a single causally selected \(u\), plus a uniqueness/comparison argument that every disturbance realization remains in \(K\). That is exactly the discriminating/leadership condition, not Nagumo.

**Counterexample if the assumption is removed.**
\(\dot x=u+w\), \(U=W=[-1,1]\), \(K=[-1,1]\). Then \(F(x)=[-2,2]\) meets \(T_K\) at every point, so \(K\) is viable. No single open-loop or state-feedback \(u\) keeps every \(w\equiv \pm 1\) in \(K\) from \(x_0=0\) except the discriminating choice \(u=-{\rm sat}(w)\) which *observes* \(w\), or a nonanticipative strategy. For \(u\equiv 0\), \(w\equiv 1\) exits. For any fixed measurable \(u(\cdot)\), an opposing \(w=-{\rm sign}(u)+\varepsilon\) exits in finite time. So viability of the union inclusion does not yield robust invariance.

**Recommended source status:** demote every “robust” claim that uses only Nagumo to a *conditional theorem* under a strong-invariance or discriminating-kernel hypothesis. Make \(\operatorname{Disc}\) the canonical Operator I object. Existential viability remains a theorem, under a different name.

**Downstream consequences.** This infects Operator I, every “one policy works for all disturbances” kernel in Articles 001–002, institutional and epistemic liftings, and the official statuses “currently viable” and “recoverable.” Until A1 is repaired, those statuses are not theorems.

**Relevant literature.**
- Nagumo, M. Über die Lage der Integralkurven gewöhnlicher Differentialgleichungen. *Proc. Phys.-Math. Soc. Japan* 24 (1942), 551–559.
- Aubin, J.-P. *Viability Theory*. Birkhäuser, 1991, Ch. 1, 4.
- Aubin, Bayen, Saint-Pierre. *Viability Theory: New Directions*. Springer, 2011.
- Clarke, Ledyaev, Stern, Wolenski. *Nonsmooth Analysis and Control Theory*. Springer, 1998, Ch. 4 (strong invariance).
- Cardaliaguet, P., Quincampoix, M., Saint-Pierre, P. Set-valued numerical analysis for optimal control and differential games. *Ann. ISDG* 4 (1999), 177–247 (discriminating kernel).
- Krasovskii, N.N., Subbotin, A.I. *Game-Theoretical Control Problems*. Springer, 1988.
- Quincampoix, M. Differential inclusions and target problems. *SIAM J. Control Optim.* 30 (1992), 324–335.

**Confidence:** high on the logical error and the corrected hierarchy. Unresolved: whether any source proof already uses a Lipschitz feedback and a Clarke-tangent test (then it is a strong-invariance theorem mislabelled as Nagumo, and may be salvaged). That requires the omitted proof text.

### Issue ID: A2 — order-minimal proof wording

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct.

**Mathematical status:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` for the universal claim; the existential claim is the intended theorem.

**Corrected statement.**
Let the resource/harvest system be monotone in the harvest control: if \(H\le H'\) (pointwise, or in the product order) then the resulting state trajectory is order-better for the protected stock (larger resource, smaller depletion). Let \(H_{\min}\) be an admissible lower bound (subsistence, mandate). Then:

- **Existential viability (correct).** If the closed-loop trajectory under \(H\equiv H_{\min}\) remains in \(K\) on \(T\), then \(z_0\in\operatorname{Viab}(K,T)\). Quantifiers: \(\exists\pi\) (namely the constant minimum-harvest policy).
- **Universal / robust-in-control claim (false).** Viability of the minimum-harvest path does *not* imply that every admissible harvest path remains in \(K\).
- **Monotone comparison (the useful companion).** If the *pointwise maximal* admissible harvest \(H_{\max}\) produces a trajectory in \(K\), and the dynamics are monotone in \(H\) as above, then every \(H\in[H_{\min},H_{\max}]\) remains in \(K\). That is the only honest “all paths” theorem.

**Minimal assumptions.** Partial order on states; Kamke–Müller / monotone control-system comparison (e.g. quasimonotone off-diagonal Jacobian in \(z\), monotone dependence on \(H\)); closed \(K\) that is a down-set or up-set as appropriate; unique solutions, or at least order-extremal solutions.

**Proof sketch.** Comparison: if \(H\le H'\) then \(z^H(t)\ge z^{H'}(t)\) in the resource coordinate. If \(K=\{z:z_{\mathrm{res}}\ge z_\flat\}\) and \(z^{H_{\min}}(t)\ge z_\flat\), nothing follows for \(H>H_{\min}\). If \(z^{H_{\max}}(t)\ge z_\flat\), comparison gives the same for every smaller harvest.

**Counterexample.** Logistic stock \(\dot x=x(1-x)-H\), \(K=[1/4,\infty)\), \(H_{\min}=0\), \(H_{\max}=1/2\), \(x_0=1/2\). The zero-harvest path stays in \(K\); \(H\equiv 1/2\) drives \(x\) to \(0\).

**Recommended source status:** corrected *theorem* (existential, or comparison-from-the-max). Do not leave the original wording.

**Downstream consequences.** Same pattern as A4 and the quota side of A8. Any “the kernel is preserved under all larger/smaller controls” claim in Article 001 must be rewritten as existential or as comparison from the *worst* control.

**Relevant literature.**
- Angeli, D., Sontag, E.D. Monotone control systems. *IEEE Trans. Automat. Control* 48 (2003), 1684–1698.
- Smith, H.L. *Monotone Dynamical Systems*. AMS, 1995.
- Kamke, E. Zur Theorie der Systeme gewöhnlicher Differentialgleichungen II. *Acta Math.* 58 (1932), 57–85.

**Confidence:** high. Unresolved: the exact order and the exact definition of “lower harvest” in the source.

### Issue ID: A3 — pollution-feedback comparison sign

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** partly correct. The displayed inequality is true; its use as an *inner* (conservative) bound may be the wrong direction.

**Mathematical status:** `NOT_AN_ERROR_BUT_NEEDS_CLARIFICATION` for the inequality itself; possible `FALSE_AS_STATED` for an “inner-bound” conclusion. `INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION` on the actual proof’s use of \(g\).

**Corrected statement.**
If \(g\) is \(C^1\) (or merely nonincreasing in \(K\)) and \(g_K\le 0\) on the segment, then
\[
K\le K_{\max}\implies g(S,K)\ge g(S,K_{\max}).
\]
Quantifiers: \(\forall(S,K)\) in the domain with \(K\le K_{\max}\).

Whether this is a conservative inner bound depends on the *role* of \(g\):

- If \(g\) is a *benefit* (regeneration, decay of a bad stock) and larger \(g\) is safer, then \(g(S,K_{\max})\) is a *pessimistic* lower bound when \(K\le K_{\max}\). Viability of the comparison system \(\dot S=g(S,K_{\max})-\cdots\) implies viability of the true system. Inner bound: yes.
- If \(g\) is *pollution generation* (the packet’s name) and larger \(g\) is worse, then \(g(S,K_{\max})\) is an *optimistic* lower bound on emissions when \(K<K_{\max}\). Viability of the comparison system does **not** imply viability of the true system. It yields an outer approximation of the kernel. Inner bound: no. The conservative comparison is \(g(S,K_{\min})\) or \(\sup_{K\le K_{\max}}g(S,K)\).

**Minimal assumptions.** Monotonicity of \(g\) in \(K\) on a convex set; comparison theorem for the resulting ODE/inclusion; closed safe set that is an order interval.

**Proof sketch of the inequality.** Mean value: \(g(S,K)-g(S,K_{\max})=g_K(S,\xi)(K-K_{\max})\ge 0\) if \(g_K\le 0\) and \(K-K_{\max}\le 0\).

**Counterexample (wrong bound type).** \(\dot P=g(S,K)=a-bK\), \(K\in[0,K_{\max}]\), safe set \(P\le P^\sharp\). Using \(g(S,K_{\max})\) underestimates \(\dot P\) whenever \(K<K_{\max}\). The comparison trajectory can stay below \(P^\sharp\) while the true trajectory (smaller abatement capital) exits.

**Recommended source status:** keep the inequality as an *identity/lemma*. Reclassify the kernel comparison as a *conditional theorem* only after fixing the direction against the typed failure (pollution is a bad). If the source already uses \(g\) as regeneration, leave the math and rename the lemma.

**Downstream consequences.** Any “inner approximation of a pollution kernel” in Article 001 that uses this sign is possibly *optimistic*, which is the dangerous direction for a sustainability claim.

**Relevant literature.** Same comparison references as A2; Müller, M. Über das Fundamentaltheorem in der Theorie der gewöhnlichen Differentialgleichungen. *Math. Z.* 26 (1927), 619–645.

**Confidence:** high on the inequality and the bound-type distinction. Low on which role \(g\) actually plays in the omitted proof.

### Issue ID: A4 — capital-consumption kernel

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct.

**Mathematical status:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` for “larger consumption also preserves the kernel.”

**Corrected statement.**
Let \(\dot k=f(k)-c\), \(c\in[c_{\min},c_{\max}]\), \(K=\{k:k\ge k_\flat\}\) closed. Assume \(f\) locally Lipschitz.

\[
k_0\in\operatorname{Viab}(K,T)
\iff
\text{the trajectory under }c\equiv c_{\min}\text{ remains in }K\text{ on }T,
\]
under the standing comparison \(\partial\dot k/\partial c<0\). Quantifiers: existential in the policy (\(\exists c(\cdot)\), and the witness is \(c_{\min}\)). Larger \(c\) need not stay in \(K\). The viability *kernel* as a set is computed with the most conservative feasible consumption; it is not invariant under arbitrary increase of \(c\).

If the constraint is only \(c\ge c_{\min}\) with no upper bound, the same statement holds. If there is a consumption *floor* that is itself a normative constraint, \(c_{\min}\) is mandatory, not a free choice; then emptiness of the \(c_{\min}\)-kernel is infeasibility, not an invitation to consume less.

**Minimal assumptions.** As in A2; unique solutions; \(K\) an up-set in \(k\).

**Proof sketch.** Comparison in \(c\). Necessity: any admissible \(c\ge c_{\min}\) depletes at least as fast as \(c_{\min}\), so if \(c_{\min}\) exits, every policy exits. Sufficiency: \(c_{\min}\) is admissible, so it is a witness.

**Counterexample.** \(\dot k=rk-c\), \(r>0\), \(k_\flat>0\), \(c_{\min}=rk_\flat\), \(c=rk_\flat+\varepsilon\). The \(c_{\min}\) path sits at \(k_\flat\); any larger constant consumption exits immediately.

**Recommended source status:** corrected *theorem* (existential characterization of the capital kernel).

**Downstream consequences.** Aligns with A2. Economic-kernel formulae in Article 001 that claim “the kernel is preserved for \(c\ge c_{\min}\)” must be rewritten. Intergenerational and institutional liftings that reused this wording inherit the error.

**Relevant literature.** Aubin, *Viability Theory*, resource-management chapters; Clark, C.W. *Mathematical Bioeconomics*, 3rd ed., Wiley, 2010.

**Confidence:** high, conditional on the dynamics being monotone in \(c\) as described.

### Issue ID: A5 — generic non-polyhedrality

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct that the algebraic-hypersurface argument is incomplete.

**Mathematical status:** `PROOF_INCOMPLETE`. Do not upgrade to a theorem by patching adjectives.

**Corrected statement (as a conjecture).**
Let \(\mathcal F\) be a Banach space of \(C^r\) vector fields (or parameterized control systems) on a compact manifold or a compact subset of \(\mathbb R^n\), \(r\ge 2\), with the Whitney or \(C^r\) topology. For a fixed closed constraint \(K\) with nonempty interior, the set of \(f\in\mathcal F\) for which \(\operatorname{Viab}_f(K)\) (or \(\operatorname{Disc}_f(K)\)) is a polyhedron is meager and closed, hence its complement is open-dense.

This is *not* a theorem in the packet, and I do not certify it.

**Minimal assumptions that a repair would need.** A precise topology on the space of dynamics; a precise meaning of “polyhedron” (finite intersection of closed half-spaces in the ambient coordinates, or a piecewise-\(C^1\) manifold with corners); a regularity theorem for the kernel boundary (it is generally only Lipschitz / prox-regular, not algebraic); a transversality or curvature computation that rules out flat pieces on a residual set.

**Why the three repair options rank as they do.**

1. *Transversality / elimination.* In principle the right language (Thom, Sard, Abraham–Robbin). In practice the viability-kernel map \(f\mapsto\operatorname{Viab}_f(K)\) is only upper semicontinuous in the Hausdorff metric, not continuous, and the boundary is defined variationally. I do not recommend this as a short repair.
2. *Explicit curvature in a parameterized family.* Feasible, and the only honest route to a *theorem* on a restricted class (e.g. planar affine-control systems, or logistic-plus-linear-harvest). This yields a *conditional theorem* for that family, not an open-dense claim in a function space.
3. *Demote to conjecture.* Recommended for the flagship.

**Counterexample to overclaim.** Linear systems \(\dot x=Ax+Bu\) with polyhedral \(K\) have polyhedral viability kernels under standard hypotheses (maximal controlled invariant subspace / polyhedral invariance). So any genericity claim must exclude, or place in a meager set, the linear/polyhedral-control locus. An “algebraic hypersurface” argument that does not treat this locus explicitly is incomplete.

**Recommended source status:** *conjecture*. Optionally a *conditional theorem* for one explicit nonlinear family with a curvature calculation, placed in a supplement.

**Downstream consequences.** None for Operators I–II if the claim is demoted. Do not use generic non-polyhedrality to justify numerical schemes or to dismiss polyhedral certificates (those remain valid sufficient inner approximations).

**Relevant literature.**
- Saint-Pierre, P. Approximation of the viability kernel. *Appl. Math. Optim.* 29 (1994), 187–209.
- Abraham, R., Robbin, J. *Transversal Mappings and Flows*. Benjamin, 1967.
- Blanchini, F., Miani, S. *Set-Theoretic Methods in Control*. Springer, 2015 (polyhedral invariance, a large structured class where polyhedrality *holds*).

**Confidence:** high that the present proof is incomplete; medium that the conjecture is true in \(C^r\); high that it is the wrong object to spend flagship pages on.

### Issue ID: A6 — Rosen uniqueness

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct.

**Mathematical status:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` if uniqueness is claimed for heterogeneous damages via a single common second derivative. `PROOF_INCOMPLETE` if DSC is invoked but not checked.

**Corrected statement.**
Let \(u_i(x)\) be \(C^2\) payoffs on a compact convex \(X=\prod_i X_i\), \(x=(x_i)\). Write \(G(x)=(\partial u_1/\partial x_1,\ldots,\partial u_n/\partial x_n)\) for the pseudo-gradient. Rosen’s diagonal strict concavity (DSC):
\[
\exists\, r\in\mathbb R_{++}^n
\quad\text{such that}\quad
JG(x)^\top R+R\,JG(x)
\quad\text{is negative definite for all }x,
\]
where \(R=\operatorname{diag}(r)\). Then the concave game has a unique Nash equilibrium.

Heterogeneous damages enter \(JG\) off-diagonally and on the diagonal *differently per player*. Substituting one common \(\partial^2 D/\partial x^2\) for every player is valid only if damages are identical (or identical up to the weights \(r_i\)). Sufficient *checkable* conditions:

- **Homogeneous damages:** \(u_i=b_i(x_i)-D(x)\), common convex \(D\), plus a uniform bound on \(b_i''\) that makes DSC hold.
- **Dominant own-effects:** \(\partial^2 u_i/\partial x_i^2\le -\alpha_i<0\) and \(\sum_{j\neq i}|\partial^2 u_i/\partial x_i\partial x_j|\le \gamma_i\) with \(\alpha_i>\gamma_i\) (strict diagonal dominance of the symmetric part).
- **Explicit heterogeneous DSC:** write the true Hessian blocks, including distinct damage second derivatives \(D_i''(x)\), and verify the quadratic form.

**Minimal assumptions.** Compact convex strategy sets; payoffs \(C^2\) and concave in own strategy; DSC as above. Existence is Rosen/Kakutani and does *not* need DSC; only uniqueness does.

**Proof sketch.** DSC \(\Rightarrow\) the map \(x\mapsto G(x)\) is strictly monotone \(\Rightarrow\) at most one solution of the variational inequality \(\langle G(x^*),x-x^*\rangle\le 0\ \forall x\), which characterizes Nash.

**Counterexample.** Two players, \(u_i=x_i(a-x_i)-d_i(x_1+x_2)^2\) with \(d_1\ll d_2\). Own concavity can hold while the symmetric part of \(JG\) has a positive eigenvalue; multiple NE are possible once a capacity constraint makes the best-response correspondence jump. (Even without a jump, claiming uniqueness from a single \(D''\) is a proof error.)

**Recommended source status:** *conditional theorem* under homogeneous damages or an explicit DSC inequality. Do not keep a uniqueness theorem that uses one second derivative for heterogeneous \(D_i\).

**Downstream consequences.** Commons and institutional uniqueness claims that rest on this step become existence-only. Multiplicity is not a nuisance; it is an Operator II issue (which equilibrium an architecture change selects).

**Relevant literature.**
- Rosen, J.B. Existence and uniqueness of equilibrium points for concave \(N\)-person games. *Econometrica* 33 (1965), 520–534. DOI: 10.2307/1911749. (Thm. 2 is DSC uniqueness.)
- Facchinei, F., Pang, J.-S. *Finite-Dimensional Variational Inequalities and Complementarity Problems*. Springer, 2003.

**Confidence:** high on the logical error. I have not seen the source’s damage specification; if damages were already homogeneous, the diagnosis would be mistaken and the status would drop to `NOT_AN_ERROR_BUT_NEEDS_CLARIFICATION`.

### Issue ID: A7 — commons obstruction

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct that “safe-harvest function” is underdefined.

**Mathematical status:** `MODEL_INCOMPLETE` (definition missing), not yet a false theorem.

**Corrected statement.**
Let \(\dot x=f(x,w)-\sum_{i=1}^n h_i\), \(x\in X\subset\mathbb R_+\), \(K\subset X\) closed, \(w\in W\). Define the *pointwise safe-harvest value*
\[
H_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)
:=
\sup\Bigl\{
H\ge 0:\
\forall w\in W,\
f(x,w)-H\in T_K(x)
\Bigr\}
\in[0,+\infty].
\]
This is a function \(K\to[0,+\infty]\), not a single number. The commons obstruction is:

- If \(\sum_i h_i(x)>H_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)\) at some reachable \(x\in\partial K\) (or in a boundary strip), then \(K\) is not robustly invariant under that harvest profile.
- If each agent has a private lower bound \(h_i\ge h_i^{\min}\) and \(\sum_i h_i^{\min}>\inf_{x\in K}H_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)\), then no feasible harvest profile robustly maintains \(K\). That is the precise obstruction.

A usable *boundary-strip drift condition* (sufficient for robust invariance of a compact \(K\) with \(C^{1,1}\) boundary): there exist \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(\eta>0\) such that on \(S_\varepsilon=\{x\in K:\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial K)\le\varepsilon\}\),
\[
\sup_{w\in W}\bigl\langle n(x),f(x,w)\bigr\rangle-\sum_i h_i(x)\le-\eta,
\]
where \(n(x)\) is the outward unit normal (or a bounded proximal-normal selection). For nonsmooth \(K\), replace by the proximal-normal inequality.

**Minimal assumptions.** Closed \(K\); \(f\) continuous; \(W\) compact; for the strip test, prox-regular boundary or \(C^{1,1}\); for the cone definition, nothing beyond the contingent cone.

**Proof sketch.** By definition of \(T_K\), \(H>H_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)\) produces a velocity outside the contingent cone, so some (in the robust case, the worst) trajectory leaves \(K\) immediately. The strip test plus a standard Lyapunov / proximal-normal argument gives a uniform exit speed of at least \(\eta\) toward the interior, hence strong invariance under Lipschitz regularity.

**Counterexample if one uses a single number \(H_{\mathrm{safe}}\).** Logistic \(f(x)=x(1-x)\), \(K=[x_\flat,1]\). Then \(H_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)=0\) at \(x=x_\flat\) if one requires invariance, while \(H_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)=x(1-x)\) in the interior. A quota equal to “the” safe harvest \(x^*(1-x^*)\) at MSY is unsafe at \(x_\flat\).

**Recommended source status:** *definition* plus a *conditional theorem* (strip test). Replace every undefined “safe-harvest function” in Article 001 by \(H_{\mathrm{safe}}(\cdot)\).

**Downstream consequences.** A8–A10 cannot be theorems until this definition is fixed. Operator I on commons systems is otherwise notationally empty.

**Relevant literature.** Aubin, *Viability Theory*; Clark, *Mathematical Bioeconomics*; Ostrom, below at A10.

**Confidence:** high on the definitional repair. Unresolved: the source’s intended domain (single stock vs spatially distributed commons).

Let \(H_{\min}\) be a lower requirement (subsistence / mandate), \(H_{\mathrm{safe}}(\cdot)\) as in A7, and \(Q\) a total quota (cap). Admissible individual harvests satisfy \(h_i\ge 0\), \(\sum h_i\le Q\), and the normative floor \(\sum h_i\ge H_{\min}\) if that floor is in force.

**Relevant literature.** Clark, *Mathematical Bioeconomics*, Ch. 2–3; standard TAC/quota comparison.

### Issue ID: A9 — sanction sufficiency

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct.

**Mathematical status:** `PROOF_INCOMPLETE` for uniqueness / “the” best response; possibly a valid *existence* of NE if downward deviations were implicitly dominated.

**Corrected statement (weaker, correct).**
In a static commons game with payoff \(u_i(h_i,h_{-i})\) strictly increasing in \(h_i\) on \([0,Q]\) (revenue dominates own-cost in that range) and a sanction \(s_i(h_i;Q)\) with \(s_i=0\) for \(h_i\le Q_i\) and
\[
u_i(h_i,h_{-i})-s_i(h_i;Q)<u_i(Q_i,h_{-i})
\quad\text{for all }h_i>Q_i,
\]
the quota profile \(Q=(Q_i)\) is a *Nash equilibrium*. It is the *unique* NE, and a strictly dominant profile, if in addition \(u_i\) is strictly increasing in \(h_i\) on \([0,Q_i]\) (no incentive to under-harvest) and the sanction inequality is strict.

Without own-payoff increasing below the quota, the proof that blocks upward deviation yields only: every NE is \(\le Q\) coordinatewise. The quota need not itself be a best response (downward deviation may improve a congested payoff), and it need not be unique.

**Minimal assumptions.** Quasi-concave payoffs for existence; strict monotonicity in own harvest below the cap for uniqueness; sanction observed and applied with probability 1 in the static game (or discounted expected sanction in a repeated game, with \(\delta\) large enough).

**Proof sketch.** Standard best-response comparison. Upward: sanction dominates the gain. Downward: lost private benefit is positive by strict increase. Unique crossing.

**Counterexample.** Standard Cournot commons with downward-sloping residual benefit: if the quota is *above* the interior NE, blocking only upward moves does nothing, and the unique NE is the interior point below the quota. If the quota is below the interior NE but own benefit can still fall (e.g. a cost kink), a downward deviation can be profitable.

**Recommended source status:** *conditional theorem* (quota is NE under one-sided sanction + own-monotonicity). Uniqueness is a separate, stricter theorem. Do not claim “the unique best response” from an upward-only estimate.

**Downstream consequences.** A10’s “sanctions suffice” language must be weakened. Institutional Operator I may use “quota is implementable as a NE,” not “the institution has a unique equilibrium at the safe point.”

### Issue ID: A10 — Ostrom sufficiency/necessity

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct that constructed “can be necessary” examples are not universal necessity.

**Mathematical status:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` if universal necessity of the design principles is claimed; `NOT_AN_ERROR_BUT_NEEDS_CLARIFICATION` if the source only exhibits examples. `INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION` on the exact quantified statement.

**Corrected statement.**

- **Not a theorem:** “Ostrom’s principles are necessary for commons sustainability.” Counter-models exist (private property with a single owner; Pigouvian tax with perfect observation; a dictator with a safe feedback). Necessity is *model-relative*.
- **Constructed necessity (legitimate, weaker).** In a specified game class \(\mathcal G\) (e.g. repeated symmetric commons with private harvest, no external enforcer, discount \(\delta<1\), binary detection), one can exhibit parameter regions where *if* a given principle fails (no monitoring, no sanction, no boundary), *then* the cooperative harvest is not SPE. That is a theorem about \(\mathcal G\), labelled as such.
- **Sound sufficiency theorem (recommended canonical form).** Fix a repeated commons game with public monitoring, harvest profile \(h^*\), safe set \(K\), detection kernel \(P(\sigma\mid h)\), sanction \(s\), and discount \(\delta\). If
  \[
  (1-\delta)u_i(h_i',h_{-i}^*)+\delta\,\mathbb E[V_i\mid \sigma]
  \le
  (1-\delta)u_i(h^*)+\delta V_i(h^*)
  \]
  for every unilateral \(h_i'\) and the continuation values \(V\) solve the associated dynamic program with state in \(K\), then \(h^*\) is a subgame-perfect equilibrium *and* the induced state path remains in \(K\). **Minimal assumptions.** Well-specified extensive-form game; specified observation σ-algebra; specified strategy class (e.g. grim, or graduated). Do not mix folk-theorem existence with constructive institutional sufficiency.

**Proof sketch.** Standard one-shot deviation principle. The only delicate step is that the continuation after detection must itself keep the *state* in \(K\), not merely punish payoffs. That is a coupled game+viability condition, which is the actual content.

**Counterexample to universal necessity.** A single owner of the resource (no “community,” no “collective-choice arrangements”) solves a standard optimal-control problem and can keep \(K\) invariant. Principle failure, viability success.

**Recommended source status:** *conditional theorem* for sufficiency in a named game class; *examples* for necessity; never a universal necessity theorem. Normative use of the principles is a *postulate*, not a theorem.

**Downstream consequences.** The master manuscript may cite Ostrom as institutional vocabulary and as a checklist that *suggests* sufficient structure. It may not treat the eight principles as necessary conditions of Operator I.

**Relevant literature.**

### Issue ID: A11 — implementation lattice

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct.

**Mathematical status:** `FALSE_AS_STATED`.

**Corrected statement.**
Let \(\mathcal U\) be the set of implementation correspondences \(U:X\rightrightarrows U_{\mathrm{raw}}\), ordered by \(U\le U'\) iff \(U(x)\subset U'(x)\) for all \(x\). Let \(\operatorname{Viab}(U)\) be the (existential or robust, consistently chosen) viability kernel of a fixed closed \(K\) under \(U\).

- **Monotonicity.** \(U\le U'\implies\operatorname{Viab}(U)\subset\operatorname{Viab}(U')\).
- **The viable-implementation class is an upper set.**
  \[
  \mathcal U_{\mathrm{ok}}:=\{U:\operatorname{Viab}(U)\ni z_0\}
  \]
  is an upset: if \(U\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{ok}}\) and \(U\le U'\) then \(U'\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{ok}}\). It is *not* a down-set. Arbitrary contraction of a viable control set can destroy viability.
- **Lattice operations.** \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{ok}}\) is closed under pointwise union (joins). It is not closed under pointwise intersection (meets). The set of *maximal* non-viable implementations and the set of *minimal* viable implementations (when they exist) are the objects of interest; the latter need not exist without a compactness/Zorn argument, and Zorn produces choice-theoretic, not implementable, selectors (see B2).

**Minimal assumptions.** The same regularity for each \(U\) so that \(\operatorname{Viab}(U)\) is defined; monotonicity of Pre in the control set.

**Proof sketch.** If \(U\subset U'\) then every \(U\)-trajectory is a \(U'\)-trajectory, so any witness policy for \(U\) is a witness for \(U'\). For the negative claim: take \(U'\) with a unique saving control \(u^*\) at the boundary and \(U=U'\setminus\{u^*\}\). Kernel collapses.

**Counterexample.** \(\dot x=u\), \(U'=[-1,1]\), \(K=[-1,1]\), \(U=\{1\}\). \(K\) is viable (in fact robustly invariant) under \(U'\); under \(U\), every point except \(\{1\}\) eventually exits.

**Recommended source status:** corrected *theorem* (upset / monotone kernel map). Reject downward closure.

**Downstream consequences.** Institutional “tightening controls preserves sustainability” is false as a general claim. Tightening is Operator I–safe only if the discarded controls were not used by *every* saving policy, i.e. only if the remaining correspondence still intersects the saving set at every relevant state. This is the right place to connect A11 to C2.

**Relevant literature.** Aubin, *Viability Theory*, dependence of kernels on the dynamics; lattice theory only at the level of upsets in a complete lattice of correspondences.

### Issue ID: B2 — selector regularity

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct that arbitrary pointwise selectors are not implementable policies.

**Mathematical status:** `PROOF_INCOMPLETE` for any theorem that concludes an implementable \(\pi\in\mathbb P^{\mathrm{impl}}\) from a pointwise, possibly nonmeasurable, selection. Choice-theoretic kernel statements can stand.

**Corrected statement.**
Split every knowledge-kernel result into two layers.

1. **Choice-theoretic kernel (AC).** \(z\in\operatorname{EViab}\) iff for every information state \(\iota\) in the tube, the set
   \[
   U^\sharp(\iota):=\{u:\forall\text{ latent }x\in\operatorname{supp}(\iota),\ F(x,u,W)\text{ meets the appropriate cone}\}
   \]
   is nonempty. No measurability. Not an Operator I policy.

2. **Implementable policy.** A causal policy exists if there is a selection \(\pi:\mathcal I\to U\) that is measurable with respect to the information σ-algebra and for which the closed loop is well posed. Minimal hypotheses:
   - \(\mathcal I\) a Polish space (e.g. \(\mathcal P(X)\) or a closed subset of the Hausdorff hyperspace);
   - \(U^\sharp:\mathcal I\rightrightarrows U\) weakly measurable, nonempty, closed-valued;
   - then Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski gives a universally measurable selection;
   - for *continuous* feedback, Michael’s theorem: \(U^\sharp\) lower hemicontinuous, closed convex values, Banach range;
   - for ODE/RFDE well-posedness with discontinuous \(\pi\), either (a) sample-and-hold / zero-order hold (preferred; matches Article 011–012), or (b) Filippov–Krasovskii regularization plus a strong-invariance test on the convexified inclusion, or (c) π-strategies / nonanticipative strategies in the Krasovskii–Subbotin sense.

**Minimal assumptions.** As above. Do not cite “a selector exists” as implementability.

**Proof sketch.** KRN: a weakly measurable closed-valued map from a Polish space into a Polish space has a universally measurable selection. Well-posedness is a separate theorem: measurable-in-\(t\), Lipschitz-in-\(z\) gives Carathéodory; merely measurable-in-\(z\) does not.

**Counterexample.** A set-valued map that is nonempty everywhere but not weakly measurable has no measurable selection. Even with a measurable selection, \(\dot x=-\operatorname{sign}(x)\) is well posed, \(\dot x=+\operatorname{sign}(x)\) is not in the classical sense; Filippov solutions of the latter leave \(\{0\}\) or stay, depending on the convention — so “a pointwise saving \(u(x)\)” need not define a unique robust trajectory.

**Recommended source status:** knowledge-kernel *existence* results remain *theorems* at the choice-theoretic layer; every claim of an implementable policy is a *conditional theorem* under KRN + a well-posedness convention. The flagship should adopt sample-and-hold as the official implementation semantics (compatible with Articles 011 and 012).

**Downstream consequences.** Operator I’s \(\exists\pi\in\mathbb P^{\mathrm{impl}}\) is otherwise undefined. Hybrid and RFDE theorems in Article 002 must name their selection and well-posedness hypotheses explicitly (B3).

**Relevant literature.**
- Kuratowski, K., Ryll-Nardzewski, C. A general theorem on selectors. *Bull. Acad. Polon. Sci.* 13 (1965), 397–403.
- Michael, E. Continuous selections I. *Ann. of Math.* 63 (1956), 361–382.

### Issue ID: C1 — fixed-point operator mismatch

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** partly correct. There is a real operator-identity issue, but the two operators *agree along the canonical descending sequence* when \(\operatorname{Pre}\) is monotone and one starts at \(S_{\mathrm{safe}}\). The error is real if the source identified the operators themselves, used a different initialization, or invoked Tarski on the wrong map.

**Mathematical status:** `TYPE_OR_DOMAIN_ERROR` if the operators were treated as the same map; `PROOF_INCOMPLETE` if iteration/limit interchange was not justified; `DIAGNOSIS_MISTAKEN` if the source only used \(\Phi_2\) as an implementation of the descending chain from \(S_{\mathrm{safe}}\) with monotone \(\operatorname{Pre}\).

**Corrected statement.**
Let \(X\) be the information-state space, \(S\subset X\) a designated safe family, \(\operatorname{Pre}:\mathcal P(X)\to\mathcal P(X)\) monotone (\(Q\subset Q'\Rightarrow\operatorname{Pre}(Q)\subset\operatorname{Pre}(Q')\)). Define
\[
\Phi_1(Q)=S\cap\operatorname{Pre}(Q),
\qquad
\Phi_2(Q)=Q\cap\operatorname{Pre}(Q).
\]

- **Object.** The greatest robustly invariant family contained in \(S\) is the greatest fixed point of \(\Phi_1\) on \(\mathcal P(X)\) (equivalently of \(\Phi_1\) on \(\mathcal P(S)\)). It is also the greatest post-fixed point: the largest \(Q\subset S\) with \(Q\subset\operatorname{Pre}(Q)\). That is the official target. It is *not* “a” fixed point; it is the *greatest* one.
- **Official Tarski operator.** \(\Phi_1\). It is monotone on the complete lattice \((\mathcal P(X),\subset)\). Tarski gives a GFP. \(\Phi_2\) is also monotone, but its GFP on \(\mathcal P(X)\) is the greatest invariant set in \(X\), *not* constrained to \(S\). Wrong object if \(S\neq X\).
- **Initialization.** \(Q_0=S\). Never start \(\Phi_1\) at \(X\) unless \(S=X\). Never start at an arbitrary inner guess if you want the *greatest* kernel (you would compute a possibly strict subset).
- **The two chains coincide from \(S\).** Let \(Q_0=S\), \(Q_{n+1}=\Phi_1(Q_n)\), \(R_{n+1}=\Phi_2(R_n)\) with \(R_0=S\). By monotonicity of \(\operatorname{Pre}\), \(\operatorname{Pre}(Q)\subset\operatorname{Pre}(S)\) whenever \(Q\subset S\), hence
  \[
  S\cap\operatorname{Pre}(Q)
  =
  Q\cap\operatorname{Pre}(Q)
  \quad\text{along this sequence, once }Q\subset S\cap\operatorname{Pre}(S).
  \]
  Concretely, \(Q_1=R_1=S\cap\operatorname{Pre}(S)\) and an easy induction gives \(Q_n=R_n\). So \(\Phi_2\) is a legitimate *implementation* of the descending Kleene chain of \(\Phi_1\), not an alternative semantics.
- **Countable descent.** Sufficient when \(\operatorname{Pre}\) commutes with countable decreasing intersections, e.g. \(X\) finite; or \(X\) compact metric, sets closed, \(\operatorname{Pre}\) outer semicontinuous in the Hausdorff metric (standard for discrete-time robust predecessors with compact-valued u.s.c. dynamics). Then \(Q_\omega=\bigcap_n Q_n\) satisfies \(Q_\omega=\Phi_1(Q_\omega)\).
- **Transfinite iteration.** Required when that interchange fails: general complete lattices, merely monotone \(\operatorname{Pre}\) with no continuity, continuous-time kernels computed as nested closed sets without a modulus of continuity, some infinite-dimensional information spaces. Iterate through ordinals: \(Q_{\alpha+1}=\Phi_1(Q_\alpha)\), \(Q_\lambda=\bigcap_{\alpha<\lambda}Q_\alpha\). In a power set the process stops by cardinality at latest at \(|\mathcal P(X)|\); in practice one discretizes and works at \(\omega\).
- **Policy selection in the limit.** Each finite iterate carries a (possibly set-valued) saving-action correspondence \(U_n^\sharp\). The limit correspondence \(U_\infty^\sharp=\operatorname{Limsup}\,U_n^\sharp\) (or the pointwise intersection, if nested) is nonempty under closed-graph / compact-value assumptions. An implementable limit policy still needs B2 (measurable selection on the GFP). Without that, the GFP is a set-theoretic kernel, not an implemented policy.

**Minimal assumptions.** Monotone \(\operatorname{Pre}\); complete lattice of subsets (or closed subsets with a topology that makes \(\operatorname{Pre}\) well-behaved); for countable convergence, interchange of \(\operatorname{Pre}\) and countable nested intersections; for policies, B2.

**Proof sketch.** Tarski: monotone map on a complete lattice has a GFP, equal to \(\bigcup\{Q:Q\subset\Phi_1(Q)\}\). The descending chain from the top element \(S\) is the standard constructive approximation from above. Equality of \(\Phi_1\) and \(\Phi_2\) along that chain is the calculation above. Failure of countable interchange: \(\operatorname{Pre}(\bigcap Q_n)\) may be a *proper* subset of \(\bigcap\operatorname{Pre}(Q_n)\) if \(\operatorname{Pre}\) is only monotone, so \(Q_\omega\subset\operatorname{Pre}(Q_\omega)\) can fail; one more application of \(\Phi_1\) is needed, possibly transfinitely.

**Counterexample (wrong lattice / wrong start).** Take \(S\subsetneq X\), start \(\Phi_2\) at \(X\): \(R_1=X\cap\operatorname{Pre}(X)=\operatorname{Pre}(X)\), which need not lie in \(S\). The sequence can converge to a robustly invariant set that is *not safe*. That is the actual operational danger of “using \(\Phi_2\) as the Tarski operator.”

**Counterexample (countable interchange).** Construct nested closed \(Q_n\) whose intersection is a singleton that is *not* a robust predecessor of itself, while each finite \(Q_n\) is. Standard in continuous-time viability without a uniform inward-drift modulus; this is why Saint-Pierre’s algorithm is a *discrete-time / grid* theorem plus a consistency argument, not a literal countable formula for the continuous-time kernel.

**Recommended source status:** official operator \(\Phi_1\), official initialization \(S_{\mathrm{safe}}\), \(\Phi_2\) permitted only as the descending implementation. Article 006’s fixed-point theorem remains *rejected* until this is rewritten; do not spend flagship space repairing 006 beyond harvesting the information-state definition.

**Downstream consequences.** This is the construction of every epistemic/institutional kernel (C2, C3, Operator I on \(\mathcal I\)). Wrong operator or wrong start silently computes either an unsafe set or a non-maximal kernel.

**Relevant literature.**
- Tarski, A. A lattice-theoretical fixpoint theorem and its applications. *Pacific J. Math.* 5 (1955), 285–309.
- Cousot, P., Cousot, R. Abstract interpretation. *POPL* 1977 (descending iteration from the top).
- Saint-Pierre, *Appl. Math. Optim.* 1994.
- Aubin, Bayen, Saint-Pierre, *New Directions*, Ch. on capture basins and algorithms.

**Confidence:** high on the lattice facts. Unresolved: Article 006’s actual initialization and whether \(\operatorname{Pre}\) was monotone (if not, both operators are unusable).

### Issue ID: C2 — common safe prescription

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct.

**Mathematical status:** `TYPE_OR_DOMAIN_ERROR`.

**Corrected statement.**
Three distinct obstruction levels, from strongest to weakest.

Let \(\iota\) be an information cell (a set of latent states, or the support of a belief). Let \(U_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)\) be the saving raw-action set at \(x\). Let \(P\) be a prescription space (messages, legal instruments, quotas). Let \(\operatorname{Impl}(x,p)\subset U_{\mathrm{raw}}\) be the implementation correspondence.

1. **Common raw action (too strong if implementation can depend on latent state).**
   \[
   \bigcap_{x\in\iota}U_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)\neq\emptyset.
   \]
2. **Common information-measurable action (correct when the actuator sees only \(\iota\)).**
   There exists \(u=u(\iota)\) with \(u\in U_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)\) for all \(x\in\iota\), i.e. the same intersection. This *is* (1). It is the right obstruction for a single physical actuator under partial observation.
3. **Common prescription, latent-dependent implementation (correct when the packet’s hypothesis holds).**
   \[
   \exists p\in P\quad
   \forall x\in\iota,\quad
   \operatorname{Impl}(x,p)\cap U_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)\neq\emptyset.
   \]
   Equivalently, the set-valued map \(x\mapsto\operatorname{Impl}(x,p)\) hits \(U_{\mathrm{safe}}(x)\) on the whole cell. This is strictly weaker than (1). The obstruction is emptiness, for every \(p\), of that hit. This is the official institutional obstruction.

A still weaker, usually too weak, reading is “for each \(x\) there is some \(p_x\)” — that is full information, not an information-compatible prescription.

**Minimal assumptions.** Declared spaces \(P\), \(U_{\mathrm{raw}}\); nonempty-valued \(\operatorname{Impl}\); a σ-algebra on \(P\) if randomized prescriptions are allowed. For implementability of \(p(\cdot)\) as a function of \(\iota\), B2.

**Proof sketch.** (1)\(\Rightarrow\)(3) by taking a constant implementation. The converse fails as soon as \(\operatorname{Impl}(x,p)\) varies with \(x\) for fixed \(p\) (a statute interpreted by local agencies; a quota enforced with local slack; a technology whose realized input depends on hidden state).

**Counterexample.** Two latent types \(x_L,x_H\), one prescription “apply the standard instrument,” implementations \(\operatorname{Impl}(x_L,p)=\{u_L\}\), \(\operatorname{Impl}(x_H,p)=\{u_H\}\), with \(u_L\in U_{\mathrm{safe}}(x_L)\), \(u_H\in U_{\mathrm{safe}}(x_H)\), but \(U_{\mathrm{safe}}(x_L)\cap U_{\mathrm{safe}}(x_H)=\emptyset\). Common raw action fails; common prescription succeeds.

**Recommended source status:** *definition* plus a *theorem* stating the obstruction at level (3) for institutional kernels and at level (2) for purely epistemic kernels with a single actuator. Article 006’s raw-action intersection, if that is what it used, is superseded.

**Downstream consequences.** This is the difference between “no safe open-loop action under ambiguity” and “no safe instrument.” Operator I institutional viability must use (3). Composition contracts that share a prescription (quota, interest rate, legal standard) must also use (3).

**Relevant literature.**

- Partial-observation viability: Baras, J.S., James, M.R. Partially observed differential games, infinite-dimensional Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs equations, and nonlinear \(H_\infty\) control. *SIAM J. Control Optim.* 34 (1996), 1342–1364.

  \Pi_X\bigl(\operatorname{EViab}_{\mathcal I'}(K)\bigr)
  \subset
  \operatorname{RViab}_{\mathrm{full}}(K),
  \]
  under aligned \((\mathbb P,\Delta)\) and a projection that sends a savable information state to a physical initial condition (or set of them) that is savable. The second inclusion needs that full information can simulate any partial-information policy.
- **Order on hyperspaces.** If one uses inclusion of subsets of \(X\) as information states, refinement is \(\iota'\subset\iota\), and monotonicity is: if \(\iota\) is savable then every \(\iota'\subset\iota\) is savable (smaller ambiguity). That is an *upset* in the reverse-inclusion order, not a statement about kernels in different ambient spaces.

**Minimal assumptions.** A declared refinement morphism \(r:\mathcal I'\to\mathcal I\) (a surjective, information-forgetting map) and a projection \(\Pi_X\). Without those maps, write “not comparable.”

**Proof sketch.** A policy measurable w.r.t. the coarser σ-algebra is measurable w.r.t. the finer one, so every coarse-information witness remains a fine-information witness (after composition with \(r\)). The converse is false (Witsenhausen-type and standard POMDP counterexamples).

**Counterexample to literal inclusion.** \(\mathcal I=\mathcal P(X)\), \(\mathcal I'=X\) (full information). \(\operatorname{EViab}_{\mathcal I}\subset\mathcal P(X)\), \(\operatorname{EViab}_{\mathcal I'}\subset X\). These are not subsets of a common space. Identifying \(x\) with \(\{x\}\) makes the inclusion of *projections* meaningful; identifying them silently is a type error.

**Recommended source status:** *theorem* once the morphism is declared; otherwise a *template*. Retrofit Article 002 knowledge-kernel monotonicity statements with \(\Pi_X\) or \(r\).

**Downstream consequences.** Priority 1.4 and 8.1. Any flagship diagram that nests epistemic kernels inside physical kernels without a projection is not mathematics.

### Issue ID: D1 — hybrid phase space

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** correct.

**Mathematical status:** `TYPE_OR_DOMAIN_ERROR`.

**Corrected statement.**
Do not place a discrete institutional mode inside a continuous-time history as if it were a continuous coordinate.

### Issue ID: E1 — hold / interpolation mismatch

**Verdict on the internal diagnosis:** partly correct. The safe default is to restrict to zero-order hold. Generalization to measurable convex interpolation is possible but is a different theorem.

**Mathematical status:** `MODEL_INCOMPLETE` if an invariance proposition is stated for a sampling operator that the proofs do not cover. `INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION` on the actual proposition.

**Corrected statement.**
Let sampling period be \(\tau>0\). Two interpolation classes:

1. **Zero-order hold (official, recommended).** \(u(t)=u_k\) on \([k\tau,(k+1)\tau)\). Invariance of a closed convex \(K\) under ZOH is *not* implied by \(x(k\tau)\in K\) for all \(k\). A sufficient condition: \(K\) convex compact, \(f\) Lipschitz, and a sampled discriminating inequality with an explicit intersample remainder,
   \[
   \sup_{w(\cdot)}\operatorname{dist}\Bigl(x(k\tau)+\tau f(x(k\tau),u_k,w),K\Bigr)
   +O(\tau^2)\le 0,
   \]
   or a barrier/CBF decrease integrated over the hold interval. Better: require the entire intersample arc to satisfy a strong-invariance test for the frozen-\(u\) inclusion.
2. **Measurable convex interpolation.** \(u(t)\in\operatorname{co}\{u_k,u_{k+1}\}\) or an arbitrary measurable selection in the convex hull of a finite alphabet. This *enlarges* the attainable set. Invariance under ZOH does not imply invariance under interpolation, and conversely a convexified hold can leave a nonconvex \(K\) even if the two endpoints are safe. A correct generalization needs \(K\) convex *and* the vector field’s convex hull to satisfy the same inward-drift test.

**Minimal assumptions.** ZOH: measurable sampled inputs, Carathéodory \(f\), declared \(\tau\). Interpolation: convex \(K\), convex-valued dynamics, plus the same inward test on the convex hull.

**Proof sketch.** During a hold, \(u\) is constant, so the intersample problem is an uncontrolled (or disturbance-only) inclusion. Nagumo at sample instants is silent about \(t\in(k\tau,(k+1)\tau)\). A standard counterexample is a safe sampled pair whose connecting flow arcs out of \(K\) and returns (a fast oscillation, or a nonconvex obstacle).

**Counterexample.** \(K\) an annulus, sampling on the inner and outer circles, ZOH flow that crosses the hole. Samples in \(K\), trajectory not in \(K\). For convex \(K\), a linear system with large \(\tau\) can still exit between samples.

**Recommended source status:** *conditional theorem* restricted to ZOH plus an intersample invariance estimate. A convex-interpolation theorem is a separate conditional result, not a free upgrade. Align with B2’s sample-and-hold implementation semantics.

**Downstream consequences.** Article 011’s invariance proposition and Article 002’s sampled theorems must use the same hold class. If 011 uses a different interpolation in estimation than in the invariance proof, the empirical design is not an instance of the theorem.

**Relevant literature.**
- Francis, B.A., Georgiou, T.T. Stability theory for linear time-invariant plants with periodic digital controllers. *IEEE TAC* 33 (1988), 820–832.

**Corrected statement (minimal defensible identification sequence).**
Separate four estimands. Do not let one design answer all four.

2. **Physical / reduced dynamics.** A prospective, pre-registered comparison of a small set of held-out models (including Article 012’s delay family if that is the specialization). ### 7.1 Institutional measurement

**Compare the three alternatives, then choose what can be measured first without creating another arbitrary index.**

1. **Multidimensional operating vector**
   \[
   v=(\text{observation quality},\ \text{latency},\ \text{authority},\ \text{enforcement},\ \text{compliance},\ \text{mandate load},\ \text{learning},\ \text{budget margin}).
   \]
   This is a *definition* of a typed dashboard, not a theorem and not an index. Each coordinate must have a unit, a domain, and a failure direction. No aggregation. This is measurable first: most coordinates already exist as administrative data or can be defined as clocks and counts (time from observation to decision, detected-violation rate, budget slack).

2. **Dynamics-aware margin** \(M_\tau=T_{\mathrm{exit}}-T_{\mathrm{response}}\).
   This is a genuine one-dimensional *derived diagnostic*, not a solvency index. It is meaningful only relative to a declared disturbance class and a declared exit test (first exit of a named set). Estimation of \(T_{\mathrm{exit}}\) is a viability/reachability computation (or a conservative bound), not a bookkeeping identity. Do not treat \(M_\tau\) as “the” institutional state.

3. **Epistemic-institutional viability kernel or capture basin.**
   This is the theoretically correct object (Operator I on \((\iota,q)\), or a capture basin to a safe mode). It is *not* what one measures first. It is what the vector and the margin are evidence *for*.

**Recommendation.** Measure (1) immediately, compute (2) as a named diagnostic whenever an exit-time bound exists, and treat (3) as the official theoretical target. Publish no scalar “institutional solvency” number. A vector plus one derived margin is not an index and cannot fail in the way Article 008 failed (unit errors, exergy metaphor, necessity/sufficiency swap).

If a single operational predicate is needed for \(\mathbf J_\Omega\)’s institutional coordinate, use a *conjunction of typed thresholds* on \(v\) and a sign condition on \(M_\tau\), not a weighted sum.

**Status:** (1) definition/template; (2) definition plus a conditional theorem once \(T_{\mathrm{exit}}\) is a defined hitting time; (3) theorem target of Operator I, not a measurement design.

### 7.2 Distributive measurement

**Valid design, in pieces.**

- **Dimensionless margins.** For each protected coordinate \(i\) and each person/group \(a\),
  \[
  m_i^a=\frac{x_i^a-x_{i,\flat}^a}{s_i}
  \]
  with a declared scale \(s_i>0\) (constraint gap, not a utility). The primitive object is the family \(\{m_i^a\}\), or the random vector \(m\) under a declared population measure.
- **Conjunction.** Exact nonsmooth conjunction is \(\inf_{i,a}m_i^a\ge 0\), or \(m\in\mathbb R_+^{I\times A}\). A smooth inner certificate is
  \[
  -\tau\log\sum_{i,a}\mathrm e^{-m_i^a/\tau}
  \le
  \min_{i,a}m_i^a,
  \]
  so nonnegativity of the LogSumExp inner bound *implies* the exact conjunction. The reverse is false. Finite \(\tau\) is never “equivalent.” Use LSE only as a conservative certificate, and report \(\tau\).
- **Bottom-group.** Pre-specify the functional: essential infimum (the viability-native choice) or a named low quantile \(q_\alpha(m_i)\) with \(\alpha\) registered. Do not pool regional deciles and then treat the pooled decile as a person. If regions have different measures, the correct pool is the *mixture of the micro-level distributions*,
  \[
  \mu=\sum_r \lambda_r\mu_r,
  \]
  with declared weights \(\lambda_r\) (population, not “one per region”). Then take \(\operatorname{ess inf}_\mu\) or \(q_\alpha(\mu)\).
- **Intergenerational.** A transition kernel \(P(dx_{t+1}\mid x_t,\pi,\delta)\) on the margin vector (or on the bottom-group functional). Finite-horizon distributive viability is
  \[
  \operatorname{ess inf}\,m_t\ge 0\quad\forall t\in T
  \]
  along every admissible disturbance, or a chance version with a registered \(\alpha\). A transition *matrix* is legitimate only on a declared finite partition; otherwise it is a kernel.
- **Dynamic certificate.** A genuine CBF / viability barrier is a \(C^1\) (or Lipschitz) function \(b\) with \(\{b\ge 0\}\subset\{m\ge 0\}\) and
  \[
  \inf_{w}\sup_{u\in U^{\mathrm{impl}}}\langle\nabla b,f\rangle+\alpha(b)\ge 0
  \]
  on a neighbourhood of the boundary (robust CBF), or the corresponding contingent-cone condition. This is Operator I, not a static index.

**Reject** any construction that: mixes dimensions inside an unnormalized LSE; claims finite-smoothing equivalence; treats regional decile midpoints as observations of a common law.

**What to measure first.** The micro-level dimensionless margins and a registered bottom functional. That is already a measurement design. The CBF is the later theoretical layer.

**Status:** definitions + a conditional CBF theorem. No new scalar index.

**Literature.**
- Ames, A.D., Xu, X., Grizzle, J.W., Tabuada, P. Control barrier function based quadratic programs. *IEEE TAC* 62 (2017), 3861–3876.
- Rockafellar, Wets, *Variational Analysis* (ess inf, support).

### 8.1 Viability hierarchy

Official names and symbols are the B1 table. Short restatement:

1. Controlled existential viability: \(\operatorname{Viab}(K;F,T)\). Quantifiers \(\exists u(\cdot)\) (open loop or relaxed), some disturbance or the union inclusion.
2. Robust / discriminating: \(\operatorname{RViab}(K;\mathbb P,\Delta,T)\) or \(\operatorname{Disc}\). Quantifiers \(\exists\pi\in\mathbb P\ \forall\delta\in\Delta\).
3. Epistemic: \(\operatorname{EViab}(K;\mathcal I,T)\), subset of the information space.
4. Institutional: \(\operatorname{IViab}(K;\mathcal N,U^{\mathrm{auth}},U^{\mathrm{impl}},T)\), same physical or hybrid space as (2) but with a smaller policy class and a prescription-level obstruction (C2).
5. Finite-horizon chance: \(\operatorname{CViab}^\alpha(K;T)\). Quantifiers \(\exists\pi\) such that \(\mathbb P(\tau_{\mathrm{exit}}>T)\ge\alpha\).
6. Capture / recoverability: \(\operatorname{Capt}(C_{\mathrm{env}},K_{\mathrm{tgt}};\mathbb P,\Delta,T)\).
7. Safety under a fixed policy: \(\operatorname{Inv}(K;\pi,\Delta,T)\).

**Literal inclusions** (same \(X\), same \(K\), same \(T\), aligned classes):
\[
\operatorname{Inv}(K;\pi,\Delta)
\subset
\operatorname{RViab}
\subset
\operatorname{Viab},
\qquad
\operatorname{IViab}\subset\operatorname{RViab},
\qquad
\operatorname{RViab}\subset\operatorname{CViab}^\alpha
\]
when every admissible \(\delta\) is in the chance model’s support.

**Require a projection or aligned classes:** anything involving \(\operatorname{EViab}\); comparison across architectures; comparison across information structures (C3); chance vs robust when the probability model does not dominate \(\Delta\); capture basins with different envelopes.

**Do not draw:** \(\operatorname{EViab}\subset\operatorname{RViab}\) as subsets of one space; \(\operatorname{CViab}^\alpha\subset\operatorname{RViab}\); products of typed kernels equal to the kernel of a product (see missing issues below).

### 8.3 Transformation theorem target

**Smallest nontrivial class that is not a restatement of reach-avoid.**

**Data.** A finite architecture alphabet \(Q=\{1,\ldots,m\}\). For each \(q\):

- a compact metric state space \(X_q\subset\mathbb R^{n_q}\);
- a Marchaud (or Lipschitz) control-disturbance map \(F_q:X_q\times U_q\times W_q\rightrightarrows\mathbb R^{n_q}\);
- closed safe set \(K_q\), closed non-relaxable forbidden set \(N_q\subset X_q\), \(K_q\cap N_q=\emptyset\);
- a Lipschitz identity readout \(\iota_q:X_q\to I\), \(I\) a common compact metric space, and a closed higher-order identity set \(I^H\subset I\);
- a closed-valued implementation correspondence and a policy class \(\mathbb P_q\), disturbance class \(\Delta_q\).

**Transitions.** A directed graph \((Q,E)\). For each edge \(qq'\): a closed guard \(G_{qq'}\subset X_q\setminus N_q\), a Lipschitz reset \(R_{qq'}:G_{qq'}\to X_{q'}\), a jump-harm \(c_{qq'}\ge 0\). Identity continuity:
\[
\iota_{q'}\bigl(R_{qq'}(z)\bigr)=\iota_q(z)
\quad\forall z\in G_{qq'}.
\]
(If this is too strong for a first theorem, replace equality by a declared non-expansive bound, but then \(I^H\) must be fattened. I recommend exact preservation for the first theorem.)

**Relaxable constraints and harm.** A running cost \(\ell_q\ge 0\) and a budget coordinate \(h\in[0,H_{\max}]\),
\[
\dot h=\ell_q(z,u,w),\qquad h^+=h+c_{qq'}.
\]
Non-relaxable constraints \(N_q\) apply during flow *and* jumps. Relaxable constraints may fail only if \(h\le H_{\max}\).

**Hybrid state.**
\[
\xi=(q,z,h)\in\bigsqcup_{q\in Q}X_q\times[0,H_{\max}],
\qquad
\iota_q(z)\in I^H,\quad z\notin N_q.
\]
Meta-policies are hybrid strategies: a mode-dependent causal \(\pi_q\) and a nonanticipative jump choice on guards. Disturbances are hybrid as well (flow disturbances and, if adversarial timing is in \(\Delta\), the choice of when to hit a guard).

**Theorem (finite-mode identity-preserving transformational viability).**
The system is transformationally viable from \((q_0,z_0)\) iff the hybrid discriminating kernel of the above hybrid inclusion, inside the hybrid safe set
\[
K^\sharp
=
\bigl\{(q,z,h):z\in K_q\cup C^{\mathrm{trans}}_q,\ z\notin N_q,\ \iota_q(z)\in I^H,\ h\le H_{\max}\bigr\},
\]
is nonempty at \((q_0,z_0,0)\), where \(C^{\mathrm{trans}}_q\) is a declared closed transition envelope (the only place relaxable constraints may fail). Moreover, if \(I^H\) is a finite set, nonempty transformational viability implies existence of a path \(q_0\to\cdots\to q_\star\) in \((Q,E)\) such that:

- each reset is defined on the reachable identity fibre;
- the composed reset lands in \(\operatorname{Disc}_{q_\star}(K_{q_\star})\);
- total jump harm plus integrated flow harm \(\le H_{\max}\).

The second clause is the nontrivial, checkable content: a graph reachability problem on identity fibres plus one ordinary Operator I kernel in the destination. Impossibility within a registered architecture class is emptiness of this hybrid kernel, which for finite \(Q\) and finite \(I^H\) reduces to emptiness of finitely many ordinary discriminating kernels and a finite directed graph.

This is not “write reach-avoid on the disjoint union.” The identity-fibre obstruction and the split between \(N_q\) and the harm-budgeted envelope are the extra mathematics, and they are exactly what the specification \(\Omega\) asked for.

**Status target:** one *conditional theorem* in the flagship, proof in a supplement, under GST hybrid basic conditions plus Lipschitz resets. No category theory in the first theorem; a category of architectures is a later optional language, not a prerequisite.

### 8.4 Composition theorem target

**Restricted theorem (checkable, conclusion not assumed).**

**Class.** Two (or \(N\)) modules with compact state spaces, Lipschitz dynamics, and \(C^1\) robust control-barrier functions \(b_i\) defining
\[
K_i=\{x_i:b_i(x_i)\ge 0\}.
\]
Interface outputs \(y_i=h_i(x_i)\) enter the other module as disturbances. Each uncoupled module is ISS with respect to its interface input, with class-\(\mathcal K\) gains \(\gamma_{ij}\) relative to the barrier coordinate (or ordinary ISS gains about a robustly invariant compact set).

**Checkable condition.** Small gain:
\[
\gamma_{12}\circ\gamma_{21}<\mathrm{id}
\]
(and the cyclic small-gain condition if \(N>2\)), plus a uniform interface bound \(h_i(K_i)\subset Y_i\) used to *start* the gain computation, or a contraction \(h_i(K_i)\subset\operatorname{int} Y_i\) in the simpler cascade case.

**Conclusion (not assumed).** \(K_1\times K_2\) is robustly invariant for the coupled system, and the coupled discriminating kernel contains \(K_1\times K_2\). (Alternatively: the product is a robust *positively invariant* set under the product implementation class.) This uses the barriers and the gains; it does not assume a joint invariant set.

**Why this class.** Barriers are already the natural certificates for Operator I; small gain is checkable; the conclusion is joint robust invariance, which the packet does not already assume. Finite-state assume-guarantee contracts are a legitimate discrete companion, but they do not exercise the continuous Operator I spine. Monotone interval invariants (Angeli–Sontag) are a good *second* restricted class, especially for Article 012-style biogeochemical models, and should sit in the supplement.

**Destruction counterexample.** Two scalar modules \(\dot x_i=-x_i+u_i+y_{3-i}\), each robustly invariant in \([-1,1]\) when the interface is bounded by \(1/2\) and \(u_i\) has authority \(1\). Couple with \(y_i=3x_i\). Each module, viewing the other’s *uncoupled* output bound, looks safe; the coupled linear system has a positive eigenvalue and exits any bounded set. Moral: interface gains computed on the wrong bound destroy composition. (A commons version: two viable harvesters sharing a stock whose joint floor exceeds \(H_{\mathrm{safe}}\) — A7/A8.)

**Coupling-rescue example.** Two subsistence consumers \(\dot k_i=y_i-c_{\min}+u_i\), each facing a disturbance \(y_i\in\{0,2\}\) that can be \(0\) long enough to exit \(\{k_i\ge k_\flat\}\) individually. A transfer \(v\) with \(v=-v_{\mathrm{other}}\), \(|v|\le \bar v\), makes \(\{k_1+k_2\ge 2k_\flat,\,k_i\ge k_\flat-\varepsilon\}\) robustly invariant though neither factor is. Moral: composition can *create* an Operator I kernel. This is also the simplest institutional insurance example.

**Status target:** one *conditional theorem* in the flagship; the two examples in the flagship body; linear-polytope and finite-state contract versions in the supplement.

**Literature.**
- Dashkovskiy, S., Rüffer, B., Wirth, F. Small gain theorems for large scale systems and construction of ISS Lyapunov functions. *SIAM J. Control Optim.* 48 (2010), 4089–4118.
- Jiang, Z.P., Teel, A.R., Praly, L. Small-gain theorem for ISS systems and applications. *Math. Control Signals Systems* 7 (1994), 95–120.
- Ames et al., CBF paper cited above.
- Benveniste, A., Caillaud, B., Nickovic, D., et al. Contracts for system design. *Found. Trends Electron. Des. Autom.* 12 (2018), 124–400.

### 8.5 Empirical anchor

**Choose groundwater as the first complete domain test of the architecture.** Use phosphorus as the accounting specialization already sitting in Article 013, not as the architectural test.

**Reason, against the four named criteria.**

- **Falsifiability of the architecture, not of a stock story.** Groundwater forces hidden state, delayed observation, a common-pool harvest obstruction (A7–A9), an institutional implementation correspondence (permits, well metering, latencies), and a possible Operator II move (regime change: unmanaged pumping → permitted allocations → managed recharge). Phosphorus is a better *accounting* and spatial-coupling illustration; it can be survived as a closed moiety ledger without ever exercising \(\operatorname{EViab}\) or \(\operatorname{IViab}\). That would illustrate, not test, the spine.
- **Available measurements.** Groundwater measurements are sparser and slower, which is a scientific cost and an architectural benefit: the information structure \(\mathcal I\) is not a toy. Heads, pumping, and permit data exist in multiple jurisdictions; they are enough to instantiate the institutional vector of §7.1 and a set-valued filter, not enough to pretend exact full-state feedback. Phosphorus has cleaner concentrations and trade data; that favours 013, not Operator I–II.
- **Model-identification feasibility.** Worse for groundwater, and that is acceptable if the first complete case is allowed to deliver *outer approximations* and an honest indeterminate verdict rather than a fitted narrative. Article 012’s delay family and Article 011’s periodic-review design transfer more directly to groundwater (pumping reviews, lagged heads) than to a phosphorus trade network. Identification should follow E2 and is allowed to conclude partial identification.
- **Architectural layers exercised.** Groundwater: Operators I (robust + epistemic + institutional), recovery envelopes (managed recharge, emergency curtailment), possibly Operator II, commons theorems, sampled review. Phosphorus: accounting identities, spatial composition, trade interfaces. The latter are real layers, but they are the ones already verified in 013. Using phosphorus as the *first complete* case would overweight the one layer that is already in good shape.

**Publication risk, stated plainly.** Phosphorus would land faster and look cleaner. If the programme’s binding constraint is a first accepted applied paper rather than an architectural test, reverse the choice and say so. The packet’s stated objective does not license that reversal.

**What goes where.** 013’s phosphorus (or general moiety) ledger: flagship appendix, as a typed specialization that already works. Groundwater: the end-to-end case, either as a long flagship section plus supplement or, if length explodes, as the applied companion that *uses* the flagship operators rather than redefining them. Articles 004–005 remain templates, not papers.

## H. Major mathematical issues absent from this packet

These are not minor nits. Several of them can falsify a flagship claim even after A1–E2 are repaired.

1. **Product of typed safe sets versus kernel of a product.**
   \(\mathbf J_\Omega=(P,F,N,R,E)\) invites five safe sets. In general
   \[
   \operatorname{RViab}(K_P\times K_F\times K_N\times K_R\times K_E)
   \subsetneq
   \prod_\bullet\operatorname{RViab}(K_\bullet)
   \]
   when policies and disturbances are shared. The official system-level status therefore cannot be “viable iff each coordinate is viable” without a theorem. You need an explicit aggregation rule (recommended default: *conjunctive* — system viable iff the *joint* robust kernel of the product set is nonempty at \(z_0\); any coordinate indeterminate and none failed \(\Rightarrow\) system indeterminate). Typed failure semantics (the packet’s own standing constraint) does not by itself give this rule.

2. **Well-posedness of discontinuous robust feedback.**
   Discriminating kernels are often implemented by discontinuous \(\pi\). Without an official solution concept (sample-and-hold — my recommendation — or Filippov–Krasovskii, or π-strategies), Operator I’s \(\exists\pi\in\mathbb P^{\mathrm{impl}}\) is not defined, and numerical schemes have nothing to converge to. This is adjacent to B2/E1 but is not listed.

3. **Non-commutation of infinite horizon and viability kernels.**
   \(\operatorname{RViab}(K,[0,T])\) need not converge in Hausdorff metric to \(\operatorname{RViab}(K,[0,\infty))\) as \(T\to\infty\), and the infinite-horizon kernel can be empty under arbitrarily small outward drift. Sustainability language is infinite-horizon; every theorem in the flagship must name \(T\), and the \(T=\infty\) case needs a separate hypothesis (strict inward drift, or a viability multiplier). Related normative literature (Chichilnisky, Asheim) is not a substitute for this topology fact, but it should be cited when \(T=\infty\) is given ethical meaning.

4. **Time-consistency of \(\mathbf J_\Omega\).**
   If the five-component verdict is recomputed along a trajectory, does a currently viable state remain a state whose verdict is “currently viable” under the induced continuation specification? This is a dynamic-programming / dynamic-consistency question. Without it, “official system-level statuses” can chatter, and Operator II can be gamed by re-specification (the \(\mathcal R_A\) slot). A minimal requirement: \(\Omega\) is frozen (the packet already says this) and the verdict map is non-improving under restriction of \(T\).

5. **Specification-revision attacks.**
   \(\mathcal R_A\) is in \(\Omega\). If architecture-change rules can relax \(I^H\) or \(K\) without an identity-continuity constraint, Operator II is vacuous: everything is transformationally viable by renaming. The 8.3 identity-preservation condition must bind \(\mathcal R_A\), not only physical resets. This is a mathematical side-constraint on an object the packet treats as given.

6. **Chance models that do not dominate \(\Delta\).**
   If the stochastic model puts mass only on a proper subset of \(\Delta\), \(\operatorname{RViab}\subset\operatorname{CViab}^\alpha\) can fail (a robustly fatal disturbance with probability 0). The inclusion in 8.1 is not free. Any flagship diagram that nests chance above robust without domination is false.

7. **RFDE state space.**
   Article 012 and Article 002’s RFDE theorems live on \(C([-\tau,0];X)\) (or a Sobolev/M1 history space), not on \(X\). Literal inclusion of an RFDE kernel in an ODE kernel is a type error of the same kind as C3. The packet’s “state spaces are metric spaces” is true but not sufficient; the flagship must name history spaces when delays are present.

8. **Absence of a comparison / antitheorem section in the spine.**
   The packet has good local counterexamples (A1, A2, A11, composition). The flagship still needs a single place that records: viability \(\neq\) strong invariance \(\neq\) discriminating viability; more controls help, fewer need not; more information helps only after projection; composition can destroy and can rescue. Otherwise repaired theorems will be re-misread in domain modules.

**Literature for the missing issues.**
- Chichilnisky, G. An axiomatic approach to sustainable development. *Soc. Choice Welfare* 13 (1996), 231–257.
- Asheim, G.B. Intergenerational equity. *Annu. Rev. Econ.* 2 (2010), 197–222.
- Hale–Verduyn Lunel, *FDEs*, for (7).
- Goebel–Sanfelice–Teel, for (2) and hybrid well-posedness.
- For (1), this is elementary set-valued analysis; I do not know a single “product of discriminating kernels” theorem that the flagship can cite as a black box, which is why it must prove or avoid it.
