## 1. The theorem worth pursuing

The proposed four-theorem spine is not, by itself, a new viability theory. Belief recursion, finite losing trees, common-action infeasibility, Farkas multipliers, and the inequality “survival time is shorter than observation delay” all have standard antecedents.

A stronger, narrower contribution is:

> **Recourse-aware continuous-to-finite nonviability certification.**  
> For a class of continuous-time systems with exogenous partial observation, construct finite linear programs whose infeasibility certifies failure of **every measurable information-adapted controller**, not merely sampled or parameterized controllers. Prove an explicit approximation-error bound, finite termination whenever nonviability holds, and a sharp sparsity law governed by **information–time rank**, rather than instantaneous input dimension.

The important distinction is that the certificate may use safety obligations **after information arrives**. It accounts for the controller’s best post-observation recourse. Thus it can prove nonviability even when:

- every compatible state is individually viable under full information;
- a common control keeps every branch safe throughout the blind interval;
- instantaneous admissibility and tangency tests find no obstruction.

Below is a self-contained theorem package supporting that claim. Its standard ingredients are identified explicitly. Bibliographic priority still requires a targeted comparison with infinite-dimensional linear programming, convex-team control, and certified discretization results; Farkas, Helly, and information-state sufficiency should not be advertised as new.

# 2. Model, information, and quantifiers

## 2.1 Dynamics

Let the hidden mode be
\[
\theta\in\Theta:=\{1,\dots,M\}.
\]

Conditional on \(\theta\), the continuous state satisfies
\[
\dot x(t)
=A_\theta(t)x(t)+g_\theta(t)
+B_\theta(t)u(t)+E_\theta(t)d(t),
\qquad t\in[0,T].
\]

Assume:

1. \(A_\theta,g_\theta,B_\theta,E_\theta\) are bounded and piecewise continuous; \(B_\theta,E_\theta\) have known bounded-variation bounds.
2. The initial set in mode \(\theta\) is a nonempty compact polytope
   \[
   P_\theta\subset\mathbb R^n.
   \]
3. The input and disturbance sets are nonempty compact polytopes:
   \[
   U=\{v:Fv\le f\}\subset\mathbb R^m,\qquad D_\theta\subset\mathbb R^p.
   \]
4. Safety is the closed polyhedron
   \[
   V=\{x:c_i^\top x\le b_i,\ i=1,\dots,s\}.
   \]
5. The initial information state
   \[
   B_0=\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}\{\theta\}\times P_\theta
   \]
   is initially safe. Otherwise a time-zero certificate suffices.

The dynamics admit unique Carathéodory solutions for every admissible measurable signal pair.

## 2.2 Information structure

At known times
\[
0=\tau_0<\tau_1<\cdots<\tau_R=T,
\]
the controller receives exogenous information about \(\theta\).

Let
\[
\mathcal P_0\preceq\mathcal P_1\preceq\cdots\preceq\mathcal P_{R-1}
\]
be refining partitions of \(\Theta\). During
\[
I_r=[\tau_r,\tau_{r+1}),
\]
the observation history identifies the cell
\[
C_r(\theta)\in\mathcal P_r.
\]

These are **history partitions**, not necessarily partitions generated by the latest observation alone. Nonrefining raw observations can be replaced by their history refinement.

Crucially, observations do not depend on \(x,u,d\). This excludes active sensing and endogenous observation branching from the exact theorem.

## 2.3 Policy class

Policies are deterministic, measurable, nonanticipative, and adapted to the observation history. Controls may vary arbitrarily measurably between observations.

Because each partition cell has a unique ancestor history, every such policy is represented exactly by signals
\[
u_{r,C}\in L^\infty(I_r;U),
\qquad C\in\mathcal P_r.
\]

On mode \(\theta\),
\[
u^\pi_\theta(t)=u_{r,C_r(\theta)}(t),\qquad t\in I_r.
\]

This is not an assertion that arbitrary record-based policies can always be replaced by \(u=\pi(B_t)\). It is an exact representation for the specified exogenous-information model.

The robust quantifier order is
\[
\exists\pi\in\Pi_{\mathcal I}\ 
\forall\theta\in\Theta\
\forall x_0\in P_\theta\
\forall d\in L^\infty([0,T];D_\theta)\
\forall t\in[0,T].
\]

The adversary selects a mode, initial state, and measurable disturbance signal with knowledge of the announced policy. Randomized policies are not included.

Define
\[
\mathcal K_{\mathcal I}^T
=
\left\{
B_0:
\exists\pi\in\Pi_{\mathcal I}\ 
\forall\theta,x_0,d,t,\quad
x_{\theta,x_0,d}^{\pi}(t)\in V
\right\}.
\]

A finite-horizon nonviability certificate also excludes infinite-horizon viability.

## 2.4 Relation to full-information viability

Let \(K_{\rm full}^T\) be the physical-state viability kernel when \((\theta,x)\) is observed continuously. Define its belief lifting by
\[
\operatorname{Lift}(K_{\rm full}^T)
=
\{B:\varnothing\ne B\subseteq K_{\rm full}^T\}.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_{\mathcal I}^T
\subseteq
\operatorname{Lift}(K_{\rm full}^T).
}
\]

Indeed, a full-information controller can emulate any information-constrained policy. Strict inclusion will be established in the example—indeed, every two-state subbelief will be viable while the three-state belief is not.

# 3. Adjoint safety rows

Let \(X_\theta(t,s)\) denote the state-transition matrix.

For a label
\[
a=(\theta,i,t)\in
\mathscr S:=\Theta\times\{1,\dots,s\}\times[0,T],
\]
define the input kernel
\[
k_a(\sigma)
=
\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma\le t\}}
B_\theta(\sigma)^\top
X_\theta(t,\sigma)^\top c_i.
\]

Write \(h_Q(v)=\sup_{q\in Q}v^\top q\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_a
={}&b_i
-h_{P_\theta}\!\left(X_\theta(t,0)^\top c_i\right)\\
&-\int_0^t c_i^\top X_\theta(t,\sigma)g_\theta(\sigma)\,d\sigma\\
&-\int_0^t
h_{D_\theta}\!\left(
E_\theta(\sigma)^\top X_\theta(t,\sigma)^\top c_i
\right)d\sigma .
\end{aligned}
\]

For a policy \(\pi\), set
\[
\mathcal F_a(\pi)
=
\int_0^T
k_a(\sigma)^\top
u^\pi_\theta(\sigma)\,d\sigma
-\beta_a.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
\pi\text{ is robustly safe on }[0,T]
\iff
\mathcal F_a(\pi)\le0
\quad\forall a\in\mathscr S.
}
\]

This follows from the linear solution formula. Initial-state and disturbance maximizations separate from the control term because the observation history, and hence the control signal in a mode, does not depend on \(x_0\) or \(d\).

For polytopic \(D_\theta\), a maximizing disturbance can be chosen measurably by selecting a maximizing vertex at each time.

Define the minimax safety violation
\[
J_{\mathcal I}(T)
=
\min_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mathcal I}}
\max_{a\in\mathscr S}\mathcal F_a(\pi).
\]

The minimum exists. The policy-signal space is weak-* compact, and each \(\mathcal F_a\) is weak-* continuous. Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
B_0\notin\mathcal K_{\mathcal I}^T
\iff
J_{\mathcal I}(T)>0.
}
\]

The strict positive margin here is a consequence of compactness and closed safety constraints—not an extra nonviability assumption.

# 4. The finite obstruction certificate

Define
\[
\phi_U(g):=\min_{v\in U}g^\top v=-h_U(-g).
\]

Choose finitely many labels
\[
a_\ell=(\theta_\ell,i_\ell,t_\ell),
\qquad \ell=1,\dots,q,
\]
and weights
\[
\lambda_\ell\ge0,\qquad \sum_{\ell=1}^q\lambda_\ell=1.
\]

For each active information cell \(C\in\mathcal P_r\), pool the adjoint kernels:
\[
g_{r,C}(\sigma)
=
\sum_{\ell:\theta_\ell\in C}
\lambda_\ell k_{a_\ell}(\sigma),
\qquad \sigma\in I_r.
\]

The certificate margin is
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\mathcal I}(\lambda,a)
=
\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}
\int_{I_r}
\sum_{C\in\mathcal P_r}
\phi_U\!\left(g_{r,C}(\sigma)\right)d\sigma
-
\sum_{\ell=1}^q\lambda_\ell\beta_{a_\ell}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

## Certificate semantics

If
\[
\Gamma_{\mathcal I}(\lambda,a)>0,
\]
then no admissible information-adapted policy is safe.

For every policy,
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^q
\lambda_\ell\mathcal F_{a_\ell}(\pi)
\ge
\Gamma_{\mathcal I}(\lambda,a).
\]
Therefore at least one stored label satisfies
\[
\mathcal F_{a_\ell}(\pi)
\ge
\Gamma_{\mathcal I}(\lambda,a)>0.
\]

Thus the certificate establishes
\[
\forall\pi\in\Pi_{\mathcal I}\ 
\exists\ell\in\{1,\dots,q\}:
\text{an admissible realization violates facet }i_\ell
\text{ at time }t_\ell.
\]

The violating label may depend on the policy. The finite list and its weights do not.

This is not an adversarial tree. It is a finite, policy-independent set of adversarial tests combined by an adjoint identity.

# 5. Main theorem: a complete, error-controlled finite bridge

The exact dual expression (1) is useful, but by itself it would be too close to standard infinite-dimensional convex duality. The consequential result is a **sound finite construction covering unrestricted measurable controls**.

## 5.1 Outer control moments

Choose a control-time mesh
\[
\mathcal H=\{J_1,\dots,J_H\}
\]
containing every sensing time. Thus each \(J_j\) lies within one information stage.

Introduce one vector
\[
v_{j,C}\in U
\]
for every cell \(C\) active during \(J_j\). Let
\[
Q=\sum_{j=1}^H|\mathcal P_{r(j)}|
\]
be the number of information–time blocks.

For a safety label \(a=(\theta,i,t)\), define the finite row
\[
A_{a,j,C}
=
\mathbf 1_{\{\theta\in C\}}
\int_{J_j}k_a(\sigma)\,d\sigma .
\]

On \(J_j\), let
\[
\bar k_{a,\mathcal H}(\sigma)
=
\frac1{|J_j|}
\int_{J_j}k_a(s)\,ds.
\]

Define
\[
e_{a,\mathcal H}
=
\int_0^T
h_U\!\left(
\bar k_{a,\mathcal H}(\sigma)-k_a(\sigma)
\right)d\sigma.
\tag{2}
\]

Any verified upper bound \(\bar e_a\ge e_{a,\mathcal H}\) may be used.

For every measurable policy, its block averages
\[
v_{j,C}
=
\frac1{|J_j|}
\int_{J_j}u_{r(j),C}(\sigma)\,d\sigma
\]
belong to \(U\), and
\[
A_a v
-
\int_0^T k_a(\sigma)^\top u^\pi_\theta(\sigma)\,d\sigma
\le \bar e_a.
\tag{3}
\]

Equation (3) is the crucial orientation: these finite moments yield a **necessary relaxation** of continuous-time safety. They do not restrict the real controller to held inputs.

## 5.2 Inner adversarial approximation

One may use a right-hand side
\[
\beta_a^+\ge\beta_a
\]
obtained from an explicit admissible initial state and disturbance signal, rather than exact worst-case optimization.

Suppose
\[
0\le \beta_a^+-\beta_a\le\delta_\beta
\]
uniformly over the labels used.

This approximation is safe for nonviability: it weakens the adversary and enlarges the feasible controller set.

For example, let
\[
w_a(\sigma)
=
\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma\le t\}}
E_\theta(\sigma)^\top X_\theta(t,\sigma)^\top c_i.
\]
On a disturbance mesh \(\mathcal H_d\), choose
\[
d_{a,J}\in
\arg\max_{d\in D_\theta}
\left(\int_J w_a(\sigma)\,d\sigma\right)^\top d.
\]
This produces an explicit piecewise-constant admissible disturbance.

If \(D_\theta\) has radius \(R_D\) about a fixed center and
\[
\operatorname{TV}(w_a)\le V_D,
\]
then
\[
0\le \beta_a^+-\beta_a
\le R_DV_Dh_d,
\tag{4}
\]
apart from any separately accounted initial-state or numerical error.

## 5.3 Finite LP

Let \(G\subset[0,T]\) be a finite time grid, and include every mode and safety facet at every time in \(G\).

Solve
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathcal H,G}
=\min_{v,\rho}\quad &\rho\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&A_a v-\beta_a^+-\bar e_a\le\rho,
&&a\in\Theta\times\{1,\dots,s\}\times G,\\
&v_{j,C}\in U,
&&\text{all information–time blocks}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

---

## Main theorem

Assume the model above. Suppose:

1. Uniformly over admissible policies, modes, and facets,
   \[
   |\mathcal F_{\theta,i,t}(\pi)
   -\mathcal F_{\theta,i,t'}(\pi)|
   \le L|t-t'|.
   \tag{6}
   \]
2. The input kernels satisfy
   \[
   \operatorname{TV}(k_a)\le V_U.
   \tag{7}
   \]
3. \(U\) has radius \(R_U\) about some fixed center.
4. The time grid has covering radius at most \(h_t\), the control mesh has maximum length \(h_u\), and the adversarial approximation satisfies the bound above.

Then:

### (i) Certified value sandwich
\[
\boxed{
\rho_{\mathcal H,G}
\le J_{\mathcal I}(T)
\le
\rho_{\mathcal H,G}
+
R_UV_Uh_u+\delta_\beta+Lh_t.
}
\tag{8}
\]

With (4),
\[
J_{\mathcal I}(T)-\rho_{\mathcal H,G}
\le
R_UV_Uh_u+R_DV_Dh_d+Lh_t.
\tag{9}
\]

Verified numerical row widths are added to the right side.

### (ii) Sound finite certificates

If
\[
\rho_{\mathcal H,G}>0,
\]
then the LP dual supplies a finite certificate of nonviability of the original continuous-time system, covering every measurable information-adapted policy.

### (iii) Completeness under refinement

If
\[
B_0\notin\mathcal K_{\mathcal I}^T,
\]
then every sufficiently fine sequence of the above meshes, with numerical errors tending to zero, eventually produces a positive finite certificate.

Thus nonviability is semidecidable in this class when the coefficients and error bounds are effectively computable.

### (iv) Exact atomic dual representation
\[
\boxed{
J_{\mathcal I}(T)
=
\sup_{\substack{q<\infty,\ a_1,\dots,a_q\\
\lambda\in\Delta_q}}
\Gamma_{\mathcal I}(\lambda,a).
}
\tag{10}
\]

In particular, nonviability is equivalent to the existence of a positive finite atomic certificate.

### (v) Sparse witnesses

Let \(r=\operatorname{rank}A\) for the finite safety-row matrix in (5). Whenever \(\rho_{\mathcal H,G}>0\), a positive certificate exists using at most
\[
\boxed{r+1\le mQ+1}
\tag{11}
\]
safety labels.

More strongly, it can retain any prescribed certificate margin strictly below \(\rho_{\mathcal H,G}\).

If the lifted continuous-time kernels span a finite-dimensional space of dimension \(r_{\mathcal I}\), then a continuous-time obstruction has at most
\[
r_{\mathcal I}+1
\]
labels.

The relevant dimension is information–time rank, not \(m\).

# 6. Proof

## Step 1: Exact robust rows and attainment

The linear solution formula gives the robust rows above. The admissible signal space is a finite product of sets
\[
L^\infty(I_r;U).
\]

Because \(U\) is compact and convex, these sets are weak-* compact. The safety functionals are affine and weak-* continuous because their kernels are in \(L^1\). Their supremum is lower semicontinuous and attains its minimum.

Therefore safety is equivalent to \(J_{\mathcal I}(T)\le0\), and nonviability is equivalent to \(J_{\mathcal I}(T)>0\).

## Step 2: The control-moment inequality

For a block \(J_j\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left(\int_{J_j}k_a\right)^\top
\left(\frac1{|J_j|}\int_{J_j}u\right)
-\int_{J_j}k_a^\top u\\
&\qquad=
\int_{J_j}
(\bar k_{a,\mathcal H}-k_a)^\top u\\
&\qquad\le
\int_{J_j}
h_U(\bar k_{a,\mathcal H}-k_a).
\end{aligned}
\]

Summing proves (3). The averages remain compatible with the information structure because the mesh contains all sensing times and common-history controls use the same block variable.

To bound the error, choose a center \(u_c\) of \(U\). Since
\[
\int_{J_j}(\bar k-k)=0,
\]
the center term cancels:
\[
e_{a,\mathcal H}
\le
R_U\|k_a-\bar k_{a,\mathcal H}\|_{L^1}.
\]
For a bounded-variation function,
\[
\|k_a-\bar k_{a,\mathcal H}\|_{L^1}
\le h_u\operatorname{TV}(k_a).
\]
Hence
\[
e_{a,\mathcal H}\le R_UV_Uh_u.
\]

## Step 3: The lower bound in (8)

Take any admissible continuous-time policy and form its block averages.

For every grid label,
\[
A_av-\beta_a^+-\bar e_a
\le
\int k_a^\top u^\pi_\theta-\beta_a
=
\mathcal F_a(\pi).
\]

The finite LP can therefore attain a value no larger than the maximum robust violation of that policy. Minimizing over policies gives
\[
\rho_{\mathcal H,G}\le J_{\mathcal I}(T).
\]

## Step 4: The upper bound in (8)

Take an optimizer \(v^*\) of (5) and implement the corresponding piecewise-constant information-adapted control.

For this controller,
\[
\int k_a^\top u^{v^*}_\theta=A_av^*.
\]
Consequently, at grid times,
\[
\mathcal F_a(v^*)
\le
\rho_{\mathcal H,G}+\bar e_a+\delta_\beta.
\]

Equation (6) extends this bound to every time:
\[
\max_{a\in\mathscr S}\mathcal F_a(v^*)
\le
\rho_{\mathcal H,G}
+R_UV_Uh_u+\delta_\beta+Lh_t.
\]

Since \(J_{\mathcal I}(T)\) minimizes over all admissible policies, the upper bound follows.

Notice the asymmetry:

- averaging arbitrary controls proves the lower bound;
- implementing piecewise-constant controls proves the upper bound.

Confusing these two directions is what makes naïve “infeasible control discretization” arguments unsound.

## Step 5: Finite dual certificate

Write the block input constraints as
\[
F_{\rm blk}v\le f_{\rm blk}.
\]

The LP dual is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\lambda,\mu}\quad&
-\lambda^\top(\beta^++\bar e)-\mu^\top f_{\rm blk}\\
\text{subject to}\quad&
A^\top\lambda+F_{\rm blk}^\top\mu=0,\\
&\lambda\ge0,\quad \mathbf1^\top\lambda=1,\quad \mu\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

Therefore a positive dual objective certifies infeasibility of
\[
Av\le\beta^++\bar e,\qquad v\in U^Q.
\]

By Step 2, every genuinely safe measurable controller would generate a feasible \(v\). Thus the finite infeasibility certificate excludes every such controller.

Moreover, the weighted continuous-time safety violation is at least the dual objective. A normalized certificate with margin \(\gamma>0\) therefore guarantees an actual facet violation of at least \(\gamma\) in one stored test.

## Step 6: Completeness and exact duality

If the system is nonviable, let
\[
\gamma=J_{\mathcal I}(T)>0.
\]
Choose meshes making the error in (8) smaller than \(\gamma\). Then
\[
\rho_{\mathcal H,G}>0,
\]
and finite LP duality yields a positive certificate.

For every finite atomic choice,
\[
\Gamma_{\mathcal I}(\lambda,a)\le J_{\mathcal I}(T).
\]
Conversely, an optimal dual solution of (5) gives an atomic continuous-time lower bound at least as large as \(\rho_{\mathcal H,G}\). Letting the mesh error tend to zero proves (10).

No belief-state recursion is used.

## Step 7: Sparsity

Factor the finite row map through its rank:
\[
Av=L(Rv),\qquad Rv\in\mathbb R^r.
\]

The attainable moment set
\[
\mathcal M=\{Rv:v\in U^Q\}
\]
is compact and convex. Each safety row defines a halfspace in \(\mathbb R^r\).

If the common feasible set is empty, Helly’s theorem gives an obstruction involving at most \(r+1\) safety halfspaces. Applying finite duality to that subproblem yields a sparse positive certificate.

To preserve a prescribed margin \(\gamma_0<\rho_{\mathcal H,G}\), apply the same argument to the infeasible shifted constraints
\[
A_av\le\beta_a^++\bar e_a+\gamma_0.
\]

The continuous finite-rank case follows by the same argument on the compact attainable moment body of the lifted kernels. ∎

# 7. What is genuinely strengthened

The nonstandard content should be isolated as follows.

### Not new by itself

- robust safety as a family of linear inequalities;
- nonanticipativity constraints;
- an infinite-dimensional separating functional;
- Farkas or Helly;
- monotonicity under observation refinement.

### The theorem-driven contribution

The theorem establishes, in one construction:

1. **Correct abstraction direction for nonviability:** outer approximation of controller possibilities, inner approximation of adversarial tests.
2. **Coverage of all measurable adapted controls:** no hidden zero-order-hold restriction.
3. **A computable two-sided error bound** between a finite LP and the continuous-time safety value.
4. **Finite termination for every nonviable instance** in the stated class.
5. **A finite policy-independent adversarial test set**, with numerical verification margins.
6. **A sharp information–time sparsity law**, including failure of any input-dimension-only bound.

This is the theorem that remains after standard safety-game machinery is removed.

# 8. Sharp limitation: \(m+1\) is false for blind control functions

The instantaneous Helly bound must not be transferred to arbitrary control signals.

## Proposition: arbitrarily large minimal obstructions with one input

For every integer \(q\ge1\), there is a continuous-time system with scalar input
\[
u(t)\in[0,1]
\]
and \(q+1\) indistinguishable modes such that:

- every proper subbelief is viable;
- the full \(q+1\)-mode belief is not;
- every obstruction certificate must involve all \(q+1\) modes.

### Construction

Let \(T=q\) and
\[
I_j=[j-1,j],\qquad j=1,\dots,q.
\]

For mode \(j\le q\),
\[
\dot x_j(t)=\mathbf1_{I_j}(t)(u(t)-1),
\qquad x_j(0)=0.
\]

For mode \(q+1\),
\[
\dot x_{q+1}(t)=-u(t),
\qquad x_{q+1}(0)=q-\varepsilon,
\]
where \(0<\varepsilon<1\).

Require
\[
0\le x(t)\le q.
\]
There are no observations.

Let
\[
w_j=\int_{I_j}u(t)\,dt.
\]

Safety requires
\[
-w_j\le-1,\qquad j=1,\dots,q,
\]
and
\[
\sum_{j=1}^q w_j\le q-\varepsilon.
\]

Their sum gives \(0\le-\varepsilon\), a contradiction.

If mode \(q+1\) is removed, use \(u\equiv1\). If mode \(j\le q\) is removed, use \(u=0\) on \(I_j\) and \(u=1\) elsewhere. Every remaining branch is safe.

Equal weights \(1/(q+1)\) give a certificate margin
\[
\frac{\varepsilon}{q+1}.
\]

The kernel rank is \(q\), and \(q+1\) witnesses are necessary. ∎

### Consequence

A statement such as

> “At most \(m+1\) compatible states suffice to witness nonviability”

is valid for convex common-action sets in \(\mathbb R^m\), but false for a common blind control function.

The correct dimension counts independent temporal and informational control decisions. This distinction is consequential for review intervals, delayed sensing, and certificate-size claims.

# 9. Recourse and information refinement

## 9.1 Why the dual captures information loss

Suppose a coarse information cell \(C\) is split into finer cells \(C_1,\dots,C_k\). Because
\[
\phi_U(g_1+\cdots+g_k)
\ge
\sum_{j=1}^k\phi_U(g_j),
\]
the same witness has
\[
\Gamma_{\rm coarse}\ge\Gamma_{\rm fine}.
\]

The exact difference is
\[
\int
\left[
\phi_U\!\left(\sum_jg_{C_j}\right)
-\sum_j\phi_U(g_{C_j})
\right]dt.
\tag{13}
\]

For \(U=[-\bar u,\bar u]^m\), this becomes
\[
\bar u\int
\left[
\sum_j\|g_{C_j}(t)\|_1
-\left\|\sum_jg_{C_j}(t)\right\|_1
\right]dt.
\]

Thus the certificate identifies the precise time intervals on which indistinguishable branches demand conflicting control effort.

## 9.2 A sound extension to general post-delay feedback

The exact theorem assumes exogenous mode observations. A useful sound extension does not.

Suppose finitely many admissible initial-state/disturbance scenarios are provably indistinguishable until time \(\tau\). After \(\tau\), give the controller **separate arbitrary control signals for every stored scenario**, even if the real sensor would not reveal that much information.

This is an oracle relaxation of the true policy class. If
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^\tau
\phi_U\!\left(\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell k_\ell(t)\right)dt\\
&\quad+
\sum_\ell\int_\tau^T
\phi_U\!\left(\lambda_\ell k_\ell(t)\right)dt
-\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell\beta_\ell
>0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
then no actual output-feedback policy can be safe.

Here \(\beta_\ell\) is computed from the explicitly stored scenario, not by silently assuming that a post-delay feedback control is independent of its state or disturbance.

This corollary covers arbitrary sensing after a known blackout. It is sound but generally incomplete.

# 10. Computation and independent verification

## 10.1 Algorithm

**Input:** dynamics, prior polytopes, sensing schedule, safety polyhedron, \(U,D_\theta\), horizon, and certified regularity bounds.

**Output:** either a verified nonviability certificate or an unresolved approximation with a certified value interval.

1. Build the observation-history partitions.
2. Choose time, control-moment, and disturbance meshes.
3. At each mode/facet/time label:
   - obtain an initial-state support witness;
   - construct a piecewise-constant admissible disturbance witness;
   - compute validated adjoint integrals;
   - compute the control-moment error bound.
4. Solve (5).
5. Extract dual multipliers.
6. Sparsify the active safety-label family.
7. Verify the certificate independently.
8. If no positive verified margin is obtained, refine the meshes and arithmetic precision.

A nonviable instance eventually terminates with a certificate. A boundary-viable instance may remain unresolved.

## 10.2 No unnecessary vertex enumeration

Because the initial-state dependence is affine, one need not enumerate all vertices of \(P_\theta\). At each label, solve
\[
\max_{\xi\in P_\theta}
c_i^\top X_\theta(t,0)\xi
\]
and store a feasible maximizing or near-maximizing point.

For certificate soundness, the verifier only needs to check that the stored point belongs to \(P_\theta\). It need not verify global optimality. Near-optimality is needed for the convergence guarantee, not for a positive certificate’s validity.

The same distinction applies to stored disturbance signals.

## 10.3 Numerical certificate verification

For rational outer rows
\[
\widehat A v\le\widehat b,
\qquad F_{\rm blk}v\le f_{\rm blk},
\]
the ideal certificate is
\[
\lambda,\mu\ge0,\qquad
\widehat A^\top\lambda+F_{\rm blk}^\top\mu=0,
\]
and
\[
\lambda^\top\widehat b+\mu^\top f_{\rm blk}<0.
\]

Exact stationarity is not necessary if a residual is bounded.

Set
\[
r=\widehat A^\top\lambda+F_{\rm blk}^\top\mu,\qquad
c=\lambda^\top\widehat b+\mu^\top f_{\rm blk}.
\]

Writing \(r_j\) for the residual on each input block, the condition
\[
\boxed{
c+\sum_j h_U(-r_j)<0
}
\tag{15}
\]
is sufficient.

This follows because any feasible \(v\) would satisfy
\[
-\sum_jh_U(-r_j)\le r^\top v\le c.
\]

All integration, rounding, and model-enclosure errors must be included before applying this test.

A critical orientation rule is:

> For a nonviability certificate, uncertain row data must be relaxed toward a larger feasible controller set.

For example, overestimating the worst disturbance contribution and thereby shrinking \(\beta\) can create a false obstruction. Use a certified lower bound on the adverse contribution, an explicit admissible disturbance, or a correctly buffered interval calculation.

## 10.4 Complexity

With \(Q\) information–time blocks and \(N_t+1\) time labels, the LP has

\[
mQ+1
\]
variables and at most
\[
Ms(N_t+1)+p_UQ
\]
inequalities, where \(p_U\) is the number of input-polytope facets.

The LP is polynomial-time in these dimensions and the rational data size, conditional on the validated coefficient bounds being available.

A deletion-based sparsification uses at most the number of safety rows in additional feasibility solves. Exact rank compression requires verified rank; floating-point singular-value truncation must instead be charged to the error budget.

If \(J_{\mathcal I}(T)=\gamma>0\), a mesh satisfying
\[
R_UV_Uh_u+R_DV_Dh_d+Lh_t<\gamma
\]
guarantees detection. This gives an explicit, though not claimed optimal, margin-dependent certificate-size bound.

# 11. A four-dimensional, three-branch example

This example is analytically checkable, polyhedral, and genuinely three-way: every pair of compatible states is viable, but the triple is not.

## 11.1 Plant and constraints

Consider a planar double integrator:
\[
\dot p=v,\qquad \dot v=u,
\qquad p,v\in\mathbb R^2.
\]

Define unit vectors
\[
n_1=(1,0),\qquad
n_2=\left(-\frac35,\frac45\right),\qquad
n_3=\left(-\frac35,-\frac45\right).
\]

Let
\[
U=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm n_1,\pm n_2,\pm n_3\}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
U=
\left\{
u:
|u_2|\le\frac45,\quad
|2u_1+u_2|\le2,\quad
|2u_1-u_2|\le2
\right\}.
\]

Safety is
\[
n_i^\top p\le2,\quad i=1,2,3,
\qquad
|v_1|,|v_2|\le\frac65.
\]

There are three possible initial states:
\[
p_j(0)=\frac{34}{25}n_j,\qquad v_j(0)=n_j.
\]

There is no disturbance. The mode \(j\) is unobserved until time \(\tau\), when it is revealed exactly.

Every initial state is strictly inside the physical safety set.

## 11.2 Full-information viability

If \(j\) is known, choose
\[
u=-n_j
\]
for one second and then \(u=0\).

The maximum critical position is
\[
\frac{34}{25}+\frac12
=\frac{93}{50}=1.86<2.
\]

Thus all three physical states are indefinitely viable under full information.

## 11.3 Exact delay threshold

Take three witness labels, each using its own position facet at
\[
t_*=\tau+1.
\]

Their kernels are
\[
k_j(s)=\mathbf1_{\{s\le t_*\}}(t_*-s)n_j,
\]
and
\[
\beta_j=\frac{16}{25}-t_*.
\]

Choose
\[
\lambda_1=\frac38,\qquad
\lambda_2=\lambda_3=\frac5{16}.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_j\lambda_jn_j=0.
\]

Before observation, the pooled kernel is identically zero. After observation, each mode has its own control, and
\[
\phi_U\bigl(\lambda_j(t_*-s)n_j\bigr)
=-\lambda_j(t_*-s).
\]

Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\tau)
&=-\int_\tau^{\tau+1}(\tau+1-s)\,ds
-\left(\frac{16}{25}-\tau-1\right)\\
&=\boxed{\tau-\frac7{50}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Therefore
\[
\tau>\frac7{50}=0.14
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B_0\notin\mathcal K_{\mathcal I}.
\]

Conversely, use \(u=0\) before observation and \(u=-n_j\) for one second afterward. The maximum critical position is
\[
\frac{34}{25}+\tau+\frac12.
\]
This is safe exactly when \(\tau\le0.14\).

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\max}=0.14
}
\]
is exact, and for horizons containing the stopping time,
\[
\boxed{
J_{\mathcal I}(\tau+1)=\tau-0.14.
}
\]

## 11.4 Why a blind-window exit test misses the obstruction

At \(\tau=0.2\), the common control \(u=0\) keeps all three branches safe throughout the blind interval:
\[
n_j^\top p_j(0.2)=1.56<2.
\]

No exit occurs before information arrives.

Nevertheless, the best post-observation recourse cannot recover all branches:
\[
\Gamma(0.2)=0.06>0.
\]

The obstruction is not “information arrives after unavoidable physical exit.” It is:

> Information arrives after some indistinguishable branch has necessarily accumulated more braking liability than the remaining control authority can discharge.

That is precisely what the post-observation adjoint witnesses certify.

## 11.5 Every pair is viable at delay \(0.2\)

For the pair \(\{1,2\}\), use the common blind control
\[
w=\left(-\frac25,-\frac45\right).
\]
For \(\{1,3\}\), reflect its second component. For \(\{2,3\}\), use \(w=(1,0)\).

After revelation, first brake the normal velocity with \(-n_j\), then brake the remaining tangential velocity.

If
\[
\alpha=-n_j^\top w,
\]
the maximum critical position is
\[
\frac{34}{25}+\tau-\frac{\alpha\tau^2}{2}
+\frac{(1-\alpha\tau)^2}{2}.
\]

At \(\tau=0.2\):

| Pair | \(\alpha\) | Maximum critical position |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(\{1,2\}\), \(\{1,3\}\) | \(0.4\) | \(1.9752\) |
| \(\{2,3\}\) | \(0.6\) | \(1.9352\) |

The tangential stopping inputs belong to \(U\), preserve the critical facet, and keep the other facets strictly safe. Speeds remain at most one.

Therefore every proper nonempty subbelief is viable, while the triple is not.

Over the seven nonempty priors formed from these three states, exactly six are viable at delay \(0.2\). This is a calculation over a prior family, not a claim that all seven are reachable beliefs from one fixed initial record.

# 12. Explicit finite LP and Farkas certificate

Set
\[
\tau=\frac15,\qquad
T=\frac65,\qquad
h=\frac1{10}.
\]

There are:

- two common pre-observation control blocks;
- ten post-observation blocks for each of three modes.

Thus
\[
Q=32,\qquad mQ=64.
\]

For each selected endpoint position row, the exact moment error is
\[
e
=
12\frac{h^2}{4}
=\frac3{100}.
\]

Indeed, on each mesh interval the kernel is affine, and
\[
h_U(\alpha n_j)=|\alpha|.
\]

The endpoint right-hand side is
\[
\beta=\frac{16}{25}-\frac65=-\frac{14}{25}.
\]
The relaxed right-hand side is
\[
\beta+e=-\frac{53}{100}.
\]

The exact continuous certificate has margin
\[
\frac3{50}=0.06.
\]
The finite moment certificate has margin
\[
\boxed{\frac3{100}=0.03}.
\]

## 12.1 Input-facet multipliers

Order the input constraints as
\[
F=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1\\
0&-1\\
2&1\\
2&-1\\
-2&1\\
-2&-1
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
f=
\begin{bmatrix}
4/5\\4/5\\2\\2\\2\\2
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Define nonnegative vectors
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta_1&=(0,0,0,0,1/4,1/4),\\
\eta_2&=(0,1/2,0,3/10,0,0),\\
\eta_3&=(1/2,0,3/10,0,0,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
They satisfy
\[
F^\top\eta_j=-n_j,\qquad f^\top\eta_j=1.
\]

For a post-observation interval \(J_k\), put
\[
a_k=\int_{J_k}(T-s)\,ds,
\qquad
\mu_{j,k}=\lambda_j a_k\eta_j.
\]

Before observation, stationarity holds because
\[
\sum_j\lambda_jn_j=0.
\]

After observation, it holds because
\[
\lambda_j a_kn_j+F^\top\mu_{j,k}=0.
\]

Finally,
\[
\sum_k a_k=\frac12,
\]
so the Farkas contradiction is
\[
\lambda^\top(\beta+e)+\mu^\top f_{\rm blk}
=
-\frac{53}{100}+\frac12
=
-\frac3{100}<0.
\]

This is an exact rational certificate.

## 12.2 Minimal independent verifier

    (Q(1), Q(0)),
    (Q(-3, 5), Q(4, 5)),
    (Q(-3, 5), Q(-4, 5)),
]
lam = [Q(3, 8), Q(5, 16), Q(5, 16)]

F = [(0, 1), (0, -1), (2, 1),
     (2, -1), (-2, 1), (-2, -1)]

eta = [
    [0, 0, 0, 0, Q(1, 4), Q(1, 4)],

assert sum(lam) == 1

assert all(sum(lam[j] * n[j][d] for j in range(3)) == 0

# 13. Comparison and scope

| Approach | What it establishes here | Limitation relative to the proposed certificate |
|---|---|---|
| Full-information viability | All three initial physical states are viable | Does not decide common information-constrained viability |
| Initial admissibility/tangency | No initial obstruction; all physical constraints are strict | Does not capture delayed braking liabilities |
| Safety before first informative observation | A common safe blind control exists | Misses failure caused by insufficient post-observation recourse |
| Exact finite safety game | Can detect the obstruction if the continuous dynamics are represented soundly | The continuous-to-finite certificate bridge remains to be justified |
| Naïve held-control infeasibility | Excludes the chosen held-control class | Need not exclude arbitrary measurable controllers |
| Theorem above | Finite certificate excluding every controller in the stated information class | Exactness requires exogenous information and affine dynamics |

A related-work section should explicitly distinguish:

- belief-state safety games and set-valued estimation;
- full-information viability and robust invariance;
- infinite-dimensional linear programming;
- semi-infinite programming and finite-support duality;
- convex-team/scenario-tree nonanticipativity;
- certified continuous-time discretization.

Useful starting references include Aubin’s *Viability Theory*, Anderson and Nash’s *Linear Programming in Infinite-Dimensional Spaces*, and Hettich–Kortanek’s survey of semi-infinite programming. The publication claim must be tested against the last three categories, not only against POMDP safety literature.

## Delimitations

The exact theorem does **not** cover:

- control-dependent observations or active sensing;
- arbitrary continuous-state measurements;
- nonlinear dynamics without additional validated enclosures;
- nonconvex actuator sets without a relaxation gap;
- rate-constrained actuators without modifying the approximation;
- unrestricted infinite-horizon completeness.

For general sensing after a blackout, the oracle-recourse certificate is sound but incomplete. For nonlinear systems, a uniform validated flow-error bound can be added to the row budgets, but that yields a sound extension—not automatic completeness.

kimi:
# Robust Viability Under Partial Observation: What Survives, What Is New, and How to Certify It

**Direct answer to the key question.** Once belief-state DP, predecessor fixed points, viability kernels, robust controlled invariance, barrier/tangency, and robust LP duality are all declared *standard*, exactly **three theorems** survive as nonstandard and technically consequential:

1. **A sparse dual obstruction theorem** — nonviability of the *common-action* problem at a belief node is witnessed by at most m_u + 1 branches with a Farkas vector of support ≤ m_u + 1, and this finite object *dualizes to an adversarial winning strategy*, not merely to an infeasibility fact. This converts an uncountable universal quantifier (∀u ∈ U) into a small, exactly checkable object, and it composes along the game tree.
2. **A uniform adverse-margin theorem** — a *pointwise, checkable* hypothesis (every common action admits a compatible active branch with one-sided descent rate ≤ −η, uniformly on an explicit tube enclosure) implies finite-time exit with an **explicit** time bound T̄ = g_B/η, where the defeating branch may switch with u and in time. This is the rigorous continuous-dynamics→finite-certificate bridge; its hypothesis is exactly what fails in the counterexample showing pointwise tangency is insufficient.
3. **A blind-window timing certificate with an exact LP reduction** — for sampled-data linear systems, safety of *all* blind policies over a window reduces **exactly** to one LP (via an averaging lemma for held controls), so σ*(B₀) ≤ Δ* < T_obs is a finite, solver-independent certificate; and K_I^{(τ)} ⊆ {B : σ*(B) ≥ τ} gives a quantitative delayed-information bound on the viability kernel.

The recursion (T1) and the finite obstruction tree (T2) are *standard content* (belief-space sure-safety recursion; finite-game determinacy). They are stated as anchors with exact assumptions, and the paper's claims of novelty are explicitly *not* attached to them.

## 1. Model and Semantics (all quantifiers fixed)

**D1 (Plant, discrete/sampled-data).** 𝓜 = (Z, U, D, Y, F, h, V): state z ∈ Z; action u ∈ U ⊆ ℝ^{m_u}; disturbance d ∈ D; observation y ∈ Y; transition F : Z × U × D → Z; observation h : Z → Y; safe set V ⊆ Z. *Finite model:* Z, D, Y finite. *Class PC (polyhedral class):* Z finite cells with rational polytopes, U compact polytope, and for each (z, d, z′) the level set {u : F(z,u,d) = z′} is rational-polyhedral; *continuous-time:* ż = f(z,u,d), Carathéodory conditions, f bounded by M and L-Lipschitz in z uniformly in (u,d), D compact.

**D2 (Information state).** The info state at stage k is I_k = (B₀, u_{0:k−1}, y_{0:k}) with **beliefs from histories**:
B_{k+1} = Φ(B_k, u_k, y_{k+1}) := (F(B_k, u_k, D)) ∩ h^{−1}(y_{k+1}), where F(B,u,D) := ⋃_{z∈B,d∈D} F(z,u,d). Write Y(B,u) := {y : Φ(B,u,y) ≠ ∅}.

**D3 (Policy classes).** Π_rec (record-based nonanticipative): u_k = π_k(B₀, u_{0:k−1}, y_{0:k}). Π_bel (belief-based): u_k = π_k(B_k) — *used only after Prop. 1.1 justifies it*. Π_CE ⊂ Π_out (point-estimate/certainty-equivalence): remark only. Randomized policies: **Remark 1.A** — for *sure* safety, randomization cannot enlarge the kernel, since safety must hold on the whole support of each mixed action; all kernels below are unchanged.

**D4 (Adversary classes; who sees what).** Δ_CA (causal, action-clairvoyant): d_k = δ_k(z_{0:k}, u_{0:k}, y_{0:k}) — disturbance **sees the current action**. Δ_n: sees only past actions. Δ_OL: open-loop sequence chosen at k = 0. **All certificates below are certified against Δ_CA unless stated; a certificate against a weaker class is a stronger statement, and we flag whenever one is obtained.**

**D5 (Quantifier order — fixed once).** N-stage viability: B₀ ∈ W_N ⇔ ∃π ∈ Π_rec ∀δ ∈ Δ_CA ∀z₀ ∈ B₀ : z_k ∈ V ∀k ≤ N. Note this is **∃π ∀(z₀,d)** — *not* ∀z∃u_z∀d. The gap between the two is the subject of §2.

**D6 (Predecessor).** For a family 𝓒 ⊆ 2^Z of beliefs,
Pre(𝓒) := { ∅ ≠ B ⊆ V : ∃u ∈ U_ad(B) with (i) F(B,u,D) ⊆ V and (ii) ∀y ∈ Y(B,u) : Φ(B,u,y) ∈ 𝓒 },
where U_ad(B) := ⋂_{z∈B} U_ad(z) is the **common admissible action set**. W₀ := {B : ∅ ≠ B ⊆ V}, W_{k+1} := Pre(W_k), W_∞ := ⋂_{N≥0} W_N.

**D7 (Kernels).** K_I := {B₀ : ∃π ∀δ ∀z₀ ∈ B₀ : z_k ∈ V ∀k ≥ 0} (info-state / output-feedback viability kernel). K_full ⊆ Z: same with full-information history-dependent policies. Shadow map: S : 2^Z \ {∅} → 2^Z, S(B) = B; lifted kernel S^{−1}(K_full) := {B : B ⊆ K_full} ("every compatible state individually viable").

**D8 (General-space standing assumptions, for exactness statements only).** (A1) U compact, D compact; (A2) F(z,·,d) continuous, F(·,u,d), h measurable; (A3) V closed; (A4) the measurable-selection and universal-measurability hypotheses of Bertsekas–Shreve (Ch. 7–8). Under (A1)–(A4) the recursion is exact; otherwise exactness can fail, and we say so.

## 2. Separation: Partial-Observation Nonviability Is a Common-Action Phenomenon

**Proposition 2.1 (Containment; elementary).** K_I ⊆ S^{−1}(K_full): info-state viability implies branchwise full-information viability.
*Proof.* A record-based observation policy is a degenerate full-information policy (compute y_k = h(z_k) internally). The same π serves each z₀ ∈ B₀. ∎

**Proposition 2.2 (Strict separation, with certificate).** The inclusion is strict in general: every compatible state may be viable while the belief is not, and the obstruction is an *empty intersection of branchwise safe-action sets*, not uncertainty per se.

*Counterexample 1 (finite, then convex).* Z = {a,b} ∪ {⊥}, U = {1,2}, D singleton, h(a) = h(b) = y₀, V = {a,b}; F(a,1) = a, F(a,2) = ⊥, F(b,2) = b, F(b,1) = ⊥. Then {a},{b} ∈ K_full (state feedback μ(a) = 1, μ(b) = 2), but B₀ = {a,b}: u = 1 kills b, u = 2 kills a ⇒ B₀ ∉ W₁. *Convex form:* U = [0,2], branch a safe iff u ≤ 1−ε, branch b safe iff u ≥ 1+ε. Stacked system A u ≤ c with A = [1; −1], c = [1−ε; −(1+ε)]; Farkas vector λ = (1,1): λᵀA = 0, λᵀc = −2ε < 0. **This pair of numbers is the entire certificate; anyone can verify it with two multiplications, and it uses r = 2 = m_u + 1 branches — the Helly bound is tight already here.** ∎

**Proposition 2.3 (Localization of failure).** In the finite model, B₀ ∈ S^{−1}(K_full) \ K_I iff at some stage along some adversarial branch the recursion hits a belief B with ⋂_ω G_ω(B) = ∅: failure is *always explained by common-action incompatibility at some node* (Theorem 2 makes this a tree; Theorem 3 makes each node a small dual object).

**Remark 2.A (Saturation, deliberately minor).** O(K) := {B : h(B) ∈ h(K)} satisfies K_I = O^{−1}(O(K_I)) whenever h-saturated beliefs inherit membership; if a measurable certifier is required, O(K) must be measurable. One line; not a contribution.

**Remark 2.B (Certainty equivalence, deliberately minor).** Π_CE ⊆ Π_out ⇒ K_CE ⊆ K_out; add state c with F(c,3) = c ∈ V, h(c) = y₀, F(·,3) = ⊥ for a,b: belief {a,b,c} ∈ K_I (play u ≡ 3) while any point-estimate policy that ever trusts a or b fails: strict inclusion. Example, not theorem.

## 3. Theorem 1 — Exact Finite-Horizon Recursion (ANCHOR; standard content, stated for exactness)

**Theorem 1.** *Finite model. B₀ ∈ W_N ⇔ D5 holds, and W_N depends only on B₀ (belief sufficiency: Π_rec may be replaced by Π_bel without loss). Under (A1)–(A4), the same exactness holds in Borel spaces.*

*Proof.* (⇒) Induction on N. Base: W₀ definition. Step: B ∈ W_{k+1} gives a witness u(B) ∈ U_ad(B) with (i),(ii); define belief-based π by π(B) := u(B) on W_{N−k} and arbitrary off it. Any realized trajectory from z₀ ∈ B₀ has beliefs B_k tracking the recursion; stage safety (i) keeps z_k ∈ V; (ii) keeps B_{k+1} ∈ W_{N−k−1}. (⇐) Given record-based π surviving N from B₀, backward induction on k shows every π-reachable belief at stage k lies in W_{N−k}; the record only enters through B_k. Measurability under (A1)–(A4): Bertsekas–Shreve. ∎ *(This is the standard belief-game sure-safety recursion; no novelty claimed.)*

**Proposition 3.1 (Finite stabilization).** The chain W₀ ⊇ W₁ ⊇ … stabilizes at some N* ≤ 2^{|Z|}; K_I = W_{N*} = ν𝓩.Pre(𝓩), and a stationary belief-based winning policy exists.

**Proposition 3.2 (General spaces: the gap is real).** (a) *Counterexample.* Z = {0} ∪ {(n,k) : 1 ≤ k ≤ n} ∪ {⊥}, U = ℕ, D, h trivial/full. From 0, action n jumps to (n,n); (n,k) → (n,k−1); (n,1) → ⊥. Then {0} ∈ W_N for every N (pick u = N), so {0} ∈ W_∞, yet *every* fixed policy dies in u₀ + 1 stages: {0} ∉ K_I; moreover Pre(W_∞) = ∅ ≠ W_∞ — descent continues past ω. (b) *Sufficient condition.* If Z, U, D compact, V closed, F continuous, then W_∞ = νPre and B₀ ∈ W_∞ implies an infinite-horizon policy (product-topology compactness of strategy sets; finite intersection property). ∎

**Distinguish:** fixed-point existence (lattice argument) ≠ policy existence (selection + compactness). All certificate claims below are finite-horizon unless stated.

## 4. Theorem 2 — Obstruction Trees in the Finite Model (certificate packaging of determinacy)

**Theorem 2 (Completeness of finite obstruction trees).** *Finite model, |U| finite. B₀ ∉ W_N iff there exists a finite rooted tree 𝓣 with:*
- *internal nodes (B, k), k ≥ 0, B ∉ W_k;*
- *for each u ∈ U_ad(B), exactly one assigned adversarial move: either a **violation leaf** (z,d) ∈ B × D with F(z,u,d) ∉ V, or a **continuation** (z,d) with y = h(F(z,u,d)) and child (Φ(B,u,y), k−1) with Φ(B,u,y) ∉ W_{k−1};*
- *depth-0 nodes (B,0) with B ⊄ V are leaves labeled by z ∈ B \ V.*

*Proof.* Completeness by induction on k from the definition of Pre (failure of "∃u" gives, per u, a defeating (i)- or (ii)-witness; (ii)-witness y is realized by some (z,d)). Soundness: any π induces, against the tree-defined causal adversary (the adversary conditions on the *true* current z ∈ B, which it sees, being Δ_CA), a realized path ending at a violation leaf within N stages. ∎

**Certificate semantics.** Verifier input: (𝓜, N, 𝓣). Checks: each leaf's F(z,u,d) ∉ V by evaluation; each continuation's Φ recomputed and matched to the child; coverage of all u ∈ U_ad(B) at each node. Runtime O(|𝓣|·|Z||D||U|), exact arithmetic, **no trust in the prover**. Size: tree ≤ (|U||B||D|)^N; merging equal beliefs gives a DAG with ≤ 2^{|Z|} nodes. **Complexity context (honesty):** deciding N-stage nonviability in finite partial-observation games is PSPACE-hard (Reif '84; Peterson–Reif '79), so certificates may be exponentially large in the worst case; our guarantee is *verification linear in certificate size* and *small certificates when obstructions are shallow/sparse* — which Theorem 3 engineers.

## 5. Theorem 3 — Sparse Dual Obstruction (NEW core result)

The obstruction ∀u ∈ U_ad(B) ∃ defeat ranges over a continuum when U is continuous. Theorem 3 compresses it.

**Setup at a node (B, 𝓒).** Defeat scenarios ω ∈ Ω(B) := (B × D) ∪ Y (finite in the finite model). Good sets:
G_{z,d} := {u ∈ U_ad(B) : F(z,u,d) ∈ V ∧ Φ(B, u, h(F(z,u,d))) ∈ 𝓒}, G_y := {u : Φ(B,u,y) ∈ 𝓒 ∪ {∅}}.
Then **B ∈ Pre(𝓒) ⇔ ⋂_{ω∈Ω(B)} G_ω ≠ ∅** (definition-chasing; the quantifier order ∃u∀ω is exactly D5/D6).

**(Conv)** Each G_ω is convex. **(Poly)** Each G_ω = {u : A_ω u ≤ c_ω} with rational data. *(Holds, e.g., for sampled-data control-affine systems with constant input matrix, polyhedral V, polytopic D, and convex-representable 𝓒 — verified explicitly for the running examples; where (Conv) fails, the outer-approximation principle (f) below still yields sound certificates.)*

**Theorem 3 (Sparse dual obstruction).** *Assume U compact convex, (Conv). Then:*
(a) **Finite witness (Helly).** B ∉ Pre(𝓒) ⇒ ∃ ω₁,…,ω_r, r ≤ m_u + 1, with ⋂_{i≤r} G_{ω_i} = ∅.
(b) **Dual certificate (Farkas + Carathéodory).** Under (Poly): ∃λ ∈ ℚ_{≥0}^{ρ}, ρ = total stacked rows of the r subsystems, with λᵀA_sub = 0, λᵀc_sub ≤ −1; and λ can be chosen with **|supp λ| ≤ m_u + 1** (basic feasible solution of {λ ≥ 0 : λᵀA_sub = 0, λᵀc_sub = −1}, which has m_u + 1 equality constraints).
(c) **Vertex reduction.** If B = conv{v₁,…,v_p} and each constraint row is bi-affine in (z,u) — true for LTI sampled-data tube constraints at fixed times — then ⋂_{z∈B} S_z = ⋂_{j≤p} S_{v_j}: checking **vertices of the belief polytope suffices**.
(d) **Semi-infinite / atomic dual.** If B is a compact continuum, z ↦ (A_z, c_z) continuous, U compact: ⋂_{z∈B} S_z = ∅ ⇒ ∃ atoms z₁,…,z_r ∈ B, r ≤ m_u+1, with empty sub-intersection and Farkas vector as in (b). *Interpretation:* the dual of the semi-infinite common-action feasibility problem admits an **atomic measure supported on ≤ m_u+1 compatible states** — the sparse dual of "robustness over a belief continuum."
(e) **Strategy extraction (dual → adversary).** Given (J, λ) and any played u: membership tests u ∈ G_{ω_i} find a defeated scenario (exists since ⋂_J G = ∅); stage-type witnesses give immediate violations, y-type witnesses give losing successor beliefs. The dual object is thus not a mere infeasibility proof but an **executable adversarial strategy**.
(f) **Outer-approximation soundness.** If G̃_ω ⊇ G_ω are *any* convex outer approximations and ⋂ G̃_ω = ∅ (certified by λ), then B ∉ Pre(𝓒). Certificates may use sound relaxations; completeness is lost, soundness is not.

*Proof.* (a) Helly's theorem for the finite family {G_ω} in ℝ^{m_u}. (b) Farkas' lemma applied to the stacked rational system; support bound by Carathéodory/BFS. (c) For fixed u each row of A(z)u − c(z) is affine in z, hence maximized over B at a vertex; u is feasible over B iff feasible at all vertices. (d) The complements U \ S_z form an open cover of compact U; finite subcover ⇒ finite empty subfamily; Helly ⇒ r ≤ m_u+1; Farkas closes. (e) Soundness of the induced Δ_CA-strategy follows as in Theorem 2, with the finite-witness set J replacing full enumeration. (f) ⋂ G_ω ⊆ ⋂ G̃_ω = ∅. ∎

**Certificate semantics.** Node certificate: (J, λ, rational data). Verification: |J| ≤ m_u+1 membership checks of the listed polyhedra's definitions, then exact rational arithmetic: λ ≥ 0, λᵀA_sub = 0, λᵀc_sub < 0 — O((m_u+1)²·max rows). **Robustness lemma.** If λᵀc ≤ −μ and data perturbations satisfy ‖Δc‖_∞ ≤ ε with ‖λ‖₁ = Λ (and λᵀA = 0 is structural, hence exact), then λᵀ(c+Δc) ≤ −μ + Λε < 0 for ε < μ/Λ: **the certificate degrades gracefully; margin μ quantifies its numerical robustness.**

**Extraction.** Run a feasibility LP on ⋂_ω G_ω; on infeasibility read off the dual ray; reduce support to ≤ m_u+1 by BFS extraction or a deletion filter (O(|Ω|) LPs). This is a *byproduct of one LP solve* — the scalable dual computation method.

**Theorem 4 (N-stage composite certificate, class PC).** *In class PC, B₀ ∉ W_N iff there is a finite certificate DAG whose nodes are beliefs B ∉ W_{N−k}, each carrying either (i) a stage-exclusion Farkas certificate (Theorem 3 applied to stage constraints only), or (ii) a finite rational-polyhedral partition of U_ad(B) — e.g., the sign-pattern cells of the arrangement of the level-set boundaries {∂{u : F(z,u,d) = z′}} — with, per cell, a stage-violation containment proof or |Y| children with constant successor beliefs. Size: ≤ 2^{|Z|} belief-nodes × O(ρ^{m_u}) cells (ρ = number of defining hyperplanes); depth ≤ N. Under (Conv), per-node data compress to ≤ m_u+1 polyhedra + one Farkas vector (Theorem 3), and children are indexed by ≤ m_u+1 witness scenarios with finite image-families {Φ(B,·,y)}.*

*Proof sketch.* Finiteness of the arrangement (finite level sets, rational polyhedral) gives the partition; per-cell constancy of Φ gives well-defined children; recursion closes as in Theorem 2; Helly–Farkas compression is Theorem 3 node-wise. ∎ *(Adjacency honesty: existence of a finite semialgebraic representation of winning regions is model-theoretically standard (definability/o-minimality); the contribution is the explicit certificate object, its size bounds, its verifier, and the Helly–Farkas compression.)*

## 6. Continuous Time: From Pointwise Checks to Finite-Interval Certificates

**D9.** V = {z : q_i(z) ≥ 0, i ∈ I}, q_i ∈ C¹; g(z) := min_i q_i(z). Common admissible U^B(B) := ⋂_{z∈B} U_ad(z); robust tangential set R_V^B(B) := {u ∈ U^B(B) : ∀z ∈ B, ∀i with q_i(z) = 0, ∀d ∈ D : Dq_i(z; f(z,u,d)) ≥ 0}; review-interval tube set A_tube(B,Δ) := {u ∈ U^B(B) : Reach_{[0,Δ]}(B,u) ⊆ V}.

**Theorem 5 (Sound nesting — and its failure to converse).** (a) A_tube(B,Δ) ⊆ R_V^B(B) ⊆ U^B(B). Hence U^B = ∅ or R_V^B = ∅ **soundly** certifies A_tube = ∅. *Proof:* u ∈ A_tube with Dq_i(z;f(z,u,d)) < 0 at a boundary-active z gives immediate exit — contradiction. ∎
(b) **Counterexample (tangency ↛ tube safety).** ż₁ = 1, ż₂ = u, V = {q = z₂ − z₁² ≥ 0}, u ≡ 0.1 held: at z = 0, q = 0 active and Dq = 0.1 ≥ 0 (tangency holds), yet q(z(t)) = 0.1t − t² < 0 for t ∈ (0.1, 1]: the tube exits. *Instantaneous tangency and finite-interval tube safety are not equivalent; the paper's continuous-time certificates therefore never claim the converse.* Also the partial-observation sharpening: two branches whose tangency-restoring actions differ force R_V^B = ∅ even though each branch is tangentially safe.

**Theorem 6 (Uniform adverse margin ⇒ explicit exit bound; NEW).** *Assumptions: f measurable in (u,d), bounded by M, L-Lipschitz in z; D compact; q_i C¹; B compact. Hypothesis (uniform adverse margin): ∃η > 0 such that for every z ∈ R := B ⊕ ball(M·g_B/η) and every u-value v ∈ U^B(B), there exist i ∈ A(z) := {i : q_i(z) = g(z)} and d ∈ D with ∇q_i(z)·f(z,v,d) ≤ −η, where g_B := min_{z₀∈B} g(z₀). Then for every measurable open-loop (or trivially-adapted) control u(·) with values in U^B(B), there exist z₀ ∈ B and a measurable disturbance d(·) ∈ Δ_CA with exit time τ_V(z₀,u,d) ≤ T̄ := g_B/η.*

*Proof.* Fix u(·). Pick z₀ ∈ argmin_B g. The map F̄(z,v) := min_{d∈D} min_{i∈A(z)} ∇q_i(z)·f(z,v,d) is ≤ −η on R × U^B; A(z′) ⊆ A(z) near z (continuity of q_i) ⇒ F̄ is lower semicontinuous in z, continuous in v (compact D), hence Borel; by Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski there is a measurable selector d*(z,v) attaining the inner min; set d(t) := d*(z(t),u(t)) ∈ Δ_CA. Along the resulting (absolutely continuous) trajectory, g(t) := g(z(t)) is AC (min of finitely many AC functions) and ġ(t) = min_{i∈A(z(t))} ∇q_i·f(z(t),u(t),d(t)) ≤ −η a.e. while z(t) ∈ R. Hence g(t) ≤ g_B − ηt; g hits 0 by T̄ = g_B/η; and R was chosen to contain all trajectories from B on [0, T̄] (‖z(t) − z₀‖ ≤ Mt). Exit from V occurs at or before T̄. Uniformity in u is by construction (the hypothesis quantifies over all v). ∎

*Why this is the right strengthening of tangency:* the margin (i) holds at **every** point of an explicit tube enclosure, not just at t = 0; (ii) lets the defeating branch/constraint/disturbance depend on u and on t (branch switching is the adversary's prerogative); (iii) yields a **computable number** T̄, which is what the timing certificate (Thm 7) consumes.

**Certificate format (finite, checkable).** (B, η, R, grid {z^ℓ} covering R with mesh r, constants M, L, L_q, Q := max‖∇q‖). Verify: R ⊇ B ⊕ ball(M g_B/η) (arithmetic); on each grid cell, max_{v ∈ vert(U^B)} min_{i,d ∈ vert D} ∇q_i(z^ℓ)·f(z^ℓ,v,d) ≤ −η_grid with η := η_grid − (L_q M + QL) r > 0 (Lipschitz inflation; both f affine-in-v and polytope U^B, D reduce the inner checks to **vertices**). All arithmetic exact/interval-directed. Sound, incomplete, explicit.

**Lemma 6.1 (Averaging lemma — exact LP reduction for sampled-data linear systems; NEW).** *Let ż = Az + Bu + Ed, controls held constant on review subintervals { [t_j, t_{j+1}) }, z₀ ∈ polytope B, D box. Then for every grid time t_ℓ and each vertex v ∈ vert(B), z_v(t_ℓ) is affine in the held-control vector (u₀,…,u_{ℓ−1}), and sup over d(·) of any linear constraint aᵀz_v(t_ℓ) has the closed form ∫‖aᵀe^{A(t_ℓ−s)}E‖-type support integrals. Hence the set of held-control sequences keeping the whole vertex-tube in V at all grid times is a **polyhedron** in (u₀,…,u_{L−1}); its emptiness (with inter-sample inflation subtracted for soundness) certifies that **no** blind sampled-data policy survives the window; its Farkas ray is the certificate; its support has ≤ m_uL + 1 atoms, interpretable as "branch v, time t_ℓ, constraint i" culprits.*

*Proof.* Variation of constants; linearity in z₀ (⇒ vertices suffice, Thm 3(c)); support function of box D under linear maps; held controls are exactly the policy class, so no relaxation of the class occurs — only inter-sample times are relaxed, which is sound. ∎

## 7. Theorem 7 — Blind Windows, Delayed Information, and the Timing Certificate (NEW)

**D10.** Observation process with first branch-informative observation at T_obs; **blind window** [0, T_obs). *Condition (Triv):* the observation σ-field on [0, T_obs) is independent of (z₀, d(·)) — true for scheduled/periodic sensing, false for event-triggered sensing (flagged).

**Lemma 7.1 (Open-loop reduction).** Under (Triv), any policy adapted to the observation filtration equals a deterministic measurable u(·) on [0, T_obs). *Proof.* F_t = {∅,Ω} ⇒ F_t-measurable u(t) is a.s. constant. ∎

**D11.** Blind survival value σ*(B₀) := sup_{blind π} inf_{z₀∈B₀, d(·)} τ_V(z₀, π, d) ∈ [0, ∞].

**Theorem 7.** (a) *Upper bounds:* σ*(B₀) ≤ g_B/η (Thm 6, when its hypothesis holds) and σ*(B₀) ≤ Δ* := inf{Δ : the Lemma 6.1 polyhedron is empty} (LTI sampled-data case; **exact** w.r.t. the held-policy class at grid times).
(b) **Principal timing certificate.** σ*(B₀) ≤ T̄ < T_obs ⇒ B₀ ∉ K_I for every policy class whose window-restrictions are blind-adapted.
(c) **Delayed-information bound.** With all observations delayed by τ: K_I^{(τ)} ⊆ {B : σ*(B) ≥ τ} — a computable outer approximation quantifying how delay erodes the viable kernel. Finer partitions enlarge K_I monotonically (one line).

*Proof.* (b) Any admissible π restricts to a blind policy on [0,T_obs); by Lemma 7.1 it is open-loop there; by definition of σ* and σ* < T_obs there exist z₀ ∈ B₀, d(·) with τ_V ≤ (σ*+T_obs)/2 < T_obs: π fails before any information arrives. (c) τ-delayed policies are blind on [0,τ); apply (b). ∎

**This is the delayed-information theorem the program required:** the *proof* is three lines because the work was correctly localized in Lemma 7.1 and Theorem 6/Lemma 6.1 — and that is the point: the certificate's content is a computed number compared against a sensing deadline.
