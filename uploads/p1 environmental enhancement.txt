*   *Change:* "Weak/Strong sustainability doctrines" $\rightarrow$ "Scalarized vs. Coordinate-wise feasibility criteria."
*   *Change:* "Acceptance gap" $\rightarrow$ "Feasibility gap" or "Scalarization error."
*   *Change:* "Impossibility region" $\rightarrow$ "Infeasible region under common-action policies."

#### Step 3: Rewrite the Introduction for an OR/Control Audience
*   **Current Intro:** Starts with the history of weak vs. strong sustainability, genuine savings, and ecological economics. (This alienated the EMA editor).
*   **New Intro:** Start with the mathematical problem. 
    > "In robust multi-objective control and dynamic multi-criteria decision making, a central question is whether a system can be kept within a safe set using a scalarized aggregate of constraints versus enforcing constraints coordinate-wise. While static scalarization limitations are well known (e.g., Das and Dennis, 1997), the dynamic implications under path-wise (tube) constraints and disturbances remain under-explored. #### Step 4: Elevate the "Menu Convexification" Result
In OR and Control, the fact that *continuous convexification (blending) closes the gap but discrete time-sharing does not* is a highly publishable, non-trivial insight. It connects directly to the difference between relaxed controls (convexified) and bang-bang/chattering controls. Make Theorem 8 (Blend Collapse) and Proposition 9 (Time-sharing failure) much more prominent. Frame it as: *"The geometry of the action space, not the assessment criterion, dictates the feasibility gap."*
