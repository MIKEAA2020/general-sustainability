\begin{abstract}
This note gives a scoped spatial analysis of selected cores in the capital-liquidation program. It distinguishes mean-field institutions, for which governance variables are spatially lumped, from local institutions, for which memory and effort are spatial fields. For the frozen-active-pool three-state core, every nonzero Neumann mode of the mean-field field decays exactly, so field stability is equivalent to stability of the homogeneous DDE. The same statement holds for the well-mixed active-pool four-state core, for the specified two-stage mean-field harvest models, and for vector products under an explicit yield-gap and modal-Hurwitz hypothesis. The finite-donor primitive ledger has no $C^1$ Neumann steady state with positive extraction on a set of positive measure, so there is no interior exploited rest whose spatial stability can be tested. The logistic Jensen identity is exact and identifies the nonlinear variance forcing of the mean mode. For local institutions, the exact frozen-$A$ modal cubic and gain ratio are stated; diffusion reduces loop gain only above an explicit frequency threshold and can increase low-frequency gain. No general local delayed-spatial-Hopf exclusion is claimed. Delay-free claims are restricted to the explicitly analyzed scalar or two-state blocks; negative diagonal entries alone are not asserted to exclude higher-dimensional Turing instabilities.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and spatial stability}
\label{sec:scope}

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d$ be bounded, connected, and sufficiently regular, with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions. Let
\[
-\Delta\varphi_j=\mu_j\varphi_j,
\qquad 0=\mu_0<\mu_1\le\mu_2\le\cdots,
\]
where the eigenfunctions are orthonormal in $L^2(\Omega)$ and $\varphi_0=|\Omega|^{-1/2}$. The symbol $k_{\mathrm{soft}}$ is reserved for the companion's softplus sharpness; $j$ denotes a spatial mode and $\mu_j$ its Laplacian eigenvalue.

\begin{definition}[Mean-field spatial equivalence]
A homogeneous rest is mean-field spatially equivalent to its lumped DDE if the $j=0$ variational mode is exactly the DDE linearisation and every $j\ge1$ variational mode is Hurwitz. In that case, the field is linearly stable if and only if the homogeneous DDE rest is linearly stable.
\end{definition}

This definition does not assert that Candidate~A is stable for every delay. Its zero mode inherits the companion DDE instability for the corresponding delay intervals.

This note treats four restricted objects: the frozen-$A$ three-state core, the working four-state core with either a well-mixed or a local active pool, the finite-donor primitive natural block, and the two-stage mean-field harvest models. The full working ledger, local vector Liebig PDE, and local stage-structured delayed field are not claimed to be spatially stable by the results below.

\section{Frozen-$A$ three-state core}
\label{sec:three}

Let $S(N)=rN(1-N/K)$ and consider the mean-field field
\begin{equation}
\partial_tN=D\Delta N+S(N)-qE(t)N,
\qquad
\dot Z=\tau_m^{-1}\bigl[\Phi(qE\bar N-\overline{S(N)})-Z\bigr],
\label{eq:mf3}
\end{equation}
with the companion gated effort equation evaluated at $Z(t-\tau)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact logistic Jensen identity]
\label{thm:jensen}
For every $N\in L^2(\Omega)$,
\begin{equation}
\overline{S(N)}=S(\bar N)-\frac rK\operatorname{Var}_\Omega(N).
\label{eq:jensen}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $N=\bar N+\widetilde N$ with $\overline{\widetilde N}=0$. Expanding the quadratic logistic polynomial and averaging gives~\eqref{eq:jensen}.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Mean-field spatial equivalence, three-state core]
\label{thm:mf3}
At any interior logistic rest satisfying $qE^*N^*=S(N^*)$, the $j=0$ mode is the companion three-state DDE linearisation. Every $j\ge1$ mode satisfies
\begin{equation}
\dot n_j=\left(S'(N^*)-qE^*-D\mu_j\right)n_j
=\left(-\frac{rN^*}{K}-D\mu_j\right)n_j.
\label{eq:n3}
\end{equation}
Thus every nonzero mode decays at rate at least $rN^*/K$, uniformly in the institutional delay.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The mean-field institution depends on $N$ through $\bar N$ and $\overline{S(N)}$. The first variation of the variance term vanishes on every $j\ge1$ mode at a homogeneous state, so governance sees only $j=0$. The equilibrium identity gives $S'(N^*)-qE^*=-rN^*/K$.\end{proof}

The decay assertion is linear. At quadratic order, spatial variance forces the zero-mode deficit through~\eqref{eq:jensen}.

\section{Working four-state core}
\label{sec:four}

The working four-state core in the large-reservoir limit is
\begin{equation}
\dot N=R(N,A)-qEN,
\qquad
\dot A=-B(N,A)+\omega_A(A^{\mathrm{eq,W}}-A),
\label{eq:four-ode}
\end{equation}
with
\[
R=rN(1-N/K)s(A),
\qquad
B=R+\kappa_A Ns(A),
\qquad
s(A)=\frac{A}{A+A_0}.
\]
The same $(Z,E)$ equations are driven by $qEN-R$.

\subsection{Well-mixed active pool}

Let $A(t)$ be spatially lumped and let uptake be averaged:
\begin{equation}
\partial_tN=D\Delta N+R(N,A)-qE(t)N,
\qquad
\dot A=-\overline{B(N,A)}+\omega_A(A^{\mathrm{eq,W}}-A).
\label{eq:mf4}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Well-mixed four-state spatial equivalence]
\label{thm:mf4}
At an interior rest with $N^*\in(0,K)$ and $A^*>0$, the $j=0$ mode is the working four-state DDE linearisation. Every $j\ge1$ mode of $N$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\dot n_j=\left(-\frac{rN^*s^*}{K}-D\mu_j\right)n_j,
\qquad s^*=s(A^*),
\label{eq:n4}
\end{equation}
so the field is mean-field spatially equivalent to the working four-state DDE.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first variations of the spatial averages $\overline R$ and $\overline B$ vanish on nonzero modes. At the rest,
\[
\partial_NR-qE^*=r(1-2N^*/K)s^*-r(1-N^*/K)s^*=-rN^*s^*/K.
\]
The remaining governance and active-pool variables therefore see only the zero mode.\end{proof}

\subsection{Local, nondiffusing active pool}

If $A=A(x,t)$ is local but nondiffusive while $Z,E$ remain lumped, the nonzero $(N,A)$ block is
\begin{equation}
\frac d{dt}\begin{pmatrix}n_j\\a_j\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_N-D\mu_j&A_A\\
-B_N&J_{AA}
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}n_j\\a_j\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:local4block}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
A_N&=-rN^*s^*/K<0,\qquad
A_A=rN^*(1-N^*/K)s_A^*>0,\\
B_N&=s^*\bigl[r(1-2N^*/K)+\kappa_A\bigr],\\
J_{AA}&=-\partial_AB(N^*,A^*)-\omega_A<0.
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional local active-pool stability]
\label{thm:local4}
If $B_N\ge0$, then every matrix in~\eqref{eq:local4block} is Hurwitz for every $D\ge0$ and $j\ge1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\alpha=-A_N>0$, $\gamma=-J_{AA}>0$, and $\delta=D\mu_j\ge0$. The trace is $-(\alpha+\delta+\gamma)<0$ and the determinant is
\[
(\alpha+\delta)\gamma+A_AB_N>0.
\]
The two-by-two Hurwitz criterion completes the proof.\end{proof}

At the two recorded Paper I rests, the stated $B_N\ge0$ condition holds. This theorem concerns the nonzero physical $(N,A)$ block; it is not a stability theorem for a genuinely local delayed $(N,A,Z,E)$ field.

\section{Finite-donor primitive ledger}
\label{sec:donor}

For the finite-donor natural block of Paper II, with $C^A=0$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\partial_tN&=D_N\Delta N+R-qEN,\\
\partial_tA^{\mathrm{act}}&=D_A\Delta A^{\mathrm{act}}-B+e_{GA}-e_{AG}+\gamma_UU,\\
\partial_tA^{\mathrm{geo}}&=D_G\Delta A^{\mathrm{geo}}-e_{GA}+e_{AG},\\
\partial_tU&=D_U\Delta U+T-\gamma_UU,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fd}
\end{equation}
where $B=R+T$ and all exchanges are internal primitive fluxes.

\begin{theorem}[No interior extracted steady state]
\label{thm:norest}
Every sufficiently regular Neumann steady state of~\eqref{eq:fd} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\int_\Omega qE(x)N(x)\,dx=0.
\label{eq:noextract}
\end{equation}
Hence no steady state has positive extraction on a set of positive measure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Add the four equations and integrate. Neumann diffusion terms and all internal primitive transfers cancel, leaving $-\int qEN$. At a steady state this integral is zero.\end{proof}

Thus the finite-donor primitive system has no interior exploited rest to which a spatial-stability calculation could apply. This does not assert stability of the extinction–geochemical rest. For example, on $N=0$ the zero-mode growth rate is $rs_*-qE^*$, while nonzero modes acquire the additional term $-D_N\mu_j$.

\section{Stage-structured mean-field harvest}
\label{sec:stage}

For adult or juvenile take, write
\begin{align}
\dot X_A&=\tfrac1gX_J-d_AX_A-\chi_{\mathrm{ad}}qEX_A,\\
\dot X_J&=B(X_A)-\tfrac1gX_J-d_JX_J-\chi_{\mathrm{juv}}qEX_J,
\label{eq:stage-ode}
\end{align}
where $(\chi_{\mathrm{ad}},\chi_{\mathrm{juv}})=(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$ and
\[
B(X_A)=P_0X_Ae^{-X_A/N_c}s.
\]
Under mean-field governance, $Z,E$ depend only on spatial means and therefore do not enter nonzero modes.

\begin{lemma}[Ricker slope at an interior rest]
\label{lem:ricker}
At an adult- or juvenile-take rest,
\[
B'(X_A^*)=\Theta\left(1-\frac{X_A^*}{N_c}\right),
\]
where
\[
\Theta_{\mathrm{ad}}=(d_A+qE^*)(1/g+d_J),
\qquad
\Theta_{\mathrm{juv}}=d_A(1/g+d_J+qE^*).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Mean-field stage-mode stability]
\label{thm:stage}
For either harvest channel and every $D_A,D_J\ge0$, each nonzero mode has negative trace and positive determinant. Hence it is Hurwitz and the stage field is mean-field spatially equivalent to the corresponding lumped stage DDE.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For adult take the modal matrix is
\[
M_j^{\mathrm{ad}}=
\begin{pmatrix}
-d_A-qE^*-D_A\mu_j&1/g\\
B'(X_A^*)&-1/g-d_J-D_J\mu_j
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is bounded below by
\[
(d_A+qE^*)(1/g+d_J)-\frac{\Theta_{\mathrm{ad}}}{g}
\left(1-\frac{X_A^*}{N_c}\right)
=\frac{\Theta_{\mathrm{ad}}X_A^*}{gN_c}>0.
\]
Diffusion only adds nonnegative terms. The juvenile case is identical after replacing the harvest term and using $\Theta_{\mathrm{juv}}$.\end{proof}

This theorem concerns the specified mean-field stage models. It does not establish stability of a local stage-structured delayed field.

\section{Vector Liebig product under a yield gap}
\label{sec:vector}

Let a product of specialized blocks be coupled through a smooth soft-minimum service and an optional physical remainder. Assume a binding index $k$ maintains a uniform yield gap on a compact neighborhood of a homogeneous product rest, so that off-limiting softmax derivatives are $O(e^{-\rho\Delta_y})$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Modal slack stability]
\label{hyp:modal-slack}
For every slack block and every $j\ge1$, the uncoupled nonzero-mode physical operator is Hurwitz with a uniform margin $\gamma>0$ after its diffusion shift.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional mean-field modal robustness]
\label{thm:vector}
Under the uniform yield gap and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:modal-slack}, sufficiently small coupling
\[
\varepsilon_c=O(e^{-\rho\Delta_y})+O(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{phys}})
\]
preserves Hurwitz stability of all nonzero mean-field modes. The zero mode is the coupled finite-dimensional DDE and receives an $O(\varepsilon_c)$ perturbation. Hence field stability is equivalent, up to the stated perturbation, to stability of the coupled zero-mode DDE and the assumed slack-block margins.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At zero coupling the nonzero-mode operator is block diagonal and Hurwitz by Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:modal-slack}. The yield-gap terms are a small bounded perturbation on the compact neighborhood. Standard finite-dimensional modal perturbation preserves the spectral margin for sufficiently small $\varepsilon_c$, while the zero mode is the coupled DDE block.\end{proof}

This is a mean-field modal perturbation result, not a local PDE–RFDE NHIM theorem. It does not apply at yield parity.

\section{Local institutions and delay-free scope}
\label{sec:local}

For the local frozen-$A$ three-state field, the exact modal symbols are
\[
L_j(\lambda)=B_E(\lambda+D\mu_j),
\qquad
P_j(\lambda)=(\lambda-A_N+D\mu_j)(\lambda+d)(\lambda-C_E),
\]
and the modal Hopf cubic is
\begin{equation}
H_j(x)=\bigl(x+(A_N-D\mu_j)^2\bigr)(x+d^2)(x+C_E^2)-C_Z^2B_E^2\bigl(x+D^2\mu_j^2\bigr).
\label{eq:Hj}
\end{equation}
The exact gain ratio is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Gamma_j(\omega)}{\Gamma_0(\omega)}
=\sqrt{1+\frac{(D\mu_j)^2}{\omega^2}}
\frac{\sqrt{\omega^2+A_N^2}}
{\sqrt{\omega^2+(A_N-D\mu_j)^2}}.
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
Thus diffusion reduces gain only when

a\[
\omega^2>-rac12D\mu_jA_N,
\]
and can increase low-frequency gain. No general local delayed spatial-Hopf exclusion is claimed.

\begin{proposition}[Restricted delay-free Turing statement]
A delay-free Turing exclusion is asserted here only for the explicitly analyzed scalar or two-state blocks under their trace/determinant conditions. Negative diagonal entries alone are not used as a general exclusion theorem for arbitrary higher-dimensional local systems.
\end{proposition}

\section{Conclusion}

The spatial results that survive across the program are conditional rather than universal. Mean-field nonzero modes decay for the explicitly analyzed three-state, well-mixed four-state, and two-stage models, so their field stability is exactly the stability of the homogeneous DDE. The finite-donor primitive ledger has no interior exploited steady state. A yield-gap vector product inherits mean-field modal stability only under an explicit slack-mode hypothesis. Local institutions retain a separate delayed spatial-Hopf problem. Delay-free Turing claims are restricted to the scalar and two-state blocks for which the relevant Hurwitz and determinant conditions are actually checked. None of these results asserts that Candidate~A is stable for all delays or that the full mass-conserved program has a universal spatial-stability theorem.
