A3. Why the source proof is insufficient. Four independent fatal gaps:
(i) Wrong theorem class: viability/weak invariance gives ∃ viable trajectory, not ∀; concretely ẋ=√|x|, K={0} (or K={0}×ℝ etc.) has a viable trajectory and an escaping one; F(0)={0}⊆T_K(0); F continuous. So "F_k⊆T_K + viability theorem" cannot yield all-solutions conclusions even with no disturbance, even with continuous k, even with convex F.
(ii) Regularity gap: measurable k makes F_k non-Lipschitz (indeed non-continuous); no standard invariance theorem applies; and the ODE ẋ=f(x,k(x),d(t)) is not Carathéodory (discontinuous in x), so "solutions" are undefined until a solution concept (Filippov) is fixed — under Filippov, containment fails to transfer (averaging across discontinuity surfaces).
(iii) Disturbance quantifier: the inclusion encodes d only if every trajectory of F_k is realized by some measurable d(·) (Filippov selection) AND every (d,x) pair solves the inclusion; the source never establishes this correspondence, and with non-convex F_k and no Lipschitzness the correspondence can break (relaxed vs ordinary solutions).
(iv) Global existence/forward completeness never addressed: invariance on the maximal interval is vacuous if solutions blow up (f=x² example); need linear growth or completeness.

Also (v): if the source convexified F_k to satisfy Marchaud, it silently changed the dynamics (relaxed solutions) — all-solutions statement for relaxed solutions does not describe closed-loop behavior under a realizable feedback unless relaxation theorem hypotheses hold and even then it gives approximation, not equivalence... Actually careful: if all relaxed solutions stay in K, do all ordinary solutions stay in K? Ordinary ⊆ relaxed (every ordinary solution is a relaxed solution: chattering/Young measures include Dirac ones). So strong invariance of the relaxed inclusion DOES imply strong invariance of the original. The problem is only that weak invariance (existence) of relaxed gives nothing. So the convexification is harmless for a strong-invariance conclusion; it's fatal only because the source used a viability (existence) theorem. But also: the Filippov regularization (essential limsup over x'→x) is NOT the same as convexifying in d only — it also averages over neighboring states, which is where reentrant-corner escape enters. Distinguish: (a) convexification in u/d at fixed x (relaxation) — sup_{v}⟨ζ,v⟩ unchanged, harmless; (b) Filippov regularization over x'→x — changes the Hamiltonian condition, not harmless.
