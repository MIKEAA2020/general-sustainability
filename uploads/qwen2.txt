# Option (a): The Abstract Predecessor Schema (APS)

By abstracting the predecessor construction, we can unify the diverse viability kernels of **Article 002** (sampled, tube, RFDE, hybrid, information-state) and **Article 001** (belief-state fixed point) into a single mathematical object. This directly addresses **Priority 1** (canonicalize the formal spine) and discharges several live errors in the review packet (B2, B3, C1).

---

## 1. The Abstract Predecessor Signature

An **Abstract Predecessor Schema (APS)** is a tuple $\Sigma = (\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{U}, \mathcal{W}, \mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{K}_{\text{phys}}, \Phi, \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{R})$ where:

1. **$\mathcal{S}$ (Knowledge Space):** A compact metric space representing the controller's exact pre-observation knowledge state. This could be the physical state space $\mathcal{X}$, a hyperspace of compact physical sets $\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{X})$, a history space $\mathcal{H}(\mathscr{C})$, or an abstract information space $I$.
2. **$\mathcal{U}$ (Action Space):** A compact metric space of admissible commands.
3. **$\mathcal{W}$ (Disturbance/Branch Space):** A compact metric space encoding all unobserved process disturbances, structural model branches, and unobserved hybrid resets over one review interval.
4. **$\mathcal{Y}$ (Observation Space):** The space of observation symbols (e.g., a finite discrete alphabet, or a trivial singleton $\{*\}$ for full-state feedback).
5. **$\mathcal{K}_{\text{phys}}$ (Physical Safe Set):** The closed/compact constraint set in the underlying physical space.
6. **$\Phi$ (Transition Semantics):** A pair of maps defining the system's evolution:
   - $\Phi_{\text{tube}}(s, u, w)$: The exact physical footprint (the "tube") over the inter-sample interval $t \in (0, h]$.
   - $\Phi_{\text{next}}(s, u, w)$: The exact successor knowledge state at the next review.
7. **$\mathcal{O}$ (Observation/Conditioning Map):** A map defining how the next observation restricts the current knowledge state $s$ into fibres $C_y = \mathcal{O}^{-1}(y)$.
8. **$\mathcal{R}$ (Selector Regularity):** A parameter specifying the required regularity of the policy mapping (e.g., *Arbitrary/Axiom of Choice*, *Borel Measurable*, or *Continuous*).

---

## 2. The Universal Predecessor Operator

Given a target family of safe knowledge states $\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathcal{S}$, the **Universal Predecessor** $\operatorname{Pre}_\Sigma(\mathcal{A})$ is the set of all $s \in \mathcal{S}$ such that:

$$
\exists u \in \mathcal{U} \text{ (satisfying } \mathcal{R} \text{) such that } \forall y \in \mathcal{Y} \text{ consistent with } s \ (s \cap C_y \neq \emptyset), \text{ and } \forall w \in \mathcal{W}:
$$

1. **Tube Safety:** $\Phi_{\text{tube}}(s|_{C_y}, u, w) \subseteq \mathcal{K}_{\text{phys}}$
2. **Successor Admissibility:** $\Phi_{\text{next}}(s|_{C_y}, u, w) \in \mathcal{A}$

**The Greatest Fixed Point:**
Set $\mathcal{K}_0 = \{s \in \mathcal{S} : \text{base physical safety holds}\}$ and $\mathcal{K}_{n+1} = \mathcal{K}_n \cap \operatorname{Pre}_\Sigma(\mathcal{K}_n)$. Under the compactness and continuity conditions native to the specific instantiation, the intersection $\mathcal{K}_\infty = \bigcap_n \mathcal{K}_n$ is the **Robust Epistemic Viability Kernel** for that architecture.

## 4. Discharging the Error Docket

Formalizing the APS immediately resolves several contested issues in the review packet:

### Error B2: Selector Regularity (Implementability)
*The Problem:* 002’s theorems rely on arbitrary pointwise selectors (Axiom of Choice), which yields mathematically valid but physically unimplementable policies.
*The APS Resolution:* The APS isolates selector regularity as the parameter $\mathcal{R}$. The topological proofs of kernel compactness (the intersection of nested closed sets in a compact space) **do not depend on $\mathcal{R}$**. We can state the theorems choice-theoretically, and then add a **Measurable Selection Corollary**: *If the APS maps $\Phi$ and $\mathcal{O}$ are Borel measurable, and $\mathcal{U}$ is a compact metric space, the Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski theorem guarantees the existence of a measurable selector, upgrading the kernel from "theoretical" to "implementable" without altering the core proof.*

### Error B3: Exact Filters vs. Constructed Properties
*The Problem:* The papers sometimes blur the line between assuming an exact filter exists and proving one can be built.
*The APS Resolution:* The APS explicitly defines $\Phi_{\text{next}}$ and $\mathcal{O}$ as *inputs* to the schema. The theorems are then cleanly stated as conditional implications: *If* an application can supply a compact knowledge space and a Hausdorff-continuous $\Phi_{\text{next}}$ (e.g., via a declared finite-clopen partition or an exact bounded-jump reachable set), *then* the predecessor closes and the kernel exists. This separates the **viability theorem** from the **filtering/estimation problem**.

### Error C1: Fixed-Point Operator Mismatch (Article 006)
*The Problem:* 006 debated whether the information-state recursion should be $K_{n+1} = K_n \cap \operatorname{Pre}(K_n)$ or $S_{\text{safe}} \cap \operatorname{Pre}(K_n)$.
*The APS Resolution:* The APS clarifies that the predecessor operator *already inherently restricts* to safe successors (via the Tube Safety and Successor Admissibility conditions). Therefore, the correct Tarski/Knaster-Tarski operator on the knowledge lattice is simply $\Phi(\mathcal{Q}) = \mathcal{K}_0 \cap \operatorname{Pre}_\Sigma(\mathcal{Q})$. Because $\operatorname{Pre}_\Sigma$ is monotone and preserves compactness, countable descent $\bigcap_n \Phi^n(\mathcal{K}_0)$ is guaranteed to reach the greatest fixed point without requiring transfinite iteration, provided the knowledge space $\mathcal{S}$ is compact.
