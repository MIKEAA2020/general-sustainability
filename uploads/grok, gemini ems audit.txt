### 1. Major Discrepancies Between Graphics, Captions, and Text

#### A. Figure 1(a): Severe Mislabelling and Missing Floor Breach
* **The Claim:** 
  * Caption of Figure 1(a): *“the ecological margin $s_1$ is breached mid-transition ($-4/5$ adverse, $-3/10$ benign)...”*
  * Section 4.1 (p. 5): *“ecological floor reaches $-4/5 < 0$ mid-transition...”*
* **The Flaw in the Graphic:**
  1. The legend in Figure 1(a) identifies the green line (with triangle markers) as `ecological margin s_1 (no heatwave)`.
  2. However, at $t = 0$, this green line starts at $y = 2.4$. The witness state is explicitly defined in Section 4.2 as $(x, s_1, s_2) = (1/2, 6/5, 6/5)$. Therefore, the ecological margin $s_1$ at $t=0$ is $6/5 = 1.2$, **not** $2.4$. 
  3. Notice that $1.2 + 1.2 = 2.4$, which is the initial value of the **composite index** ($s_1 + s_2$).
  4. Furthermore, at mid-transition ($t = 1/2$), the green curve dips only to approximately $+0.9$ (well above the red dashed zero floor). It **never breaches zero**. 
  5. If $s_1$ were breached on the benign branch at $-3/10 = -0.3$ and $s_2 = 1.2$, the composite index would be $-0.3 + 1.2 = +0.9$. 
* **Conclusion:** The green curve in Figure 1(a) **is plotting the composite index under benign conditions**, but the legend erroneously labels it as the ecological margin $s_1$. Consequently, the promised benign breach of $-3/10$ is completely absent from the plot, and the graphic directly contradicts both its own legend and the caption.

### 2. Mathematical and Theoretical Inconsistencies

#### A. Five Operators Announced vs. Four Operators in the Inclusion Chain
* **Section 2.1 (p. 2–3):**
  * *“Five operators are distinguished, following Abaee [1]... [1] noncompensatory typed operator... [2] scalarized aggregate operator... [3] exact-tube physical and [4] endpoint-only physical operators... and [5] the typed-endpoint operator...”*
  * Immediately followed on p. 3 by:
    $$E_{\text{typ}}(z) \subseteq E_w(z) \subseteq E_{\text{tube,phys}}(z) \subseteq E_{\text{end}}(z)$$
* **Flaws:**
  1. **Missing Operator:** Only 4 operators appear in the inclusion chain; one of the five is omitted without explanation.
  2. **Ambiguity of $E_{\text{end}}$:** The text introduces *both* the *“endpoint-only physical operator”* and the *“typed-endpoint operator”*. Which one is $E_{\text{end}}(z)$?
  3. **Inclusion Breakdown:** If $E_{\text{end}}$ were the *typed-endpoint* operator, the inclusion $E_{\text{tube,phys}}(z) \subseteq E_{\text{end}}(z)$ would be false: an action satisfying physical bounds along the continuous tube does not necessarily satisfy typed floor constraints at the endpoint. Thus, $E_{\text{end}}$ must be the endpoint-only physical operator, leaving the typed-endpoint operator entirely unplaced in the mathematical hierarchy.

---

#### B. Truncation Inconsistency in Rescue Threshold $\kappa^*(x)$
* **Abstract & Section 4.1 (p. 1, p. 5):** State $\kappa^* = 1 - x$.
  * Section 4.1: *“rescue threshold $\kappa^* = 1 - x$ on the non-typed-viable region, with shortfall $1/2$ at the false-positive witness and full financing at the rescue witness $x = 3/2$.”*
* **Figure 2 Caption & Plot (p. 6):** State $\kappa^*(x) = \max(0, 1 - x)$.
* **Flaw:** Evaluating the linear formula $\kappa^* = 1 - x$ at the rescue witness $x = 3/2$ yields $-1/2$, not $0$. The truncation $\max(0, \cdot)$ is mathematically necessary to define full financing (zero shortfall) and avoid negative financing thresholds; omitting it in the Abstract and Section 4.1 is an error.

---

#### C. Conceptual and Terminological Confusion in Belief Recursions
* **Section 2.2 (p. 3):**
  * *“states carry observation labels, an action is admissible for a belief when no state in the belief’s fibre enters a violation en route, and beliefs are updated to the labels reachable under the action.”*
* **Flaws:**
  1. **Fibre of an Observation vs. Belief:** In partial observation / epistemic systems, an *observation label* $y$ has an inverse fibre $h^{-1}(y) \subseteq X$. A *belief* $B$ is already a subset of states (or information set). Speaking of *“the belief’s fibre”* conflates observation labels with epistemic beliefs.
  2. **Belief Updates:** If beliefs are *“updated to the labels reachable under the action”*, then beliefs are being equated to single observation labels. Yet Section 3.3 states that *“Belief enumeration bounds itself (4 096 reachable beliefs)”*, which corresponds to arbitrary subsets of states (e.g., $2^{12} = 4096$, the power set of a 12-state space). The text vacillates between defining beliefs as observation labels and defining beliefs as general subsets of states.

#### D. Undefined Normalization for Farkas Multipliers
* **Section 2.3 (p. 3):** States that *“the normalized multipliers $\lambda \ge 0$ with $\lambda^\top A = 0$ and $\lambda^\top b < 0$ are returned as a Farkas certificate, together with the infeasibility margin $-\lambda^\top b > 0$.”*
* **Section 4.3 (p. 6):** For $u \le 2/5$ and $-u \le -3/5$, returns $\lambda = (1/2, 1/2)$ and margin $1/10$.
* **Flaw:** The normalization rule is never formally specified. Unnormalized addition gives multipliers $(1, 1)$ with bound $\lambda^\top b = -1/5$ (margin $1/5$). The returned vector $\lambda = (1/2, 1/2)$ assumes an $\ell_1$-normalization ($\sum \lambda_i = 1$). Without defining the norm $\|\lambda\|_1 = 1$ in Section 2.3, the scalar *“infeasibility margin”* is arbitrary up to positive scaling.

#### E. Ill-Formed Definition of Transition Datum
* **Section 2.1 (p. 2):** 
  * *“A typed transition datum is a phase state $z = (q, x, s_1, \dots, s_n)$ with a phase flag $q$, fund coordinates $x$, and typed floors $s_i$ normalized to zero; a finite action set; a set of disturbances with, for each action and disturbance, an exact tube $\text{Tube}(a, d)$...; a transition-safe set $S$; and a destination set $G$ reached under an endpoint reset.”*
* **Flaw:** Grammatically and ontologically, this defines a "transition datum" as being a "phase state". A transition datum is a mathematical tuple $\mathcal{D} = (z, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{D}, \text{Tube}, S, G)$ containing the state, actions, disturbances, tube map, safe set, and destination set; it is not itself a state.
