# Major technical inconsistencies

## 1. The belief recursion is described inconsistently and may be mathematically unsound

### Locations

- Abstract, p. 1:
  > “backward recursions over state and belief spaces”
- Introduction, pp. 1–2:
  > “a finite-horizon belief-space recursion that is sound and complete in finite systems”
- Section 2.2, p. 3:
  > “a belief (a set of states consistent with the observations so far)”
- Section 2.2, p. 3:
  > “the belief updates to the set of labels reachable under the action”
- Section 2.2, p. 3:
  > “Memberships certify viability in all finite systems; completeness ... holds when the observation is injective on reachable states”

### Problems

These statements cannot all be correct as written.

1. A belief is defined as a **set of states**, but its successor is described as a **set of labels**. A set of labels generally loses information needed for future control. Different states with the same label can have different transitions and safe actions.

2. The Introduction says the recursion is sound and complete in finite systems. Section 2.2 says completeness holds only when the observation is injective on reachable states.

3. Injectivity on reachable states largely removes the partial-observation problem: if observations uniquely identify all reachable states, beliefs collapse to singletons after observation. That is an unexpectedly restrictive “exactness condition” for a method presented as a belief-space partial-observation recursion.

4. A standard finite belief-state recursion can normally be complete without injective observations, provided beliefs retain states and are updated by transition and observation:
   \[
   B'_{a,o}
   =
   \{z' : \exists z\in B,\ z'\in T(z,a),\ \operatorname{obs}(z')=o\}.
   \]
   If SafeTransition instead replaces this set by reachable labels, it is using an abstraction whose soundness and completeness require a separate proof.

5. The sentence
   > “an action is admissible for a belief ... when no state in its observation fibre enters a violation”
   
   is also ambiguous. A history-dependent belief can be a strict subset of the full current observation fibre. Checking the entire fibre may be conservative and incomplete.

### Required correction

The paper must provide the exact recursion, including:

- whether beliefs are sets of states, labels, or equivalence classes;
- transition quantification;
- disturbance quantification;
- observation branching;
- whether policies are memoryless in observations or history-dependent through beliefs;
- the definition of \(W_0\) and \(W_k\);
- a proof or precise citation for soundness and completeness.

The contradiction should be resolved in one of two ways:

- **If beliefs remain state sets:** replace “set of labels reachable” with the exact state-based update and remove the injectivity restriction if it is unnecessary.
- **If beliefs are abstracted to labels:** state clearly that the algorithm is an abstraction, give the abstraction relation, and distinguish soundness from completeness.

The abstract and Introduction should not claim unconditional finite-system completeness unless the algorithm actually has it.

## 2. The manuscript contradicts itself about whether tubes are exact or conservative

### Locations

Section 2.1, p. 2:

> “each tube is the exact visited set and no outer approximation enters”

Section 4.1, p. 6:

> “the declared piecewise-linear plan tubes are exact by construction, while the Schaefer realization’s tubes are certified conservative outer enclosures”

Conclusion, p. 10:

> “under exact or certified conservative tubes”

### Problem

Section 2.1 makes a universal claim about the library’s transition datum: every tube is exact and no outer approximation enters. Section 4.1 and the Conclusion explicitly allow conservative outer enclosures.

These are materially different semantics. If \(\widehat T\) is an outer enclosure of the true tube \(T\), then:

- proving every point of \(\widehat T\) safe certifies the true tube safe;
- finding a violation in \(\widehat T\) does **not** prove the true trajectory violates the floor.

Therefore, exact and conservative tubes do not support symmetric acceptance and rejection conclusions.

### Required correction

Define separate tube statuses and verdict semantics, for example:

- `EXACT`;
- `OUTER_CERTIFIED`;
- possibly `UNVERIFIED`.

Then state explicitly:

| Tube type | Passing all constraints means | Finding a violation means |
|---|---|---|
| Exact tube | certified safe | certified unsafe for represented trajectory |
| Certified outer tube | certified safe | inconclusive unless violation is known reachable |
| Unverified tube | conditional result only | conditional result only |

Section 2.1 should say that the interface accepts either exact tubes or certified outer enclosures. It should not say “no outer approximation enters.”

The benchmark should also state which object establishes the claimed typed/aggregate separation: the exact declared plan tube or the conservative Schaefer enclosure. If the rejection depends only on an outer-enclosure point, the separation is not established for the realized dynamics.

## 3. The observation-fibre criterion is overclaimed

### Locations

Section 2.3, p. 3:

> “The observation-fibre criterion decides whether an exact observation-only certifier can exist at all”

Section 4.3, p. 8:

> “ruling out any exact observation-only certifier”

### Problem

Constancy of safe-set membership on observation fibres is an if-and-only-if criterion for a much narrower question:

> Can current safe-set membership be determined exactly from the current observation alone?

It is not automatically a criterion for:

- existence of an observation-based control policy;
- finite-horizon viability;
- transition safety;
- a conservative certifier;
- a certifier using observation history;
- a certifier returning three-valued outputs such as safe/unsafe/unknown.

For example, safe-set membership can vary within a fibre while a robust common action still keeps every possible state safe. Conversely, fibre-constant current safety does not guarantee a viable future policy.

### Required correction

Rename the result, for example:

> **Observation-only predicate-classification criterion.** Exact classification of current membership in \(S\) from the current observation exists iff membership in \(S\) is constant on every observation fibre.

Then distinguish it from:

- common-action existence;
- belief viability;
- history-dependent policy existence.

The phrases “can exist at all” and “any exact observation-only certifier” are too broad.

## 4. The common-action obstruction is underspecified

### Location

Section 2.3, p. 3:

> “the safe-action sets of pairwise-compatible states intersect emptily while each is nonempty”

### Problems

“Pairwise-compatible states” is undefined. It could mean:

- states sharing an observation;
- states jointly possible in one belief;
- states whose action sets intersect pairwise.

Those interpretations are different. In particular, pairwise nonempty intersections of action sets do not generally imply a nonempty global intersection.

The obstruction also proves no immediate common action only if the sets contain the relevant recursively viable actions. If they contain merely one-step-safe actions, it does not by itself prove no finite-horizon policy exists.

### Required correction

Define:

- the compatibility relation;
- the exact action set attached to each state;
- whether it is immediate-safe or recursively viable;
- the horizon of the obstruction;
- the policy class being ruled out.

A formal statement should look like:

\[
\bigcap_{z\in B} A_k(z)=\varnothing,
\qquad
A_k(z)\ne\varnothing\ \forall z\in B,
\]
where \(A_k(z)\) is explicitly defined.

## 10. The claimed three-regime rational family omits necessary assumptions

### Location

Section 4.3, p. 8:

> “Across the rational family of floor pairs \((s_1,s_2)\) and dip depths the arrangement has three regimes...”

with thresholds
\[
\frac{\mathrm{dip}-s_1}{s_2},
\qquad
\frac{s_1}{\mathrm{dip}-s_2}.

### Problem

These formulas and the claimed ordering require denominator and sign assumptions. At minimum, the derivation appears to require conditions such as:

\[
s_1>0,\qquad s_2>0,\qquad \mathrm{dip}>s_1,\qquad \mathrm{dip}>s_2.

If \(\mathrm{dip}\le s_2\), the second denominator is zero or negative and the inequality direction or interpretation changes. If \(\mathrm{dip}\le s_1\), the first threshold can be nonpositive, so the associated plan may be licensed for every positive ratio.

### Required correction

State the admissible parameter region and separately handle degenerate cases:

- zero denominator;
- negative threshold;
- coincident thresholds;
- thresholds outside the positive-ratio domain;
- plans licensed everywhere or nowhere.

The weight-partition certificate should cover these cases if the interface is presented as general.

# Certificate and checker issues

## 13. The “infeasibility margin” is representation-dependent

### Location

Section 2.3, p. 3:

> “the infeasibility margin \(-\lambda^\top b>0\) is defined by this normalization”

### Problem

Normalizing \(\lambda\) so that \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\) removes arbitrary scaling of the multiplier, but it does not make the margin invariant to rescaling the original inequalities. Replacing one row by a positive scalar multiple changes the normalized certificate and generally changes \(-\lambda^\top b\).

Therefore this is not an intrinsic geometric distance to feasibility. It is a margin relative to a particular row representation and normalization.

### Required correction

Call it a **normalized certificate contradiction value** or explicitly state:

> The margin is invariant to positive scaling of the certificate vector but not to rescaling or renormalization of the input rows.

If margins are compared across instances, define canonical row normalization first.

If the checker re-derives the constraint system from serialized transition data, say so and document the schema. If it merely checks a supplied matrix, narrow the claim.

## Executive assessment

The manuscript has a credible and potentially useful core: a small, exact-rational, certificate-carrying layer for transition-safety verdicts in sustainability assessment. The strongest claims are also the ones that need the most repair. The current version is not yet internally consistent enough for its strongest statements about belief-space completeness, exact versus conservative tubes, weight-space completeness, independent verification, and scaling. 

## 1. Belief-space recursion: the most serious correctness problem

**Flaw.** The paper gives mutually incompatible descriptions:

- A belief is “a set of states” (§2.2).
- The belief updates to “the set of labels reachable under the action” (§2.2).
- The Introduction claims the recursion is “sound and complete in finite systems.”
- §2.2 later restricts completeness to the case where observation is injective on reachable states.
- Admissibility is described as checking “no state in its observation fibre enters a violation,” but a history-dependent belief can be a strict subset of the current fibre.

**Why it matters.** A set of labels generally loses the state information needed to determine future safe actions. If the algorithm abstracts beliefs to labels, soundness and completeness require a separate proof. If observations are injective on reachable states, the partial-observation problem largely collapses to singletons, which is an unexpectedly restrictive condition for a belief-space method.

**Improvement.** State the exact recursion formally:

- Beliefs are sets of states, or explicitly define the label abstraction.
- Define the transition and observation update:
  \[
  B'_{a,o}=\{z':\exists z\in B,\ z'\in T(z,a),\ \operatorname{obs}(z')=o\}.
  \]
- Specify disturbance quantification.
- Specify whether policies are memoryless in observations or history-dependent through beliefs.
- Define \(W_0\) and \(W_k\).
- Prove soundness and completeness under stated hypotheses, or cite exact theorem numbers from the companion manuscripts.
- Remove unconditional “sound and complete in finite systems” unless that is actually proved.
- If injectivity is required, explain why and whether it can be relaxed.

## 2. Exact versus conservative tubes are contradictory

**Flaw.** §2.1 says “each tube is the exact visited set and no outer approximation enters.” §4.1 says the Schaefer realization’s tubes are “certified conservative outer enclosures.” The Conclusion says “exact or certified conservative tubes.”

**Why it matters.** Exact and outer tubes support asymmetric verdicts:

- Exact tube: safe everywhere ⇒ certified safe; violation ⇒ certified unsafe.
- Outer tube: safe everywhere ⇒ true tube safe; violation ⇒ inconclusive unless the violating point is known reachable.

**Improvement.** Introduce explicit tube statuses:

- `EXACT`
- `OUTER_CERTIFIED`
- `UNVERIFIED`

Define verdict semantics per status. The API should return at least:

- `CERTIFIED_SAFE`
- `CERTIFIED_VIOLATION`
- `NOT_CERTIFIED_SAFE_FROM_OUTER_TUBE`

For the benchmark, state whether the typed/aggregate separation is established by an exact declared plan tube or only by an outer Schaefer enclosure. If the rejection depends only on an outer-enclosure point, the separation is not established for the realized dynamics.

## 3.1 Replace the single notion of “tube” with explicit tube status

The paper currently mixes exact tubes and conservative outer enclosures. These have different logical consequences.

### Required conceptual distinction

Introduce three tube statuses:

```text
EXACT
OUTER_CERTIFIED
UNVERIFIED
```

### Required verdict semantics

Add a table such as this:

| Tube status | If all constraints hold on tube | If a constraint is violated on tube |
|---|---|---|
| `EXACT` | Certified safe relative to the supplied datum. | Certified unsafe relative to the supplied datum. |
| `OUTER_CERTIFIED` | Certified safe for the true trajectory, because true tube ⊆ certified enclosure. | Inconclusive unless the violating point is independently known to be reachable. |
| `UNVERIFIED` | Conditional result only. | Conditional result only. |

### API consequence

The library should not return a bare Boolean `admissible` when conservative tubes are involved. It should return an enum such as:

```text
CERTIFIED_SAFE
CERTIFIED_VIOLATION
NOT_CERTIFIED_SAFE_FROM_OUTER_TUBE
CONDITIONAL_ON_DATUM
```

### Manuscript correction

Replace:

> “each tube is the exact visited set and no outer approximation enters”

with:

> “A datum supplies either an exact tube or a certified conservative enclosure. The library records the tube status and returns verdicts whose logical strength depends on that status. Exact tubes support both certification and rejection relative to the supplied datum; certified outer enclosures support one-sided safety certification.”

### Benchmark consequence

For the fishery benchmark, the paper must state precisely which object proves the separation between typed and scalarized operators:

- If the separation depends on the **Schaefer outer enclosure**, say that the result is a conservative certification, not a conclusive rejection of the nonlinear realization.

## 3.2 Refactor the belief recursion into a formally sound and complete finite-horizon construction

This is the single most important correctness repair.

The current manuscript has three incompatible statements:

1. A belief is a set of states.
2. The belief updates to a set of labels.
3. The recursion is sound and complete in finite systems, except later completeness requires injective observation.

These cannot all remain as written.

### Recommended resolution

Use **state-set beliefs**, not label-set beliefs, for the finite-system completeness claim.

A belief should be a set of states consistent with the observation history:

\[
B \subseteq Z.
\]

After taking action \(a\) and receiving observation \(o\), the updated belief is

\[
B'_{a,o}
=
\left\{
z' \in Z:
\exists z\in B,\ \exists d\in D(a),\ 
z' = R(y_{z,a,d}^{-},a,d),\ 
\operatorname{obs}(z') = o
\right\},
\]

where \(y_{z,a,d}^{-}\) is the terminal pre-reset state on the tube from \(z\) under action \(a\) and disturbance \(d\), and \(R\) is the reset map.

If disturbances are included robustly, the update must include all successors compatible with the observation and the disturbance model.

### Recommended finite-horizon belief recursion

Let \(W_k\) be the set of \(k\)-step viable beliefs.

Define \(W_0\) according to the terminal condition. For example:

\[
W_0 = \{B \subseteq Z : B \subseteq G\},
\]

or another explicitly stated terminal acceptance condition.

For \(k+1\):

\[
B\in W_{k+1}
\]

if and only if there exists an action \(a\) such that:

1. **Immediate safety:** for every state \(z\in B\) and every admissible disturbance \(d\), the tube from \(z\) under \(a,d\) satisfies all floor and physical constraints.

2. **Recursive viability:** for every observation \(o\) that can arise after taking \(a\), the updated belief \(B'_{a,o}\) belongs to \(W_k\).

Formally:

\[
B\in W_{k+1}
\iff
\exists a\in A:
\left[
\forall z\in B,\ \forall d\in D(a),\ 
\mathrm{TubeSafe}(z,a,d)
\right]
\land
\left[
\forall o\in O_{B,a},\ 
B'_{a,o}\in W_k
\right].
\]

### Theorem to state in the paper

Add a theorem such as:

> **Theorem: Soundness and completeness of finite belief recursion.**  
> Let the state space, action set, disturbance set, and observation set be finite, and let policies be allowed to depend on the full observation history through the induced belief. Then the recursion above returns exactly the set of beliefs from which there exists an observation-history policy that is viable over horizon \(k\) under the stated disturbance semantics.

### Important qualification

If the intended policy class is **memoryless in the current observation**, then completeness may fail unless the observation is Markovian or satisfies a quotient condition. The paper must state which policy class it uses.

Recommended wording:

> “Completeness is stated for observation-history policies, equivalently belief-based policies. For memoryless observation-only policies, additional Markov or quotient conditions are required.”

### If the implementation uses labels

If SafeTransition’s implementation updates beliefs as sets of observation labels rather than states, then the manuscript must not claim unconditional completeness. It should instead say:

> “The label-based recursion is a quotient abstraction. It is sound for finite systems. It is complete when the quotient preserves safety, action enablement, and observation-labelled successor structure.”

Injectivity of observation is a sufficient condition but is so strong that it largely eliminates the partial-observation difficulty. It should not be the primary completeness condition unless the paper explicitly accepts that limitation.

### Add the three-state recourse example in full

The paper’s principal belief demonstration must be reproducible from the article. Add a compact table such as:

| State | Observation | Available actions | Successor under action \(\alpha\) | Successor under action \(\beta\) | Immediate tube safe? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(z_0\) | \(o_0\) | \(\alpha,\beta\) | \(z_2\) | \(z_2\) | yes |
| \(z_1\) | \(o_0\) | \(\alpha,\beta\) | \(z_2\) | \(z_2\) | yes |
| \(z_2\) | \(o_1\) | \(\alpha,\beta\) | \(z_2\) | \(z_2\) | no |

Then show:

\[
B_0 = \{z_0,z_1\}\in W_1,
\qquad
B_0\notin W_2.
\]

Explanation:

- At horizon 1, either action is safe from \(z_0\) and \(z_1\).
- The post-belief after either action is \(\{z_2\}\).
- \(\{z_2\}\notin W_1\) because no action keeps the tube safe from \(z_2\).
- Therefore \(B_0\in W_1\setminus W_2\).

Include the actual counterexample object returned by `explain_belief_failure`, preferably in JSON or pseudo-JSON.

## 3.3 Formalize the five operators and prove the inclusion chain under explicit assumptions

The current inclusion chain is asserted before the operators are fully defined. The revision should define each operator as a constraint relaxation.

### Recommended definitions

Let \(z\) be a state. Let \(T(z,a,d)\) be the tube from \(z\) under action \(a\) and disturbance \(d\). Let \(y^{-}(z,a,d)\) be the terminal pre-reset state, and let

\[
z^{+}(z,a,d)=R(y^{-}(z,a,d),a,d)
\]

be the post-reset successor.

Let the typed safe set be

\[
S_{\mathrm{typ}}
=
\{y : s_i(y)\ge 0\ \forall i,\ \mathrm{Phys}(y)\}.
\]

Let the physical safe set be

\[
S_{\mathrm{phys}}
=
\{y : \mathrm{Phys}(y)\}.
\]

Assume a common finite disturbance set \(D(a)\), common destination condition \(G\), and common physical predicate \(\mathrm{Phys}\).

Define:

\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)
=
\left\{
a:
\forall d\in D(a),\ 
T(z,a,d)\subseteq S_{\mathrm{typ}},\ 
z^{+}(z,a,d)\in G
\right\}.
\]

For a strictly positive weight vector \(w\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^n\), define:

\[
E_w(z)
=
\left\{
a:
\forall d\in D(a),\ 
\forall y\in T(z,a,d),\ 
w\cdot s(y)\ge 0,\ 
\mathrm{Phys}(y),\ 
z^{+}(z,a,d)\in G
\right\}.
\]

Define the exact-tube physical operator:

\[
E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}(z)
=
\left\{
a:
\forall d\in D(a),\ 
T(z,a,d)\subseteq S_{\mathrm{phys}},\ 
z^{+}(z,a,d)\in G
\right\}.
\]

Define the endpoint physical operator:

\[
E_{\mathrm{end,phys}}(z)
=
\left\{
a:
\forall d\in D(a),\ 
y^{-}(z,a,d)\in S_{\mathrm{phys}},\ 
z^{+}(z,a,d)\in G
\right\}.
\]

Define the typed endpoint operator:

\[
E_{\mathrm{end,typ}}(z)
=
\left\{
a:
\forall d\in D(a),\ 
y^{-}(z,a,d)\in S_{\mathrm{typ}},\ 
z^{+}(z,a,d)\in G
\right\}.
\]

### Inclusion chain

Under these definitions, for every \(w\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^n\),

\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)
\subseteq
E_w(z)
\subseteq
E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}(z)
\subseteq
E_{\mathrm{end,phys}}(z).
\]

Also,

\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)
\subseteq
E_{\mathrm{end,typ}}(z)
\subseteq
E_{\mathrm{end,phys}}(z).
\]

### Why the chain holds

- Coordinatewise floor satisfaction implies nonnegative weighted sum for positive weights.
- Removing floor constraints weakens the condition.
- Requiring a condition along the full tube is stronger than requiring it only at the endpoint.
- Typed constraints imply physical constraints if physical constraints are part of \(S_{\mathrm{typ}}\).

### Manuscript addition

Add a short proposition:

> **Proposition: Operator inclusion.**  
> If all operators share the same finite disturbance set, destination condition, reset map, and physical predicate, and if weights are strictly positive, then the above inclusions hold. The inclusions may be strict.

### Clarify the role of \(S\)

The transition-safe set \(S\) should not disappear. Define whether \(S\) means:

- typed safety: floors + physical constraints;
- physical safety only;
- destination feasibility;
- a graph-level admissible state set.

Recommended wording:

> “The recursion uses \(S_{\mathrm{typ}}\) for typed viability and \(S_{\mathrm{phys}}\) for physical viability. The operator at a single state checks tube constraints and the destination condition; the backward recursion additionally requires successors to remain inside the appropriate viable set.”

## 3.5 Fix the weight domain once and for all

The paper alternates between “positive weight cone” and intervals including \(r=0\). Choose one convention.

### Recommended convention for two-dimensional weights

Use the projective nonnegative simplex:

\[
\Delta_1
=
\{(w_1,w_2)\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^2 : w_1+w_2=1\}.
\]

Define the ratio

\[
r=\frac{w_2}{w_1}\in[0,\infty],
\]

with:

- \(r=0\) corresponding to \((1,0)\);
- \(r=\infty\) corresponding to \((0,1)\).

Then intervals such as

\[
[0,2/3)
\]

are meaningful.

### Alternative convention

If the paper insists on strictly positive weights, use:

\[
r\in(0,\infty),
\]

and replace all intervals containing zero or infinity by open intervals or limiting statements.

### Recommended manuscript wording

> “Weights are nonnegative and not both zero. For two criteria, the projective weight domain is represented by \(r=w_2/w_1\in[0,\infty]\). The endpoints correspond to the coordinate axes of the weight simplex.”

### Consequence for weight partition

The weight-partition certificate must cover the full domain it claims to cover, including endpoints and degenerate thresholds.

# 4. Formal certificate framework to add to the paper

The paper should introduce a small certificate ontology. This will greatly strengthen the EMS contribution.

---

## 4.1 Certificate classes

Add a subsection called **Certificate classes** and define the following.

### 1. Farkas infeasibility certificate

For a rational system

\[
Au\le b,
\]

a Farkas certificate is a vector \(\lambda\ge 0\) such that

\[
\lambda^\top A = 0,
\qquad
\lambda^\top b < 0.
\]

Normalize:

\[
\sum_i \lambda_i = 1.
\]

The certificate proves infeasibility of the supplied row system.

Important qualification:

> The normalized contradiction value \(-\lambda^\top b\) is invariant under positive rescaling of \(\lambda\), but not under rescaling of the input rows. It is therefore a normalized certificate contradiction value, not an intrinsic geometric distance.

### 2. Feasibility certificate

If `certify_polyhedron` returns feasible, it should return either:

- a rational witness \(u\) satisfying \(Au\le b\), checked exactly; or
- a statement that feasibility is returned as a Boolean without witness.

Recommended API:

```text
Feasible(witness=u)
Infeasible(farkas=λ)
```

Do not call a bare Boolean a certificate.

### 3. Weight-partition certificate

For each plan \(p\), store the robust aggregate minimum as a piecewise-rational function of \(r\). Store:

- domain convention;
- thresholds;
- region coverage;
- inclusivity of endpoints;
- licensed plan set per region;
- binding constraints at boundaries;
- degenerate cases.

The checker should re-derive:

- threshold order;
- region coverage;
- licensed set at sampled rational ratios;
- boundary witnesses.

### 4. Tube certificate

For exact tubes:

- breakpoints;
- coordinate expressions per segment;
- floor minima;
- proof that extrema occur at breakpoints for the supported predicate class.

For outer-certified tubes:

- enclosure relation;
- interval bounds;
- monotonicity or derivative certificate;
- rational inequalities proving true tube ⊆ enclosure.

### 5. Belief-failure certificate

For a belief \(B\notin W_k\), store:

- belief identifier;
- horizon \(k\);
- for each candidate action, the reason for failure;
- if failure is due to a post-belief, recursively include that post-belief’s failure certificate;
- if failure is due to a fibre state entering violation, include the violating state, action, disturbance, and time or segment.

This makes `explain_belief_failure` machine-checkable.

### 6. Common-action obstruction certificate

Define:

- the compatibility relation, e.g. same observation fibre or same belief;
- the recursively viable action set \(A_k(z)\) for each state;
- proof that each \(A_k(z)\neq\varnothing\);
- proof that \(\bigcap_z A_k(z)=\varnothing\);
- proof of inclusion-minimality.

Recommended statement:

> “The returned subfamily is inclusion-minimal: the intersection over the retained states is empty, but removing any one retained state makes the intersection nonempty.”

Do not claim minimum cardinality unless the algorithm computes it.

### 7. Fibre-criterion certificate

Rename this certificate:

```text
observation-only safety-classification obstruction
```

It proves only that no function of the current observation alone can classify current safe-set membership exactly.

It does **not** prove that no observation-based policy exists.

## 5.1 Transition datum

Insert in Section 2.1:

> A typed transition datum consists of:

> The disturbance set is finite or effectively enumerable. Universal quantification over disturbances is implemented by exact enumeration in the reported examples.

## 5.2 Endpoint reset

Insert:

> Let \(y^{-}(z,a,d)\) denote the terminal pre-reset state of the tube \(T(z,a,d)\). Let
> \[
> z^{+}(z,a,d)=R(y^{-}(z,a,d),a,d)
> \]
> denote the post-reset successor. Endpoint-only operators evaluate constraints at \(y^{-}\), while destination constraints are evaluated at \(z^{+}\). This distinction prevents ambiguity between the terminal visited state and the reset successor.

## 5.3 Accepted-state sets and quantifier order

Insert:

> For an operator \(E\), define
> \[
> V[E]=\{z:E(z)\neq\varnothing\}.
> \]
> For a scalarized operator at weight \(w\), define \(V_w=V[E_w]\). The compensatory accepted set is
> \[
> V_{\mathrm{weak}}=\bigcap_{w}V_w.
> \]
> This means
> \[
> z\in V_{\mathrm{weak}}
> \iff
> \forall w,\ \exists a\in A:\ a\in E_w(z).
> \]
> It does not imply
> \[
> \exists a,\ \forall w:\ a\in E_w(z).
> \]
> The distinction is central to the quantifier-order separation between scalarized and noncompensatory assessment.

This addition is especially valuable because the paper’s theme is quantifier order.

## 14.1 Exactness claim

## 14.2 Tube claim

Original:

Replace with:

## 14.3 Belief completeness claim

Original:

Replace with:

## 14.4 Fibre criterion claim

Original:

> “decides whether an exact observation-only certifier can exist at all”

Replace with:

## 14.5 Minimal subfamily claim

Replace with:
