\begin{abstract}
A sustainability theory cannot be obtained merely by placing accounting identities, stock--flow models, delay equations, sampled institutions, and empirical screens under one vocabulary. It requires explicit maps between these objects and a hierarchy separating proved results from conditional results, conjectures, numerical propositions, and empirical hypotheses. We develop a research architecture around typed primitive fluxes, service possibility sets, observation and assessment operators, signed governance channels, uncertainty classes, and robust viability. An exact observation-fibre criterion and a local threshold-horizon bound are proved, while conservation, invariance, substitution, delayed viability, and model reduction are separated into attained restricted results and open theorem targets. A ten-state template is retained as an admissibility stress test, not as a realization of the architecture or a registered numerical model. Its formal mass cancellation coexists with a finite-donor boundary failure, non-realizable and unclosed variance dynamics, an unspecified output closure, and no proved reduction to the lower-dimensional model families. Because the displayed equations do not determine a unique autonomous delay system, no spectral threshold from that template is admitted here as a numerical proposition. The result is a formal research architecture rather than a claim that one validated universal model has been obtained.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope of the research architecture}

The companion papers establish bounded objects:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item a componentwise stock--flow ledger and correctly named diagnostics;
    \item a registered family of delayed extractive-feedback models;
    \item sampled-governance calculations, a structured field-case search, a spectral screen and power analysis, and a prospective management strategy evaluation (MSE) design.
\end{enumerate}

These objects are useful, but common notation does not establish mathematical derivation among them. Their state spaces, effort laws, diagnostic definitions, and boundary assumptions differ. A canonical architecture must therefore be broad enough to express these models while requiring specialization and reduction to be given as mathematical maps rather than verbal analogies.

The intended theory must distinguish at least:

\begin{itemize}
    \item material amount from ecological function;
    \item current service adequacy from trajectory safety;
    \item accounting identity from constitutive dynamics;
    \item observation from latent state;
    \item command from deployment and realised pressure;
    \item extractive from protective feedback;
    \item stability from viability;
    \item parameter uncertainty from structural uncertainty;
    \item specialisation, approximation, projection, and singular reduction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Candidate canonical architecture}

\subsection{Typed physical and functional states}

Let $\mathcal T$ be a type system specifying moiety, compartment, location, life stage, unit, and ownership or jurisdiction where relevant. Let
\[
x(t)\in X\subseteq\R_+^m
\]
contain physical amounts and
\[
y(t)\in Y
\]
contain non-conserved states such as prices, institutional memory, information, effort commands, technology states, and ecological indices not interpretable as conserved amounts.

Internal physical dynamics use non-negative primitive fluxes:
\begin{equation}
\dot x=S_{\mathcal T}v(x,y,\theta)+B_{\mathcal T}u_{\partial}(t)+d_x(t),
\qquad v\ge0.
\label{eq:typed-flux}
\end{equation}
Here $S_{\mathcal T}$ is a typed stoichiometric/incidence operator, $B_{\mathcal T}u_{\partial}$ contains declared boundary transfers, and $d_x$ belongs to a stated disturbance class. This incidence bookkeeping follows the stoichiometric-subspace and conservation-law structure of reaction-network theory \cite{feinberg2019}, without assuming mass-action kinetics for the economic, ecological, or governance fluxes. If $L^\top S_{\mathcal T}=0$, then
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}(L^\top x)=L^\top B_{\mathcal T}u_{\partial}+L^\top d_x.
\label{eq:conservation}
\end{equation}
This supplies one conservation law per conserved moiety and boundary. It does not create a scalar ``sustainability mass'' across incommensurable systems.

Forward invariance is a separate requirement. Every primitive outflow must vanish or be limited when its donor compartment is empty. A target-relaxation flux from a finite donor is admissible only after donor limitation is made explicit.

\subsection{Services and feasible substitution}

Let $\Gamma(x,y)$ be a service possibility correspondence:
\[
\Gamma(x,y)\subseteq\R_+^n.
\]
A service vector $s$ is feasible if $s\in\Gamma(x,y)$. Demand or entitlement is $d\in\R_+^n$, and current adequacy is the componentwise relation $s\ge d$. This is not yet a trajectory claim. Treating critical components as non-substitutable is consistent with strong-sustainability and critical-natural-capital arguments \cite{neumayer2013,ekins2003}; the correspondence below makes any permitted substitution pathway explicit rather than presuming unrestricted compensation.

Substitution should be represented by a pathway set, not an elasticity parameter alone. In a linear technology approximation one might write
\begin{equation}
\Gamma(x,y)=\{s\ge0:\exists a\ge0,\ Ra\le x,\ Qa\ge s,\ Ea\le e_{\max}\},
\label{eq:technology-cone}
\end{equation}
where activities $a$ consume typed resources through $R$, deliver services through $Q$, and obey energy/exergy or infrastructure constraints through $E$. A Farkas-type separation theorem may eventually characterize infeasible service claims in this linear case. For nonlinear, nonconvex, or irreversible technologies, that result does not transfer automatically.

\subsection{Observation, assessment, and identifiability}

A real institution does not observe $x$ directly. Let
\begin{align}
Y_k&=\mathcal O_k(x_{[0,t_k]},y_{[0,t_k]})+\varepsilon_k,\\
\widehat x_k&=\mathcal A_k(Y_{0:k},\mathcal M_k),
\end{align}
where $\mathcal O$ is the observation process, $\mathcal A$ an assessment operator, and $\mathcal M_k$ the assessment model. Aggregation, missingness, changing technology, and model discrepancy enter here.

The exact static certification criterion is given in Theorem~\ref{thm:observation-fibre}. Dynamic safety is harder: observationally equivalent states can still admit a common safe action, while states that require incompatible actions cannot share a robust observation-based controller. A dynamic impossibility result must therefore declare the information pattern, disturbance class, safe set, and admissible policy class rather than infer non-viability from aggregation alone. The algebraic scalar non-compensation result is one special warning, not a complete dynamic identifiability theorem.

\subsection{Signed governance architecture}

Governance should be decomposed into

\begin{equation}
\text{observation}\longrightarrow
\text{assessment}\longrightarrow
\text{command}\longrightarrow
\text{deployment}\longrightarrow
\text{realised pressure}.
\end{equation}

Let $c_k$ be a command, $p(t)$ realised pressure, and $\mathcal D_{\rm dep}$ a deployment operator. Protective and extractive channels are distinct components:
\begin{align}
c_{k+1}^{-}&=\mathcal G^{-}(\widehat x_k,c_k) &&\text{protective: decline tends to reduce pressure},\\
c_{k+1}^{+}&=\mathcal G^{+}(\widehat x_k,c_k) &&\text{extractive: decline tends to increase pressure},\\
p(t)&=\mathcal D_{\rm dep}(c_{0:k},\text{compliance},\text{capacity}).
\end{align}
The superscripts indicate feedback sign with respect to pressure, not moral evaluation. Continuous, delayed, sampled, event-triggered, and legally fixed controllers occupy different subclasses.

\subsection{Safe-and-just sets and viability}

Let $K\subseteq X\times Y$ encode physical thresholds, minimum services, maximum harms, and distributional constraints. This joint ecological-and-social construction is aligned with empirical safe-and-just-space and Earth-system-boundary programmes \cite{oneill2018,rockstrom2023}, while leaving the numerical thresholds, authority, scale, and affected groups application-specific.

The information pattern is part of the definition. Here $\mathbb P$ denotes a declared class of causal, non-anticipative policies. A policy $\pi=(\pi_t)_{t\ge0}$ maps the observation and command history available through time $t$ to the current command; two available histories that agree through $t$ induce the same commands through $t$. Thus $\pi$ is selected as a rule before the disturbance realization, but it is not a precommitted open-loop signal. If past disturbances are directly observed, they must be included explicitly in the available history; future disturbances may not be used. With a declared disturbance class $\mathcal D$, define
\begin{equation}
\Viab_{\mathbb P,\mathcal D}(K)
=\left\{z_0\in K:\exists\pi\in\mathbb P\ \forall d\in\mathcal D,
\ z^{\pi,d}(t;z_0)\in K\ \forall t\ge0\right\}.
\label{eq:viability}
\end{equation}
This follows the viability perspective of \cite{aubin1991,cury2005}. The quantifier order asks for one causal policy that is robust to every declared disturbance; it does not by itself imply that the kernel is empty. Persistent adverse disturbances empty the kernel only when their effect overwhelms every admissible causal policy for the particular dynamics and safe set.

Equation~\eqref{eq:viability} is state-indexed. If $z_0$ is fully observed, that state may be included in the initial information. If only an observation is available, selecting a different policy separately for each latent $z_0$ would leak unavailable information. An observation-based application must instead propagate an initial compatibility set $B_0$ (or belief/information state) and require one policy to work for every compatible state:
\[
\mathfrak K_{\mathbb P,\mathcal D}(K)
=\left\{\varnothing\ne B_0\subseteq K:\exists\pi\in\mathbb P\ \forall z_0\in B_0\ \forall d\in\mathcal D,
\ z^{\pi,d}(t;z_0)\in K\ \forall t\ge0\right\}.
\]
The state-indexed kernel and this knowledge-kernel family are different objects; neither is identified with sampled, delayed, or latent-state governance without an information-state theorem. Three questions must be reported separately:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Actual-policy safety}: does the implemented policy keep this trajectory in $K$?
    \item \textbf{Viability}: does some admissible policy do so?
    \item \textbf{Robust viability}: does one admissible policy work for all disturbances in the declared class?
\end{enumerate}

A parameter set of the form $\Theta_{\rm safe}=\{\theta:\mathcal A_\theta\subset\operatorname{int}K\}$, where $\mathcal A_\theta$ is an attractor, is an attractor-containment set rather than a viability kernel. Attractor containment can miss unsafe transients and dependence on initial history.

\subsection{Diagnostic and threshold taxonomies}

The word ``deficit'' should not compress five different objects:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{3.2cm}X}
\toprule
Object & Example and interpretation\\
\midrule
Throughput excess & extraction or waste flow above a declared contemporaneous rate; a flow comparison, not necessarily stock decline\\
Stock drawdown & $[-\dot x_i]_+$ or an integrated loss of a typed stock\\
Threshold proximity & signed distance or margin to a declared boundary of $K$\\
Resilience loss & loss of recovery rate, viable-control margin, basin size, or return capacity; not inferred from stock level alone\\
Service/welfare shortfall & failure of a group-specific service or entitlement floor, which can occur without immediate material drawdown\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Thresholds likewise require a type: a hard physical boundary such as exhaustion; an ecological functional threshold; a service or basic-needs floor; a harm or rights ceiling; a precautionary policy trigger; or a statistical detection threshold. The last two are decision and evidence objects, not automatically physical tipping points. Their uncertainty, authority, affected group, spatial support, and time horizon should be recorded.

\subsection{Recoverability, capture, and intergenerational scope}

For a constraint set $D$, target set $C\subseteq D$, horizon $H$, policy class $\mathbb P$, and disturbance class $\mathcal D$, define the robust capture basin
\[
\operatorname{Capt}^{H}_{\mathbb P,\mathcal D}(C;D)
=\{z_0\in D:\exists\pi\in\mathbb P\ \forall d\in\mathcal D,
\ z^{\pi,d}(t)\in D\text{ until }z^{\pi,d}(t_C)\in C
\text{ for some }t_C\le H\}.
\]
A state is recoverable only relative to these five declared objects. Calling a change ``irreversible'' means exclusion from the corresponding capture basin; changing the horizon, admissible technologies, information, or disturbance quantifier can change the verdict. Basin membership under one autonomous model is therefore not a universal recoverability result.

Intergenerational justice also needs an explicit recursion. If generation $g$ occupies an interval $I_g$, a candidate criterion requires group-specific service and harm constraints throughout $I_g$ and a terminal state in a set viable for generation $g+1$. A terminal stock total alone can conceal within-generation harm, degraded material quality, or an empty continuation kernel. This is a definition target for the future theory, not a welfare theorem established by the companion models.

\section{Modules required only when invoked}

A general architecture should be modular rather than maximal in every application. A restricted theorem may omit a module if its boundary assumptions are stated. The following modules become mandatory when the claim depends on them.

\subsection{Stage structure}

Recruitment suppression and adult harvest are physically different. A stage model should include at least juvenile and adult compartments, maturation, stage-specific mortality, and routing of harvested material. Prevented recruitment is not material removed from adult biomass and cannot be routed to product or waste as if it were harvested mass.

Spawning-stock biomass is a biological adult stock. Treating it as an abiotic support pool is a type error. A fisheries theory needs demographic states and fishing selectivity; an abiotic support-pool claim needs separate donor measurements.

\subsection{Space and heterogeneity}

For nonlinear local dynamics $f$, generally
\[
\mathbb E[f(X)]\ne f(\mathbb E[X]).
\]
Two restricted statements are exact. If $\mu_X=\mathbb E[X]$ and $R(X)=rX(1-X/K)$, then
\[
\mathbb E[R(X)]
=r\mu_X\left(1-\frac{\mu_X}{K}\right)
-\frac{r}{K}\operatorname{Var}(X).
\]
More generally, if $f\in C^2(I)$ and $X$ is supported in $I$, Taylor's theorem gives
\[
\left|\mathbb E[f(X)]-f(\mathbb E[X])\right|
\le \frac12\|f''\|_{\infty,I}\operatorname{Var}(X).
\]
These are a quadratic identity and a curvature bound, respectively; neither closes the dynamics of the variance. Exact dynamic moment closure occurs only for special functional forms or distributions, as spatial moment methods and their closure problem make explicit \cite{bolker1997,murrell2004}. Spatial conservation also requires explicit transport fluxes across cells and boundaries, while heterogeneous harvest introduces covariance terms such as $-q\operatorname{Cov}(E,X)$.

\subsection{Nonsmooth constraints and distributed memory}

An exact Liebig minimum, an active-set service constraint, or a hard deployment bound produces a piecewise-smooth or set-valued system rather than an automatically smooth one. Inside a region with a fixed binding constraint the equations simplify, but they do not generally become scalar: nonbinding stocks, memory states, and governance states can remain dynamically coupled. Switching surfaces may support simultaneous binding, boundary equilibria, or sliding and require complementarity, differential-inclusion, or piecewise-smooth methods. A smooth minimum is therefore a declared constitutive approximation whose sharpness can affect global dynamics, not a universal harmless regularisation.

A point delay is likewise one memory model, not a primitive that must be used universally. Distributed kernels and compartmental cascades should be compared against it. An Erlang kernel admits a finite-dimensional linear-chain realization through the linear-chain technique \cite{hurtado2019}, which can simplify analysis and may represent heterogeneous processing times. Such a realization preserves a material ledger only when its cascade states are themselves correctly typed physical compartments; an assessment or decision queue is informational and should not be relabelled as conserved matter.

\subsection{Polycentric agents and institutional networks}

When several extractors or authorities act on a shared stock, the governance state should include agent-specific observations, commands, deployment constraints, objectives, and delays together with an interaction network. This module is motivated by the social--ecological-systems and polycentric-governance lineage \cite{ostrom2009}, but the equations must still specify the actual agents and coupling. Aggregate effort then contains covariance and strategic-response terms that are absent from a representative-agent controller. Delay heterogeneity is not assumed to be stabilizing: its sign depends on network coupling, controller signs, gain distribution, and the delay law. Stability under increasing heterogeneity is an empirical hypothesis or a theorem target for a declared network class, not a general consequence of polycentricity.

\subsection{Energy and exergy}

Material conservation does not imply that every transformation is energetically feasible. A technology or governance action may require free-energy or exergy throughput; exergy-based resource accounting provides one established orientation for such distinctions \cite{dewulf2008}. Energy/exergy should enter as typed feasibility constraints or state-dependent capacities. It should not be converted into one compensatory sustainability scalar unless an application-specific equivalence is proved.

Material amount also does not imply retained quality or accessibility. A recycling module should type stocks by material, location, and quality grade. Down-cycling, grade-preserving recovery, and upgrading are different fluxes; upgrading requires a declared yield, residual route, and exergy/capacity input. Routing generic retired product directly into undifferentiated ``natural capital'' would erase these constraints.

\subsection{Justice and distribution}

A global service total can satisfy aggregate demand while violating local or group entitlements. Distributional constraints belong inside $K$ or the service correspondence. Weights in a welfare objective do not by themselves certify minimum component rights.

\section{Exact bridge results and conjectures}

The architecture contains two elementary exact statements. The remaining bridges are legitimate ambitious targets only with their conjectural status visible.

\begin{theorem}[Observation-fibre certification]
\label{thm:observation-fibre}
Let $D$ be a set of admissible latent states, let $\mathcal O:D\to\mathcal Y$ be an observation map, and let $K\subseteq D$ be a safety set. There exists a deterministic observation-only certificate $c:\mathcal Y\to\{0,1\}$ satisfying
\[
c(\mathcal O(z))=\mathbf 1_K(z)\qquad\text{for every }z\in D
\]
if and only if membership in $K$ is constant on every observation fibre: whenever $\mathcal O(z_1)=\mathcal O(z_2)$, one has $\mathbf 1_K(z_1)=\mathbf 1_K(z_2)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If such a certificate exists and $\mathcal O(z_1)=\mathcal O(z_2)$, then
\[
\mathbf 1_K(z_1)=c(\mathcal O(z_1))=c(\mathcal O(z_2))=\mathbf 1_K(z_2),
\]
so safety membership is constant on each fibre. Conversely, suppose fibre constancy holds. For $y\in\mathcal O(D)$, choose any $z\in D$ with $\mathcal O(z)=y$ and set $c(y)=\mathbf 1_K(z)$. Fibre constancy makes this definition independent of the chosen preimage. Define $c$ arbitrarily on $\mathcal Y\setminus\mathcal O(D)$. Then $c(\mathcal O(z))=\mathbf 1_K(z)$ for every $z\in D$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:observation-fibre} concerns certification of current latent-state membership. It does not imply that observation-based robust control is impossible whenever a fibre contains distinct states: those states may admit a common safe action. A dynamic impossibility theorem must instead show incompatibility of every admissible causal policy over observationally indistinguishable histories.

\begin{theorem}[Local threshold-horizon bound]
\label{thm:local-horizon}
Let $A:[0,T]\to\R$ be absolutely continuous, let $A(0)>A_{\min}$, and let $v_0>0$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$. Set $H_0=(A(0)-A_{\min})/v_0$ and suppose $T\ge H_0/(1-\varepsilon)$. Assume that
\[
(1-\varepsilon)v_0\le -\dot A(t)\le(1+\varepsilon)v_0
\]
for almost every $t$ for which $A(s)>A_{\min}$ for all $s\in[0,t]$. Then a first crossing time $H$ exists no later than
$H_0/(1-\varepsilon)$, and
\[
\frac{H_0}{1+\varepsilon}\le H\le\frac{H_0}{1-\varepsilon},
\qquad
|H-H_0|\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}H_0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $t_*=H_0/(1-\varepsilon)\le T$. If no crossing occurs before $t_*$, the assumed lower rate bound holds almost everywhere on $[0,t_*]$. Absolute continuity then gives
\[
A(t_*)\le A(0)-(1-\varepsilon)v_0t_*=A_{\min}.
\]
Continuity therefore gives a first crossing time $H\le t_*$. Integrating both rate bounds over $[0,H]$ and using $A(0)-A(H)=A(0)-A_{\min}=v_0H_0$ yields
\[
(1-\varepsilon)v_0H\le v_0H_0\le(1+\varepsilon)v_0H.
\]
Division by the positive factors proves the two-sided estimate. The upper relative deviation is $1/(1-\varepsilon)-1=\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)$, which dominates $1-1/(1+\varepsilon)$ and gives the absolute-error bound.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:local-horizon} formalizes a local diagnostic only. It fails when depletion reverses, the rate approaches zero, or feedback moves the trajectory outside the declared rate bounds.

\paragraph{Conjecture 1: periodic-orbit-fold persistence.}
A transverse fold of periodic orbits in a registered scalar DDE persists under sufficiently small typed vector coupling and compatible additional fixed delays if normal hyperbolicity, a spectral gap, and regularity of the Poincar\'e map hold. The open work is not satisfied by citing Fenichel theory alone \cite{fenichel1971,fenichel1979}: the hypotheses must be verified for the actual infinite-dimensional system, and the baseline fold must first be established by periodic-branch continuation.

\paragraph{Conjecture 2: exergy-gated suppression.}
For a declared class of autocatalytic extractive controllers, sufficiently low deployable exergy reduces the loop gain below every admissible Hopf-frequency modulus condition. This is not universal: depletion of institutional capacity may also disable protective action or create hysteresis.

\section{A ten-state admissibility stress test}

The architecture and theorem programme state what a general theory would require. The following template instead serves as a concrete negative stress test: it shows that combining several modules and obtaining a cancellation identity do not establish a well-posed, physically admissible model. It is not a specialization or realization of the canonical architecture. In particular, it has no observation or assessment operator, no command--deployment separation, no protective governance channel, no declared disturbance strategy, and no computed viability kernel.

\subsection{Displayed equations and closure status}

The displayed state list is the ten-state vector
\[
(\bar X_A,X_J,P,U,A,G,V_N,Z,K_C,E).
\]
The first six entries form the material-sum block; variance, signed memory, capital/exergy, and effort are additional states. The equations below are retained to make the admissibility audit exact. They are a template rather than a closed autonomous DDE because neither output $Q$ nor the covariance term is supplied as a functional of the ten-state history.

The explicit juvenile compartment nevertheless has a legitimate methodological role independent of the template's closure status. It separates recruitment, juvenile mortality, maturation, adult mortality, and adult-selective removal, rather than identifying recruitment instantaneously with adult biomass. It also makes the maturation time scale $g$ explicit and allows an observation map from stage abundance to spawning-stock biomass to be specified in a separately closed stage model. These advantages do not make the combined ten-state template dynamically complete or empirically identified.

Let $\psi\in[0,1]$ split depletion pressure into adult-stock removal $C_{\rm stock}=\psi qE\bar X_A$ and suppressed recruitment $C_{\rm recruit}=(1-\psi)qE\bar X_A$. The material equations are
\begin{align}
\dot{\bar X}_A &= \frac{1}{g}X_J-d_A\bar X_A-\psi qE\bar X_A, \label{eq:scaf-XA}\\
\dot X_J &= g_B(\bar X_A,A,V_N,E)-\left(\frac{1}{g}+d_J\right)X_J, \label{eq:scaf-XJ}\\
\dot P &= (1-\alpha)\psi qE\bar X_A-\lambda_P P, \label{eq:scaf-P}\\
\dot U &= d_A\bar X_A+d_JX_J+\alpha\psi qE\bar X_A+\lambda_PP-\gamma_UU, \label{eq:scaf-U}\\
\dot A &= -g_B(\bar X_A,A,V_N,E)+\gamma_UU+\omega_A(A^{\rm eq}-A), \label{eq:scaf-A}\\
\dot G &= -\omega_A(A^{\rm eq}-A). \label{eq:scaf-G}
\end{align}
The floored birth-transfer function and second-order Ricker variance term are
\begin{align}
g_B(\bar X_A,A,V_N,E)
&=\max\left\{0,\left[P_0\bar X_Ae^{-\bar X_A/N_c}
+\Phi_{\rm var}(\bar X_A,V_N)\right]\frac{A}{A+A_0}
-(1-\psi)qE\bar X_A\right\}, \label{eq:scaf-gB}\\
\Phi_{\rm var}(\bar X_A,V_N)
&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{P_0}{N_c}e^{-\bar X_A/N_c}
\left(\frac{\bar X_A}{N_c}-2\right)V_N. \label{eq:scaf-var}
\end{align}
If $R(x)=P_0xe^{-x/N_c}$, then
\[
\Phi_{\rm var}(\bar X_A,V_N)=\tfrac12R''(\bar X_A)V_N.
\]
Thus Eq.~\eqref{eq:scaf-var} is exactly the second-order Taylor contribution of a Ricker recruitment law and is negative when $\bar X_A<2N_c$. It is not an exact Ricker moment identity: unlike the quadratic logistic case, third and higher centred moments generally remain. This interpretation identifies the origin of the term but does not close Eq.~\eqref{eq:scaf-V} or guarantee that a trajectory of $V_N$ is realizable by a non-negative spatial distribution.

The factor $(1-\psi)qE\bar X_A$ is a reduction of the $A\to X_J$ birth-transfer rate, not an additional material outflow. Because the same $g_B$ enters $\dot X_J$ and $\dot A$ with opposite signs, material not transferred to juveniles remains in $A$; there is no unmatched ``ghost'' sink in the six-state ledger. Whether this constitutive interpretation is biologically appropriate is separate from the exact cancellation.

The remaining four equations are
\begin{align}
\dot V_N &=2\left[P_0e^{-\bar X_A/N_c}\left(1-\frac{\bar X_A}{N_c}\right)-d_A-\psi qE\right]V_N
-\delta_DV_N-2q\bar X_A\operatorname{Cov}(E,\bar X_A), \label{eq:scaf-V}\\
\dot Z &=\frac{\ell-Z}{\tau_m},\qquad \ell=-\dot{\bar X}_A, \label{eq:scaf-Z}\\
\dot K_C &=\varsigma Q-K_C(\delta_K+c_EE), \label{eq:scaf-K}\\
\dot E &=\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\max}}\right)
\left(1-e^{-K_C/K_0}\right)
\left[\eta E\frac{Z(t-\tau)}{\Delta_{\rm ref}}+\delta_0-\eta\frac{E^2}{E_{\max}}\right]-\mu_EE. \label{eq:scaf-E}
\end{align}

On a regular interior branch with $V_N^*=Z^*=0$, $A^*=A^{\rm eq}$, and the argument of the maximum in Eq.~\eqref{eq:scaf-gB} strictly positive, the material equilibrium calculation used
\begin{equation}
\bar X_A^*=N_c\log\!\left[
\frac{P_0A^{\rm eq}/(A^{\rm eq}+A_0)}{\Theta(E^*)}
\right],
\qquad
\Theta(E)=d_A(1+gd_J)+qE(1+\psi gd_J),
\label{eq:scaf-XAstar}
\end{equation}
subject to $P_0A^{\rm eq}/(A^{\rm eq}+A_0)>\Theta(E^*)$. The term $\Theta$ retains a useful stage-structured distinction. At fixed $E$, recruitment suppression ($\psi=0$) contributes $qE$ to the equilibrium drag, whereas adult-selective removal ($\psi=1$) contributes $qE(1+gd_J)$ because replacing removed adults requires additional recruitment that also compensates juvenile mortality during maturation. This is an algebraic interpretation within the displayed stage balance, not evidence that the two pressure channels are empirically interchangeable.

If
\[
h_0=1-\frac{E^*}{E_{\max}},
\qquad
g_0=1-e^{-K_C^*/K_0},
\]
the reported effort-law linear coefficients were
\begin{equation}
C_Z=h_0g_0\frac{\eta E^*}{\Delta_{\rm ref}},
\qquad
C_K=\frac{\mu_EE^*}{K_0}\frac{1-g_0}{g_0}.
\label{eq:scaf-C}
\end{equation}
Here $C_Z$ is the local sensitivity of effort growth to the delayed decline signal and is multiplied by the deployable-capital gate $g_0$. The positive coefficient $C_K$ is the local sensitivity to $K_C$ after the interior-equilibrium identity has been used; it becomes a damping pathway only through its coupling to the separate $K_C$ dynamics, not by its sign alone. The apparent factor $1/g_0$ cannot be extrapolated to $g_0=0$, where the assumed regular positive interior branch and the algebra used to obtain Eq.~\eqref{eq:scaf-C} need not persist.

These formulas are conditional on a regular interior equilibrium, including $0<g_0<1$; they do not resolve the closure or boundary limitations identified next. Moreover, an interior equilibrium with $Z^*=0$ and $E^*>0$ must satisfy
\begin{equation}
h_0g_0\left(\delta_0-\eta\frac{(E^*)^2}{E_{\max}}\right)=\mu_EE^*>0,
\label{eq:scaf-effort-bound}
\end{equation}
and hence
\[
E^*<\sqrt{\frac{\delta_0E_{\max}}{\eta}}.
\]
For the baseline values $\delta_0=0.3$, $E_{\max}=1$, and $\eta=0.914$, the upper bound is approximately $0.573$. This is not a failure of boundary invariance: Eq.~\eqref{eq:scaf-E} gives $\dot E=\delta_0(1-e^{-K_C/K_0})\ge0$ at $E=0$ and $\dot E=-\mu_EE_{\max}<0$ at $E=E_{\max}$. It is instead a restrictive equilibrium consequence of placing the linear loss outside the multiplicative gate. Moving loss terms would define a different model and requires renewed analysis rather than being an algebraic correction.

\subsection{Formal cancellation and admissibility limitations}

Summing Eqs.~\eqref{eq:scaf-XA}--\eqref{eq:scaf-G} gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}(\bar X_A+X_J+P+U+A+G)=0.
\label{eq:scaf-cancel}
\end{equation}
This is an algebraic cancellation only. It does not prove forward invariance of the six material states or physical admissibility of every term.

In particular, Eq.~\eqref{eq:scaf-G} is not donor limited. At $G=0$ and $A<A^{\rm eq}$ it gives $\dot G<0$. A physically admissible formulation must replace the fixed-target exchange by separate non-negative donor-limited fluxes $e_{GA}(G,A)$ and $e_{AG}(A,G)$ satisfying $e_{GA}(0,A)=0$ and $e_{AG}(0,G)=0$.

The variance boundary is also not invariant. Equation~\eqref{eq:scaf-V} gives
\[
\dot V_N\big|_{V_N=0}=-2q\bar X_A\operatorname{Cov}(E,\bar X_A),
\]
which can be negative. Moreover, the covariance is not determined by the ten displayed states. Thus the variance dynamics are both unclosed and not guaranteed to be realizable by a non-negative spatial distribution.

Equation~\eqref{eq:scaf-K} contains output $Q$ without a displayed state equation or constitutive closure. The broader production function cannot silently supply the omission because it depends on capital, labour, productivity, and service states that are absent from this ten-state list. Likewise, $\operatorname{Cov}(E,\bar X_A)$ is not a functional of the displayed state. A prescribed time path for either quantity would produce a non-autonomous problem, while constant or state-dependent closures would produce different autonomous systems and different linearisations. The ten equations therefore do not determine a unique autonomous DDE or characteristic quasi-polynomial.

The memory $Z$ is signed because its input $-\dot{\bar X}_A$ changes sign; it is an informational decline-rate filter, not a conserved non-negative material stock. Finally, the maximum in Eq.~\eqref{eq:scaf-gB} is nonsmooth on its switching surface. The interior equilibrium and characteristic linearisation apply only while that surface is not active; a trajectory or branch that reaches it requires piecewise-smooth analysis or a separately declared smooth approximation.

\subsection{Status of spectral calculations and lower-dimensional relations}

A numerical proposition requires a registered model that determines its vector field and characteristic equation. The displayed template does not meet that standard: different choices for $Q$ and the covariance closure change the equilibrium and Jacobian, while donor-limiting the geological exchange and changing the birth-floor branch can change both local and global dynamics. Consequently, values associated with an elevated-forcing calculation---a crossing near $\tau^*\approx43$ and a period near 263 time units---are not numerical propositions of this article. They are retained only as reproduction targets pending recovery and registration of the constitutive closures actually used. This status decision follows from the incomplete mathematical specification, not from any inference about whether a computation was executed.

These entries provide targets against which a closure-complete reconstruction can be checked or falsified. They do not specify the missing $Q$ and covariance rules, the complete parameter vector and histories, the characteristic-root algorithm, or numerical tolerances. A registered reconstruction must supply those items and may confirm, revise, or reject the recorded outputs.

The reported sign $d\operatorname{Re}\lambda/d\tau<0$, if recovered at a simple crossing under a complete registered closure, describes a stabilizing local crossing: the tracked conjugate pair moves from the right half-plane to the left as $\tau$ increases. Subject to the absence of other unstable roots, the equilibrium is therefore locally unstable just below and locally stable just above that crossing. Such a result cannot support language asserting that increasing delay creates oscillatory instability. A full stability statement additionally requires an unstable-root count and treatment of the nonsmooth switching surface.

The template's effort law, signed memory, stage cascade, boundary structure, and omitted architecture modules differ from the lower-dimensional model families. No specialization, projection, or singular limit is known to recover those models, so the template cannot establish persistence of their periodic-orbit folds. Conversely, its inadmissibility does not invalidate the registered, model-specific calculations in the lower-dimensional companion papers.

\paragraph{Cross-model synthesis.}
The companion results support a narrower conclusion than a universal delay-instability thesis. The two-crossing window is parameter-restricted in the ungated three-state core; the typed primitive-flux baseline has no local Hopf over its tested delay interval and lies near a response-gain boundary; sampled review removes the tested cod-class continuous-delay band; and the retrospective search finds no unconfounded field case. The ten-state template contributes no additional spectral proposition until it is closed and re-registered. In this operational sense, delay-amplified extractive instability is architecture- and calibration-contingent in the registered models. This synthesis is not a structural impossibility theorem: changing response gain produces Hopf pairs in the primitive-flux model, and the registered three-state models retain their own numerical branches. The appropriate general hypothesis concerns gain, phase, observation, and controller architecture, not a portable calendar-year threshold.

Before application, the geological exchange must be donor-limited, the variance equation must receive a realizable closure, $Q$ must be defined, and the information and governance operators must be declared. Calibration from spawning-stock biomass and fishing mortality alone would remain underidentified: juvenile abundance, recruitment, maturation, natural mortality, selectivity, active-pool measurements, spatial variance/covariance, capital/exergy, and governance timing are also required. Application should therefore begin with a minimum module set and measured observables rather than fit all ten states from two series.

\section{Empirical hypotheses for the future theory}

The cross-model synthesis identifies architecture-specific distinctions that can be tested without treating the ten-state scaffold as a validated field model. A general architecture remains scientific only if restricted models yield discriminating observations. Candidate empirical hypotheses include:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item independently measured assessment-to-deployment lag predicts phase relationships between decline signals and realised extraction, after process confounders are controlled;
    \item component safety cannot be reconstructed from declared aggregate indicators in systems with empirically demonstrated observational equivalence;
    \item responsive extractive and protective controllers with the same review period generate opposite effort--stock phase relations;
    \item models with explicit juvenile compartments outperform adult-only models when recruitment suppression is the dominant pressure;
    \item spatially aggregated predictions fail systematically where measured variance places the nonlinear moment correction outside its uncertainty bound;
    \item exergy/capacity constraints alter deployment and threshold risk in directions predicted before fitting the outcome;
    \item under a preregistered deficit distribution and componentwise loss, compensatory indicators exhibit misclassification or undetected-violation risk predicted from their observation fibres;
    \item in a declared polycentric controller class, the effect of delay heterogeneity on phase margin follows the sign predicted from the identified interaction network rather than a presumed universal stabilizing effect.
\end{enumerate}

Each hypothesis requires a registered minimal model, data-generating process, estimator, and falsification rule. The full architecture should never be fitted merely because a smaller model fails.

\section{Development sequence}

A disciplined programme should proceed in the following order:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item freeze the typed ledger, primitive fluxes, units, and donor-boundary conditions;
    \item define service feasibility and safe/justice constraints;
    \item declare the observation process, assessment operator, information pattern, and causal policy class;
    \item register protective, extractive, and fixed governance classes with command--deployment separation;
    \item establish well-posedness, forward invariance, and finite-time sampled limits in a minimal model;
    \item compute robust viability only after the disturbance class and information structure are fixed;
    \item close or replace the ten-state template's output, covariance, geological-exchange, and switching laws before assigning any spectral proposition to it;
    \item propose each specialization, projection, approximation, or singular limit separately, then prove its domain and error bound rather than assert a commutative reduction lattice;
    \item add stage, spatial, exergy, network, and justice modules only when a claim requires them;
    \item run prospective empirical tests and closed-loop management strategy evaluations (MSEs), promoting conjectures only after their assumptions and proofs are complete.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

A credible research architecture for sustainability begins by making physical types, service possibilities, observations, governance signs, uncertainty classes, policy information, and safe sets explicit, then proving results within stated domains and constructing explicit maps among restricted models. The companion studies supply accounting, delayed-feedback, sampled-governance, and empirical components. The present article adds exact observation-fibre and local-horizon results together with a bounded theorem and conjecture agenda; it does not assert a computed robust viability kernel, persistence of delayed periodic-orbit folds under vector coupling, a validated ten-state field model, or empirical generality. The ten-state exercise instead demonstrates a methodological point: formal cancellation does not establish state admissibility, and an incomplete constitutive closure cannot support a unique spectral proposition. Physical reformulation, closure registration, identifiability analysis, and explicit reductions are prerequisites to any broader theory or application.
