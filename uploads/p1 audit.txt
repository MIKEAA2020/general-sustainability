### 2. Mathematical, Conceptual, and Theoretical Flaws

#### A. The "Chattering" / Time-Sharing Fallacy (Section 4.11 & Proof of Proposition 9)
* **Text (p. 15, Proposition 9 Proof; p. 17, top):**
  * *“A sequential policy that spends any positive time on FAST visits every $s_1$ in $[s_1 - 2, s_1]$ and any positive time on SLOW visits every $s_2$ in $[s_2 - 2, s_2]$...”* (p. 28)
  * *“In the language of relaxed controls, the collapse theorem is a statement about the convexified (relaxed) action program and the converse a statement about chattering: sliding among plans at full strength does not buy what fractional allocation does.”* (p. 17)
* **The Flaw:**
  1. In continuous-time dynamics, if the state rates are bounded ($\dot{s}_1 = -4$), spending an infinitesimal time $\Delta t > 0$ on FAST can only change $s_1$ by at most $4\Delta t$. It is physically and mathematically impossible for an infinitesimal duration of FAST to cause the trajectory to plunge across the entire interval $[s_1 - 2, s_1]$.
  2. In control theory, **chattering** (infinitesimally fast switching between extreme vector fields $u_{\text{FAST}}$ and $u_{\text{SLOW}}$) is well known (via Filippov’s theorem and the classical relaxed control theorem of Warga and Gamkrelidze) to produce an effective state trajectory that **identically tracks the convex combination** $\delta u_{\text{FAST}} + (1-\delta) u_{\text{SLOW}}$.
  3. Claiming that chattering fails to close the gap while continuous convexification does is an artifact of defining sequential time-sharing as the static set union of whole, indivisible trajectory tubes $\text{Tube}(\text{FAST}) \cup \text{Tube}(\text{SLOW})$. Asserting that this is a property of "chattering" or temporal sharing in dynamic systems is a severe theoretical misrepresentation.

#### B. Error-Bound Modulus Conflation (Section 5.6, Page 22)
* **Text (p. 22):** *“Along the resource route the minimal increment is the affine function $f(z) = (1 - x)_+$, whose error-bound modulus (Fabian et al., 2010) is identically 1 on $\{x < 1\}$; the rescue threshold $\kappa^*$ therefore coincides with the error-bound modulus along this route.”*
* **The Flaw:** 
  * An **error-bound modulus** is a conditioning constant $\tau > 0$ (here, $\tau = 1$) relating distance-to-feasibility to constraint violation: $\text{dist}(x, S) \le \tau [g(x)]_+$.
  * The rescue threshold $\kappa^*(z) = (1 - x)$ is a **state-dependent function** taking values in $(0, 1]$ for $x \in [0, 1)$. 
  * A varying function $\kappa^*(x) = 1 - x$ cannot "coincide" with a constant modulus of $1$. The author has conflated the *distance function* with the *modulus*.
