# 1. The Core Theoretical Breakthrough: The Minimax Epistemic Duality Theorem

### The Fundamental Duality
In classical viability under full information, viability of state $x$ is the primal condition:
$$\exists u \in U(x) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \forall d \in D(x), \; \forall j \in J(x), \quad \nabla q_j(x) \cdot f(x, u, d) \ge 0.$$

Under incomplete observation, the regulator does not know $x$; it only knows an information set (belief) $B \subseteq V$ generated by the observation record (e.g., an observation fibre $B = O^{-1}(y)$). Viability requires a **single, common control** $u \in U^B(B) = \bigcap_{x \in B} U(x)$ that is simultaneously safe for all compatible pairs $(x, d)$.

Hitherto, the papers treated the emptiness of this set via separate, disparate tools (Farkas' lemma for polyhedra, Helly's theorem for bounds, Dini derivatives for exit, POMDP alpha-vectors for probabilities). 

The following theorem proves that **all of them are manifestations of one dual variational principle**.

---

### Theorem (Universal Minimax Epistemic Duality)
Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be the state space, $V = \{x \in X : q_j(x) \ge 0, \, j=1, \dots, m\}$ a closed safe set defined by $C^1$ constraint functions, $D: X \rightrightarrows \mathbb{R}^p$ a compact-valued, upper semicontinuous disturbance correspondence, and $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^k$ a compact, convex control set. 

Consider dynamics affine in the control:
$$f(x, u, d) = f_0(x, d) + G(x) u,$$
where $f_0$ is continuous and $G(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}$ is continuous.

Let $B \subseteq V$ be a compact information set (an observation fibre or estimation tube). Define the active boundary-disturbance bundle of $B$:
$$\mathcal{K}(B) := \left\{ (x, j, d) : x \in B, \; j \in \{1, \dots, m\} \text{ s.t. } q_j(x) = 0, \; d \in D(x) \right\}.$$
Assume $\mathcal{K}(B)$ is compact and nonempty.

For any $(x, j, d) \in \mathcal{K}(B)$ and $u \in U$, define the inward constraint drift:
$$\psi(x, j, d; u) := \nabla q_j(x) \cdot \left[ f_0(x, d) + G(x) u \right].$$

Then:
$$\max_{u \in U} \min_{(x, j, d) \in \mathcal{K}(B)} \psi(x, j, d; u) = \min_{\mu \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))} \max_{u \in U} \int_{\mathcal{K}(B)} \psi(x, j, d; u) \, d\mu(x, j, d),$$
where $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))$ is the space of Borel probability measures on $\mathcal{K}(B)$ endowed with the weak-$*$ topology.

Consequently, the common safe-action set is empty,
$$\mathcal{R}_V^B(B) := \bigcap_{x \in B} \mathcal{R}_V(x) = \emptyset,$$
**if and only if** there exists an adversarial probability measure $\mu^* \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))$ and a strictly positive margin $\varepsilon > 0$ such that:
$$\max_{u \in U} \mathbb{E}_{(x, j, d) \sim \mu^*} \left[ \nabla q_j(x) \cdot (f_0(x, d) + G(x) u) \right] \le -\varepsilon < 0. \tag{$\star$}$$

Furthermore, by Carathéodory’s theorem on the convex hull in $\mathbb{R}^k$, the adversarial measure $\mu^*$ can **always be chosen as a finite atomic measure supported on at most $k+1$ points**:
$$\mu^* = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} \lambda_i^* \delta_{(x_i, j_i, d_i)}, \quad \lambda_i^* \ge 0, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} \lambda_i^* = 1,$$
where $k = \dim(U)$ is the dimension of the control space.

---

### Proof of the Theorem
1. **Compactness and Continuity:** Because $U \subset \mathbb{R}^k$ is compact and convex, and $\mathcal{K}(B)$ is compact, the space of Radon probability measures $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))$ is compact and convex under the weak-$*$ topology (Banach-Alaoglu Theorem).
2. **Bilinearity / Concavity-Convexity:** For any fixed $(x, j, d)$, the mapping $u \mapsto \psi(x, j, d; u)$ is affine in $u$ because $f(x, u, d)$ is affine in $u$. Therefore, the expected drift functional:

# 1. The Core Theoretical Breakthrough: The Minimax Epistemic Duality Theorem

### The Fundamental Duality
In classical viability under full information, viability of state $x$ is the primal condition:
$$\exists u \in U(x) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \forall d \in D(x), \; \forall j \in J(x), \quad \nabla q_j(x) \cdot f(x, u, d) \ge 0.$$

Under incomplete observation, the regulator does not know $x$; it only knows an information set (belief) $B \subseteq V$ generated by the observation record (e.g., an observation fibre $B = O^{-1}(y)$). Viability requires a **single, common control** $u \in U^B(B) = \bigcap_{x \in B} U(x)$ that is simultaneously safe for all compatible pairs $(x, d)$.

Hitherto, the papers treated the emptiness of this set via separate, disparate tools (Farkas' lemma for polyhedra, Helly's theorem for bounds, Dini derivatives for exit, POMDP alpha-vectors for probabilities). 

The following theorem proves that **all of them are manifestations of one dual variational principle**.

---

### Theorem (Universal Minimax Epistemic Duality)
Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be the state space, $V = \{x \in X : q_j(x) \ge 0, \, j=1, \dots, m\}$ a closed safe set defined by $C^1$ constraint functions, $D: X \rightrightarrows \mathbb{R}^p$ a compact-valued, upper semicontinuous disturbance correspondence, and $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^k$ a compact, convex control set. 

Consider dynamics affine in the control:
$$f(x, u, d) = f_0(x, d) + G(x) u,$$
where $f_0$ is continuous and $G(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}$ is continuous.

Let $B \subseteq V$ be a compact information set (an observation fibre or estimation tube). Define the active boundary-disturbance bundle of $B$:
$$\mathcal{K}(B) := \left\{ (x, j, d) : x \in B, \; j \in \{1, \dots, m\} \text{ s.t. } q_j(x) = 0, \; d \in D(x) \right\}.$$
Assume $\mathcal{K}(B)$ is compact and nonempty.

For any $(x, j, d) \in \mathcal{K}(B)$ and $u \in U$, define the inward constraint drift:
$$\psi(x, j, d; u) := \nabla q_j(x) \cdot \left[ f_0(x, d) + G(x) u \right].$$

Then:
$$\max_{u \in U} \min_{(x, j, d) \in \mathcal{K}(B)} \psi(x, j, d; u) = \min_{\mu \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))} \max_{u \in U} \int_{\mathcal{K}(B)} \psi(x, j, d; u) \, d\mu(x, j, d),$$
where $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))$ is the space of Borel probability measures on $\mathcal{K}(B)$ endowed with the weak-$*$ topology.

Consequently, the common safe-action set is empty,
$$\mathcal{R}_V^B(B) := \bigcap_{x \in B} \mathcal{R}_V(x) = \emptyset,$$
**if and only if** there exists an adversarial probability measure $\mu^* \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))$ and a strictly positive margin $\varepsilon > 0$ such that:
$$\max_{u \in U} \mathbb{E}_{(x, j, d) \sim \mu^*} \left[ \nabla q_j(x) \cdot (f_0(x, d) + G(x) u) \right] \le -\varepsilon < 0. \tag{$\star$}$$

Furthermore, by Carathéodory’s theorem on the convex hull in $\mathbb{R}^k$, the adversarial measure $\mu^*$ can **always be chosen as a finite atomic measure supported on at most $k+1$ points**:
$$\mu^* = \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} \lambda_i^* \delta_{(x_i, j_i, d_i)}, \quad \lambda_i^* \ge 0, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{k+1} \lambda_i^* = 1,$$
where $k = \dim(U)$ is the dimension of the control space.

### Proof of the Theorem
1. **Compactness and Continuity:** Because $U \subset \mathbb{R}^k$ is compact and convex, and $\mathcal{K}(B)$ is compact, the space of Radon probability measures $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))$ is compact and convex under the weak-$*$ topology (Banach-Alaoglu Theorem).
2. **Bilinearity / Concavity-Convexity:** For any fixed $(x, j, d)$, the mapping $u \mapsto \psi(x, j, d; u)$ is affine in $u$ because $f(x, u, d)$ is affine in $u$. Therefore, the expected drift functional:
   $$\Phi(u, \mu) := \int_{\mathcal{K}(B)} \psi(x, j, d; u) \, d\mu(x, j, d)$$
   is continuous and affine (hence concave) in $u \in U$, and continuous and linear (hence convex) in $\mu \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))$.
3. **Minimax Equality:** By Sion’s Minimax Theorem (or the Ky Fan Minimax Theorem), we can commute the max and min:
   $$\max_{u \in U} \inf_{\mu \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))} \Phi(u, \mu) = \min_{\mu \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))} \max_{u \in U} \Phi(u, \mu).$$
   Since the infimum of a linear functional over $\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(B))$ is attained at extreme points (Dirac measures $\delta_{(x, j, d)}$), $\inf_{\mu} \Phi(u, \mu) = \min_{(x, j, d) \in \mathcal{K}(B)} \psi(x, j, d; u)$. This establishes the equality.
4. **Necessary and Sufficient Obstruction:** The primal condition for nonviability is that every $u \in U$ is unsafe for at least one active constraint, i.e., $\max_{u \in U} \min_{(x, j, d)} \psi(x, j, d; u) < 0$. By the minimax equality, this holds if and only if the dual value is strictly negative, which guarantees the existence of $\mu^*$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ satisfying $(\star)$.
5. **Finite Sparsity ($k+1$ Witnesses):** For any $(x, j, d)$, define the drift gradient vector $v(x, j, d) := G(x)^\top \nabla q_j(x) \in \mathbb{R}^k$ and scalar $c(x, j, d) := \nabla q_j(x) \cdot f_0(x, d) \in \mathbb{R}$. The condition $\Phi(u, \mu) \le -\varepsilon$ for all $u \in U$ is a statement about the aggregated affine function:
   $$u \mapsto \bar{c}_\mu + \bar{v}_\mu^\top u, \quad \text{where } \bar{v}_\mu = \int v \, d\mu \in \mathbb{R}^k, \; \bar{c}_\mu = \int c \, d\mu \in \mathbb{R}.$$
   The image set $\{ (v(x, j, d), c(x, j, d)) : (x, j, d) \in \mathcal{K}(B) \}$ lies in $\mathbb{R}^{k+1}$. # 4. From Obstruction to Synthesis: The Minimum Viable Monitoring (MVM) Formulas

Instead of merely certifying that a system is doomed, we invert the Minimax Duality Theorem into an **analytical design calculus for monitoring networks**.

An environmental or industrial agency has three design levers:
1. **Sampling Cadence:** How often to measure ($T_{\text{obs}}$).
2. **Sensor Precision / Partition Resolution:** How coarse the observation map $O(x)$ can be ($\delta_{\text{sensor}}$).
3. **Sensor Bias Calibration:** How much systematic bias $b$ can be tolerated.

Using the dual margin $\varepsilon(\mu^*) := -\max_{u \in U} \mathbb{E}_{\mu^*}[\nabla q \cdot f]$, we obtain **exact, closed-form design specifications**:

### Design Formula 1: Maximum Safe Inspection Interval ($T_{\text{obs}}^*$)
To prevent the delayed-information obstruction, the inspection period $T_{\text{obs}}$ must satisfy:
$$T_{\text{obs}} < T_{\text{obs}}^* := \inf_{\mu \in \mathcal{P}(B_0 \times D)} \frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[q(x_0)]}{\max_{u \in U} \left( -\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\nabla q(x) \cdot f(x, u, d)] \right)}.$$
*Human Meaning:* If an agency samples more slowly than $T_{\text{obs}}^*$, there exists an adversarial trajectory that breaches the environmental floor before the next report is filed, irrespective of the controller.

### Design Formula 2: Coarsest Allowable Sensor Quantization ($\delta_{\max}^*$)
Let the state space be monitored by a quantized grid with resolution $\delta$ (so fibres have diameter $\|x - x'\| \le \delta$).
Let $L_f$ be the Lipschitz constant of the vector fields $f(x, u, d)$ and $L_q$ the Lipschitz constant of $\nabla q(x)$.
The dual margin degrades at most linearly with fibre diameter:
$$\varepsilon(B) \ge \varepsilon_{\text{full}} - (L_q \|f\|_\infty + L_f \|\nabla q\|_\infty) \delta.$$
To prevent the common-action obstruction ($\varepsilon(B) > 0$), the sensor resolution must be strictly finer than:
$$\delta < \delta_{\max}^* := \frac{\varepsilon_{\text{full}}}{L_q \|f\|_\infty + L_f \|\nabla q\|_\infty},$$
where $\varepsilon_{\text{full}} = \min_{x \in \partial V} \max_{u \in U} \min_{d \in D} \nabla q(x) \cdot f(x, u, d) > 0$ is the classical full-information viability margin.
*Human Meaning:* This gives the exact number of bits/resolution required for an ecological indicator. Coarser indicators mathematically guarantee common-action collapse.

### Design Formula 3: The Anti-Greenwashing Aggregation Bound
Let $y = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i$ be a composite index aggregating $n$ natural stocks, each subject to individual floors $x_i \ge x_{i, \min}$.
