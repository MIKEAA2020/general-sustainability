### Error A1: Robust Tangency versus Strong Invariance

1. **Issue ID:** `A001-ERR-A1`
2. **Verdict:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` (Correct and restrict). The diagnosis in the review packet is correct: asserting that $\forall x \in \partial K, \, \exists u \in U(x), \, \forall w \in W(x), \, f(x,u,w) \in T_K(x)$ yields robust invariance (i.e., the existence of a single causal feedback $\pi$ such that all disturbances $w(\cdot)$ are contained in $K$) is false. The pointwise condition $\forall x, \exists u, \forall w$ is merely a necessary infinitesimal condition for a *discriminating kernel* (in the sense of Cardaliaguet, Quincampoix, and Saint-Pierre), whereas the standard Nagumo condition $\forall x, \forall w, \exists u$ characterizes the weaker *viability kernel under disturbance* (where $u$ may adapt instantaneously to the disturbance $w$).
3. **Corrected statement:**
   Let $X = \mathbb{R}^n$, let $K \subset X$ be non-empty, closed, and let $U: X \rightrightarrows \operatorname{Comp}(\mathbb{R}^m)$ and $W: X \rightrightarrows \operatorname{Comp}(\mathbb{R}^p)$ be upper semicontinuous set-valued maps with non-empty compact convex values. Let $f: X \times \mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^p \to X$ be continuous, affine in $(u, w)$, and locally Lipschitz in $x$. Define the continuous-time game dynamics $\dot{x}(t) = f(x(t), u(t), w(t))$.
   
   The set $K$ is **robustly strongly invariant under non-anticipative feedback** (i.e., $K = \operatorname{Disc}_f(K)$) if and only if:
   $$\forall x \in \partial K, \quad \exists u \in U(x) \quad \text{such that} \quad \forall w \in W(x), \quad f(x, u, w) \in T_K(x),$$
   where $T_K(x)$ is the Bouligand contingent cone to $K$ at $x$, provided $K$ is a Clarke regular set (e.g., prox-regular or convex) with a locally Lipschitz boundary.
   
   If $K$ is merely closed, the discriminating kernel $\operatorname{Disc}_f(K)$ is the largest closed subset $Q \subseteq K$ such that for every $x_0 \in Q$ and every non-anticipative strategy $\alpha: \mathcal{W} \to \mathcal{U}$ (in the sense of Varaiya–Roxin–Elliott–Kalton), the unique trajectory satisfies $x^{\alpha[w], w}(t) \in Q$ for all $t \ge 0$ and all measurable disturbances $w(t) \in W(x(t))$.
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - $K \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ closed, with non-empty interior.
   - $f(x, \cdot, w)$ convex/affine to prevent chattering gap.
   - Information pattern: Non-anticipative strategies $\alpha \in \Gamma$, where $\alpha[w_1](t) = \alpha[w_2](t)$ almost everywhere on $[0, \tau]$ whenever $w_1(s) = w_2(s)$ a.e. on $[0, \tau]$.
   - Hyperplane separation / Clarke regularity of $K$ at $\partial K$ to equate the contingent cone $T_K(x)$ with the Clarke tangent cone $C_K(x)$.
5. **Proof sketch:**
   - *Necessity:* Suppose $K$ is robustly invariant. Fix $x \in \partial K$. For any non-anticipative feedback $\pi(x) \in U(x)$, all disturbance trajectories $w(\cdot) \equiv w \in W(x)$ must satisfy $\lim_{h \downarrow 0} \operatorname{dist}(x + h f(x, \pi(x), w), K)/h = 0$, which implies $f(x, \pi(x), w) \in T_K(x)$ for all $w \in W(x)$.
   - *Sufficiency:* Consider the Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs (HJI) lower Hamiltonian $H^-(x, p) = \sup_{u \in U(x)} \inf_{w \in W(x)} \langle -p, f(x,u,w) \rangle$. By minimax theorem (Fan's Theorem) under convexity of $U(x), W(x)$ and bilinearity/affinity of $f$, $\sup_{u} \inf_{w} \langle p, f(x,u,w) \rangle = \inf_{w} \sup_{u} \langle p, f(x,u,w) \rangle$. For Clarke regular $K$, $T_K(x) = \{ v : \langle \eta, v \rangle \le 0, \, \forall \eta \in N_K^C(x) \}$. The condition $\exists u \in U(x), \forall w \in W(x), \langle \eta, f(x,u,w) \rangle \le 0$ guarantees that the directional derivative of the signed distance function $d_K(x)$ is non-positive for all disturbance realizations under the feedback selector $u(x)$, preventing boundary exit.
6. **Counterexample if assumptions are weakened:**
   Let $X = \mathbb{R}^2$, $K = \{ (x_1, x_2) : x_1 \ge 0, x_2 \ge 0 \}$. Let $\dot{x}_1 = u - w$, $\dot{x}_2 = -u + w$, with $U = [0, 1]$ and $W = [0, 1]$.
   At the boundary point $(0, 1) \in \partial K$, the condition requires $\dot{x}_1 \ge 0 \implies u \ge w$. To satisfy this $\forall w \in [0, 1]$, we must choose $u = 1$.
   At the boundary point $(1, 0) \in \partial K$, the condition requires $\dot{x}_2 \ge 0 \implies u \le w$. To satisfy this $\forall w \in [0, 1]$, we must choose $u = 0$.
   At the corner $(0, 0)$, we require simultaneously $u \ge w$ and $u \le w$ for all $w \in [0, 1]$, which forces $u = w$. But $u$ must be chosen independently of $w$ (since $u \in U(x)$ precedes $w$). No single $u \in [0, 1]$ satisfies $\forall w \in [0, 1], (u - w \ge 0 \land -u + w \ge 0)$. Thus the discriminating kernel is empty, even though the standard viability condition $\forall w, \exists u, f(x,u,w) \in T_K(x)$ holds everywhere.
7. **Recommended source status:** `Theorem` (replaces flawed statement in A001).
8. **Downstream consequences:** Replaces all viability claims in A001 Section 4 that conflated open-loop disturbance handling with feedback discriminating invariance. Sets the mathematical standard for Operator I.
9. **Relevant literature:** 
   - Cardaliaguet, P. (1996). *Non-local approach to target problems in differential games.* Advances in Differential Equations, 1(3), 329–352.
   - Cardaliaguet, P., Quincampoix, M., & Saint-Pierre, P. (1999). *Set-valued numerical analysis for optimal control and differential games.* Annals of the ISDG, 4, 177–247.
   - Aubin, J.-P. (2001). *Viability Theory.* Birkhäuser.
10. **Confidence level & unresolved questions:** High confidence (99%). Fully resolved.

### Error A2: Order-Minimal Proof Wording

1. **Issue ID:** `A001-ERR-A2`
2. **Verdict:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` (Correct and clarify). The text improperly asserted universal viability over all harvest policies from the viability of the minimal-harvest trajectory. Viability is an existential property ($\exists u(\cdot)$), while universal invariance ($\forall u(\cdot)$) is false whenever harvest can be chosen large enough to deplete the stock.
3. **Corrected statement:**
   Let the stock dynamics be $\dot{S} = G(S) - H$, where $G: \mathbb{R}_+ \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuously differentiable, strictly concave with $G(0) = G(C) = 0$, and $H \in [H_{\min}, H_{\max}]$ with $H_{\min} \ge 0$. The safe constraint set is $K = [S_{\min}, C]$ with $S_{\min} > 0$.
   
   Define the partial order on controls: $H_1 \preceq H_2 \iff H_1(t) \le H_2(t)$ a.e. $t \ge 0$.
   If the minimal control trajectory $H^*(t) \equiv H_{\min}$ generates a trajectory $S^*(t)$ that satisfies $S^*(t) \in K$ for all $t \ge 0$, then:
   $$\operatorname{Viab}_G(K) \neq \emptyset \quad \text{and} \quad S_0 \in \operatorname{Viab}_G(K) \iff S^*(t; S_0, H_{\min}) \in K \quad \forall t \ge 0.$$
   Furthermore, $\operatorname{Viab}_G(K) = [S^*, C]$, where $S^* = \inf \{ S \in [S_{\min}, C] : G(S) \ge H_{\min} \}$.
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - Dynamics $\dot{S} = G(S) - H$ are monotone with respect to the Kamke-Müller condition (trivially satisfied in 1D: $\partial \dot{S}/\partial H = -1 < 0$).
   - Admissible harvest set $\mathcal{H} = \{ H(\cdot) \text{ measurable} : H(t) \in [H_{\min}, H_{\max}] \}$.
5. **Proof sketch:**
   By the standard comparison theorem for 1D ODEs, for any admissible $H(t) \ge H_{\min}$, the solution satisfies $S(t; S_0, H) \le S(t; S_0, H_{\min})$ for all $t \ge 0$. Thus, if there exists *any* admissible control $\tilde{H}(t)$ keeping $S(t) \ge S_{\min}$, then $S(t; S_0, H_{\min}) \ge S(t; S_0, \tilde{H}) \ge S_{\min}$. Hence, existence of a viable path is strictly equivalent to viability of the lower-extremal path $H(t) \equiv H_{\min}$.
6. **Counterexample if assumptions are weakened:**
   If harvesting were non-monotone (e.g., dynamic depensation where unharvested biomass rots and suffocates the stock: $\dot{S} = G(S) - H - \alpha \max(0, S - S_{crit})$), the minimal harvest does not generate the maximal trajectory, and the lower extremal path can exit $K$ while an intermediate harvest remains viable.
7. **Recommended source status:** `Theorem`.
8. **Downstream consequences:** Restores formal correctness to all scalar bioeconomic threshold proofs in A001 and A002 without altering numerical thresholds.
9. **Relevant literature:**
   - Walter, W. (1970). *Differential and Integral Inequalities.* Springer-Verlag.
   - Clark, C. W. (2010). *Mathematical Bioeconomics: The Mathematics of Conservation.* John Wiley & Sons.
10. **Confidence level & unresolved questions:** High confidence (100%).

### Error A3: Pollution-Feedback Comparison Sign

1. **Issue ID:** `A001-ERR-A3`
2. **Verdict:** `DIAGNOSIS_MISTAKEN` in draft text / `PROOF_INCOMPLETE` in manuscript. The mathematical inequality $g(S, K) \ge g(S, K_{\max})$ for $g_K \le 0$ and $K \le K_{\max}$ is standard; the error in the manuscript was a sign inversion in the differential inequality step ($\dot{S} \le \dots$ instead of $\ge$).
3. **Corrected statement:**
   Let stock-pollution dynamics be governed by:
   $$\dot{S} = F(S, K) - H, \qquad \dot{K} = P(S, H) - \delta K,$$
   with $S \in [S_{\min}, S_{\max}]$, $K \in [0, K_{\max}]$. Assume $\frac{\partial F}{\partial K} \le 0$ on the domain.
   Then for all $(S, K) \in [S_{\min}, S_{\max}] \times [0, K_{\max}]$, we have:
   $$F(S, K) \ge F(S, K_{\max}).$$
   Consequently, the 1D comparison system $\dot{z} = F(z, K_{\max}) - H_{\min}$ provides a strict inner bound for the viable domain of $S$:
   $$\operatorname{Viab}_{1D}([S_{\min}, S_{\max}]) \subseteq \operatorname{Proj}_S \left( \operatorname{Viab}_{2D}([S_{\min}, S_{\max}] \times [0, K_{\max}]) \right).$$
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - $\partial F/\partial K \le 0$ globally on the safe set $K_{\text{safe}}$.
   - $P(S, H) \ge 0$, $\delta > 0$, ensuring forward invariance of $K \ge 0$.
5. **Proof sketch:**
   Let $z(t)$ satisfy $\dot{z}(t) = F(z(t), K_{\max}) - H_{\min}$ with $z(0) = S(0)$. For any coupled trajectory $(S(t), K(t))$ with $K(t) \le K_{\max}$ and $H(t) \equiv H_{\min}$, we have:
   $$\dot{S}(t) = F(S(t), K(t)) - H_{\min} \ge F(S(t), K_{\max}) - H_{\min}.$$
   By standard ODE comparison theorems (Kamke's Theorem), $S(t) \ge z(t)$ for all $t \ge 0$. If $z(t) \ge S_{\min}$ for all $t$, then $S(t) \ge S_{\min}$ for all $t$.
6. **Counterexample if assumptions are weakened:**
   If $\partial F/\partial K > 0$ (e.g., nutrient enrichment acting as a fertilizer at low levels), $F(S, K_{\max}) > F(S, K)$, and the lower comparison system bound reverses, invalidating the decoupling certificate.
7. **Recommended source status:** `Theorem`.
8. **Downstream consequences:** Clarifies the bounding lemma for coupled ecological-pollution models in Article 001 and enables decoupled domain decompositions.
9. **Relevant literature:**
   - Smith, H. L. (1995). *Monotone Dynamical Systems: An Introduction to the Theory of Competitive and Cooperative Systems.* AMS Mathematical Surveys and Monographs, Vol. 41.
10. **Confidence level & unresolved questions:** High confidence (100%).

### Error A4: Capital-Consumption Kernel

1. **Issue ID:** `A001-ERR-A4`
2. **Verdict:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` (Correct and restrict). In the Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans / Dasgupta-Heal viability model, the text claimed that any consumption path $c(t) \ge c_{\min}$ preserves the capital kernel. This is false: choosing $c(t) \gg f(k(t))$ rapidly depletes capital $k(t)$ below $k_{\min}$. Viability requires an *existential* control, not universal control.
3. **Corrected statement:**
   Let capital dynamics be $\dot{k} = f(k) - \delta k - c$, with safe set $K = [k_{\min}, k_{\max}]$, where $k_{\min} > 0$, $f: \mathbb{R}_+ \to \mathbb{R}_+$ is strictly concave, $f(0) = 0$, $f'(0) > \delta$, and consumption is constrained to $c \in [c_{\min}, c_{\max}]$ with $c_{\min} > 0$.
   
   The viability kernel $\operatorname{Viab}(K)$ is non-empty if and only if:
   $$\max_{k \in [k_{\min}, k_{\max}]} (f(k) - \delta k) \ge c_{\min}.$$
   When non-empty, $\operatorname{Viab}(K) = [k^*, k_{\max}]$, where $k^*$ is the smallest solution in $[k_{\min}, k_{\max}]$ of $f(k^*) - \delta k^* \ge c_{\min}$ if $f(k_{\min}) - \delta k_{\min} < c_{\min}$, or $k^* = k_{\min}$ if $f(k_{\min}) - \delta k_{\min} \ge c_{\min}$.
   
   A consumption policy $c(t)$ preserves viability if and only if $c(t) \le f(k(t)) - \delta k(t)$ whenever $k(t) = k^*$.
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - Standard neoclassical production function $f \in C^1$, $f'' < 0$.
   - Admissible controls $\mathcal{U} = \{ c(\cdot) \in L^\infty(\mathbb{R}_+, [c_{\min}, c_{\max}]) \}$.
5. **Proof sketch:**
   The boundary point $k_{\min}$ has outward normal pointing in the $-k$ direction. The contingent cone is $T_K(k_{\min}) = [0, \infty)$. Viability tangency requires $\exists c \in [c_{\min}, c_{\max}]$ such that $f(k_{\min}) - \delta k_{\min} - c \ge 0$. If $f(k_{\min}) - \delta k_{\min} < c_{\min}$, no control at $k_{\min}$ can prevent exit. Thus the boundary of the viability kernel is the stationary point $k^*$ where $f(k^*) - \delta k^* = c_{\min}$. By concavity of $f(k) - \delta k$, the set of viable states is the connected closed interval $[k^*, k_{\max}]$.
6. **Counterexample if assumptions are weakened:**
   If $c(t) = c_{\max} > f(k) - \delta k$ is applied at $k(t) = k^*$, $\dot{k}(t) < 0$ and $k(t)$ exits $[k_{\min}, k_{\max}]$ in finite time $t \le (k^* - k_{\min})/(c_{\max} - f(k^*) + \delta k^*)$.
7. **Recommended source status:** `Theorem`.
8. **Downstream consequences:** Replaces flawed existential-vs-universal assertions in A001 economic sections.
9. **Relevant literature:**
   - Heal, G. (1998). *Valuing the Future: Economic Theory and Sustainability.* Columbia University Press.
   - Martinet, V. (2011). *Economic Theory and Sustainable Development.* Routledge.
10. **Confidence level & unresolved questions:** High confidence (100%).

### Error A5: Generic Non-Polyhedrality of Viability Kernels

1. **Issue ID:** `A001-ERR-A5`
2. **Verdict:** `PROOF_INCOMPLETE` (Demote to Conjecture or state as Restricted Analytical Theorem). The proof in Article 001 attempted to prove generic non-polyhedrality of viability kernels for nonlinear systems via an informal algebraic elimination argument. This argument is insufficient because smooth nonlinear boundaries can sometimes generate piecewise flat/polyhedral viability boundaries under saturation or linear constraints.
3. **Corrected statement:**
   **Theorem (Curvature of Viability Boundaries under Nonlinear Drift):**
   Let $\dot{x} = f(x) + g(x)u$, $u \in U \subset \mathbb{R}^m$ compact convex. Let $K = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^n : h_i(x) \le 0, \, i=1,\dots,p \}$ be a polyhedral domain ($h_i(x) = a_i^\top x - b_i$). 
   Suppose that on an active constraint facet $\Gamma_i = \{ x \in K : a_i^\top x = b_i \}$, the drift satisfies the Lie bracket non-degeneracy condition:
   $$\exists x_0 \in \Gamma_i, \quad a_i^\top \nabla f(x_0) v \neq 0 \quad \text{for some } v \in \operatorname{ker}(a_i^\top),$$
   and the optimal control $u^*(x) = \arg\max_{u \in U} a_i^\top (f(x) + g(x)u)$ is unique and $C^2$ in a neighborhood of $x_0$.
   Then the boundary $\partial \operatorname{Viab}(K)$ separating viable from non-viable initial states in that neighborhood is not a flat hyperplane and possesses non-zero Gaussian curvature.
   
   *Global genericity across all $C^k$ vector fields remains an open Conjecture.*
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - Vector fields $f, g \in C^2(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{R}^n)$.
   - Second-order transversality / non-vanishing acceleration along the boundary manifold: $L_f^2 h_i(x_0) \neq 0$.
5. **Proof sketch:**
   The boundary $\partial \operatorname{Viab}(K)$ is generated by semi-permeable surfaces (Isaac's surfaces) whose normal $\eta(x)$ satisfies the adjoint ODE $\dot{\eta} = -(\nabla f(x) + \nabla g(x)u^*)^\top \eta$. The second fundamental form of the boundary hypersurface $\Sigma$ at $x_0$ is given by $I\!I(v, v) = \langle \nabla_v \eta(x_0), v \rangle$. By the non-degeneracy condition, $\nabla \eta(x_0)$ is non-zero on the tangent space $T_{x_0} \Sigma$, which implies that the principal curvatures do not all vanish identically. Thus, $\Sigma$ is strictly curved and cannot be polyhedral.
6. **Counterexample if assumptions are weakened:**
   Let $\dot{x}_1 = x_2$, $\dot{x}_2 = u$, with $K = \{ (x_1, x_2) : x_1 \le 0 \}$, $U = [-1, 1]$. Here the viability boundary is $x_1 + \frac{1}{2} x_2^2 \le 0$ for $x_2 > 0$, which is parabolic (non-polyhedral). But if $\dot{x}_1 = -x_1$, $\dot{x}_2 = -x_2 + u$, $K = \{ x_1 \le 1, x_2 \le 1 \}$, the kernel is exactly the polyhedron $K$ itself, because the drift is inward-pointing along all faces.
7. **Recommended source status:** Demote the general claim to `Conjecture`; retain the local curvature result as a `Proposition`.
8. **Downstream consequences:** Prevents unjustified overclaiming regarding the topological complexity of all general viability kernels, while retaining the essential insight that linear programming / polyhedral approximations cannot exact-match general nonlinear kernels.
9. **Relevant literature:**
   - Cardaliaguet, P. (1997). *Regularity of the boundary of the viability kernel and the capture basin.* European Journal of Control, 3(3), 209–218.
   - Bardi, M., & Capuzzo-Dolcetta, I. (1997). *Optimal Control and Viscosity Solutions of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman Equations.* Birkhäuser.
10. **Confidence level & unresolved questions:** High confidence in local proposition (95%); global genericity proof is non-trivial and correctly classified as Conjecture.

### Error A6: Rosen Uniqueness with Heterogeneous Damages

1. **Issue ID:** `A001-ERR-A6`
2. **Verdict:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` (Correct and restrict). The source assumed that diagonal strict concavity (Rosen's condition) holds for asymmetric games by simply substituting a single average second derivative. In the presence of heterogeneous damage/cost parameters, the symmetric condition fails; Rosen's condition requires the negative definiteness of the symmetrized pseudogradient Jacobian matrix with positive diagonal weights $r_i$.
3. **Corrected statement:**
   **Theorem (Existence and Uniqueness of Coupled Commons Equilibrium):**
   Let $N$ agents have action sets $E_i = [0, E_{i, \max}] \subset \mathbb{R}_+$, with payoffs:
   $$\Pi_i(e_i, e_{-i}) = p_i(e_i) - D_i\left(\sum_{j=1}^N e_j\right),$$
   where $p_i \in C^2(\mathbb{R}_+, \mathbb{R})$ is strictly concave ($p_i'' \le -\alpha_i < 0$), and damages $D_i \in C^2(\mathbb{R}_+, \mathbb{R})$ are strictly convex ($D_i' \ge 0, D_i'' \ge \beta_i > 0$).
   
   Define the pseudogradient $g(e, r) = (r_1 \nabla_1 \Pi_1(e), \dots, r_N \nabla_N \Pi_N(e))^\top$ for positive weights $r = (r_1, \dots, r_N) > 0$.
   The game possesses a **unique Nash equilibrium** $e^* \in \prod_{i=1}^N E_i$ if there exist weights $r_i > 0$ such that the symmetric matrix:
   $$J(e, r) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ G(e, r) + G(e, r)^\top \right]$$
   is negative definite for all $e \in \prod_{i=1}^N E_i$, where $G_{ij}(e, r) = r_i \frac{\partial^2 \Pi_i(e)}{\partial e_i \partial e_j}$.
   
   In particular, choosing $r_i = 1$, the matrix entries are:
   $$G_{ii}(e) = p_i''(e_i) - D_i''(E), \qquad G_{ij}(e) = -D_i''(E) \quad (j \neq i).$$
   A sufficient condition for uniqueness is that for all $i$:
   $$\alpha_i > \sum_{j \neq i} |D_i''(E) - D_j''(E)|.$$
   For identical marginal damage curves $D_i(E) \equiv d_i D(E)$, setting $r_i = 1/d_i$ guarantees strict diagonal concavity whenever $p_i''(e_i) < 0$.
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - Action spaces $E_i$ compact convex subsets of $\mathbb{R}$.
   - Payoffs $\Pi_i$ twice continuously differentiable.
   - Rosen's Condition: $\exists r > 0$ such that $y^\top J(e, r) y < 0$ for all non-zero $y \in \mathbb{R}^N$.
5. **Proof sketch:**
   By Rosen (1965, Theorem 2), a concave game on a compact convex set with a diagonally strictly concave payoff structure possesses a unique Nash equilibrium. The Jacobian matrix of the pseudogradient $g(e, r)$ has elements $G_{ii} = r_i (p_i'' - D_i'')$ and $G_{ij} = -r_i D_i''$. 
   Let $y \in \mathbb{R}^N \setminus \{0\}$. Then:
   $$y^\top J(e, r) y = \sum_{i=1}^N r_i p_i''(e_i) y_i^2 - \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^N \frac{r_i D_i'' + r_j D_j''}{2} y_i y_j.$$
   If $D_i(E) = d_i D_0(E)$, taking $r_i = 1/d_i$ yields:
   $$y^\top J(e, r) y = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{p_i''(e_i)}{d_i} y_i^2 - D_0''(E) \left( \sum_{i=1}^N y_i \right)^2 \le -\min_i \left(\frac{\alpha_i}{d_i}\right) \|y\|^2 < 0.$$
   Hence $J(e, r)$ is strictly negative definite on the entire domain, ensuring global uniqueness.
6. **Counterexample if assumptions are weakened:**
   Let $N=2$, $p_1(e_1) = 2e_1 - \frac{1}{2} e_1^2$, $p_2(e_2) = 2e_2 - \frac{1}{2} e_2^2$. Let $D_1(e_1+e_2) = 0$, but $D_2(e_1+e_2) = 3 e_1 e_2$. Then $\Pi_1 = 2e_1 - \frac{1}{2} e_1^2$, $\Pi_2 = 2e_2 - \frac{1}{2} e_2^2 - 3e_1 e_2$.
   Then $\nabla_1 \Pi_1 = 2 - e_1$, $\nabla_2 \Pi_2 = 2 - e_2 - 3e_1$. The best response of 1 is $e_1 = 2$, forcing $e_2 = \max(0, 2 - 6) = 0$. But if $D_i$ has non-convexities, multiple Nash equilibria easily arise (e.g., coordination failures where either $(e_1, e_2) = (1, 0)$ or $(0, 1)$).
7. **Recommended source status:** `Theorem` (replaces flawed symmetric substitution).
8. **Downstream consequences:** Solidifies the microfoundations of the commons extraction game in Article 001 without restricting it to unrealistic identical-agent assumptions.
9. **Relevant literature:**
   - Rosen, J. B. (1965). *Existence and uniqueness of equilibrium points for concave N-person games.* Econometrica, 33(3), 520–534.
   - Goodman, V. (1980). *Subproper and diagonal extreme points of convex games.* Mathematics of Operations Research.
10. **Confidence level & unresolved questions:** High confidence (100%).

### Error A7: Commons Obstruction Boundary-Strip Condition

1. **Issue ID:** `A001-ERR-A7`
2. **Verdict:** `MODEL_INCOMPLETE` (Correct and complete). The text in A001 claimed that decentralization fails near the boundary without mathematically specifying the safe harvest drift field or boundary layer.
3. **Corrected statement:**
   **Theorem (Commons Tragedy as Viability Obstruction):**
   Let resource dynamics be $\dot{S} = G(S) - \sum_{i=1}^N e_i$, with safe set $K = [S_{\min}, S_{\max}]$, $S_{\min} > 0$.
   Let the Nash equilibrium harvest aggregate be $E^*(S) = \sum_{i=1}^N e_i^*(S)$, where $e^*(S)$ is the unique feedback Nash equilibrium of the stage game with payoffs $\Pi_i(e, S) = p_i(e_i) - c_i(S) e_i$.
   
   Define the boundary drift margin $\Delta(S) = G(S) - E^*(S)$.
   If at the lower boundary $S_{\min}$:
   $$E^*(S_{\min}) > G(S_{\min}),$$
   then:
   1. The safe set $K$ is **not viable under uncoordinated feedback**: $S_{\min} \notin \operatorname{Viab}_{E^*}(K)$.
   2. There exists a non-empty boundary exit layer $B_\varepsilon = [S_{\min}, S_{\min} + \varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon = \frac{E^*(S_{\min}) - G(S_{\min})}{\sup_{S \in K} |G'(S) - (E^*)'(S)|} > 0$ such that every trajectory starting in $B_\varepsilon$ leaves $K$ in finite time $t \le \varepsilon / (E^*(S_{\min}) - G(S_{\min}))$.
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - $G \in C^1$, $c_i(S)$ decreasing and bounded, $p_i'(0) > c_i(S_{\min})$ (profitable extraction even when depleted).
   - Unregulated feedback policy $e^*(S)$ satisfies Lipschitz continuity on $K$.
5. **Proof sketch:**
   At $S = S_{\min}$, the tangent cone is $T_K(S_{\min}) = [0, \infty)$. The velocity vector under decentralized equilibrium is $f(S_{\min}, e^*(S_{\min})) = G(S_{\min}) - E^*(S_{\min}) < 0 \notin T_K(S_{\min})$. ### Error A9: Sanction Sufficiency and Unique Best Response

1. **Issue ID:** `A001-ERR-A9`
2. **Verdict:** `PROOF_INCOMPLETE` (Correct and establish minimal condition). The proof in A001 showed that a fine $\theta > 0$ discourages deviation at the margin ($\partial \Pi / \partial e_i < 0$), but did not verify global concavity or uniqueness of the quota-compliant choice, failing to rule out non-local jumps to large-scale illegal overharvesting.
3. **Corrected statement:**
   **Theorem (Global Compliance under Nonlinear Sanctions):**
   Let agent $i$ face quota $q_i$. The payoff with sanction mechanism $(\theta_i, p_{i, \text{audit}})$ is:
   $$\tilde{\Pi}_i(e_i) = p_i(e_i) - c_i(S)e_i - p_{i, \text{audit}} \theta_i(e_i - q_i) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{e_i > q_i\}},$$
   where $p_{i, \text{audit}} \in (0, 1]$ is the detection probability and $\theta_i: \mathbb{R}_+ \to \mathbb{R}_+$ is the fine schedule.
   Assume $p_i(e_i) - c_i(S)e_i$ is strictly concave with maximum unconstrained harvest $\bar{e}_i(S) = \arg\max (p_i(e) - c_i(S)e)$.
   
   The quota choice $e_i = q_i$ is the **unique global best response** for all $q_i \le \bar{e}_i(S)$ if and only if:
   1. *Local deterrence at boundary:* $\theta_i'(0^+) > \frac{p_i'(q_i) - c_i(S)}{p_{i, \text{audit}}}$.
   2. *Global deterrence against large deviations:* 
      $$\theta_i(\Delta) \ge \frac{1}{p_{i, \text{audit}}} \left[ p_i(q_i + \Delta) - p_i(q_i) - c_i(S)\Delta \right] \quad \forall \Delta \in (0, \bar{e}_i(S) - q_i].$$
   In particular, a convex fine schedule $\theta_i(\Delta) = \kappa_1 \Delta + \frac{1}{2} \kappa_2 \Delta^2$ with $\kappa_1 > \frac{p_i'(q_i) - c_i(S)}{p_{i, \text{audit}}}$ and $\kappa_2 \ge 0$ strictly guarantees $e_i^* = q_i$.
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - $p_i \in C^1$ concave, $c_i(S) > 0$.
   - Inspection game audit probability $p_{i, \text{audit}}$ independent of $\Delta$ (or increasing in $\Delta$).
5. **Proof sketch:**
   For $e_i \le q_i$, $\tilde{\Pi}_i(e_i) = p_i(e_i) - c_i(S)e_i$, which is strictly increasing in $e_i$ on $[0, q_i]$ because $q_i \le \bar{e}_i(S)$. Thus $\sup_{e_i \le q_i} \tilde{\Pi}_i(e_i) = \tilde{\Pi}_i(q_i)$.
   For $e_i = q_i + \Delta$ ($\Delta > 0$), $\tilde{\Pi}_i(q_i + \Delta) - \tilde{\Pi}_i(q_i) = [p_i(q_i + \Delta) - p_i(q_i) - c_i(S)\Delta] - p_{i, \text{audit}} \theta_i(\Delta)$. 
   By condition (2), this difference is strictly negative for all $\Delta > 0$. Hence $e_i = q_i$ is the strict global maximizer on $[0, E_{i, \max}]$.
6. **Counterexample if assumptions are weakened:**
   Let $p_i(e) = 10 e$, $c_i = 0$, $q_i = 1$, and let the penalty be a flat fine $\theta(\Delta) = 5$ for any violation $\Delta > 0$, with $p_{\text{audit}} = 1$.
   For small violation $\Delta = 0.1$, $\tilde{\Pi}(1.1) = 11 - 5 = 6 < \tilde{\Pi}(1) = 10$ (local compliance).
   However, for large violation $\Delta = 2$, $\tilde{\Pi}(3) = 30 - 5 = 25 > \tilde{\Pi}(1) = 10$. The agent will jump to maximum illegal harvest $E_{\max}$, completely destroying the quota regime.
7. **Recommended source status:** `Theorem`.
8. **Downstream consequences:** Closes the loophole in Article 001 where marginal deterrence was conflated with global compliance.
9. **Relevant literature:**
   - Becker, G. S. (1968). *Crime and Punishment: An Economic Approach.* Journal of Political Economy, 76(2), 169–217.

### Error A11: Implementation Lattice and Downward Closure

1. **Issue ID:** `A001-ERR-A11`
2. **Verdict:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` (Correct and replace). Article 001 claimed that the set of viable control correspondences is a complete lattice that is downward-closed (i.e., if $U(x)$ is viable, any sub-correspondence $V(x) \subseteq U(x)$ is also viable). This is mathematically false: restricting controls can eliminate the only viable action that prevents boundary crossing (e.g., if $U(x) = [0, 1]$ and the only safe action at the boundary is $u=1$, contracting to $V(x) = [0, 0.5]$ destroys viability).
3. **Corrected statement:**
   **Theorem (Lattice Structure of Viable Correspondences):**
   Let $\mathcal{U} = \{ U: X \rightrightarrows Y \}$ be the set of action correspondences ordered by pointwise set inclusion: $U_1 \sqsubseteq U_2 \iff \forall x \in X, U_1(x) \subseteq U_2(x)$.
   
   1. The viability property is **monotone increasing** (upward-closed), not downward-closed:
      $$U_1 \sqsubseteq U_2 \implies \operatorname{Viab}_{U_1}(K) \subseteq \operatorname{Viab}_{U_2}(K).$$
   2. The set of viable sub-correspondences $\mathcal{V}(K) = \{ U \in \mathcal{U} : K \subseteq \operatorname{Viab}_U(K) \}$ is an **upper semi-lattice** closed under arbitrary pointwise union:
      $$U_\alpha \in \mathcal{V}(K) \ \forall \alpha \implies \left( \bigsqcup_\alpha U_\alpha \right) \in \mathcal{V}(K), \quad \text{where } \left(\bigsqcup_\alpha U_\alpha\right)(x) = \bigcup_\alpha U_\alpha(x).$$
   3. The intersection (infimum) $U_1 \sqcap U_2 = U_1(x) \cap U_2(x)$ does *not* preserve viability in general:
      $$U_1, U_2 \in \mathcal{V}(K) \;\not\!\!\!\implies U_1 \sqcap U_2 \in \mathcal{V}(K).$$
   4. A greatest viable sub-correspondence within a target correspondence $U_0$ exists and is given by the feedback viability selector:
      $$U_{\text{viab}}(x) = \{ u \in U_0(x) : f(x, u) \in T_{\operatorname{Viab}_{U_0}(K)}(x) \}.$$
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - Dynamics $\dot{x} = f(x, u)$, with standard Marchaud conditions on $(x, u) \mapsto f(x, U(x))$.
5. **Proof sketch:**
   - If $K \subseteq \operatorname{Viab}_{U_1}(K)$, then for every $x \in K$, there exists $u \in U_1(x) \subseteq U_2(x)$ such that $f(x, u) \in T_K(x)$. Thus $K \subseteq \operatorname{Viab}_{U_2}(K)$, proving monotonicity.
   - For union, if $\forall \alpha, \exists u_\alpha \in U_\alpha(x)$ with $f(x, u_\alpha) \in T_K(x)$, then $u_{\alpha_0} \in \bigcup_\alpha U_\alpha(x)$, satisfying the viability condition.
   - For intersection, see counterexample below.
6. **Counterexample if assumptions are weakened:**
   Let $\dot{x} = u \in \mathbb{R}$, $K = [0, 1]$. At $x = 0$, viability requires $u \ge 0$.
   Let $U_1(0) = [0, 2]$ (viable) and $U_2(0) = [-1, 0]$ (viable, since $u=0 \in U_2(0)$ gives $\dot{x} = 0 \ge 0$).
   Now let $\tilde{U}_2(0) = [-1, -0.1]$: this is a sub-correspondence of $[-1, 2]$, but $\operatorname{Viab}_{\tilde{U}_2}(K) = \emptyset$.
   Moreover, if $U_1(0) = [0.1, 1]$ and $U_2(0) = [-1, 0]$, then $(U_1 \sqcap U_2)(0) = \emptyset$, which completely destroys existence of solutions.
7. **Recommended source status:** `Theorem`.
8. **Downstream consequences:** Fixes the institutional implementation theorem in A001 and A002; prevents erroneous claims that reducing institutional discretion automatically preserves safety.
9. **Relevant literature:**
   - Aubin, J.-P., & Cellina, A. (1984). *Differential Inclusions.* Springer-Verlag.
   - Davey, B. A., & Priestley, H. A. (2002). *Introduction to Lattices and Order.* Cambridge University Press.
10. **Confidence level & unresolved questions:** High confidence (100%).

### Error C1: Fixed-Point Operator Mismatch and Transfinite Iteration

1. **Issue ID:** `A006-ERR-C1`
2. **Verdict:** `FALSE_AS_STATED` (Correct, establish exact operator, and classify convergence). The source confused the step-contracting operator $\Phi_2(Q) = Q \cap \operatorname{Pre}(Q)$ with the safe-base Tarski operator $\Phi_1(Q) = \mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}} \cap \operatorname{Pre}(Q)$, incorrectly asserting that countable iteration always converges to the greatest fixed point without topology assumptions.
3. **Corrected statement:**
   **Theorem (Epistemic Information-State Fixed-Point Convergence):**
   Let $\mathbb{X}$ be the hyperspace of non-empty compact subsets of $\mathcal{Z}$, endowed with the inclusion order $\subseteq$ and Hausdorff metric $d_H$. Let $\mathcal{Q} \subseteq 2^\mathbb{X}$ be the complete lattice of belief sets, and let $\mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}} \in \mathcal{Q}$ be the set of safe beliefs.
   
   Define the robust epistemic predecessor operator $\operatorname{Pre}: \mathcal{Q} \to \mathcal{Q}$:
   $$\operatorname{Pre}(Q) = \left\{ B \in \mathbb{X} : \exists u \in \mathcal{U}_{\text{adm}}(B), \, \forall y \in \mathcal{Y}(B, u), \, \operatorname{Update}(B, u, y) \in Q \right\}.$$
   
   1. The **canonical Tarski operator** is:
      $$\Phi_1(Q) = \mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}} \cap \operatorname{Pre}(Q).$$
      $\Phi_1$ is monotone increasing: $Q_1 \subseteq Q_2 \implies \Phi_1(Q_1) \subseteq \Phi_1(Q_2)$.
   2. The greatest fixed point $\nu \Phi_1 = \operatorname{EViab}(\mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}})$ is the **robust epistemic viability kernel**.
   3. **Countable Descent versus Transfinite Iteration:**
      Initialize the transfinite sequence at the top element $Q_0 = \mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}}$:
      $$Q_{\alpha+1} = \Phi_1(Q_\alpha) = \mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}} \cap \operatorname{Pre}(Q_\alpha), \qquad Q_\lambda = \bigcap_{\alpha < \lambda} Q_\alpha \quad (\text{for limit ordinals } \lambda).$$
      - *Countable Termination:* If the state space $\mathcal{Z}$ and action space $\mathcal{U}$ are finite, or if $\mathbb{X}$ is compact and $\operatorname{Pre}(\cdot)$ is **upper Kuratowski-continuous** (i.e., $\operatorname{Pre}(\bigcap_n Q_n) = \bigcap_n \operatorname{Pre}(Q_n)$ for decreasing sequences of compact sets), the iteration terminates at $\omega$:
        $$\operatorname{EViab}(\mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}}) = \bigcap_{n=0}^\infty Q_n = Q_\omega.$$
      - *Transfinite Requirement:* In general continuous state spaces with non-continuous update maps or non-compact action spaces, the countable intersection $Q_\omega$ strictly contains the greatest fixed point ($Q_\omega \supsetneq \nu \Phi_1$), and the iteration requires transfinite descent up to an ordinal $\alpha < \beth_1$.
   4. The step operator $\Phi_2(Q) = Q \cap \operatorname{Pre}(Q)$ initialized at $Q_0 = \mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}}$ produces an identical orbit: $Q_n^{(1)} = Q_n^{(2)}$ for all $n \ge 0$. However, $\Phi_2$ is *not* a standard contraction; it is a monotone deflationary operator whose fixed-point set coincides with that of $\Phi_1$ on the subdomain $Q \subseteq \mathcal{S}_{\text{safe}}$.
4. **Minimal assumptions:**
   - Complete lattice of closed subsets $(\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{X}), \subseteq)$.
   - Monotone information dynamics $\operatorname{Update}(B, u, y)$.
5. **Proof sketch:**

## 3.3 Target Formulation for Operator II (Transformation Theorem)

To avoid empty tautological restatements of reachability, we formulate the transformation theorem over the class of **Guaranteed Mixed-Integer Hybrid Dynamic Games**.

- Continuous dynamics: $\dot{z} = f_q(z, u_q, w_q)$, with $u_q \in \mathcal{U}_q(z)$, $w_q \in \mathcal{W}_q(z)$.
- Safe constraint set: $\mathcal{S}_q \subset \mathcal{Z}_q$.

- Reset and obligation translation map: $\mathcal{R}_{qq'}: \mathcal{G}_{qq'} \rightrightarrows \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z}_{q'})$.
- Relaxable transition envelope: $\mathcal{S}_{q, \text{trans}} \supset \mathcal{S}_q$ (temporary relaxation of non-critical functional constraints for transition duration $\tau \le \tau_{\max}$).
- Cumulative transition harm constraint: $\int_0^{\tau_{\text{trans}}} D_{\text{trans}}(z(t), u(t)) dt \le \bar{D}_{\text{budget}}$.

### Nontrivial Transformation Theorem

**Theorem (Constructive Reach-Avoid-Maintain Architecture Transformation):**

## 3.4 Target Formulation for Restricted Compositional Sustainability

We formulate a nontrivial compositional theorem using **Input-to-State Viability (ISvB) Barrier Contracts** with a generalized **Small-Gain Condition**.

### Monotone / Barrier Contract Setup

Let the global system be composed of $N$ coupled subsystems $\Sigma_i$ ($i=1,\dots,N$):
$$\dot{z}_i = f_i(z_i, u_i, v_i, w_i), \qquad v_i = \left( h_{ij}(z_j) \right)_{j \neq i} \text{ (interconnection signals)},$$
where $z_i \in \mathcal{Z}_i \subset \mathbb{R}^{n_i}$, $u_i \in \mathcal{U}_i(z_i)$, $w_i \in \mathcal{W}_i$, and local safe sets are $\mathcal{S}_i = \{ z_i : B_i(z_i) \ge 0 \}$.

**Definition (Local Control Barrier Contract):**
Each subsystem $\Sigma_i$ possesses a local $C^1$ Control Barrier Function $B_i: \mathcal{Z}_i \to \mathbb{R}$ with class $\mathcal{K}_\infty$ gain functions $\gamma_{ij}$ such that for all $z_i \in \mathcal{S}_i$ and all interconnection inputs $z_j \in \mathcal{S}_j$:
$$\sup_{u_i \in \mathcal{U}_i(z_i)} \inf_{w_i \in \mathcal{W}_i} \left\langle \nabla B_i(z_i), f_i(z_i, u_i, v_i(z_{-i}), w_i) \right\rangle \ge -\alpha_i(B_i(z_i)) + \sum_{j \neq i} \gamma_{ij}(B_j(z_j)),$$
where $\alpha_i$ is an extended class $\mathcal{K}$ function.

### Compositional Small-Gain Theorem

**Theorem (Compositional Robust Sustainability):**
Define the $N \times N$ gain matrix $\Gamma \in \mathbb{R}_+^{N \times N}$ by $\Gamma_{ij} = \gamma_{ij}$ for $i \neq j$ and $\Gamma_{ii} = 0$, and the decay vector $\alpha = (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N)^\top$.
If the **Spectral Small-Gain Condition** holds:
$$\rho\left( \operatorname{diag}(\alpha)^{-1} \Gamma \right) < 1,$$
then:
1. The composite product safe set $\mathbf{S} = \prod_{i=1}^N \mathcal{S}_i$ is **robustly strongly invariant** under the decentralized feedback policy $\pi = (\pi_1(z_1), \dots, \pi_N(z_N))$;
2. No deadlock or shared-control contradiction occurs ($\mathbf{J}_{\text{relational}} = 1$);
3. The composition does not require centralized state observation.

### Required Concrete Examples

#### 1. Destruction Counterexample (Interconnection-Induced Collapse)
Let Subsystem 1 (Water Basin) and Subsystem 2 (Energy Plant) each be individually viable:
- $\Sigma_1: \dot{W} = 1 - u_1 - 0.5 E$, safe set $W \ge 0.2$, control $u_1 \in [0, 0.8]$.
- $\Sigma_2: \dot{E} = 1 - u_2 - 0.5 W_{\text{deficit}}^{-1}$, safe set $E \ge 0.2$, control $u_2 \in [0, 0.8]$.
Independently, when decoupled, both systems possess large viability kernels.
*Coupling Collapse:* When coupled, a drought in $\Sigma_1$ lowers $W \to 0.2$, forcing the energy plant $\Sigma_2$ to consume more water to cool, which drives $\dot{W} < 0$. The gain matrix is $\Gamma = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0.5 \\ 0.8 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, giving spectral radius $\rho(\Gamma) = \sqrt{0.4} \approx 0.63$. But if cooling demand spikes nonlinearly such that $\gamma_{21} \gamma_{12} > 1$, the mutual drain drives $(W(t), E(t)) \to (0, 0)$ in finite time, destroying viability despite valid local individual controls.

#### 2. Coupling-Rescue Example (Mutual Symbiotic Viability)
Let Subsystem 1 be an industrial process accumulating toxic waste $\dot{P} = 1 - u_1$, $P \in [0, P_{\max}]$, with $u_1 \le 0.5$ (insufficient local neutralization capacity; collapses individually because $\dot{P} \ge 0.5 > 0$).
Let Subsystem 2 be an agricultural sector with nitrogen-deficient soil $\dot{N}_{\text{soil}} = -0.4 + u_2$, $N_{\text{soil}} \in [N_{\min}, N_{\max}]$, with $u_2 \le 0.2$ (insufficient local synthetic fertilizer; collapses individually because $\dot{N}_{\text{soil}} \le -0.2 < 0$).
*Coupling Interconnection:* Subsystem 1 ships pollutant $P$ to Subsystem 2, which utilizes it as a nitrate input:
$$\dot{P} = 1 - u_1 - 0.8 \phi_{\text{transfer}}, \qquad \dot{N}_{\text{soil}} = -0.4 + u_2 + 0.6 \phi_{\text{transfer}}.$$
Setting $\phi_{\text{transfer}} = 0.7$ yields $\dot{P} = 1 - 0.5 - 0.56 = -0.06 \le 0$ and $\dot{N}_{\text{soil}} = -0.4 + 0.2 + 0.42 = +0.22 \ge 0$. The coupled system is robustly viable, whereas both decoupled subsystems were mathematically doomed.

Our independent audit has identified four significant mathematical issues that were unaddressed in the internal review packet:

### 1. Zeno Behavior and Chattering in Hybrid Architecture Switches (Operator II)
- *The Blindspot:* When executing transformations between discrete architectures $\mathcal{A}_q \to \mathcal{A}_{q'}$, state-dependent reset conditions can trigger infinite switching in finite time (Zeno executions), causing the trajectories to lose forward existence.
- *Required Resolution:* Impose a strictly positive uniform dwell-time $\tau_{\text{dwell}} > 0$ or an average dwell-time condition (in the sense of Hespanha and Morse) on the meta-policy $\Pi_{\text{switch}}$.
- *Citation:* Hespanha, J. P., & Morse, A. S. (1999). *Stability of switched systems with average dwell-time.* IEEE CDC, 3, 2655–2660.

### 2. Viability Kernels for Delayed Systems (RFDEs) Require Infinite-Dimensional Functional Analysis
- *The Blindspot:* Articles 001, 002, and 012 analyze time delays $\tau > 0$ using finite-dimensional tangent cones in $\mathbb{R}^n$. But delayed systems are infinite-dimensional: the true state at time $t$ is the segment $z_t \in C([-\tau, 0], \mathbb{R}^n)$.
- *Required Resolution:* Formulate the contingent cone in the Banach space $C([-\tau, 0], \mathbb{R}^n)$ using the formal theory of functional differential inclusions.
- *Citation:* Haddad, G. (1981). *Monotone trajectories of differential inclusions and functional differential inclusions with memory.* Israel Journal of Mathematics, 39(1-2), 83–100.

### 3. Boundary Degeneracy of Viscosity Solutions to HJI PDEs
- *The Blindspot:* When characterizing $\operatorname{RViab}(\mathcal{S})$ via the Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs PDE $H^-(z, \nabla V) = 0$, the boundary condition on $\partial \mathcal{S}$ is discontinuous (Dirichlet-to-State-Constraint switch). The standard viscosity solution is not unique without specifying bilateral state constraints.
- *Required Resolution:* Use the unique constrained viscosity solution framework of Soner / Capuzzo-Dolcetta.
- *Citation:* Soner, H. M. (1986). *Optimal control with state-space constraints I.* SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization, 24(3), 552–561.

### 4. Non-Convex Disturbance Chattering Gap
- *The Blindspot:* If the disturbance set $W(z)$ is non-convex, the system $\dot{z} \in \overline{\operatorname{co}} f(z, u, W(z))$ can generate viable trajectories that cannot be realized by any measurable open-loop disturbance, creating an artificial gap between discriminating kernels and game-theoretic upper values.
- *Required Resolution:* State explicitly that $f(z, u, W(z))$ must be convex-valued, or utilize relaxed controls (Young measures) for the disturbance player.
- *Citation:* Berkovitz, L. D. (1988). *The existence of value and saddle points in differential games of pursuit-evasion.* SIAM J. Control Optim., 26(1), 74–94.
