## Issue A1 — Robust tangency versus strong invariance

**Diagnosis verdict:** partly correct.  
**Source status:** `PROOF_INCOMPLETE`.

**Corrected statement.**  
Let \(K\) be closed. Define the robust regulation map

\[
\mathcal R_K(x)
=
\{u\in\mathcal U(x): f(x,u,d)\in T_K(x)\ \forall d\in\Delta(x)\}.
\]

If \(\mathcal R_K(x)\neq\varnothing\) for every \(x\in K\), and a measurable feedback selection exists with well-posed closed loop, then \(K\) is robustly controlled invariant:

\[
K\subseteq \operatorname{RViab}(K).
\]

Under standard Marchaud/semicontinuity/convexity assumptions, the robust viability kernel is the largest closed subset of the constraint set satisfying this robust tangency condition.

**Minimal assumptions.**

- \(K\) closed;
- \(f\) continuous or upper semicontinuous with compact values;
- \(\mathcal U(x)\), \(\Delta(x)\) compact;
- tangent cone \(T_K(x)\) well-defined;
- measurable selection available;
- solution concept well-posed.

**Proof sketch.** Choose measurable \(k(x)\in\mathcal R_K(x)\). Then for every disturbance,

\[
f(x,k(x),d)\in T_K(x).
\]

By Nagumo/viability theorem for differential inclusions, trajectories remain in \(K\).

**Counterexample if quantifiers are weakened.** Let \(K=[0,\infty)\), \(x=0\), \(d\in\{1,-1\}\), and

\[
f(x,u,d)
=
\begin{cases}
u, & d=1,\\
-u-1, & d=-1.
\end{cases}
\]

For each \(d\) separately there is a safe action: if \(d=1\), choose \(u=0\); if \(d=-1\), choose \(u=-1\). But there is no single \(u\) satisfying both:

\[
u\ge0
\quad\text{and}\quad
-u-1\ge0
\]

cannot hold simultaneously. Thus \(\forall d\,\exists u_d\) is not strong invariance.

**Recommended source status.** Conditional theorem / robust discriminating-kernel formulation.

**Downstream consequences.** This should replace any proof that applies ordinary Nagumo to a disturbance inclusion without checking the \(\exists u\,\forall d\) quantifier.

**Literature.** Aubin, *Viability Theory*; Blanchini, “Set invariance in control,” *Automatica*, 1999.

**Confidence.** High.

## Issue A6 — Rosen uniqueness

**Diagnosis verdict:** partly correct.  
**Source status:** `PROOF_INCOMPLETE`.

**Corrected statement.**  
For the commons game

\[
U_i(h_i,H)
=
\pi_i(h_i)-d_i(H),
\qquad
H=\sum_j h_j,
\]

with \(\pi_i\) strictly concave \(C^2\) and \(d_i\) convex \(C^2\), Rosen diagonal strict concavity can be verified using positive weights \(r_i>0\). The weighted payoff is

\[
\Psi(h)=\sum_i r_i U_i(h_i,H).
\]

Its Hessian has the form

\[
\nabla^2\Psi(h)
=
\operatorname{diag}\left(r_i\pi_i''(h_i)\right)
-
\left(\sum_i r_i d_i''(H)\right)\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top.
\]

For any nonzero \(v\),

\[
v^\top\nabla^2\Psi v
=
\sum_i r_i\pi_i''(h_i)v_i^2
-
\left(\sum_i r_i d_i''(H)\right)
\left(\sum_i v_i\right)^2.
\]

If \(\pi_i''<0\) and \(d_i''\ge0\), this is strictly negative. Hence \(\Psi\) is diagonally strictly concave and the Nash equilibrium is unique.

**Minimal assumptions.**

- compact convex action intervals;
- \(\pi_i\) strictly concave;
- \(d_i\) convex;
- positive weights \(r_i\).

**Counterexample if weakened.** If damage functions are sufficiently concave, \(d_i''<0\), the Hessian can fail negative definiteness and uniqueness may fail.

**Recommended source status.** Theorem, repaired proof.

**Downstream consequences.** Supports uniqueness of Nash in commons obstruction and quota results.

**Literature.** Rosen, 1965, “Existence and uniqueness of equilibrium points for concave N-person games.”

**Confidence.** High.

may be too strong. The correct object is a prescription-level safety condition.

Let \(p\) be a prescription and let

\[
\mathcal E(p,x)
\]

be the set of realized actions induced by \(p\) when the latent state is \(x\). A prescription \(p\) is safe for belief \(B\) and target set \(Q\) if

\[
f(x,\tilde u,d)\in T_Q(x)
\]

for every \(x\in B\cap Q\), every \(\tilde u\in\mathcal E(p,x)\), and every disturbance \(d\).

The epistemic obstruction is then:

\[
\nexists p
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\forall x\in B,\ \forall \tilde u\in\mathcal E(p,x),\ \forall d,\ 
f(x,\tilde u,d)\in T_Q(x).
\]

The common-action conflict is the special case \(\mathcal E(p,x)=\{p\}\).

**Recommended source status.** Conditional theorem / formal definition.

**Downstream consequences.** Repairs institutional epistemic viability and avoids excessive raw-action intersection.

**Confidence.** High.

# Tier 3 — Larger architectural and redesign questions

## Issue B2 — Selector regularity

**Diagnosis verdict:** correct.  
**Source status:** `MODEL_INCOMPLETE`.

**Corrected statement.**  
Article 002’s kernels are valid as choice-theoretic existence results under arbitrary state-feedback selectors. For implementable policies, add measurable-selection corollaries.

**Minimal measurable-selection hypotheses.**

- state, action, disturbance spaces are compact metric;
- transition/tube maps are continuous or Borel measurable;
- predecessor witness correspondence

\[
\Gamma(z)
=
\{u\in U: \text{successor/tube conditions hold}\}
\]

has nonempty closed values;
- \(\Gamma\) is measurable, e.g. closed graph in compact metric spaces.

Then a measurable selector exists by standard measurable selection theorems, e.g. Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski.

**Continuous selectors.** Continuous selectors are not guaranteed. They require stronger conditions, such as lower semicontinuity and convex values, or approximation results. Do not claim continuous implementability without proof.

**Which results remain choice-theoretic?**

- kernel nonemptiness and maximality;
- finite-horizon characterization;
- greatest-fixed-point existence.

**Which results become implementable?**

- measurable policy corollaries;
- finite-horizon recursive measurable policies;
- stationary measurable policies if the limiting witness correspondence is measurable.

**Counterexample if weakened.** A closed-valued correspondence may have measurable selectors but no continuous selector. Thus continuous implementation is a separate theorem.

**Recommended source status.** Main theorems remain conditional choice-theoretic theorems. Add measurable implementation corollaries.

**Downstream consequences.** Essential for epistemic viability being policy-implementable rather than merely existential.

**Confidence.** High for measurable selection; moderate for infinite-horizon stationary measurable selection without additional structure.

## Priority 2 / §8.1 — Operator I hierarchy

**Recommended minimal coherent hierarchy.**

Use the following notation.

### 1. Controlled existential viability

\[
\operatorname{Viab}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_{\mathbb P}(\mathcal V,T)
=
\{z_0\in\mathcal V:
\exists\pi\in\mathbb P
\text{ such that } z^{\pi}(t)\in\mathcal V\ \forall t\in T\}.
\]

This is for a nominal disturbance or no adversarial disturbance.

### 2. Robust/discriminating viability

\[
\operatorname{RViab}_{\mathbb P,\Delta}(\mathcal V,T)
=
\{z_0\in\mathcal V:
\exists\pi\in\mathbb P\ \forall\delta\in\Delta,
\ z^{\pi,\delta}(t)\in\mathcal V\ \forall t\in T\}.
\]

This is the core Operator I object.

### 3. Epistemic viability

On information states \(i\in I\):

\[
\operatorname{EViab}_{\mathcal I,\mathbb P}(\mathcal V,T)
=
\{i_0:
\exists\pi(i_{0:k})\ 
\forall\text{ compatible latent trajectories/disturbances},
\ z(t)\in\mathcal V\}.
\]

Physical projection:

\[
\operatorname{EViab}_{\mathcal I}^{\mathrm{phys}}
=
\{z_0:B_{\mathcal I}(z_0)\in\operatorname{EViab}_{\mathcal I}\}.
\]

### 4. Institutional viability

For institution

\[
\mathfrak I=(\mathcal I,\Gamma,\mathcal E,\mathcal A),
\]

define

\[
\operatorname{IViab}_{\mathfrak I}(\mathcal V,T)
\]

as the set of belief/physical states from which there exists an institutionally implementable prescription policy whose realized actions preserve \(\mathcal V\) for all latent states and disturbances compatible with the belief.

### 5. Finite-horizon chance viability

\[
\operatorname{ChViab}_{T,p}(\mathcal V)
=
\{z_0:
\exists\pi,\ 
\Pr[z^{\pi}(t)\in\mathcal V\ \forall t\in[0,T]]\ge p\}.
\]

### 6. Capture/recoverability

\[
\operatorname{Capt}^{H}_{\mathbb P,\Delta}(\mathcal K;\mathcal E)
=
\{z_0\in\mathcal E:
\exists\pi\in\mathbb P\ \forall\delta\in\Delta\ 
\exists t_C\le H:
z(t)\in\mathcal E\ \forall t\le t_C,
\ z(t_C)\in\mathcal K\}.
\]

### 7. Fixed-policy safety

\[
\operatorname{Safe}_{\pi,\Delta}(\mathcal V,T)
=
\{z_0:
\forall\delta\in\Delta,
\ z^{\pi,\delta}(t)\in\mathcal V\ \forall t\in T\}.
\]

**Literal inclusions.**

Under common state space, disturbance class, horizon, and policy nesting:

\[
\operatorname{RViab}\subseteq\operatorname{Viab}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}.
\]

If epistemic policies are restrictions of full-information policies:

\[
\operatorname{EViab}^{\mathrm{phys}}
\subseteq
\operatorname{RViab}.
\]

If institutional implementation further restricts epistemic policies:

\[
\operatorname{IViab}^{\mathrm{phys}}
\subseteq
\operatorname{EViab}^{\mathrm{phys}}.
\]

Capture basins are not generally subsets of viability kernels. With envelope \(\mathcal E=\mathcal K\),

\[
\operatorname{Capt}_{\mathcal K}(\mathcal K)=\mathcal K.
\]

With \(\mathcal E\supset\mathcal K\), the capture basin can include states outside \(\mathcal K\).

**Recommended source status.** Adopt as the official Operator I hierarchy.

**Confidence.** High.

## Rejected formulation 7.2 — Distributive measurement

**Diagnosis verdict:** original construction invalid as written.  
**Recommended source status:** reject and redesign.

### Valid design principles

#### 1. Dimensionless normalized margins

For each group \(g\) and dimension \(j\), define lower-bound margin

\[
m_{gj}^{-}
=
\frac{x_{gj}-\ell_{gj}}{s_{gj}},
\]

where \(\ell_{gj}\) is the floor and \(s_{gj}>0\) is a declared scale.

For upper-bound burdens,

\[
m_{gj}^{+}
=
\frac{u_{gj}-x_{gj}}{s_{gj}}.
\]

All margins are dimensionless.

#### 2. Exact nonsmooth conjunction

The exact distributive safety condition is

\[
\min_{g,j} m_{gj}\ge0.
\]

This is exact and non-compensatory.

#### 3. Conservative smooth inner certificate

A smooth conservative certificate can use

\[
m_\tau
=
-\tau\log\left(\sum_{g,j}e^{-m_{gj}/\tau}\right).
\]

Then

\[
m_\tau\le \min_{g,j}m_{gj}.
\]

Thus \(m_\tau\ge0\) is sufficient but not necessary. Do not claim finite-\(\tau\) equivalence to the exact minimum.

#### 4. Bottom-group quantiles

Use group-specific quantiles or essential infimum:

\[
Q_{\alpha}(m_j)
=
\inf\{m: F_j(m)\ge\alpha\}.
\]

Do not average regional deciles. If pooling is required, form the correct mixture distribution

\[
F=\sum_g w_g F_g
\]

with declared weights, then compute quantiles from \(F\), and report group-specific quantiles separately.

#### 5. Intergenerational transition matrices

If generations or groups transition across states, use a stochastic matrix

\[
P_{ab}=\Pr(\text{next state }b\mid\text{current state }a).
\]

Intergenerational safety requires that the induced distribution over next-generation states lies in a declared continuation set with sufficient probability or satisfies recursive viability constraints.

#### 6. Dynamic viability/CBF condition

For distributional constraints

\[
h_g(z)\ge0,
\]

a robust dynamic condition is

\[
\exists u\ \forall d:
D^+h_g(z;f(z,u,d))\ge \alpha_g(h_g(z))
\]

for each protected group constraint. If using a smooth aggregate certificate, its derivative condition should be conservative relative to the exact minimum.

**Recommended source status.** Redesign as above. Do not repair the original LogSumExp equivalence or pooled decile construction.

**Confidence.** High for design principles; empirical implementation remains open.

## Major mathematical issue absent from the packet

The packet misses one major issue:

> There is no bridge theorem connecting sampled information-state kernels, continuous-time robust viability, and institutional implementation under partial observation and variable delays.

More concretely, the current corpus has:

- sampled full-state kernels;
- finite-clopen observation kernels;
- compact information-state kernels;
- bounded-jump hybrid kernels;
- continuous-time robust tangency.

But it lacks a theorem stating under what conditions an information-state kernel computed in sampled review time certifies continuous-time robust viability with partial observation, delayed deployment, and institutional implementation. This bridge requires:

- exact or bounded information process;
- inter-sample tube safety;
- selector regularity;
- delay composition;
- non-Zeno execution;
- implementation/enforcement maps.

A second absent issue is:

> There is no nontrivial theorem combining strategic compliance, commons allocation, and small-gain composition.

The current composition theorem handles deterministic/robust coupling. The master manuscript needs a strategic-commons composition theorem showing when local contracts, aggregate sink budgets, and enforcement mechanisms jointly imply global viability.

Both should be added to the open proof-obligation register.
