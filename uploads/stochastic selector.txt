### 1. Fatal Technical Contradiction: Proposition 1 vs. Theorem 2(a) & 2(e)

* **The Setup:**
  * **Proposition 1** states that belief-state safety values are computed via the standard Smallwood–Sondik (1973) backward recursion:
    $$\alpha^{a, \gamma_\cdot}(x) = \sum_{x'} T(x' \mid x, a) \sum_y g(y \mid x, a, x') \gamma_y(x'), \quad \gamma_y \in \Gamma_k$$
    $$\Gamma_{k+1} = \{\alpha^{a, \gamma_\cdot} : a \in A, \gamma_\cdot \text{ a selection}\}$$
    This is the standard Bellman backup for POMDPs, which optimizes over **all causal sequential policies** (the unrestricted policy class).
  * **Theorem 2(a)** claims:
    > "the alpha-vector recursion of Proposition 1 returns exactly the closed form
    > $$V_k(b_0) = \frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{1}[z_0 - k \ge 1] + \mathbf{1}[z_0 + k \ge 1]) \quad (k \le T_{\text{obs}}),$$
    > $$V_k(b_0) = \frac{1}{2} (1 + \mathbf{1}[z_0 \ge 1 + T_{\text{obs}}]) \quad (k > T_{\text{obs}})$$
    > over the declared hold class."
* **The Contradiction:**
  1. **Proposition 1 does not enforce a hold class.** In standard POMDP value iteration, the controller is free at each time step $t$ to select any action $a_t \in A$.
  2. For the hidden-regime system ($z^+ = z + \theta u$, $\theta \in \{-1, +1\}$, $u \in \{-1, +1\}$, floor $z \ge 1$), if the controller plays the alternating open-loop sequence $u_0 = +1, u_1 = -1, u_2 = +1, u_3 = -1, \dots$ during the blind window:
     * When $\theta = +1$: $z_0 \to z_0 + 1 \to z_0 \to z_0 + 1 \dots$
     * When $\theta = -1$: $z_0 \to z_0 - 1 \to z_0 \to z_0 - 1 \dots$
     For any $z_0 \ge 2$, the declining branch **never drops below $z_0 - 1 \ge 1$**, meaning **neither branch ever exits $V$**, regardless of the length of $T_{\text{obs}}$!
  3. The author acknowledges this in **Theorem 2(e)**:
     > *"over the unrestricted sequential blind class the value is 1 exactly on $\{z_0 \ge 2\}$ for $k \ge 2$ — the timing cells are precisely the cells where in-window adaptivity lifts the declared-class value."*
  4. **Therefore:**
     * If Proposition 1 is run as stated, its alpha-vector backup will generate the alternating policy and return $V_k(b_0) = 1$ for all $z_0 \ge 2, k \ge 2$.
     * It **cannot** return the closed form in (a) (which equals $1/2$ whenever $k > z_0 - 1$).
     * Claiming in Theorem 2(a) that *"the alpha-vector recursion of Proposition 1 returns exactly the closed form"* is **false** unless Proposition 1 is modified to artificially suppress action switching.

### 2. Conceptual Consequence: Collapse of the Timing Obstruction under Adaptive Control

This technical contradiction exposes an unaddressed limitation in the companion paper’s timing obstruction:
* In Paper 1 (Section 3.3, Theorem 3; Section 8, Table 2), the timing bound $T_{\text{obs}} > z_0 - 1$ was claimed to prove nonviability on the 12 cells with $z_0 \ge 2$ and $T_{\text{obs}} \in \{2, 3\}$.
* However, as shown by Theorem 2(e), that nonviability is **purely an artifact of imposing a hold-constant policy** ($u \equiv \text{const}$).
* If an agent in discrete time is allowed to vary actions over the blind window, the guaranteed blind survival time is not $\sigma^*(B_0) = z_0 - 1$, but $\sigma^*(B_0) = \infty$ for all $z_0 \ge 2$.
* The timing obstruction does not certify nonviability of the system; it only certifies nonviability of the zero-order hold policy class.

### 3. Formal Gaps in the POMDP Definition (Section 2)

* **Undefined Transitions on $X \setminus V$:**
  * Section 2 states: *"Augment $X$ with an absorbing unsafe state $\bot$: for $x \in V$, $T(\bot \mid x, a) = 1 - \sum_{x' \in V} T(x' \mid x, a)$ and $T(\bot \mid \bot, a) = 1$."*
  * What is $T(x' \mid x, a)$ for $x \in X \setminus V$? The text is completely silent.
  * If $X \setminus V \ne \emptyset$ and transitions from $X \setminus V$ back into $V$ are possible, the definition allows trajectories starting outside $V$ to re-enter $V$.
* **Missing Observation Model for $\bot$:**
  * Bayes' rule is defined as:
    $$P(y \mid b, a) = \sum_{x, x'} b(x) T(x' \mid x, a) g(y \mid x, a, x').$$
  * What is $g(y \mid x, a, \bot)$ or $g(y \mid \bot, a, \bot)$?
  * If the absorbing state $\bot$ emits no observation (or $g$ is undefined on $\bot$), $P(y \mid b, a)$ fails to sum to 1 whenever $b(\bot) > 0$ or $T(\bot \mid x, a) > 0$. The text does not clarify whether $b$ is an unnormalized sub-probability measure on $V$ or a normalized probability measure on $V \cup \{\bot\}$.
* **Proposition 1 Alpha-Vector Backup Formula Lacks Boundary Masking:**
  * The backup equation:
    $$\alpha^{a, \gamma_\cdot}(x) = \sum_{x'} T(x' \mid x, a) \sum_y g(y \mid x, a, x') \gamma_y(x')$$
    computes values for all $x \in X$.
  * If $x \in X \setminus V$ and transitions into $V$ were evaluated, $\alpha(x)$ could be strictly positive.
  * But survival requires $x_0 \in V$ at $t=0$, so $\alpha(x)$ must be identically 0 for all $x \notin V$. The backup formula must include the indicator $\mathbf{1}_V(x)$:
    $$\alpha^{a, \gamma_\cdot}(x) = \mathbf{1}_V(x) \sum_{x'} T(x' \mid x, a) \dots$$
    (or $X$ must be defined strictly as $X = V$).

### 5. Flawed Definition of the "Stochastic Selector" $\Gamma_k^s(b)$

* In Section 2, the stochastic selector is defined as:
  $$\Gamma_k^s(b) = \left\{ a \in A : \sum_y P(y \mid b, a) V_{k-1}(\tau(b, a, y)) = V_k(b) \right\}.$$
* The author immediately asserts:
  > *"Nonemptiness of the recursive selector corresponds to $V_k(b) = 1$; obstruction certificates correspond to deficits $1 - V_k(b) > 0$."*
* **Logical Error:**
  * Because $A$ is finite, the maximum in $V_k(b) = \max_{a \in A} \dots$ is **always attained**.
  * Therefore, $\Gamma_k^s(b)$ is **never empty** (it always contains the maximizing action), even if $V_k(b) = 0$!
  * Nonemptiness of $\Gamma_k^s(b)$ does *not* correspond to $V_k(b) = 1$. The viable action set would be $\{a \in A : \sum_y P(y \mid b, a) V_{k-1}(\tau(b, a, y)) = 1\}$. Conflating the argmax set with the set of value-1 actions is a conceptual error.

### 6. Mixing Game-Theoretic Adversaries with POMDP Expectations (Proposition 2)

* In Section 2, the framework is a standard POMDP with transition probabilities $T(x' \mid x, a)$ and expected survival probabilities.
* In **Proposition 2**, the author suddenly states:
  > *"Let the kernels be deterministic, $x^+ = F(x, a, d)$, with disturbance support $\{F(x, a, d) : d \in D\}$ read adversarially within its support..."*
* A POMDP does not have an adversarial player.
* If nature chooses $d$ adversarially after the control is chosen, the system is a partially observable game (minimax), not a standard POMDP.
* An adversarial disturbance cannot be "read" out of a standard POMDP expectation unless the transition probabilities $T(x' \mid x, a)$ are specifically structured or replaced by a robust / game-theoretic Bellman operator.

### 7. Mischaracterization of Discontinuities as a "Kink Locus" (Theorem 2(c))

* **Theorem 2(c)** states:
  > *"the attaining alpha-vector switches exactly at $z_0 = 2$: the value's kink locus is the certificate boundary"*
* As a function of initial position $z_0$, the closed-form value in Theorem 2(a) consists of step functions:
  $$V_k(b_0) = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}[z_0 - k \ge 1] + \mathbf{1}[z_0 + k \ge 1]).$$
* A step function has a **jump discontinuity** at $z_0 = 2$, not a **kink** (a point where a continuous function has a discontinuous derivative).
* While $V_k(b)$ has kinks with respect to the continuous probability vector $b \in \Delta(X)$ across cells of the belief simplex, the transition with respect to $z_0$ across the certificate boundary is a jump discontinuity.

### 9. Ambiguity in Proposition 3(ii)

* Proposition 3(ii) states: *"if the support misses the $k$-step robust kernel, $\Delta_k(b) \ge \min_{x \in \operatorname{supp} b} b(x)$"*.
* In viability theory, "the $k$-step robust kernel" is conventionally a subset of state space, $\operatorname{RViab}_k(V) \subseteq X$.
* Here, the author means the collection of viable beliefs, $W_k \subseteq 2^V$.
* Stating that *"the support misses the kernel"* is ambiguous: it conflates $\operatorname{supp}(b) \cap \operatorname{RViab}_k(V) = \emptyset$ with $\operatorname{supp}(b) \notin W_k$. The correct statement is: *"if the support $\operatorname{supp}(b)$ is not in $W_k$"*.

**Example / companion consistency (the delayed hidden-regime grid)**
- Dynamics \(z^+=z+\theta u\), \(\mathcal{V}=\{z\ge1\}\), two-point prior on \(\theta=\pm1\). A constant action always drives one branch down at rate 1; a switching open-loop sequence (e.g. \(+1,-1,+1,\dots\)) keeps both branches inside \(\{z\ge1\}\) for every \(z_0\ge2\) by oscillation (touches the boundary then returns). Consequently:
  - hold-class value \(=1\) iff \(z_0\ge1+T_{\mathrm{obs}}\) (or the one-step condition \(z_0\ge2\));
  - unrestricted sequential value \(=1\) for all horizons once \(z_0\ge2\), independently of \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\).
- The original obstruction paper (§8, Table 2, Figure 4) states the ground-truth viability condition as \(z_0\ge1+T_{\mathrm{obs}}\) and certifies the cells \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\{2,3\}\), \(z_0\ge2\) as timing-nonviable.
