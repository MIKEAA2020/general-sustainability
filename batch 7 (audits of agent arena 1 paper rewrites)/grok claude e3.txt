The design is the same disciplined object as the cod forecast paper — locked retention rule, naive baselines first, oracles excluded, two systems never pooled — and on this basin it produces a cleaner empirical result: the one-pool balance is a *certificate of the current year*, not a forecast of the next, because recharge is nearly white at an annual origin. ### 1. Direct contradictions and overclaims (fix first)

**M1 retained by a margin that the paper itself calls operationally nil.**  
Abstract: AR(1) retained, 12.84 vs 13.23 ft.  
§5.3: margin 0.39 ft on \(n=75\), “not a significance claim”; MAE 10.72 vs 10.73 (tie); at \(h=5\) M1 *loses* to persistence (21.25 vs 21.11) while the training mean wins (16.80). “At the decision scale of annual drought-stage declarations the difference is operationally nil.”

The result is: **causal stock-flow loses; AR(1) is a coin-flip at \(h=1\); climatology wins at \(h=5\); the oracle shows the map is a nowcast.** Lead with that. Put the 0.39 ft in a sentence that includes MAE, \(h=5\), and “not a significance claim.”

**M2m: listed, declined, then used as H2 comparator for climate.**  
Prop 3.1: constant fluxes ⇒ affine AR(1), declined because “the retained object is forecast-time structure, not estimator refinement.”  
Table 4: M2m 12.28 ft — the *best* non-oracle \(h=1\) score, better than M1.  
Prop 5.3: climate modules fail H2 against *M2m* (12.71 > 12.28). You call this “a protocol kink, acknowledged.”  

A referee will not treat an acknowledged kink as resolved. Either:

- make M2m a retained estimator-class cousin of M1 (then climate H2 is against M2m, which is honest), or  
- decline M2m *and* compare climate to M1 / persist only, not to a declined model.

Using a declined model as the gate that kills the climate rung is the one protocol decision that looks post-hoc, freeze date notwithstanding.

**Freeze 2026-08-25.**  
Same anachronism as the viability paper’s 2026-08-26 and the obstruction reconstruction’s 2026-09-01. If submitted before that date the freeze is future. If the archive is not public at submission, “frozen and dated before RMSE tables were computed” is unauditable. Weaker true statement: “retention rule coded before the rolling table was read.” Strip “pre-registered” from the abstract unless a protocol file with that date is in the supplement.

**“Certificate for the current year rather than a forecast of the next.”**  
Best sentence in the paper (abstract Implications, §6). It is slightly overstated. The oracle uses *realized* \(R_{t+1},P_{t+1}\) to predict \(H_{t+1}\), which is a nowcast of next year’s head given this year’s water, not a certificate of *this* year’s head. The in-sample increment \(\Delta H_t\sim R_t\) (\(r=0.74\)) is the contemporaneous certificate. Distinguish:

- **nowcast / oracle:** \(H_{t+1}\mid R_{t+1},P_{t+1}\) — 7.55 ft;  
- **forecast:** \(H_{t+1}\mid\) information at 31 Dec \(t\) — persist 13.23, M2 14.70;  
- **contemporaneous balance:** \(\Delta H_t\) vs \(R_t\) — not scored as RMSE of \(H\).

Call the oracle a nowcast. Reserve “certificate” for the contemporaneous increment, or drop the word (it is overloaded from the cod papers).

**Companion cod / “same pattern.”**  
§6: “the same pattern a companion evaluation finds on Northern cod, where a more accurate catch series does not rescue constant-productivity surplus production.” The analogy is: *the identified driver is not persistent at the forecast origin*. That is fair and should stay in one sentence. Do not let it become “both systems reject structure.” Scores are never pooled (good). If the companion is not co-submitted, one sentence is enough.

**GEMS-GER (Ohmer et al. 2026), Zhu et al. 2026.**  
Future citations, same problem as Rose (2026). Lock published / in-press / preprint DOI, or replace with published groundwater-forecast benchmarks (Daliakopoulos 2005 and Adamowski 2011 are already there; add a published multi-model comparison that actually exists). ## A. Major problems

**A1. The paper's best model is declined by a clause that is not in the retention rule, and the headline rejection of the "water balance" rests on that decline.**
M2m — the one-pool map with fitted (α, β, γ, δ) and climatological fluxes — has the lowest one-year RMSE of any admissible model (12.28 ft; persistence 13.23, M1 12.84) and is within 0.64 ft of the best at five years (17.44 vs 16.80). It satisfies (H1) and (H2) of Definition 4.2 as written. It is then "declined on class grounds" citing Proposition 3.1, a clause that appears nowhere in Definition 4.2 and is described in §5.3 as "fixed in the protocol" without evidence. Two consequences: (i) the verdict "no stock-flow module is retained" is an artefact of the decline; (ii) M2m beats M1 by 0.56 ft — a larger margin than the 0.39 ft that retains M1 — so the water-balance *identification* of the affine coefficients improves on the direct AR(1) fit. That is evidence for the stock-flow structure, not against it. What the paper actually shows is that persisting last year's recharge is a poor flux forecast (Table 6: persist-R RMSE 702 vs climatology 556). Rewrite the Abstract, Impact Statement, and §7 around "stock-flow with climatological fluxes is the best one-step forecaster tested; persisted recharge is the failure."

**A2. The climate-module rejection is circular.** The declared comparator for the climate rung is M2m — a model the protocol has just declined as "not extra structure" — and the rejection of ENSO/precipitation modules follows *only* from that choice (§5.3 concedes "the point-RMSE listing alone would not decide it"; the "protocol kink" is acknowledged). Against the retained model M1, all three variants pass (H1) and (H2). Either M2m is a legitimate rung (then A1 applies and it is retained) or it is not (then it cannot serve as the bar). One cannot have both.

**A3. The retention rule omits the horizon and the second naive baseline the Introduction promises.** §1: the bar is persistence "together with the training mean." Definition 4.2: persistence only. At h = 5 the training mean beats every model including the retained M1 (21.25 > 21.11 > 16.80). Also Definition 4.2 does not say which horizon decides; M1 fails (H1) at h = 5. State the horizon and require beating the better naive baseline at that horizon.

**A4. §5.6 contradicts its own table and mislabels the coefficient comparison.** Table 7: the 20 % cut has RMSE 7.19 ft against observed heads, lower than actual pumpage (8.56).
