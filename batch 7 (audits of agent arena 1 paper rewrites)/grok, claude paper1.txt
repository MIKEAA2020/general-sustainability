## A. Hard mathematical / consistency flags

**A1. Lemma 2 is the dual-cone characterisation of \(\mathbb{R}^n_+\); Theorem 3(ii) is Lemma 2 applied pointwise to tubes.**  
\(v\ge 0\) iff \(w\cdot v\ge 0\) for all \(w\in W_+\). Once tubes are sets of vectors, \(E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)=\bigcap_w E_w(z)\) is immediate. Proposition 1 is \(\{z:\bigcap E_\lambda\neq\varnothing\}\subseteq\bigcap\{z:E_\lambda\neq\varnothing\}\). Theorem 4 is “intersecting over a larger family shrinks.” The paper’s own §1.3 correctly refuses priority on \(\exists a\,\forall w\neq\forall w\,\exists a_w\). What remains as mathematics is Theorem 5 (the witness), Theorem 7 (erasure), Theorem 8 (blend), and the hold-prefix tautology (Theorem 6). Number the witness as the main theorem; demote 1–4.

**A2. The blend tube is a declared extra action, not a consequence of the disturbance class.**  
Theorem 8 defines \(\mathrm{BLEND}_\delta\) by taking the pointwise convex mixture of FAST and SLOW *worst-case tubes*. Under the §4.5 convention, \(\alpha\) hits the active action’s characteristic coordinate. A physical interleaving (FAST on a subset of \([0,1]\), SLOW on the complement) lets the adversary apply the characteristic dip on whichever piece is running; the worst-case \(s_1\)-drop need not be \(2\delta\). The collapse result is therefore: *if the menu is enlarged by these mixture-actions*, the gap vanishes. That is menu geometry, as you say. It is not “time-sharing FAST and SLOW under the original \(D\).” Keep the theorem; change “time-shared convexification — interleaving actions in time to approximate a convexified menu” (abstract) to “adjoining declared mixture-actions whose tubes are the convex combinations.” Otherwise a referee will attack the tube law.

**A3. Typed-endpoint operator is missing — and it is the photograph story.**  
§1.1 and §5.4: endpoint accounting is a snapshot that misses mid-interval liquidation. On the witness, FAST’s *endpoint* returns to \(s_1\), while the tube reaches \(s_1-2\). The operators of §3.1 compare typed-tube, scalarized-tube, physical-tube, and physical-endpoint. There is no \(E_{\mathrm{end,typ}}\). Physical tube vs physical endpoint collapses (Theorem 5(3)) because the only physical constraint is monotone \(x\). The illusion you advertise is typed-tube vs typed-endpoint, and you did not define the latter. Add it: on \(X_0\), FAST is typed-endpoint-admissible everywhere and typed-tube-admissible iff \(s_1\ge 2\). That is the one-line witness of “photograph vs trajectory.”

**A4. \(E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}\) does not see the floors.**  
\(S^{\mathrm{phys}}\) is effectively \(\{x\ge 0\}\) (plus destination \(q=1\)). FAST/SLOW never touch \(x\), so \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}=X_0\). The second strict inclusion in Theorem 5(5) is then a point with small \(s_1+s_2\). Correct, but “physical” here means “floors dropped,” not a stoichiometric or donor-limited constraint. Align the name with the ledger companion or say “floors omitted.”

**A5. Theorem 6 is the identity.**  
HOLD has tube \(\{z\}\) and successor \(\{z\}\). Stage-0 accepted sets are the Theorem 5 sets pulled back through the identity. Persistence of an open gap under “wait, then do the same interval” is not a propagation theorem. Keep one sentence; do not number it.

**A6. Theorem 7 is the right delimitation and should be stated as such.**  
A later coincident terminal set (\(G=G^w=\mathbb{R}^2_+\)) plus a universally typed-safe REPAIR erases the gap by backward induction. The witness of §4.5 is immune because the violations are *stage-1 tubes*, which a later stage cannot repair. That pair (tube-driven gap vs terminal-coincidence erasure) is the genuine scope result. Lead with it after Theorem 5; do not bury it under hold-prefix.

**A7. Intro cycle-closure vs the witness.**  
§1.1 recasts weak/strong as “whether the material cycle can be closed at the rate of use,” waste as relational, substitution vs deep-time regeneration (Daly). The witness is a one-interval four-action gadget with two floors and a bridge stock. Theorem 8 does not “close the acceptance gap exactly on the compensatory region where time-shared substitution keeps pace” in Daly’s sense; it adjoins mixture-actions on a finite menu. §5.1 then correctly *retreats*: not weak sustainability simpliciter, not genuine-savings, not every strong-sustainability reading. The introduction must match §5.1, or the literature will reject the paper before the witness.

**A8. Ledger companion already has the static theorem.**  
Ledger §10.1: no nonnegative weighting certifies componentwise nonnegativity; compensating vectors exist for every \(w\). That is Lemma 2 plus a static construction. This paper’s increment is *dynamic*: at each fixed trajectory the full cone is lossless, but the action that realises \(w\cdot s\ge 0\) along the tube depends on \(w\). Cite the static fact as known (Munda–Nardo; Das–Dennis; the ledger if co-submitted) and sell the noncommutation on tubes.

**A9. Disturbance convention is load-bearing and correctly scoped — once.**  
Action-indexed dips: FAST’s worst case is its own \(s_1\)-dip, not a simultaneous hit on \(s_2\). §5.3 last-but-one bullet: coupled all-floor shocks collapse the divergence into universal rejection. That caveat belongs next to the action table, not only in “what the theorem does not say.” A referee who assumes a common worst-case \(d\) for all \(a\) will think Theorem 5 is false.

**A10. \(\rho_1,\rho_2\) arithmetic is correct; boundary conventions are needed and present.**  
\(\rho_2\ge\rho_1\iff s_1+s_2\ge 2\) checks (cross-multiply with positive denominators). \(w_1=0\) and \(w_2=0\) are handled in the proof of (2). Formulas require \(s_2\in(0,2)\); §4.6 closing note is right. Figure 1 caption is careful about vertices and axes. Keep.

**A11. STAGED vs FAST/SLOW timing of \(e\).**  
FAST/SLOW: floors dip and recover; \(e\) applied at reset. STAGED: “floors grow to \(s+e\)” during the interval. Inconsistent generation of \(e\), irrelevant to Theorem 5 because STAGED’s floors are nondecreasing and FAST/SLOW successors still land in \(G\) whenever \(s\ge 0\). One sentence that \(e\) is a destination reset, not a physical yield, would stop a mass-conservation reading (the ledger companion will be sitting on a referee’s desk).

**A12. NO-SWITCH misses \(G\) by holding \(q=0\).**  
Destination is \(\{(1,x,s):x\ge 0,s\ge 0\}\). The architecture bit \(q\) exists only to force a non-hold action. Harmless, slightly baroque. You could drop \(q\) and declare NO-SWITCH’s successor out of \(G\) by fiat.

**A13. Abstract vs Theorem 8 “only.”**  
Abstract: compensatory aggregation can fail structurally; blend closes the gap *exactly* on the compensatory region; “only” scoped to the deterministic menu. That scoping is the honest one. Title and §7 still sound like a doctrinal ranking. After Theorem 8, the separation is not “weak vs strong”; it is “finite deterministic menu vs its convexification.” §5.1 already says this. Make the title carry it, or a referee will quote Theorem 8 against you.

**A14. Machine artifact does not prove the continuum.**  
You say so. \(31^3=29791\), exact integer, 25 checks. Fine as a classification audit. Do not let “verified by exact-integer computation” in the abstract read as a proof of Theorems 3–8 (the abstract already splits “continuum statements proved in full” from the instance — good; keep that split).

**A15. Canonical tuple \(\mathfrak{S}\) (13 slots) is not used.**  
Theorems use \(Z\), a finite action menu, \(D=\{\alpha,\beta\}\), \(S_0\), \(G\), and Tube/Succ. Type system, observation, command architecture, deployment/reset, policy class, four model maps, ten failure classes, four uncertainty levels: unused. §2.7’s specialisation table is the only map that earns its place. Cut §2 to types, the four operators, and the witness.

## The central issue: Theorem 8 versus the paper's thesis

Three statements in the manuscript cannot all stand:

- Abstract, last sentence: "Compensatory aggregation can thus fail *structurally*, not merely through a poor choice of weights."
- Theorem 8(iii): "the acceptance gap is a property of the deterministic menu, *not of the doctrines*."
- §7: "The bridge theorem a composite index needs — that some single transition serves every admissible weight — is exactly what the frameworks' reporting conventions should be asked to exhibit" — while Theorem 8 has just exhibited it.

Theorem 8 is, precisely, von Neumann's minimax theorem for the $2\times2$ zero-sum game in which the planner picks an action, the assessor picks a weight on the simplex, and the payoff is the worst-case aggregate $w\cdot s^{\min}(a)$. Common-plan acceptance is $\max_a\min_w\ge0$ (pure strategies); per-weight acceptance is $\min_w\max_a\ge0$; the gap is the pure-strategy duality gap; BLEND$_\delta$ is the mixed strategy; and the gap closes because the mixed extension is convex. Nothing in the paper says this, yet it is what any reader with a game-theory or optimization background will see immediately — the paper even invokes "the strict minimax pattern of game theory" in §1.2 without following through.

The same structure is the static-versus-adjustable gap in robust optimization (Ben-Tal, Goryashko, Guslitzer, Nemirovski 2004): $\exists a\,\forall w$ is the here-and-now solution, $\forall w\,\exists a_w$ the wait-and-see solution, and the literature on when the gap is zero (convexity, affine decision rules) is large. §5.2's "bounded absence in our review" of adjacent literatures is therefore not an absence; this is the closest literature and it is uncited.

What follows for the paper:

1. **Pick a thesis.** Either (A) "the compensatory/noncompensatory gap is the pure-strategy duality gap of an assessor–planner game; it exists for finite deterministic menus and closes under time-sharing, so the practical question is whether blends are implementable" — coherent, honest, modest; or (B) find a witness where blending does *not* close the gap. The paper's own modelling choice made (A) inevitable: it *defined* BLEND$_\delta$'s tube as the pointwise convex combination of the two worst-case tubes, which is the linear-intensity model under which mixing always works. If FAST and SLOW have startup transients of fixed depth (so a time-shared plan runs each transient at full depth in sequence — tube = union, not convex combination), no blend helps and the gap is genuinely structural. That is a defensible physical model, and it would rescue the headline. As written, the "time-shared" label is a name attached to a convex-combination definition, not a derivation.

2. **Restate the contribution.** Proposition 1, Lemma 2, Theorem 3, Theorem 4, and Theorem 6 are, by the paper's own admission, elementary (the paper says so in §1.3 and §5.2). Theorem 5 is a worked $2\times2$ game with one extra physical constraint; Theorem 8 is its mixed extension. That is a note, not a 9,000-word paper. The reviewers' question will be "what here is not a corollary of minimax + monotonicity of viability operators?" — and the paper needs an answer written down.
