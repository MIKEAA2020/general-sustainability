grok:

The manuscript is unusually careful about operator distinctions, evidential status, and non-transfer of stability statements. That discipline is a genuine strength. The same caution, however, produces internal tensions, unreproduced numerical claims, anachronistic citations, and a text that is often harder to read than the underlying mathematics. Below I group issues by type, with the most consequential first.

### 1. Unreproduced / mismatched computational claims (most serious)

- **§3.3 vs §3.4 reconstruction.** Abstract and §3.3 report anchovy-class windows near \(T_r \approx 3\)–\(4\) yr and sprat-class near \(6\)–\(12\) yr as results of the stage-structured map. §2.2 and §3.4 then state that the original stage computational record (solver, histories, registration) is unattached, those values have only provisional status, and a newly declared reconstruction (plan frozen 2026-09-01) **mismatches** both windows (both classes converge on the new plant). The reconstruction is correctly labelled a new object and a consistency check, not a validation. The problem is that the abstract and early results still present the unmatched windows as findings. Either withdraw/demote the original 3–4 and 6–12 yr claims to an unreproduced archive note, or make the reconstruction the sole stage result and rewrite the abstract accordingly.

- **Euler vs exact hold, logistic core.** The 47.54 yr / 79.1 yr crossings are identified as command-step artefacts relative to the exact held-assessment map’s single \(\approx 6.5\) yr crossing. That distinction is valuable and clearly reported. The annual spectral radii \(\rho=1.00035\) (exact) and \(1.00055\) (Euler) are both extremely close to 1; a 200 001-point scan with bisection is cited, but floating-point, linearisation step (\(10^{-6}\)), and continuation details for a multiplier this close to the unit circle should be in the computational archive or a short appendix. Without them the “already unstable at annual review” claim is fragile.

- **Constrained-M quantities in §3.8.** Crash-window \(M=0.46\), \(F=1.37\), unreported catch \(257.8\) kt yr\(^{-1}\), etc., are explicitly “unreproduced… hypotheses, not results.” They still appear in the narrative as if they were calculated. Move them entirely to the open-problem docket or omit the numbers.

- **Companion delay study.** Many numbers (Hopf pair 3.666 / 150.358 yr, \(\eta=0.914\), etc.) are attributed to an unpublished companion. If that paper is not co-submitted, those values cannot be treated as established.

### 2. Internal inconsistencies and status mismatches

- Abstract: “the stage-structured review map shows exploratory response windows in the multi-year range” vs later admission that those windows are unreproduced and unmatched by the only fully specified stage object.
- Abstract / §3.4: “logistic hold map’s annual-review equilibrium crosses the unit circle near 6.5 yr” — true for the *exact* map, but annual review itself is *unstable* (\(\rho>1\)). The wording can be read as if 6.5 yr were the annual-review crossing. Clarify: annual review is already outside the unit disk; the first restabilising crossing of the exact map is \(\approx 6.5\) yr.
- §2.3: “A valid architecture comparison holds the ecological plant… fixed while changing only the timing operator.” The 3–4 yr vs 6.5 yr comparison is then correctly labelled cross-plant. Later the one-plant (logistic) contrast is supplied. Keep both, but the abstract currently mixes them.
- §3.3 archived peaks: “approximately 4 yr in anchovy-class biomass with 12 yr in effort” — the text itself notes that a single periodic orbit cannot have two fundamental periods. Either drop the dual-period claim or treat it strictly as unresolved spectral content of transients/modulation.
- Protective vs extractive: Euler protective instability beyond 2.31 yr is an artefact; exact protective is stable everywhere tested. The model-level closed-loop run in §3.4 (30 % error, ten seeds) shows Euler protective crashing at \(T_r=12,20\) while exact protective does not. That is a strong, useful result; it should be flagged earlier so the reader is not left thinking the protective architecture itself is fragile.
- Dates: Rose (2026), reconstruction plan frozen 2026-09-01, reopening 26 June 2024. If the manuscript is submitted before late 2026 these are future citations / a frozen plan that has not yet occurred. Either update or mark as in-press / planned.

### 3. Mathematical / modelling notes (mostly minor, a few worth fixing)

- **Proposition 2.1.** Correct for scalar autonomous ODEs. The subsequent scope statements (assessment estimates \(\neq\) exact trajectory; removals not fixed) are properly made. No change needed except perhaps a one-sentence reminder that time-varying \(C(t)\) or non-autonomous \(f(t,x)\) immediately escape the obstruction.
- **Lemma 2.2.** Algebra for the cubic production function and the two extra-loss cases is correct, including the distinction \(f_C(0)=-C<0\) vs \(f_M(0)=0\). The coalescence/disappearance language is accurate. Minor: “the same threshold-shift reading applies” is slightly loose for the proportional case (smaller root in \((\mathfrak{s},S_h)\)); the proof already states it precisely.
- **Proposition 3.1.** Positivity argument is standard and correct. The displayed “explicit” exponential for \(N(t)\) still contains \(N(s)\) inside the integrand; it is only an integral equation, not a closed form. The later closed form in §3.4 is the one that should be used if an explicit expression is wanted. Boundedness \(N\le\max\{N(t_n),K\}\) holds only while harvest is non-negative, which it is.
- **Effort law \(F_B\).** The rational term is undefined at \(\widehat{Z}=-Z_{\rm ref}\); the non-negativity restriction is therefore necessary, not decorative. The factor \((1-E/E_{\max})\) together with projection is a common saturation device; it does, however, make the linearisation at the boundary more delicate. Worth a sentence.
- **Hybrid convention.** Pre-review / flow-then-update is clearly registered. Good. A one-line diagram of the timing (hold on \([t_n,t_{n+1})\), read \(Z(t_{n+1}^-)\), Euler/exact step) would still help.
- **Neimark–Sacker language.** “Spectral signature of a Neimark–Sacker-type crossing; nonlinear nondegeneracy not verified” is exactly the right strength. Keep it.
- **Catchability \(q\).** Logistic core uses \(q=0.001\); stage reconstruction reports both \(0.001\) and \(0.1\), the latter destabilising annual review for every class. The paper notes that no \(q\) was originally declared for the stage map. That sensitivity is important and should appear in the abstract or §4.1, because it shows the stage windows are not robust to a scale the original model left unspecified.

### 4. Empirical layer

- **42-stock screen.** Eligibility is an analysis-side annual-review criterion, not a database classification of controller sign. FDR (BH, BY fallback) is appropriate; dependence is acknowledged. Zero robust target-band peaks is correctly reported as a selected-cohort consistency check, not absence of the mechanism. Power is low for the anchovy-class effort signal (0.02–0.14); that limitation is stated and should remain prominent.
- **Case search.** Author-curated, four criteria, zero eligible cases. The structural hypotheses (visible-crisis selection; century-scale continuous-delay periods vs 3–12 yr stage windows belonging to different operators) are reasonable but not identified. Peruvian anchoveta / SOI analysis is the strongest empirical piece in the section; the era-split (association confined to 1950–1984) is a useful negative for a stationary institutional cycle.
- **Northern cod.** The two-window descriptive split is the right evidential status. Formulation-dependence of the crash (NCAM M-shift vs constrained-M / unreported catch) is correctly left unresolved. Phase-line obstruction is applied only as a model-class diagnostic, with the two conditions (exact trajectory, fixed removals) explicitly not met by the assessment record. Post-2015 stall (Rose 2026) is used only to show the second identification problem persists. This section is one of the better-calibrated parts of the paper; keep the tone.

### 5. Style, structure, and readability

- **“Declared / registered / preregistration” overload.** The open-science posture is admirable but saturates the prose. Move the bulk of registration requirements, solver configuration, seed lists, and “this is a new labelled object” language to a computational-reproducibility appendix or the archive. Keep only the load-bearing status statements in the main text.
- **Length and nesting.** Methods already contain results-status caveats; results contain a full new reconstruction plus a comparator MSE run; discussion repeats operator non-transfer three more times. A reader trying to extract “what is actually claimed” has to assemble it from half a dozen places. Consider a one-page “claims at their exact status” box after the abstract.
- **§4.6 distributive constraints.** The mismatch table (licence holders vs census subdivisions, fishing income vs all-resident income, undeclared floor) is honest, but the section sits awkwardly after the technical discussion and is not foreshadowed. Either integrate it as a brief “what the case does not measure” paragraph or move it to supplementary material. The MPI / PIP “world-hooks” sentence is especially disconnected.
- **Title.** Accurate but long. The subtitle already lists three objects; the main title could drop “as Sampled Governance” or the third clause.
- **Keywords.** Fine.
- **Figure 1.** Caption is clear. If the four update pairs (Euler/exact \(\times\) mobilising/protective) are the core methodological figure, consider adding a second panel with the continuous-delay Hopf pair on the same plant so the operator contrast is visual.

### 6. Smaller line-level items

- Abstract “several dozen” vs body “42”. Use 42.
- Table 1: \(\tau_m\) “not a discrete delay” — good, keep.
- Eq. (3): \(\widehat{Z}_n=\mathcal{A}_n(\{Y_j:t_j\le t_n-\tau_{\rm dec}\})\). Contemporaneous exact assessment is the baseline; the general form is unused in the reported computations. Either use it or simplify.
- Softplus \(\Phi_k\): the shift \(\delta\) and floor are fine; \(k=10\) is given only later. State it with the equation.
- §2.4: “12–60 yr band is poorly resolved on records shorter than about three candidate periods” — correct and important; many RAM series will fail this.
- §3.6: “100–200 yr synthetic records… exceed many eligible series.” Keep this next to the power numbers.
- §4.4 falsification standard: the five outcomes are well-posed. Outcome 4 (protective dominates extractive without claimed volatility) is already partially illustrated by the model-level run in §3.4; a forward pointer would help.
- §4.5 designs: none executed, no registration ID. The text already says so; a single sentence at the start of 4.5 is enough.
- Citation McManus et al. 2016 (Russell Cycle) is used as a disciplinary template; the analogy is fair if kept at “diagnostics \(\neq\) causal claims.”
- “sample-and-hold governance” / “sampled governance” — pick one term after first definition.

### 7. Suggested priority fixes

1. Resolve the stage-map status: either attach the original record or rewrite abstract + §3.3 so the only stage claims are those of the fully specified reconstruction (annual stability, protective stability, long-horizon band, \(q\)-sensitivity, mismatch with archived windows).
2. Soften or relocate the 47.54 / 79.1 yr Euler numbers; lead with the exact-map 6.5 yr crossing and the artefact diagnosis.
3. Add a short “claims ledger” (one table or box) listing every numerical window, \(\rho\), and empirical count together with its exact evidential tag (closed-form, finite-grid trajectory, reconstruction, unreproduced, hypothesis).
4. Cut or appendix the densest registration meta-text.
5. Fix future-dated citations / frozen-plan date or mark them as such.
6. Keep the operator-non-transfer discipline; it is the paper’s clearest contribution. Make sure the abstract does not mix plants when stating windows.

The modelling distinction (sample-and-hold vs continuous delay vs annual discrete step; Euler vs exact hold; extractive vs protective) is worth publishing. The empirical layer is correctly modest. The main risk is that the current draft asks the reader to treat unreproduced or mismatched numbers as results while simultaneously telling them they are not. Tightening that single point would substantially improve the manuscript.

## A. Major problems

**A1. The paper's headline operator result contradicts itself on the stability of the undelayed loop.**
§3.4 states "On the logistic hold-map core, the undelayed equilibrium is already unstable, so annual review is unstable." §3.2 states that under hyperbolicity the sampled multipliers satisfy μ = 1 + T_r λ + O(T_r²), so small-T_r sampled stability follows continuous no-delay stability. The "one-plant operator contrast" then says annual review is "stable under continuous delay (τ = 1 yr < τ_−)" with the Hopf pair at 3.666 and 150.358 yr bracketing the *unstable* window — which implies the delay equation is stable at τ = 0. Either the undelayed loop is unstable (then the continuous-delay equation must be unstable at τ → 0 and needs an extra crossing in (0, 1) yr that is never reported) or it is stable (then "undelayed equilibrium is already unstable" is false and the reported sampled instability all the way down to T_r = 0.2 yr contradicts §3.2's transfer statement). One of these three statements is wrong, or the "companion" continuous-delay loop is not the same loop as G. This must be resolved explicitly, with the continuous eigenvalue λ reported.

**A2. "Annual review is unstable" rests on ρ = 1.00035.**
From μ ≈ 1 + T_rλ, this is λ ≈ +3.5 × 10⁻⁴ yr⁻¹ — an e-folding time of ~3,000 yr. Practically neutral. The paper repeats "annual review is unstable" as a headline (§3.4 twice, Fig. 1 caption, §4.1) without ever reporting the margin, its sensitivity to the (unpublished) parameter vector, or numerical precision of the monodromy. Report the dominant multiplier modulus and angle θ, and a sensitivity band.

**A3. The stage-map windows (3–4 yr, 6–12 yr) are presented as findings in the Abstract, §1, §3.3, §3.4 ("relocates to T_r ≈ 3–4 yr"), §4.1 ("instability crossing near 3–4 yr") — yet the reconstruction in §3.4 fails to reproduce them and §2.2 admits their computational record is unattached.** Worse, §3.4 asserts "Both statements are det(M − e^{iθ}I) = 0 on the map to which they refer", when §2.2 explicitly says no multipliers were ever computed for the archived stage record. The Abstract should state the reconstruction outcome; §4.1 must not call the archived window an "instability crossing".

**A4. The spectral screen's target bands are derived from the unreproduced stage-map peaks.** §2.4's 4–8 yr and 12–60 yr bands come directly from §3.3's "4 yr biomass / 12 yr effort; 8 yr biomass / 60 yr effort" archived diagnostics — which §3.3 itself says cannot belong to a single periodic orbit and are unresolved. The screen is therefore anchored to provisional outputs the paper cannot reproduce. This dependency is nowhere acknowledged.

**A5. The "companion delay study" is load-bearing and uncited.** It supplies the 47.54 yr reproduction, the "certified" Hopf pair, the exact-hold record, the r ≈ 0.022 / 0.061 windows used for spruce budworm, and the 3.7 "centuries" claim. It appears in no reference. Either cite it (preprint/DOI) or import the needed material into this paper.

**A6. The core parameter vector is never given.** r, K, q, E_max, η, δ₀, Z_ref, Δ_ref, δ, k, τ_m for the logistic hold-map appear only piecemeal (q = 0.001 and k = 10 surface for the first time inside the stage reconstruction; η = 0.914 only in the continuous-delay aside). None of the crossing values (47.536, 79.143, 2.306, 6.501 yr; ρ = 1.00035, 0.9838, 0.9967) is checkable. A parameter table is mandatory. The same holds for E*, N*, Z* (which equilibrium is linearised — interior, or E* = E_max with the projection active?).

**A7. Two incompatible "continuous-delay instability windows" at η = 0.914.** §3.3: r ∈ (0.77, 0.81) yr⁻¹ for g = 2 yr with delay interval 2.6–7.8 yr. §3.7: baseline window upper edge ≈ 0.022 yr⁻¹, and "on the continuous-delay parameterisation that produces the instability window (rg ≈ 1.5–1.6) the predicted period is centuries" — but with g = 1–2 yr and r ≈ 0.8–1.6 yr⁻¹ the period is years, not centuries. Two different objects (delayed-recruitment continuous-delay plant vs slow-r logistic delay) are being conflated under one label.

**A8. Internal contradictions about what has been executed.** §2.1: "The comparators are declared for the prospective MSE (Section 4.5), not presented as completed experiments", followed two sentences later by "The model-level run of the comparator evaluation … is executed and reported in Section 3.4." §1 promises "five identification designs and a closed-loop evaluation design" (six); §4.5 says "Five designs are specified" and lists five including the MSE; §4.5 also says "none has been executed" although a model-level MSE run is reported.

**A9. The slow-stock reconstruction result is reported as agreement with the spectral record when it is a disagreement.** Slow-stock class: "no crossings anywhere (ρ(50) = 0.67)", yet the trajectory classification finds a *persistent* oscillation at T_r = 10 yr from the declared initial condition. A persistent oscillation at a linearly stable fixed point is either bistability, a non-decayed transient, or a classification-threshold artefact — it is not "agreement". Similarly, "oscillates persistently at T_r = 30–50 yr" under 30 % multiplicative assessment error with a 2 % relative tail-SD threshold is noise-driven variance, not oscillation; the threshold is not noise-adjusted.

**A10. §4.6 (distributive constraints) is disconnected from the paper.** It uses none of the paper's objects, introduces undefined jargon ("world-hooks", "master scalar", m_k(G,t) with G undefined), and concedes it operationalises nothing. For an ICES JMS methods paper it should be cut or moved to Supplementary.

## B. Section-by-section notes

**Title/Abstract.**
- Title: "Selected 42-Stock"; Abstract: "several dozen"; "thirty-plus" vs §1 "more than thirty". Use 42 throughout.
- "the logistic hold map's annual-review equilibrium crosses the unit circle near 6.5 yr" — an equilibrium does not cross; its multiplier does, as T_r varies. Also omits the direction (unstable → stable with increasing T_r) and the fact that annual review is (marginally) unstable — the actual finding.
- "no stability statement transfers between them" is contradicted by §3.2's small-T_r transfer under hyperbolicity. Soften to "does not transfer in general".
- Final sentence ("The cadence and form of periodic review … determine whether governance stabilises or destabilises") is a causal generalisation the body repeatedly disclaims (§4.7 (i)). Qualify as a model-class statement.

**§1.**
- "in the lineage of Gurney, Blythe, and Nisbet (1980)" — that paper is a delayed-recruitment *biological* model (blowflies), not a model of institutional response lag. Mis-attribution; cite bioeconomic delay-control literature instead.
- "This is the demand-side form of the overshoot loop" — "overshoot loop" undefined.
- "sampled governance" (§1) vs "sample-and-hold governance" (Abstract) — unify.

**§2.1.**
- Eq. (3): Ẑ_n is built from observations of N alone; Z depends on the deficit qE_nN − S(N), so the assessment operator needs E and the production law. Say so, or define Y as an observation of the deficit.
- "the effort law's rational term is undefined at Ẑ = −Z_ref and changes sign beyond it" — the sign of Z/(Z_ref+Z) already changes at Z = 0. Rephrase.
- Eq. (4) yields E^cmd_{n+1}; §2.2 then writes E_{n+1}. State E_{n+1} ≡ E^cmd_{n+1} when τ_dep = 0.
- The prefactor (1 − E/E_max) makes F_B = 0 at E_max, so projection at the upper end is redundant; worth noting, and it matters for whether the equilibrium is E* = E_max.
- Φ_k: δ undefined and collides visually with δ₀. If δ = 0, the floor is active at the equilibrium (deficit 0) and the map is non-differentiable there, which would invalidate every multiplier in §3.4. State δ > 0 and that the equilibrium is interior to all nonsmooth regions.
- "Because the increment scales with T_r, a sweep in T_r changes both the hold duration and the accumulated gain per review" — good, but then "Comparing its monodromy with the forward-Euler map's separates review cadence, zero-order holding, and Euler discretisation" (§3.4): two maps cannot separate three effects.

**§2.2.**

**§2.3.**
- "A fixed point is locally asymptotically stable if and only if every eigenvalue … lies inside the unit disk" — false as an "iff". Strictly inside ⇒ LAS; LAS does not imply strictly inside. Write "if"; add "unstable if some eigenvalue lies outside".
- "Three objects are in play" — there are at least four (Euler hold map, exact hold map, stage map, continuous delay). §4.7 (iii) says "two operators" then lists three.

- det(M − e^{iθ}I) = 0: M is not defined as the monodromy here, and M is also natural mortality (§2.7, Table 2) and the production maximum (Lemma 2.2).

**§2.4.**
- "selected by a separate annual-review eligibility criterion" — never stated. The criterion, the 42 identifiers, series lengths, and number of series per observable are essential, not "a declared registration requirement".
- "detrends according to a declared rule" — state the rule.
- Effort band 12–60 yr requires ≥180-yr records at the long end under the paper's own "three candidate periods" rule; no RAM series is that long. The band is largely untestable; say so directly rather than "partly a trend test".
- Was Benjamini–Yekutieli run? If BH yields zero, BY trivially does; state that.
- "tested statistic is band-integrated power, and a peak is separately classified" — clarify that the null count refers to band power, since "robust peak" language (§3.5) suggests otherwise.

**§2.5.** Cohen (1988) is filler for a simulation-based power estimate. Power at realistic RAM record lengths (30–70 yr) is the relevant quantity and is not reported.

**§2.6.** Criterion (iii) is written as an exclusion and then, in the same sentence, replaced by a comparative rule because the exclusion "is not falsifiable". Write the comparative rule as the criterion.

**§2.7.**
- "The Schaefer model is the degenerate member of this family… No value of 𝔰 makes the displayed factor identically 1" — if no parameter value yields it, it is not a member of the family. Pick one framing.

More importantly, **Lemma 2.2 is never used anywhere** in §3 or §4, even though the obvious application (seal predation as M_x shifting the effective threshold in the non-recovery window) is sitting in §3.8. Either apply it or drop it.
