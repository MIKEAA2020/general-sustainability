grok: 
**Line-level review: flaws, inconsistencies, and improvements**

The paper is the groundwater twin of the cod viability article: frozen protocol, policy-fixed robust kernels, a retention rule, certified erosion, and a scoped slogan. Here the slogan is the opposite — reactive rules are *retained* — and that is the interesting result, if it survives its own small print. It mostly does at the **nominal, 618-ft, UC-min** reading, and it does **not** survive at the certified layer, at 660 ft, or under the floor-class supply measure you yourself compute in Table 5. The abstract leads with the hybrid (worst-case protection + historical-mean entitlement) and buries that the +3.3% exists only in wet years the robust class excludes. That is the same geometry-vs-identified split the cod paper handled more honestly. Freeze 2026-08-26 is again in the future; the governed map is the one the companion said *not* to use for forecasts; BAU is training-mean pumpage, not historical paths; and “certified kernels” are a Lipschitz stress bound on a defect the out-of-sample audit already exceeds.

Issues grouped by severity.

---

### 1. Direct contradictions and overclaims (fix first)

**The +3.3% / +0.4% are hybrid, not robust.**  
Abstract: “retained at the nominal level, with 3.3% (Stage I) and 0.4% (cascade) more permitted supply than the kernel-matched flat-90% cap.”  
Impact Statement: same numbers, no hybrid caveat.  
§2.4: protection scored under perpetual floors; supply scored as 1934–1990 historical replay; “under the floor classes alone, the reactive rules are identically their matched flat caps and carry no supply margin.”  
Table 5: under UC-min, S1 supply 226.7 vs flat-80% 225.7 (+0.4%); CPM 174.5 vs flat-60% 169.3 (+3.1%) — trigger-lag on the descent from 1934, not a wet-year harvest.  

So the headline 3.3% is **historical-replay entitlement in years the robust class says do not occur**. You state this in 3.4 and 3.6. The abstract and Impact Statement do not. A *Groundwater* editor will treat 3.3% as operational. Put the hybrid in the first results sentence: “nominal retention is worst-case invariance at historical-mean entitlement; under the floor itself the supply margin collapses to a trigger lag of 0.4%.”

**Kernel-matched vs attractor-twin.**  
S1 matches *invariance* with every cut ≥10%, including flat-90%. R2 then compares supply to “the most protective flat cap with matched protection,” resolved as the largest-supply member of the matched class = flat-90%. That is why +3.3% is vs 90%, while the attractor twin is flat-80% (+16.2%). Both are in the body; the abstract quotes only vs flat-90%. Either:

- R2 should compare to the *attractor-matched* cap (flat-80% for S1, flat-60% for CPM), which is the physically matched object, or  
- keep R2 as written and say “vs the *least severe* cap that also makes [618,710] invariant,” which is a weaker claim (any 10% cut already does).

Matching S1 to flat-90% (a milder cut that still just clears 618) inflates the supply win. Matching to flat-80% is the honest twin (Table 1: both attractors 622.04 ft). CPM vs flat-90% (+0.4%) is almost empty; vs flat-60% (+50.6%) is the real number and is *only* the hybrid.

**Certified retention fails, abstract still says “retained.”**  
§3.3–3.4: at the one tabulated certified horizon, S1/CPM inherit BAU’s boundary 706.7 > flat-80% 697.8, so certified dominance **fails**. “The +3.3%/+16.2% are nominal-level… Certified retention is a different, horizon-truncated object.”  
Abstract lists retention in Results without “nominal only,” then Implications: “reactive rules match flat-cap robust protection while preserving more supply.”  
Lead Results with two lines: retained nominally at 618 ft / UC-min; **not** retained certified; **not** retained at 660 ft.

**BAU is not business-as-usual.**  
Def: \(P\equiv\bar P=282.16\) on 1934–1990, “a flat 100% cap, not the historical pumpage path.” Historical pumpage varies; post-1996/2007 permits are lower. Calling this BAU makes every “cut vs BAU” a cut vs *mean unregulated pumping*, not vs the Authority’s current baseline. For *Groundwater* that is a serious label error. Rename: “training-mean pumping” / “unregulated mean cap.” Then “BAU’s attractor 615.72 ft sits below 618” is “mean historical pumping cannot hold 618 under perpetual 1956 recharge,” which is still a result.

**Companion forecast: this map lost.**  
Same issue as the cod viability paper. The companion rejected causal stock-flow (14.70 vs persist 13.23); oracle 7.55. You use the *causal* map as the governed object. Defensible only as “the same misspecified map, now closed-loop.” Sentence 1 of the abstract should say so. The information-gap (7.55 vs 13.23) is reused as if it validated the map; it validated the *nowcast*, not the closed-loop under adversarial \(R\).

**Freeze 2026-08-26.**  
Same future date as the cod viability protocol. Tables 4–5 are post-freeze (“declared post-freeze layers”). Abstract says protocol frozen before any score. Restrict the abstract’s numbers to frozen objects (Tables 1–2, §§3.1–3.4) or stop claiming the freeze covers “the” results.

**OOS defect exceeds declared ε.**  
Training max residual 15.41 ft; OOS max 21.81 ft. “Recorded below and not repaired, per protocol.” Then certified \(r_T\) uses 15.41, and §4 admits the bound is **optimistic**. Abstract Implications: “Certified claims stay limited by the fitted model defect.” True, and too mild: the conversion is already exceeded out of sample. Either recompute \(r_T\) at 21.81 as a sensitivity (post-freeze, labelled) or say certified emptiness at \(T=3\) is a *lower* bound on how fast certification dies.

### 2. Retention rule: still close to a tautology, but less than the cod paper

**R1 at 618 ft / UC-min is almost automatic for every cut ≥10%.**  
BAU kernel empties ~13 years; every cut in the family makes [618,710] invariant. So R1 holds for S1, CPM, and every flat cap except possibly flat-90% if 7.2% is the interpolant — but Table 1 says flat-90% attractor 618.88 > 618, so invariant. Then **every** non-BAU policy in the family passes R1 at this reading. Retention reduces entirely to R2 (supply vs matched flat). That is a supply-at-matched-protection rule, not a viability-first rule. Say so. The cod paper’s H1 could not retain anyone; this R1 cannot *reject* anyone who cuts ≥10%. Opposite tautologies, same design family.

**R2 hybrid is the whole game.**  
You are explicit in 2.4 and 3.6. A referee who accepts hybrid scoring will still ask why protection and supply are scored on **different measures** (perpetual 1956 vs 1934–1990 occupancy). The floor-class Table 5 is the within-scenario supply you need, and it kills the headline margin. Either:

- change R2 to floor-class supply (then S1 is *not* retained vs flat-90%, and vs flat-80% the margin is 0.4%), or  
- keep hybrid and put “wet-year entitlement” in the abstract.

**660 ft: nothing retained — this is the better finding.**  
Trigger-on-boundary invisibility: S1/CPM cut only below 660, so they cannot improve invariance of \(\{H\ge 660\}\). Exact, geometric, form-independent. Flat caps extend horizon, not invariance; even zero pumping attractor 647.3 < 660. “Protected by wet years, not by the pumping family.” This is the Edwards analogue of the cod slogan, and it is cleaner because it is geometry of the *institution’s own trigger*. Promote to abstract Results item (2), not a clause at the end.

**S1 “verified against the Authority’s published rule”; stages II–IV “declared scenarios.”**  
Good. CPM cascade as scored is then a hybrid of one verified stage and three scenarios. Do not call it “the CPM cascade” in the Impact Statement as if it were EAA’s rule. “A four-stage sketch with Stage I verified” .

### 4. Attractors and the 7.2% interpolant

**Table 1 arithmetic.** Affine closed loop under constant \(R=\underline R\), constant \(P=\rho\bar P\):  
\[
H^\star=\frac{\alpha+\beta\underline R+\gamma\rho\bar P}{-\delta}
\]
(since \(\Delta H=0\Rightarrow \delta H=-(\alpha+\beta R+\gamma P)\), \(H=-(\alpha+\beta R+\gamma P)/\delta\)).  
\(\delta=-0.2539\), \(\bar P=282.16\).  
BAU, UC-min \(R=43.7\): \(\alpha+\beta R+\gamma P=163.49+0.0198\times43.7-0.02844\times282.16=163.49+0.865-8.025=156.33\), \(H^\star=156.33/0.2539\approx615.7\). Matches 615.72.  
Flat-90%: \(\gamma P\) 10% smaller in magnitude, +0.802, \(H^\star\approx618.88\). Matches.  
Zero pumping: \(163.49+0.865=164.355/0.2539\approx647.3\). Matches.

**7.2% interpolant.** Between BAU 615.72 and flat-90% 618.88, threshold 618. Linear in \(\rho\) because \(\gamma P\) is linear. Gap 3.16 ft for 10% cut ⇒ 2.28/3.16 × 10% ≈ 7.2%. Outside the family, as you say. Useful. Do not put it in the Impact Statement as if it were a recommended cut; it is the intercept of this affine map with 618 under perpetual 1956 \(R\).

**S1 = flat-80% on the attractor** because 622.04 < 660, cut always on. CPM = flat-60% at 628.36 because 628.36 < 630, Stage IV always on under UC-min. Under UC-q05, CPM attractor 636.37 is *between* 630 and 640, so Stage III not IV — that is why CPM 636.37 ≠ flat-60% 637.95 under q05. Table 1 is consistent. Caption of Fig 1 says “reactive rules coincide with their attractor twins (S1 = flat-80%, CPM = flat-60%)” — true for **UC-min only**. Correct the caption.

**BAU kernel empty beyond ~13 years is domain-top, not attractor.** Attractor 615.72 < 618 already; the kernel still has a finite-horizon set of high initial heads that take 13 years to fall through 618. \(T=12\) boundary 692.6, ceiling 710, crossover 12.7 years. Good distinction. Then “BAU is not robustly viable against perpetual drought-of-record” is true at infinite horizon and for the observed range (max head ~704). Observed 1992 mean 691.96 is almost at the \(T=12\) boundary.

### 5. 660 ft finding (keep and promote)

**Trigger-on-boundary invisibility** is the paper’s cleanest theorem. A rule that cuts only when \(H<K^*_{\mathrm{inst}}\) cannot enlarge the invariant set of \(\{H\ge K^*_{\mathrm{inst}}\}\). Equality of S1/CPM/BAU kernels at 660 is geometry. Flat caps help finite-horizon boundaries (flat-80% ~673 vs BAU 675.1 at \(T=1\)) and extend emptiness from \(T=3\) (BAU) to \(T\approx6\) (zero pumping) because they slow the fall — but attractors stay below 660. Frequency management vs invariance: the actual CPM rationale, well put. NRC 2015 is an appropriate citation if used as “monitoring under drought,” not as endorsing your kernels.

Do not call this a “negative certificate” in the abstract without the geometric one-liner; the cod paper overloaded “certificate.”

The publishable core is already there: on this affine J-17 map, mean historical pumping cannot hold 618 ft under perpetual 1956 recharge; a ~7–10% cut (or a Stage-I-style cut that is active on the drought attractor) can; reactive rules match that invariance and look supply-generous only if you count pumping in wet years; the 660-ft trigger cannot make \(\{H\ge 660\}\) invariant under those floors because it only fires below the floor; certified claims die in three years because the residual is large relative to contraction. ## A. Major problems

**A1. The headline split (BAU fails, a 10 % cut secures) is a 2.3-ft difference on a map with 5.6-ft residual SD and an acknowledged 8.1-ft high bias in the drought it is scored on.**
BAU's attractor (615.72) misses 618 by 2.28 ft; flat-90 % clears it by 0.88 ft; the interpolated 7.2 % cut is the point where a 3.16-ft-per-10 % lever crosses that gap. §3.5 records that the map under-predicts the 1951–56 decline by up to 8.1 ft, so the true attractors are lower than reported and the "securing" caps may not secure. No standard errors on (β, γ, δ), no bootstrap of the attractor, no sensitivity to fit window. The certified layer, which exists to absorb this, empties (A5). The honest nominal-level statement is: the fitted map cannot resolve whether any member of the family protects 618 ft under a perpetual 1956.

**A2. BAU is the 1934–1990 mean pumpage (282 kaf), not current use, and every implication is stated relative to it.** Pumpage over 1991–2023 averaged ~382 kaf (the companion's Table 7). At that level the UC-min attractor is ≈604.5 ft and the securing cut is ≈31 %, not 7.2 %; Stage I (20 %) would then *not* secure the physical threshold and would not coincide with flat-80 %. Every "percent cut" and every supply margin is relative to an obsolete entitlement. State the baseline choice in the Abstract and re-run at current permitted or current actual pumpage.

**A3. The retention rule is contradictory as written and its tie-break decides the verdict.** R2: "more permissive than the *most protective* flat cap with matched protection", resolved as "the *largest-supply* member of the matched class" — opposite selections. Under the kernel reading all caps ≥ 10 % (including flat-0) tie at 618.0 at every horizon, so the comparator is chosen by grid: on the 10 % grid it is flat-90 % (+3.3 % S1, +0.4 % CPM); on a 1 % grid the smallest securing cap is 92 % (supply 259.6, S1 +1.1 %, **CPM fails**); at the paper's own interpolated 7.2 % (supply 261.8) S1 leads by 0.2 % and CPM fails by 2.7 %. Excluding the 7.2 % cut as "outside the declared family" while publishing it as a result is not defensible; it is the matched comparator R2 describes.

**A4. The hybrid criterion retains reactive rules by scoring supply on an open-loop replay of actual 1934–1990 heads.** Under the protection scenario S1 ≡ flat-80 % and is dominated by flat-90 % at identical invariance (§3.4 admits it). The supply margin comes only from the 35.1 % of historical years below 660 ft — but those heads were produced by unregulated pumping; with a 20 % cut in force the heads would have been higher and the cut would have fired less. The proper supply measure is a *closed-loop* replay under historical recharge with the policy applied, which is never computed (Table 5 does closed-loop only under the floors, where margins shrink to 0.4 %/3.1 %). As it stands the "positive selection result" is the tautology that a rule which cuts only sometimes permits more than a rule which cuts always.

**A5. The certified layer is misdiagnosed and, on the paper's own audit, invalid.** (i) The Discussion says the erosion "credits no feedback" and is therefore "conservative against state-dependent rules". In an affine map every cut shifts only the intercept, so the contraction a is identical for all policies and (H4) is exact, not an approximation; the certified kernel *is* the closed-loop nominal kernel of a raised threshold, feedback included. Reactive rules collapse to BAU at the certified level for a different reason: r_T lifts the threshold into the trigger band (K* + r_3 = 653.5, r_4 → 659.9, r_5 → 664.7 > 660), reproducing the trigger-on-boundary invisibility of §3.2 by construction. Any threshold-erosion certificate turns every trigger rule into BAU once r_T ≥ 42 ft (T ≥ 4). (ii) The certified kernel uses K* + r_T at every step; the correct tube needs K* + r_t at step t, so the bound is unnecessarily conservative. (iii) ε = 15.41 is exceeded out of sample (21.81), the paper says the bound is "optimistic, not conservative", and yet certified horizons appear in the Abstract and Conclusions as findings.

**A6. "Empty beyond ~13 years" is domain-top arithmetic.** Emptiness at 618 ft/UC-min occurs when 615.72 + 2.28/aᵀ exceeds the arbitrary ceiling 710 ft (T = 12.7); with a ceiling at the observed maximum annual mean (692 ft) it is T ≈ 12, and with no ceiling the kernel is never empty in the domain sense — every state simply converges to 615.72. The same applies to the 660-ft horizons (T = 3, 6, 11). Report the horizon as a function of the ceiling or drop the "empties" language.

**A7. The 660-ft "negative certificate" evaluates the CPM against the wrong safe set.** The Stage-I trigger is the *earliest* stage of a cascade whose purpose is to protect springflow (≈618 ft), not to keep the head above 660. Scoring a rule that acts only below 660 on the invariance of {H ≥ 660} is a category error the paper half-concedes ("frequency-management rationale … outside the robust-kernel frame") and then reports as a finding in the Abstract, Impact Statement and Conclusions ("protected by wet years, not by pumping rules").

**A8. Uncited companion studies** (forecast evaluation; Northern cod) carry the object, the defect, the coefficients' provenance and the "mirror verdict". "Registered twenty-column analysis panel" is undefined.
