## What checks out

Assuming Eq. (1) is dM/dt = ρM(1 − M/Mₘₐₓ) − γeP(t−τₘ) and Eq. (4) is dP/dt = rP(t)[1 − P(t−τₚ)/K(M(t))] with K = b₀M/rₒₚₜ:

## Flaws and inconsistencies

**1. The stability "requirement" a₁₁ < 0 (M* > Mₘₐₓ/2) is wrong for the system actually being analyzed.**
It is stated as a requirement, but it is (a) a condition on a different object (the M-equation with p ≡ 0), and (b) neither necessary nor the correct condition for the coupled linearization. At zero delay the characteristic equation is

λ² + (r − a₁₁)λ + (γea₂₁ − ra₁₁) = 0,

so Routh–Hurwitz requires r − a₁₁ > 0 and γea₂₁ − ra₁₁ > 0. Using the equilibrium condition ρ(1 − M*/Mₘₐₓ) = γeb₀/rₒₚₜ (which follows from P* = K*), the constant term equals rρM*/Mₘₐₓ > 0 identically. Hence the zero-delay stability condition is a₁₁ < r, not a₁₁ < 0. Equilibria with 0 < a₁₁ < r are stable at zero delay, and the text's condition would wrongly exclude them. The condition is also never used afterward, so it dangles in the narrative.

**2. a₁₁ is not a free parameter, and the condition M* > Mₘₐₓ/2 is a strong, unacknowledged parameter restriction.**
From the equilibrium condition, M* = Mₘₐₓ(1 − γeb₀/(ρrₒₚₜ)) and a₁₁ = 2γeb₀/rₒₚₜ − ρ. So "a₁₁ < 0" is really γeb₀/rₒₚₜ < ρ/2, i.e. harvesting pressure below half the intrinsic regeneration rate. Existence of the equilibrium only needs γeb₀/rₒₚₜ < ρ. The text presents a stability-looking condition that in fact halves the admissible parameter range, and says nothing about existence (M* > 0) at all.

**3. The "verification" claims do not verify what matters.**
- Checking that the zero-delay eigenvalues satisfy the equation "to machine precision" is circular: at τₘ = τₚ = 0 the equation is just det(λI − (A+B+C)) = 0, which the eigenvalues satisfy by construction. It says nothing about whether e^{−λτₘ} and e^{−λτₚ} multiply the right terms—the only nontrivial content of Eq. (13).
- A residual "< 10⁻¹⁶" is below double-precision ε (≈ 2.2×10⁻¹⁶) for O(1) quantities; either the eigenvalues are small in magnitude (making the number uninformative) or the figure is overstated.
- "Symbolic computation and independent re-derivation" is an unverifiable assertion, not evidence.

**4. Which delay attaches to which term depends on unstated features of Eq. (4).**
The derivation silently assumes (i) the outer factor is P(t), not P(t−τₚ), and (ii) the carrying capacity is evaluated at the current time, K(M(t)), not K(M(t−τₚ)). If Eq. (4) has the delay on the whole ratio P/K, the a₂₁ term picks up e^{−λτₚ} and the coupling term becomes γea₂₁e^{−λ(τₘ+τₚ)}; if the outer factor is delayed, the structure changes again. The text writes "K(t)" and "K(M)" interchangeably without ever stating the form of Eq. (4), so consistency with the main text cannot be confirmed from what is given.

**5. The linearization of Eq. (1) is likewise stated without the equation.**
The absence of a −∂(harvest)/∂M · m term implies the harvesting term is independent of M (pure γeP). If Eq. (1) has, e.g., γeMP or a saturating term, a₁₁ acquires an extra contribution and the "M* > Mₘₐₓ/2" reading is wrong even as a condition on the isolated M-equation.

**6. Terminology is inconsistent.**
"Constant-parameter subsystem" (α = 0, b = b₀) and "non-delayed subsystem" (the M-equation with p set to zero?) are used as if related, but refer to different reductions. The M-equation does contain a delay (τₘ), so "non-delayed subsystem" is ambiguous unless p ≡ 0 is meant, which should be said.

**7. Minor.**
- "Applying the chain rule" to P/K: it is the quotient rule (or chain rule on K⁻¹); trivial, but the labelling is sloppy.
- The trivial equilibrium P* = 0 is discarded without comment; the claim P* = K* presupposes P* ≠ 0.

## A. Errors in the analytical core

**A1. The headline result ("no single delay can destabilize the sustainable baseline") holds only on a measure-zero parameter set, and the stated sufficient condition is wrong for one of the two cases.**

From Eq. (13), eliminating the delay via |·|² gives:

- τₚ = 0: ω⁴ + (r² + a₁₁²)ω² + [r²a₁₁² − (γea₂₁)²] = 0
- τₘ = 0: ω⁴ + (a₁₁² − r² − 2γea₂₁)ω² + [(γea₂₁)² − r²a₁₁²] = 0

The constant terms have **opposite signs**. With the baseline numbers, a₁₁ = −0.5, γea₂₁ = 0.01, so r²a₁₁² = (0.02·0.5)² = 0.0001 = (γea₂₁)². The constant term is *exactly zero* in both cases—which is why both of the paper's polynomials carry a factored ω². This is not generic: it happens because ρ = 3γeb₀/rₒₚₜ (1.5 = 3·0.5), i.e. M*/Mₘₐₓ = 2/3 exactly. Perturb any parameter and one of the two single delays destabilizes:

- If r²a₁₁² < (γea₂₁)² (e.g. e slightly above 1), the τₚ = 0 polynomial has a negative constant → τₘ alone produces a Hopf. (This is exactly Scenario B, and the paper's τₘ ≈ 83 yr, ω ≈ 0.026 check out.)

The Discussion's gloss that the property is "specific to the balanced case (e = rₒₚₜ)" is likewise spurious—it is specific to ρ = 3γeb₀/rₒₚₜ, not to e = rₒₚₜ.

Corollary: the two-delay threshold "τₘ + τₚ ≈ 80 yr" is suspiciously close to the classical Hutchinson threshold π/(2r) = 78.5 yr for a delayed logistic with r = 0.02. ## 2. Corrected core model

### 2.1 Environmental stock: net (deficit-driven) depletion with an accounting switch

dM/dt = ρ M (1 − M/Mₘₐₓ) − γ [ E(t−τₘ) − θ B(t−τₘ) ],  θ ∈ [0, 1].   (1′)

- θ = 0 recovers the original gross-depletion form; θ = 1 is the GFN-consistent form in which only the *deficit* liquidates stock and a surplus accrues as regrowth ("interest vs. principal"). Presenting θ as an accounting parameter preserves the original model as a special case while making the bank-account metaphor literally true.
- Because E − θB = b(f − θ)M at P = K (with f = e/rₒₚₜ), the depletion term now scales with M: M = 0 becomes a genuine equilibrium rather than a clipped boundary (fixes C1), and a "less stock → less absolute depletion" story is available honestly.
- Optional but recommended: if the paper wants a threshold "from which recovery is impossible" (as §4–§5 repeatedly assert), add a strong Allee term:

 ρ M (M/M_A − 1)(1 − M/Mₘₐₓ),  0 < M_A < Mₘₐₓ.

 This makes the threshold real, generates bistability and hysteresis, and aligns with the tipping-point literature (Scheffer et al.) and with the paper's own Limitations paragraph. It also gives the basin-shrinkage narrative of Scenario D a rigorous object: the separatrix of the Allee threshold in the delayed system.

### 2.2 Delay placement matched to the text

- Environmental lag: keep a lagged component but allow an immediate one, since §2.1 says extraction is immediate:

 depletion = γ [ (1−w) X(t) + w X(t−τₘ) ],  X ≡ E − θB, w ∈ [0,1].

 Or use the linear-chain (gamma-distributed) form, dQ/dt = (X − Q)/τₘ, depletion = γQ, which adds one linear state and keeps the characteristic equation rational-exponential. Report discrete-delay results as the w = 1 limit.
- Demographic lag: delay the *conditions*, as the narrative requires:

 dP/dt = r P(t) [ 1 − P(t−τₚ)/K(t−τₚ) ] = r P(t) [ 1 − Ω_P(t−τₚ) ].   (4′)

 People respond to the overshoot ratio they experienced τₚ years ago. This is the Hutchinson form and forces the paper to confront (rather than obscure) the classical threshold rτₚ = π/2, which is where the "≈ 80 yr" number was coming from.

### 2.3 Carrying capacity: true vs perceived

The original defines K with rₒₚₜ but consumes with e, so for e ≠ rₒₚₜ the state P = K is not an equilibrium of the full model (B11). Make this explicit and turn it into content:

K_true = B/e,  K_perc = B/r_perc,  f = e/r_perc.

The overshoot factor f becomes a *perception–consumption gap*: demography is calibrated to a per-capita need lower than actual consumption. P → K_perc is a false attractor; the model then shows what happens when a society regulates itself to the wrong target. Keep rₒₚₜ as r_perc; add the promised co-evolution (B6) with a minimal adaptation law,

de/dt = −ε e [Ω(t−τₑ) − 1],

so that footprint contracts under perceived scarcity (ε = 0 recovers the original). This closes the loop, creates true equilibria for f > 1, and introduces a *policy-response delay* τₑ that the Discussion already talks about.

### 2.4 Debt and technology with the asymmetry actually built in

D: dD/dt = softplus_k(E − θB) − η D,  (6′)

(k → ∞ recovers max(·,0); the smooth version removes the kink for linearization; η ≥ 0 makes "first-order process" true).

b(t) = [ b₀ + T_b(t) ] · exp(−α D),  (7′)

Multiplicative debt erodes technological gains too. Now the paper's central asymmetry is a theorem: T_b is bounded, D is unbounded under persistent overshoot (η small), so b → 0 regardless of Δb. Under the original additive form the claim was false (B5).

Two technology channels, because they behave oppositely:

- yield technology T_b (raises b, raises K, invites rebound: higher K → higher P → higher E — the Jevons channel that actually caused Scenario E to collapse faster);
- efficiency technology T_e (lowers e and r_perc together, e.g. e = e₀/(1 + T_e)).

Each is a sum of logistic waves as in Eq. (8). The comparison "yield vs efficiency innovation" is a genuinely new result the original was blind to and is highly policy-relevant.

### 2.5 Half-Earth as a continuous, internally consistent lever

Human-usable share σ ∈ (0,1]:

B_h = σB,  K = B_h/r_perc,  Ω = E/B_h,  dD/dt uses (E − θB_h)₊,

with optional conservation benefit ρ(σ) or Mₘₐₓ(σ) increasing as (1−σ) grows. This fixes the F-scenario bookkeeping (B9) and gives a stability map in σ—far more informative than one scenario at σ = 0.5.

## 3. Corrected analytics

### 3.1 Characteristic equation of the corrected constant-parameter subsystem

With (1′) (w = 1), (4′), constant b, e, r_perc, linearize at (M*, P*). Using a₁₁ = ρ(1 − 2M*/Mₘₐₓ), a₂₁ = r b/r_perc:

(λ − a₁₁ − γθb e^{−λτₘ})(λ + r e^{−λτₚ}) + γ e a₂₁ e^{−λ(τₘ+τₚ)} = 0.   (13′)

Two structural gains over Eq. (13):

- The loop-gain term carries the *round-trip* delay τₘ + τₚ, because a perturbation must pass through both lags to return to its origin. This gives a mechanistic reason for the empirical "instability aligns with contours of τₘ + τₚ" finding, which the original could only report.
- Using the equilibrium identity ρ(1 − M*/Mₘₐₓ) = γb(f − θ), the zero-delay constant term equals rρM*/Mₘₐₓ > 0 for all θ: the interior equilibrium is never a saddle; the only zero-delay condition is the trace condition a₁₁ + γθb < r. Replace Eq. (12) and the "upper branch" language with this (fixes A4).

### 3.2 Replace "no single delay can destabilize" with a classification theorem

For the original structure (θ = 0, current K), the one-delay quartics are

τₚ = 0: ω⁴ + (r² + a₁₁²)ω² + Λ₋ = 0, Λ₋ ≡ r²a₁₁² − (γea₂₁)²
τₘ = 0: ω⁴ + (a₁₁² − r² − 2γea₂₁)ω² − Λ₋ = 0.

The constant terms have opposite signs, so generically **exactly one** lag is individually destabilizing, selected by the sign of

Λ ≡ γ e a₂₁ − r|a₁₁|  (loop gain minus product of self-dampings):

- Λ > 0: environmental lag τₘ alone can trigger a Hopf (over-consumption regime; Scenario B's 83 yr is this case).
- Λ < 0: demographic lag τₚ alone can trigger a Hopf (Hutchinson regime).
- Λ = 0: neither (measure-zero curve; the original baseline sat exactly on it because ρ = 3γeb₀/rₒₚₜ).

State this as Proposition 1, give Λ its physical reading, and then ask the more interesting question the paper was reaching for: how the second lag shifts the threshold of the first. That is the two-delay problem, to be done properly with stability-crossing curves (Gu, Niculescu & Chen 2005) and verified by full-spectrum computation (DDE-BIFTOOL or Julia's DelayDiffEq + spectral solver), replacing the single-branch root tracker (fixes A1–A3, C3).

Note the caveat for the τₘ = 0 quartic: its ω² coefficient can be negative, allowing two positive roots and hence stability *switches* in τₚ—worth reporting, since it is the mechanism by which a moderate demographic lag can be stabilizing.

### 3.3 The productivity illusion as a proposition

With (7′), B = (b₀ + T_b) e^{−αD} M and

d ln B/dt = T_b′/(b₀ + T_b) − αD′ + d ln M/dt.

Biocapacity rises while stock falls iff T_b′/(b₀ + T_b) > αD′ − d ln M/dt. Because T_b′ → 0 (logistic saturation) while D′ > 0 under persistent overshoot, the inequality must eventually fail: the weak-sustainability window is transient, and the peak-biocapacity time t_peak is computable. This converts the paper's rhetorical centrepiece into a proved statement and supplies an observable (the sign change of d ln B/dt decomposed into stock and yield components).

### 3.4 Name the actual collapse mechanism

In the θ = 0, discrete-delay model, depletion in the pipeline γE(t−τₘ) does not scale with M, so once past demand exceeds current regeneration the stock is exhausted in finite time. Call this *committed degradation* (by analogy with committed warming). It is the honest version of "the environmental lag sustains high depletion while the demographic lag delays the response," and it is a stronger message than the fictitious Mₘₐₓ/2 threshold. With the Allee variant, the mechanism becomes a genuine crossing of a separatrix, and Scenario D's basin claim can be demonstrated with (M₀, P₀) basin maps at fixed (τₘ, τₚ), η large.

## (b) Units

**The reviewer is right, and the problem is deeper than a stray "yr⁻¹".** The manuscript mixes two conventions: the GFN convention, in which a global hectare (gha) is *already* an annual flow (the biocapacity of one world-average productive hectare in one year), and a physics convention in which stocks and flows must be distinguished by an explicit time unit. Writing E in "gha yr⁻¹" double-counts the year; writing D in "gha" then makes accumulated overshoot dimensionally identical to a stock of land, which is exactly the confusion Eq. (1) exploits when it subtracts a footprint flow from a land stock one-for-one (γ = 1).

**Revision.** Adopt the GFN convention throughout and separate physical area from productivity-weighted area explicitly:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| A (was M) | Stock of biologically productive area | ha (physical) |
| b | Bioproductivity of that area, i.e. yield factor × equivalence factor | gha ha⁻¹ (per year implicit) |
| B = bA | Biocapacity (annual regenerative flow) | gha |
| E = eP | Ecological footprint (annual demand) | gha |
| e, r_perc | Per-capita footprint / reference requirement | gha cap⁻¹ |
| K = B/r_perc | Carrying capacity | cap |
| Ω = E/B | Overshoot ratio | – |
| D = ∫(E − B)₊ dt | Accumulated ecological deficit | gha·yr |
| α | Productivity sensitivity to accumulated deficit | (gha·yr)⁻¹ |
| γ | Physical area lost per unit of accumulated deficit | ha (gha·yr)⁻¹ |
| ρ, r, η, κ | Rates | yr⁻¹ |
| τₘ, τₚ, τₑ | Lags | yr |

Consequences for the equations:

- Eq. (1) becomes dA/dt = ρA(1 − A/Aₘₐₓ) − γ [E − θB](t−τₘ), with γ now a genuine conversion coefficient (physical hectares degraded per gha·yr of overshoot) rather than a dimensionless "1". Its magnitude is then an empirical question (see (e)), not a modelling convenience.
- Eq. (6) accumulates gha·yr, matching the reviewer's requirement and matching how GFN and Dasgupta (2021) describe cumulative deficit.
- Footnote 1 is deleted and replaced by the table above plus one paragraph explaining the gha convention and why the paper distinguishes A (hectares) from B (gha): this distinction *is* the stock/yield decomposition on which the "productivity illusion" argument rests, so getting the units right strengthens rather than weakens the paper's central point.
- All simulation parameters will be reported in these units, with the non-dimensionalization (t̂ = rt, a = A/Aₘₐₓ, …) stated so that readers can see which combinations actually govern the dynamics.

## (c) Static parameters

**Agreed.** Per-capita requirement and bioproductivity are the two quantities that have changed most in the historical record (per-capita footprint ≈ 2.3 → 2.6 gha, per-capita biocapacity ≈ 3.1 → 1.5 gha, 1961–2022), and treating them as constants was inconsistent with the paper's own claim to model "co-evolving" resource needs.

**Revision.** Both become dynamic; each dynamic law is stated with its assumption and its constant-parameter limit.

*Per-capita footprint e(t)*: three components, any of which can be switched off.
- Affluence drift: de/dt = g_e e (exogenous, Kaya-style; calibrated to 1961–2022 trend).
- Efficiency waves: e = e₀ / (1 + T_e(t)), T_e a sum of logistic waves (bounded gains in resource efficiency—the Green Revolution, decarbonization).
- Scarcity adaptation: de/dt ∋ −ε e [Ω(t−τₑ) − 1], i.e. footprint contracts in response to *perceived* overshoot with a policy lag τₑ (ε = 0 recovers the original).

The reference requirement r_perc(t) is tied to e(t) through the perception gap f = e/r_perc, which is itself allowed to close over time (df/dt = −ξ(f − 1) once Ω is observed), representing improving accounting and awareness.

*Bioproductivity b(t)*: b = [b₀ + T_b(t)]·exp(−αD) (multiplicative debt), with T_b the yield-technology wave. This removes the false additive floor on b and makes the saturation-vs-accumulation asymmetry a property of the equations. A state-dependent regeneration rate ρ(A) and land-type-specific γ are offered as variants in the Supplement.

The paper will make explicit which of these are treated as endogenous (D, b via D, e via adaptation) and which as exogenous forcings (technology wave timing, affluence drift), and will show the sensitivity of the headline results to each.
