Yes — the pasted paper makes the missing empirical object very clear. The World NFA file gives you only the aggregate identity:

\[
B_t,\quad E_t,\quad P_t,\quad R_{B,t}=E_t/B_t,
\]

and therefore supports the per-capita/population decomposition

\[
d\ln B=d\ln(B/P)+d\ln P.
\]

But it does **not** support the two-book composition decomposition because the public World file does not contain:

1. the split between fast provisioning land \(A_f\) and ecological-capital land \(A_c\);  
2. the split between yield/area inside global-hectare biocapacity;  
3. independent proxies for \(b_f\), \(b_c\), or \(b_{G,c}G_c\).

So the empirical programme needs an **external observation operator**. The minimal missing series are:

\[
A_f(t),\quad A_c(t),\quad b_f(t)
\]

and, if possible,

\[
b_c(t),\quad G_c(t),\quad b_{G,c}.
\]

Below is the practical way to find and construct them.

---

# 1. What the paper actually needs to identify

Your model has

\[
B=b_fA_f+b_cA_c+b_{G,c}G_c(A_c).
\]

The NFA World file gives only \(B\). To decompose \(B\), you need independent measures of:

| Model object | Meaning | Empirical object needed |
|---|---|---|
| \(A_f\) | fast provisioning land | cropland / intensive agricultural area |
| \(A_c\) | ecological capital land | forest, wetland, natural land, regenerative land |
| \(b_f\) | fast-land flow yield | crop output per provisioning hectare |
| \(b_c\) | direct capital-land yield | forest/non-timber product yield per capital hectare |
| \(b_{G,c}G_c(A_c)\) | regeneration value | forest growth/increment, ecological capital regeneration proxy |

The first three are the essential missing proxies. The last two are much harder and may have to be treated as residual or scenario parameters unless you bring in forest inventory/ecological data.

# 2. Minimum viable proxy set for the world, 1961–2022

For the paper’s empirical period, the most defensible minimal proxy set is:

## Fast provisioning land \(A_f\)

Use **FAOSTAT land use**:

\[
A_f \approx \text{Arable land} + \text{Permanent crops}.
\]

If you want to include intensive grazing in the fast book, optionally add:

\[
+ \text{Permanent meadows and pastures},
\]

but then you must say explicitly that the fast book includes improved/intensive grassland. The cleanest two-book mapping is:

\[
A_f = \text{cropland only}.
\]

Source:

- FAOSTAT, Land Use domain  
  https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/RL

Variables to download:

- `Arable land`
- `Permanent crops`
- `Permanent meadows and pastures`
- `Forest area`
- `Other land`
- `Total land area`

Coverage:

- 1961 onward for many land-use items.
- Forest area is more limited in FAOSTAT; see below.

---

## Ecological capital land \(A_c\)

The most consistent observable capital-land proxy is **forest area**, because forest is the largest measured ecological-capital land class.

Use:

\[
A_c \approx \text{Forest area} + \text{other natural land},
\]

or, as a sentinel proxy,

\[
A_c \approx \text{Forest area}.
\]

For 1990 onward, use:

- FAOSTAT Forest area  
  https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/RL

or:

- FAO Global Forest Resources Assessment, FRA  
  https://www.fao.org/forestry/en/database/fra/

For 1961–1990, FAO statistical forest area is less consistent. You need a historical land-use reconstruction. Use one of:

### LUH2

- Land-Use Harmonization version 2  
- annual, 850–2015, 0.25°
- cropland, pasture, primary/secondary land, forest states

https://luh.umd.edu/

Use LUH2 forest/primary/secondary natural land as the pre-1990 capital-land proxy, then calibrate or scale it to FAO/FRA forest area where they overlap.

### HYDE 3.2

- History Database of the Global Environment
- long-run cropland, pasture, built-up, unmanaged land
- useful for pre-industrial and 20th-century land-use history

https://www.pbl.nl/en/image/hyde

Citation:

- Klein Goldewijk et al., 2017, *Anthropogenic land use estimates for the last 12,000 years – HYDE 3.2*, Earth System Science Data.

### Recommended capital-land construction

For a world series 1961–2022:

\[
A_c(t) =
\begin{cases}
\text{LUH2 or HYDE natural/forest land, scaled to FRA/FAO forest area}, & 1961\text{–}1990,\\
\text{FAO/FRA forest area}, & 1990\text{–}2022.
\end{cases}
\]

If you want a broader ecological-capital book, add land-cover classes such as wetland, shrubland, and natural grassland from ESA CCI or Copernicus for the satellite era.

---

## Fast-land yield \(b_f\)

The best FAO-yield proxy for the fast book is:

\[
b_f(t) \approx \frac{\text{crop production}}{\text{arable land + permanent crops}}.
\]

You can construct this from FAOSTAT:

- FAOSTAT Crops and Livestock Products  
  https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/QCL

Download:

- `Production`
- `Area harvested`
- `Yield`

for crop items.

The simplest robust proxy is:

\[
b_f(t)=\text{cereals yield}_{FAOSTAT}(t).
\]

This is easy, transparent, and replicable. But cereals are not the whole fast book.

A better proxy is an aggregate crop productivity index:

\[
\tilde b_f(t)=
\frac{\sum_i Q_i(t)}{\text{Arable land}(t)+\text{Permanent crops}(t)},
\]

where \(Q_i(t)\) is production of crop \(i\) in a common physical unit.

Because FAOSTAT production is reported in tonnes by commodity, you should convert to a common productivity metric if possible. The best options are:

1. **calories per hectare**, using FAO food-balance conversion factors;
2. **dry matter per hectare**;
3. **crop production index per hectare**;
4. **cereal yield per hectare** as a simple sentinel.

For a paper whose main claim is identifiability rather than calibration, a normalized index is acceptable:

\[
b_f(t)=b_f(t_0)\frac{\tilde b_f(t)}{\tilde b_f(t_0)}.
\]

Set \(t_0=1961\) or another base year.

| NFA land type | Possible book mapping |
|---|---|

Thus the empirical hierarchy should be:

1. **NFA land-type biocapacity plus physical area/yield factors**, if available.  
2. **FAOSTAT land-use area plus FAOSTAT yields**, as independent external proxies.  
3. **Remote-sensing/historical land cover plus EarthStat/MapSPAM/GAEZ yield proxies**, for validation or extension.

# 5. FAO-yield sources for \(b_f\)

## FAOSTAT crop yields

Primary source:

- FAOSTAT Crops and Livestock Products  
  https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/QCL

Elements:

- `Production`
- `Area harvested`
- `Yield`

Possible yield proxies:

### Simple sentinel yield

\[
b_f(t)=\text{FAOSTAT cereals yield}(t).
\]

Pros:

- simple;
- transparent;
- available 1961 onward;
- globally comparable.

Cons:

- cereals are only part of cropland;
- does not capture all fast-book production.

### Aggregate crop yield

\[
b_f(t)=\frac{\sum_i \text{crop production}_i(t)}{\text{arable land}(t)+\text{permanent crops}(t)}.
\]

Pros:

- broader representation of cropland productivity.

Cons:

- requires commodity aggregation;
- physical tonnes are not homogeneous;
- calories or dry matter are better but require conversion.

### Calorie-based yield

Construct:

\[
b_f(t)=
\frac{\sum_i \text{kcal production}_i(t)}
{\text{arable land}(t)+\text{permanent crops}(t)}.
\]

This is probably the most biocapacity-consistent physical yield proxy because global hectares are productivity-weighted, not price-weighted.

Use FAO food balance sheets or FAO conversion factors to convert commodity tonnes to kcal.

FAOSTAT Food Balances:

- https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/FBS

# 7. Recommended empirical mapping for the paper

For a clean, honest empirical programme, I would use the following mapping.

## Fast book

\[
A_f(t)=\text{FAOSTAT Arable land}(t)+\text{FAOSTAT Permanent crops}(t).
\]

Yield proxy:

\[
\tilde b_f(t)=
\frac{\text{FAOSTAT aggregate crop production}(t)}
{A_f(t)}.
\]

Normalize:

\[
b_f(t)=b_f(t_0)\frac{\tilde b_f(t)}{\tilde b_f(t_0)}.
\]

If NFA cropland biocapacity is available, calibrate:

\[
b_f(t_0)=\frac{B_{\text{cropland,NFA}}(t_0)}{A_f(t_0)}.
\]

Then \(b_f(t)\) is in gha/ha/yr at the base year and grows with the FAO yield index.

---

## Capital book

First-order proxy:

\[
A_c(t)=\text{Forest area}(t).
\]

Better proxy:

\[
A_c(t)=\text{Forest area}(t)+\text{natural grassland/shrub/wetland proxy}(t).
\]

For 1961–1990, use LUH2 or HYDE natural/forest land scaled to FRA forest area. For 1990–2022, use FAO/FRA forest area.

\[
B_c(t)=b_cA_c+b_{G,c}G_c(A_c)
\]

can be treated as residual if you have \(B(t)\), \(A_f(t)\), and \(b_f(t)\):

\[
B_c^{\text{res}}(t)=B_{\text{NFA}}(t)-b_f(t)A_f(t).
\]

But this residual must be interpreted carefully because of units, equivalence factors, and missing land types. It is better to say:

> The capital-book contribution is not directly identified; we report a residual capital-book component after removing the proxy fast-book component.

That is consistent with the paper’s identifiability claim.

# 9. How to construct the empirical decomposition

Let:

\[
B_t = \text{NFA world biocapacity}.
\]

Let:

\[
A_{f,t} = \text{FAOSTAT arable land + permanent crops}.
\]

Let:

\[
\tilde b_{f,t} = \frac{\text{FAOSTAT crop production}}{A_{f,t}}.
\]

Normalize:

\[
b_{f,t}=b_{f,0}\frac{\tilde b_{f,t}}{\tilde b_{f,0}}.
\]

Then define a proxy fast-book biocapacity:

\[
B_{f,t}^{\text{proxy}}=b_{f,t}A_{f,t}.
\]

The residual capital-book component is:

\[
B_{c,t}^{\text{res}}=B_t-B_{f,t}^{\text{proxy}}.
\]

This gives a partial two-book decomposition:

\[
B_t \approx B_{f,t}^{\text{proxy}}+B_{c,t}^{\text{res}}.
\]

But you should state that this is a **proxy reconstruction**, not full structural identification.

A more honest growth decomposition is:

\[
d\ln B_t
=
d\ln A_t
+
d\ln Y_t
+
d\ln \text{composition}_t
+
\varepsilon_t,
\]

where:

- \(A_t\) is land area from FAOSTAT/land cover;
- \(Y_t\) is yield from FAOSTAT;
- composition captures shifts between fast and capital land;
- \(\varepsilon_t\) captures equivalence-factor and residual-capital effects.

# 12. Important caveats to state explicitly

## 1. Forest area is not all ecological capital

The model’s \(A_c\) includes forest, soil, wetland, long-rotation and regenerative systems. Empirically, forest area is the most available sentinel proxy, but it is incomplete.

Say:

> Forest area is used as a sentinel proxy for the ecological-capital book; it does not measure soil carbon, wetland capital, fishery capital, or non-forest regenerative systems.

---

## 2. FAO yields are national statistical yields, not gha yields

FAOSTAT yields are physical output per hectare, not global hectares per hectare. To compare with NFA \(B\), you must either:

- normalize the FAO yield index and use it for growth decomposition; or
- calibrate it to NFA land-type biocapacity if available.

Do not claim absolute \(b_f\) in gha/ha unless calibrated.

---

## 3. The NFA equivalence/yield factors are inside the unit

This is your central point. Even with land areas and FAO yields, full identification of \(b_c\), \(b_{G,c}\), and \(\rho_c\) is not possible without ecological valuation and regeneration data.

The externally observable combinations are closer to:

\[
b_fA_f,
\quad
b_{c,\text{eff}}A_c,
\quad
b_{G,c}G_c,
\]

not the separate structural parameters.

---

## 4. The full two-book decomposition is partially identified at best

The strongest honest empirical claim is:

> Aggregate biocapacity rose while independent ecological-capital proxies fell, but the exact split of biocapacity into yield, area, and book composition is only partially identified.

That is stronger than pretending the proxies solve everything.
