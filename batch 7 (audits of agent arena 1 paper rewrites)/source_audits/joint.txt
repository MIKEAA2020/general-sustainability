## Overall verdict

The joint verification is **substantively credible and improves the paper**. It does three things well:

1. **It finds a real root-cause defect** in the original S5.4 computation: the technology wave \(T_b\) was accidentally left on in the recovery integrator, while the base model uses \(T_b=0\). That defect explains the earlier alarming results:
   \[
   R_c(900)=2.07,\quad A_c\to 2.39,\quad A_r< A_r^{\min},\quad P/P^{\rm init}=0.26.
   \]

2. **It replaces those artefactual numbers with bounded, conserved, in-domain base-model results**, where over-restoration is modest:
   \[
   R_c(900)\approx 1.07\text{–}1.27,\quad P/P^{\rm init}\approx 0.90\text{–}0.97
   \]
   for \(\chi_r=0.05\text{–}0.10\).

3. **It adopts the correct interpretation**: surplus-gating is not “better”; it is phase-dependent. It is inert during an active deficit, can restore only after a surplus appears, and may over-restore relative to the reference target. Deficit-gating is not falsified; it is necessary during the shortfall but can be swamped by conversion unless sized or combined with a conversion limit.

That said, there are still a few **internal-consistency and presentation improvements** that should be made before treating this as fully verified and final.

# 1. What is convincingly resolved

## 1.1. The main S5.4 artefact has been plausibly identified

The key correction is:

> The recovery integrator applied the technology step \(T_b\) unconditionally, even though the reference model uses \(T_b=0\).

This is exactly the kind of bug that can generate dramatic but spurious recovery/overshoot behaviour, because \(T_b\) raises \(b_f\), alters \(B\), \(K\), \(P\), \(E\), debt, and the restoration gate. The corrected base-model behaviour is much more plausible:

# 6. Substantive interpretation I would use

I would summarise the corrected S5.4 result like this:

> The corrected base model shows that surplus-gated restoration is not a general rescue mechanism. It is inactive from a degraded equilibrium because \(B=E\), and it is inactive during an active deficit because \(B-E<0\). It can restore capital only after the system has moved into a surplus state. When it does, it may restore \(A_c\) beyond the reference level, but this over-restoration is bounded and is associated with a modestly lower aggregate biocapacity and supported population. Deficit-gated restoration is the only gate that can act during the shortfall, but at the tested restoration rates it is insufficient because conversion pressure dominates. Therefore the recovery result is not “surplus beats deficit”; it is that recovery requires the right gate, adequate strength, and a bound such as a target cap or conversion ceiling.

That is the strongest and safest version.
