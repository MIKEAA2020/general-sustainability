# Is retitling to a methods framing appropriate now?

## Short answer

**More appropriate than before, but still not clearly warranted.** The simulation changes the calculus in the paper's favor, but not enough to make it a methods paper in the sense that a methods title would promise. The locked title remains defensible; a methods title would require additional work to be honest.

## What changed with the simulation

The paper now has three things it did not have before:

1. **A validated decision instrument.** The retention rule has measurable operating characteristics — specificity 0.97–0.99, false-retention 0.044, power conditional on identifiability. That is a methods contribution.
2. **A diagnostic decomposition.** The H2-only vs full-rule table and the "true module has lowest RMSE" metric are reusable diagnostics, not just case-study findings.
3. **An information-set audit.** Table 2b is a reporting template that other hindcast evaluations could adopt.

These are real. They push the paper toward a methods framing.

## What a methods title would promise

A methods title — something like *"A retention rule and information-set framework for evaluating added structure in stock-assessment forecasting"* — promises:

- The rule is generalizable beyond this stock.
- The framework is reusable by other assessment scientists.
- The operating characteristics are established across conditions.
- The contribution is the method, with northern cod as the worked application.

The current package delivers on the first two partially, the third weakly, and the fourth only if the paper is restructured so that the method leads and the application follows.

## What is missing to deliver on that promise

**1. The simulation is too narrow to support a general claim.**
Only \(T=33\), two \(\sigma\) values, four DGPs, all from the ladder's own class. No misspecified truth, no sensitivity to the tie band or horizon pair, no variation in catch contrast. The adequate-power finding for D1 is explicitly an upper bound. A methods paper needs a power map, not a power point.

**2. No comparison to alternative rules.**
The paper never asks: how does this retention rule compare to AIC, BIC, MASE alone, or simply "beat persistence by any margin"? Without that, the reader cannot judge whether the rule is a contribution or just one reasonable choice among many.

**3. No demonstration of reuse.**
The Edwards Aquifer application is mentioned but not included. A framework with one application is a case study; a framework with two is a framework.

**4. The rule's power is conditional on identifiability, and the paper does not propose a pre-check.**
The simulation's most useful finding is that the rule has power when the true structure is identifiable and none when it is not. A methods paper should turn that into a protocol: *check identifiability first; apply the retention rule only where it has power.* That step is not yet taken.

**5. The simulation is reported as a diagnostic, not integrated into the method.**
It sits alongside the empirical result. In a methods paper it would be the validation of the instrument, with the empirical result as the demonstration.

## If the title stays locked

The paper remains **a case study with a pre-registered validation of its decision rule.** That is a strong paper. The methods contribution lives in the body, signaled by the abstract, keywords, and a clearly labeled simulation section. The locked title remains the question the paper answers.

This is the safer path. It does not overpromise, and it does not require the additional work that a methods title would demand.

## If the title is unlocked

The paper becomes **a methods contribution with northern cod as the worked application.** That requires:

- Restructuring so the retention rule, information-set framework, and simulation lead; the cod result follows as demonstration.
- Adding a comparison to alternative selection rules.
- Expanding the simulation to include misspecified truth and sensitivity to the tie band and horizon pair.
- Ideally, adding the Edwards Aquifer application.

Without those, a methods title overpromises and invites reviewer skepticism.

## What more can be done to elevate the framework

Ranked by value-to-effort:

**1. Add a misspecified-truth DGP (highest value, low effort).**
All current DGPs are from the ladder's own class, so power is an upper bound. Add one DGP where the truth is outside the ladder — e.g., an age-structured or regime-switching process — and report power. This gives a lower bound and makes the power claim honest.

**2. Add a "when to apply this rule" pre-check (high value, low effort).**
Formalize the simulation's central finding: the rule has power only when the true structure is identifiable from the data. Propose a pre-check — a profile-likelihood or parameter-compensation diagnostic — that tells the analyst whether to apply the rule at all. This turns a negative finding into a positive protocol.

**3. Compare the rule to alternatives (high value, moderate effort).**
Apply the same simulation to: (a) no rule, just RMSE; (b) AIC-based selection; (c) MASE alone; (d) a simpler "beat persistence" rule. Report power and specificity for each. If the retention rule dominates, that is a methods contribution. If not, that is also a finding.

**4. Add a sensitivity analysis (moderate value, low effort).**
Vary the tie band (1%, 5%, 10%), the horizon pair (1 and 3, 1 and 5, 1 and 10), and the loss function (RMSE, MAE, log-RMSE). Report how retention rates change. This shows whether the rule's properties are robust or fragile.

**5. Add the Edwards Aquifer application (high value, high effort).**
A second application turns the framework into a framework. If Abaee (2026a) is available and coordinated, include it.

**6. Formalize the rule as an algorithm (moderate value, low effort).**
Add pseudocode or a decision flowchart for the retention rule, including the identifiability pre-check. This makes the method portable.

**7. Add a "how to report" template (moderate value, low effort).**
The information-set table is a good start. Add a reporting checklist: what to report, in what order, with what diagnostics. This is what a methods paper offers its readers.

**8. Report the simulation's power map as a figure (moderate value, low effort).**
A heatmap of power by DGP and \(\sigma\), with the pre-declared thresholds marked, would communicate the result more effectively than a table.

## My judgment

**Retitling is defensible but not yet warranted.** The simulation is a necessary step, not a sufficient one. If you want a methods title, do items 1–4 above and consider item 5. If you are not prepared to do those, keep the locked title and present the simulation as a validation of the rule within a case study. That is an honest, publishable paper.

**The single highest-value addition** is the identifiability pre-check. It converts the simulation's mixed result into a positive methodological contribution: *here is a rule, here is when it works, and here is how to tell in advance.* That is what a methods paper delivers, and it is achievable without the full multi-DGP expansion.
