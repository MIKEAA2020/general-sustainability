## 1. Fatal / high-severity issues

### 1.1 The units of ecological debt \(D\) are inconsistent in Eq. (11)

The symbol table says:

\[
D \text{ has units } \mathrm{gha\cdot yr}
\]

and later the text claims:

> “\([E-B]_+\) (in \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr^{-1}}\)) integrates to \(D\) (in \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr}\))”

But an integral of \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr^{-1}}\) over time gives \(\mathrm{gha}\), not \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr}\).

Moreover, Eq. (11):

\[
\frac{dD}{dt}=[E-B]_+-\eta D
\]

is dimensionally inconsistent if \(D\) is \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr}\):

- \(dD/dt\): \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr / yr} = \mathrm{gha}\)
- \([E-B]_+\): \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr^{-1}}\)
- \(\eta D\): \(\mathrm{yr^{-1}\cdot gha\cdot yr} = \mathrm{gha}\)

So the right-hand side contains both \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr^{-1}}\) and \(\mathrm{gha}\). This is not a minor wording issue; it affects the debt equation, degradation exponent \(\alpha D\), and all later debt-related claims.

**Fix:** Most likely \(D\) should be in \(\mathrm{gha}\), not \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr}\). Then \(\eta D\) has units \(\mathrm{gha\cdot yr^{-1}}\), matching \([E-B]_+\), and \(\alpha\) must be \(\mathrm{gha^{-1}}\), not \(\mathrm{gha^{-1}\cdot yr^{-1}}\).

## 1.1. The baseline `A_{c,max}` conflicts with the demonstration initial condition

### Where

Baseline registry:

> `$A_{c,\max}$ & $1.2$\,ha & capital carrying-area ceiling`

Demonstration:

> `With a fixed demand $E=0.825$ ... $A_{f0}=0.6$, $A_{c0}=1.5$, $P_0=1.5$`

Also in Results:

> `$A_c:1.500\to0.558$`

### Problem

If \(A_{c,\max}=1.2\) is a “carrying-area ceiling”, then \(A_{c0}=1.5\) is outside the admissible domain. With the logistic regeneration function

\[
G_c(A_c)=\rho_c A_c\left(1-\frac{A_c}{A_{c,\max}}\right),
\]

\(G_c\) becomes negative for \(A_c>A_{c,\max}\). That may be mathematically possible, but it contradicts the interpretation of \(A_{c,\max}\) as a ceiling. It also makes the demonstration run start in a region where “regeneration” is negative, which confuses the ecological interpretation.

### Fix

Choose one:

1. **Raise \(A_{c,\max}\)** so that all demonstration initial values are inside the domain, e.g. \(A_{c,\max}\ge 1.5\) or \(1.6\).  
2. **Change the demonstration initial condition** to \(A_{c0}\le A_{c,\max}\).  
3. **Reinterpret \(A_{c,\max}\)** not as a hard ceiling but as the peak-flow stock level, and explicitly allow \(A_c>A_{c,\max}\) with negative net regeneration. But then “carrying-area ceiling” is misleading.

This must be fixed because the same parameter appears in analytic results, baseline tables, and numerical demonstrations.

## 1.2. `G_c` is called “not a land-area transition”, but the equation makes it one

### Where

Convention 4:

> “Regeneration is a growth flow, not a land-area transition. $G_c(A_c)$ adds to capital-land accounting capacity ... it is not an $A_r\to A_c$ area transfer unless a restoration flow $R_{rc}$ is explicitly active.”

But Eq. (5):

\[
\frac{dA_c}{dt}=G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))-\frac{S}{\kappa}-L_c+R_{fc}+R_{rc}.
\]

And Eq. (7):

\[
A_r=A_{tot}-A_f-A_c.
\]

### Problem

If \(A_c\) is measured in hectares and \(A_{tot}\) is fixed, then any positive \(G_c\) increasing \(A_c\) automatically reduces \(A_r\). That is, dynamically, an area transfer from reserve to capital, even if it is not written as an explicit \(A_r\to A_c\) term.

So the statement that \(G_c\) is “not a land-area transition” is inconsistent with the model equations unless \(A_c\) is not physical area but some effective “capital capacity” index. But the table says \(A_c\) has units ha and \(A_r\) is reserve land.

### Fix

Choose one interpretation and state it cleanly.

#### Option A: Treat \(G_c\) as succession from reserve to capital

Then rewrite convention 4:

> Regeneration represents natural succession or recovery that increases effective capital land and correspondingly reduces reserve land through the land balance.

#### Option B: Treat \(A_c\) as effective capital capacity, not physical area

Then change the table and language:

> \(A_c\) is effective capital-land capacity measured in hectare-equivalents, not necessarily physical land area; \(A_r\) is an accounting residual.

But then “reserve land” and “total bioproductive area” need careful interpretation.

As written, the text contradicts the equations.

## 1.3. The deficit \(S\) and debt \(D\) use different overshoot definitions

### Where

Eq. (4):

\[
S=[E-\sigma_f Y_f-\sigma_c Y_c]_+.
\]

Eq. (11):

\[
\frac{dD}{dt}=[E-B]_+ - \eta D.
\]

Convention 1:

> “only a positive shortfall above the harvestable/share-adjusted flow converts land or accrues debt”

### Problem

Conversion uses the share-adjusted available flow:

\[
\sigma_f Y_f+\sigma_c Y_c.
\]

Debt uses total biocapacity:

\[
B=Y_f+Y_c.
\]

If \(\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1\), these are consistent. But the model defines \(\sigma_f,\sigma_c\) and never gives baseline values. If \(\sigma_f,\sigma_c<1\), then it is possible to have:

\[
S>0
\]

while

\[
E<B,
\]

so conversion occurs but debt does not accrue. That contradicts the convention that the same shortfall “converts land or accrues debt”.

### Fix

Either:

1. **Set and state** \(\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1\) in the baseline registry, and simplify Eq. (4) to  
   \[
   S=[E-B]_+,
   \]
   or

2. **Change debt equation** to match the share-adjusted deficit:  
   \[
   \frac{dD}{dt}=[E-\sigma_fY_f-\sigma_cY_c]_+ - \eta D,
   \]
   or

3. **Explicitly distinguish** two deficits:  
   - access deficit \(S\), driving conversion;  
   - ecological overshoot \([E-B]_+\), driving debt.

But then do not say that the same positive shortfall does both unless conditions are specified.

This is central because several later claims about masking with \(R_B<1\) depend on this distinction.

## 1.4. The relation \(R_A=R_B/\psi_c\) is algebraically incorrect as defined

### Where

Analytic results:

\[
R_B=\frac{E}{B},\qquad R_A=\frac{E}{Y_f},\qquad \psi_c=\frac{b_cA_c}{Y_c},\qquad R_A=\frac{R_B}{\psi_c}.
\]

### Problem

Given the definitions,

\[
\frac{R_B}{\psi_c}
=
\frac{E/B}{b_cA_c/Y_c}
=
\frac{E Y_c}{B b_c A_c},
\]

which is not generally equal to

\[
R_A=\frac{E}{Y_f}.
\]

The identity would require something like

\[
\psi_f = \frac{Y_f}{B},
\]

then

\[
R_A = \frac{E}{Y_f}=\frac{E/B}{Y_f/B}=\frac{R_B}{\psi_f}.
\]

But \(\psi_c=b_cA_c/Y_c\) is the share of the capital-book yield that is direct yield, not the fast-book share of total biocapacity.

### Fix

Either:

1. Define a fast-book share  
   \[
   \psi_f=\frac{Y_f}{B},
   \]
   and write  
   \[
   R_A=\frac{R_B}{\psi_f}.
   \]

2. Or remove the identity  
   \[
   R_A=R_B/\psi_c.
   \]

This should be corrected because the macro-ratio section relies on it.

## 1.5. Sign error in \(\partial S/\partial D\)

### Where

Conversion-loop section:

\[
\frac{\partial S}{\partial D}=-\alpha\sigma_fY_f.
\]

### Problem

From

\[
Y_f=b_f A_f,
\]

and

\[
b_f=(b_{f0}+T_b)e^{-\alpha D},
\]

we have

\[
\frac{\partial b_f}{\partial D}=-\alpha b_f.
\]

Thus

\[
\frac{\partial Y_f}{\partial D}=-\alpha Y_f.
\]

Since

\[
S=E-\sigma_fY_f-\sigma_cY_c,
\]

then

\[
\frac{\partial S}{\partial D}
=
-\sigma_f\frac{\partial Y_f}{\partial D}
=
+\alpha\sigma_fY_f.
\]

The sign should be positive: more debt reduces \(b_f\), reduces available fast yield, and therefore increases the deficit.

### Fix

Replace with:

\[
\frac{\partial S}{\partial D}=+\alpha\sigma_fY_f.
\]

This matters because the debt feedback loop is described as a positive feedback. The sign error contradicts the verbal loop description.

## 1.6. The masking condition \(R_B<1\) while \(\dot A_c<0\) is inconsistent unless additional assumptions are stated

### Where

Analytic results:

> “the masking signal is $R_B<1$ (or $R_A\lesssim1$) while $\dot A_c<0$”

### Problem

If \(\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1\), then

\[
S=[E-B]_+.
\]

If \(R_B<1\), then \(E<B\), so \(S=0\). With no timber liquidation \(L_c\) and no restoration, Eq. (5) gives

\[
\dot A_c=G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))\ge 0
\]

for \(A_c\in[0,A_{c,\max}]\). So capital land does not fall when \(R_B<1\).

Therefore, \(R_B<1\) with \(\dot A_c<0\) can occur only if:

1. \(\sigma_f,\sigma_c<1\), so demand can exceed the available share-adjusted flow even while \(E<B\);  
2. there is timber liquidation \(L_c>0\);  
3. \(A_c>A_{c,\max}\), so \(G_c<0\); or  
4. some other exogenous capital loss is present.

But the paper does not state which of these is operating in the masking prediction.

### Fix

Qualify the prediction:

> When available access shares satisfy \(\sigma_f,\sigma_c<1\), or when liquidation \(L_c>0\), capital land can decline even while aggregate biocapacity exceeds footprint, i.e. \(R_B<1\).

If the baseline assumes \(\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1\), then the masking signal should not be written as \(R_B<1\). It would instead be associated with \(R_B>1\) or with a different observable.

This is important because Prediction 1 is one of the falsifiable predictions.

## 1.7. The empirical proxy decomposition is not additive as presented

### Where

Proxy two-book decomposition table:

| Term | \(d\ln\) |
|---|---:|
| Biocapacity \(B\) | \(+0.207\) |
| Land area \(A_f\) | \(+0.160\) |
| Yield / technology \(b_f\) | \(+1.128\) |
| Capital-book residual \(B_c^{\mathrm{res}}\) | \(-0.404\) |

### Problem

The components do not sum:

\[
0.160+1.128-0.404=0.884\neq 0.207.
\]

If the table reports unweighted log changes of components, that is fine, but then they are not contributions to \(d\ln B\). If they are intended as contributions to aggregate growth, they need weights.

For

\[
B = X+Z,
\]

where

\[
X=b_fA_f,
\qquad
Z=B_c^{\mathrm{res}},
\]

a log-growth decomposition is approximately

\[
d\ln B
\approx
\frac{X}{B}d\ln X
+
\frac{Z}{B}d\ln Z,
\]

and

\[
d\ln X=d\ln b_f+d\ln A_f.
\]

So the contribution of yield growth is not simply \(d\ln b_f=1.128\); it is

\[
\frac{X}{B}d\ln b_f.
\]

Without weights, the statement

> “the growth of aggregate biocapacity is dominated by the yield channel ($+1.13$ ln units)”

does not follow.

### Fix

Either:

1. Present the table as **component log changes**, not contributions:

   > The fast-book yield index rose by \(+1.128\) log points, cropland area by \(+0.160\), while the residual capital-book component fell by \(-0.404\). These are not additive contributions to aggregate \(d\ln B\) because the components enter with changing budget shares.

2. Or present a proper weighted growth decomposition:

   \[
   \Delta \ln B
   =
   \bar w_X \Delta\ln X
   +
   \bar w_Z \Delta\ln Z,
   \]

   with  
   \[
   \Delta\ln X=\Delta\ln b_f+\Delta\ln A_f.
   \]

   Report the weights \(\bar w_X,\bar w_Z\).

Also, if \(B_c^{\mathrm{res}}\) becomes zero or negative, a log decomposition is not well-defined. In that case use level changes:

\[
\Delta B = \Delta(b_fA_f)+\Delta B_c^{\mathrm{res}}.
\]

> “Aggregated measured biocapacity rose while independent ecological-capital proxies either fell or were not recoverable...”

### Problem

The residual

\[
B_c^{\mathrm{res}}=B_{\mathrm{NFA}}-b_fA_f
\]

is not independent of \(B_{\mathrm{NFA}}\). It is an accounting residual. It also contains every non-cropland component of the NFA: grazing land, forest products, fishing grounds, built-up land, equivalence-factor effects, and measurement error. It is therefore not a clean ecological-capital proxy.

Calling it “independent” conflicts with the paper’s own identifiability message.

### Fix

Use more cautious language:

> The capital-book component was not independently observed; it was inferred as a residual after removing a proxy fast-book component. This residual should not be interpreted as a direct ecological-capital measure, because it also includes grazing, forestry, fishing, built-up land, and accounting-convention effects.

Then the strongest claim becomes:

> Independent cropland-area and crop-yield proxies show that the fast-book yield channel grew strongly, while the residual non-fast-book component declined. This is consistent with the composition-mask mechanism but does not structurally identify ecological capital.

That is more defensible and actually strengthens the identifiability argument.

## 1.9. Annual growth rates in the real-series paragraph appear arithmetically inconsistent

### Where

> “Over 1961--2022 aggregate biocapacity rose $+23.0\%$ ($+0.377\%\,\text{yr}^{-1}$; $\ln B=+0.207$); per-capita biocapacity $B/P$ fell $-52.9\%$ ($-0.867\%\,\text{yr}^{-1}$) ...”

### Problem

For 1961 to 2022 there are 61 annual intervals.

If

\[
\Delta\ln B=0.207,
\]

then the average log growth rate is

\[
0.207/61=0.00339=0.339\%\,\text{yr}^{-1},
\]

not \(0.377\%\).

If per-capita biocapacity fell by \(52.9\%\), then

\[
\ln(1-0.529)=\ln(0.471)=-0.752.
\]

Dividing by 61 gives

\[
-0.0123=-1.23\%\,\text{yr}^{-1},
\]

not \(-0.867\%\).

The contribution percentages — population \(+464\%\), per-capita \(-364\%\) — are consistent with the log changes \(+0.959\) and \(-0.752\). But the annualised rates need correction or a clear statement of a different convention.

### Fix

Recompute all annualised rates using the exact sample length and state the convention. For 1961–2022:

\[
\text{annual log growth}=\frac{\Delta\ln x}{2022-1961}.
\]

So, if the log changes are correct:

- \(B\): \(+0.207\) over 61 years \(\Rightarrow +0.34\%\,\text{yr}^{-1}\)
- \(B/P\): \(-0.752\) over 61 years \(\Rightarrow -1.23\%\,\text{yr}^{-1}\)
- \(P\): \(+0.959\) over 61 years \(\Rightarrow +1.57\%\,\text{yr}^{-1}\)

If the reported annual rates come from a different period length or a non-log compound average, state that explicitly.

## 1.10. Hysteresis is described as bistable, but the stated branches do not establish bistability

### Where

Discussion table:

> “Hysteresis / path dependence & Conditional & A property of the fold (bistable equilibria), not of the deficit dynamics”

Earlier:

> “At an active-conversion equilibrium the low-$A_c$ (high-conversion) branch is a saddle ... and the high-$A_c$ (low-conversion) branch is stable.”

### Problem

A fold with one stable branch and one saddle branch is not by itself bistable. Hysteresis generally requires two alternative stable equilibria separated by an unstable branch. If the only stable branch is the high-\(A_c\) branch and the other branch is a saddle, then the fold can generate a threshold/collapse, but not necessarily hysteresis in the strict dynamical-systems sense.

### Fix

Either:

1. Show that there is another stable branch, perhaps under a different active set or with restoration, and then claim hysteresis; or  
2. Replace “bistable equilibria” with “fold-induced threshold dynamics” or “saddle-node transition”.

Suggested wording:

> Hysteresis is conditional and requires an additional stable branch; the fold itself establishes a threshold, not necessarily bistability.

#### 1.1 The Double-Counting of $G_c(A_c)$ and the Land-Conservation Breach
Equation (5) and the accounting identity in Equation (7) break the physical conservation of land area and double-count biological growth:

#### 2.1 Dimensional Inconsistency of Ecological Debt $D$
In Section 2.1 and Equation (11):
$$\frac{dD}{dt} = [E - B]_+ - \eta D \tag{11}$$
- Section 2.1 defines $D$ with units $\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}$, and $[E - B]_+$ as a flow in $\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}^{-1}$.

*Correction*: The integral of an annual flow $[E - B]_+$ ($\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}^{-1}$) over time $dt$ ($\text{yr}$) has units $\mathbf{gha}$, not $\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}$. $$R_B = \frac{E}{B}, \qquad R_A = \frac{E}{Y_f}, \qquad \psi_c = \frac{b_c A_c}{Y_c}$$

### 4. Epistemological & Methodological Fallacies

#### 4.1 The $d\ln B$ Population Artifact Fallacy
In Section 12.3, the manuscript argues:
> *"Because the identity $d\ln B = d\ln(B/P) + d\ln P$ is exact, the contribution split over the period is unambiguous: population $+0.959$ ($+464\%$ of $d\ln B$) and per-capita $-0.752$ ($-364\%$). So the aggregate biocapacity rise in these accounts is a population-scale artefact --- it masks a $53\%$ per-capita (per-person) halving, and the population term is larger than the whole rise."*

This conflates an accounting identity with a causal mechanism:
- Biocapacity $B$ is a measure of biophysical area and agricultural yield ($\sum \text{Area}_i \times \text{Yield Factor}_i \times \text{Equivalence Factor}_i$). It is calculated independently of human population size.
- Defining $y = B/P$ means $B \equiv y \cdot P$. Differentiating gives $d\ln B = d\ln y + d\ln P$.
- Attributing the growth of $B$ to $P$ via this identity is mathematically equivalent to defining the land area of a continent as $A = (A/P) \cdot P$ and asserting that the continent's physical area is an artifact of population growth because $d\ln P > 0$ while $d\ln(A/P) < 0$.
- Global biocapacity rose $+23\%$ between 1961 and 2022 because agricultural crop yields tripled ($b_f \uparrow$). Because population grew faster ($+167\%$), per-capita biocapacity fell. Population growth did not create the $23\%$ rise in biocapacity; it caused per-capita provisioning to decline despite rising yields.

#### 4.2 Mischaracterization of Critical Slowing Down (CSD)
In Section 4.2, Section 5, and Table 4, the manuscript claims:
> *"Critical slowing down is present at the fold... verified: $\lambda_+ = 0.058 \to 0.0379 \to 0.0225 \to 0.0120 \to 0.0003$ as $E \to E_{\text{ceil}}$"*

This misapplies bifurcation theory:
- $\lambda_+$ is the **unstable eigenvalue of the saddle branch**. On an unstable saddle, trajectories diverge exponentially; perturbations do not recover, so return time is undefined.
- Critical slowing down is a phenomenon of the **stable attractor branch** ($\lambda_- \to 0^-$). As the stable node approaches the fold, the recovery rate of perturbations back to the attractor vanishes, leading to increased variance and autocorrelation.
- Reporting the decay of an unstable eigenvalue on an unobservable saddle branch as evidence of observable critical slowing down indicates a confusion between attractor dynamics and separatrix dynamics.

## 1. Fatal: `S/κ` does not have the units of `dA/dt`

## 2. Fatal: `G_c` cannot sit in `dA_c/dt` if `A_c` is land that sums to `A_{tot}`

You state both of the following:

1. Regeneration “is a growth flow, not a land-area transition… not an `A_r → A_c` transfer unless `R_{rc}` is active” (avoids “book-mixing”).
2. `A_r = A_{tot} - A_f - A_c` and “the fixed-total land constraint is satisfied *dynamically* by conservation of (5)–(7)”.

From (5)–(6) with `S=L_c=R_{fc}=0`:

\[
\dot A_f + \dot A_c = G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g)) - \eta_f A_f.
\]

The residual `A_r` then **must** fall when `G_c>0`. That *is* colonization of reserve by capital land, which you forbade. If instead `G_c` is standing-stock increment (biomass, m³, gha of timber) it does not belong in an equation whose left-hand side is **hectares of land**.

Same object cannot be:

- a conserved land-cover compartment (`A_f + A_c + A_r = A_{tot}`, conversion `S/κ`), and
- a logistic biomass/capacity stock (`G_c = ρ_c A_c(1-A_c/A_{c,max})`).

Machine-precision `|A_f+A_c+A_r-A_{tot}|=2.2\times10^{-16}` is **vacuous**: it holds by definition of (7), whatever (5)–(6) do. It is not evidence of conservation.

**Minimal well-posed split** (one extra state, still Allee-free):

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `A_c` | capital **area** (ha), converts 1:1 with `A_f`, `A_r` |
| `Q_c` | standing quality/stock on that area (tC ha⁻¹ or gha ha⁻¹) |
| `G_c` | acts on `Q_c`, not on `A_c` |

Conversion moves **area**; timber harvest `L_c` draws down `Q_c`; `Y_c = Y_c(A_c,Q_c)`. Without that split you have not built two books. You have built one book with two names.
