# Evaluation, Strengthening, and Completion — Which v3?

## 1. Verdict on the memo

The memo is correct on its central call. **Pursue v3-C as the margin specification; decline v3-A; absorb v3-B as the other sign.** The third-pool finding (Barton Springs / Lovelady) is real, and my prior "no valid v3 exists" verdict was overreaching — I generalized a Uvalde-specific structural fact ("no physical threshold analog") into a global impossibility. The memo catches that, and the catch is fair.

But the memo has **four load-bearing gaps** and **one internal tension** that need to be closed before v3-C can be written. None of them overturn the recommendation; all of them determine whether the resulting v3 is actually confirmatory or just looks that way.

---

## 2. What to adopt verbatim

These moves are correct and should be preserved unchanged:

- **"The exploratory pool is spent as a confirmatory object."** This is the sharpest formulation of the problem, and it applies to Uvalde without exception.
- **B and C are signs of one relation.** `m = K − H*₀`. The memo's unification is right, and the argument that a v3 should state the relation rather than one sign is the single best structural insight in the memo.
- **v3-A is a correction to a frozen paper, not a generalization.** The "revises not scopes" argument is decisive and should be adopted as a standing rule: *a v3 generalizes a paper; it does not silently retire one.*
- **The discipline note.** Verifying existence without computing statistics is exactly right. The pool is naive precisely because the memo resisted the urge to check.
- **The window-matched recharge correction.** 0.172 vs 0.168 is the like-for-like figure; my 0.182 was cross-window. Accepted.

---

## 3. Where I was wrong (on the record)

Three specific errors in my prior memo:

1. **"No valid v3 exists" was an overreach.** The correct claim is narrower: *no valid v3 exists whose confirmatory test is Uvalde or whose constructive leg requires a Uvalde-type pool.* The memo's restatement is right.
2. **I collapsed "no third pool named" into "no third pool exists."** Those are different. The memo names one.
3. **I treated v3-A/B/C as rival generalizations.** The memo's B/C unification is correct. My framing was wrong.

The memo does not need to be defensive about correcting these. It should say so plainly and move on.

---

## 4. Where the memo needs strengthening

### 4.1 The R04 argument for Barton Springs is asserted, not demonstrated

The memo says Barton Springs is "separately regulated by a different district than the EAA — so under R04 it is a distinct pool." That is a **jurisdictional** argument, not an **R04** argument. R04 is about "judgment transfer across failing typed-field maps." The questions R04 actually asks are:

- What is the typed-field map for Barton Springs / Lovelady?
- Is it a *failing* map by the same criterion that made Uvalde's a failing map?
- If it is failing, is the v3 transferring a *judgment* across it, or is it transferring a *scalar relation* (the margin sign) that is not a judgment about the map?

The memo's own §5.2 answer — *"the margin lemma is unit-agnostic, it is a property of a linear stock-flow map, not of feet"* — is the right R04 answer, but it belongs in §1, not buried in the pre-registration sketch. **The R04 case should be: no judgment is transferred; a sign prediction about a scalar relation is tested.** If that argument fails, Barton Springs is not a valid test pool, regardless of its hydrology.

### 4.2 The "declared family" equivalence is unstated

E4 studies "the declared pumping family `P ∈ [0, K_PHYS]`" with a specific functional form (constant, reactive, etc.). BSEACD's HCP cap is a *curtailment rule*, not obviously the same family. The margin lemma depends on the family's functional form. Before v3-C can be pre-registered on Barton Springs, the memo needs to state:

- What is the BSEACD declared family, functionally?
- Is it a subset of, superset of, or the same as E4's family?
- If different, does the margin lemma still apply?

This is the most consequential gap. The lemma is only as strong as its family assumptions.

### 4.3 H1/H2 failure modes are unspecified

The memo says: "require H1 `0 < a < 1` and H2 `γ < 0` to hold on the new pool, and *report* them rather than assuming them." Good. But it does not say what happens if they **fail**. Three cases need pre-specification:

- **H1 fails (`a ≥ 1`):** the fixpoint formula `H*₀ = (α + βF)/(1 − a)` is undefined. Does this falsify the v3, or is it a scope exclusion?
- **H2 fails (`γ ≥ 0`):** pumping increases head, which is physically implausible but statistically possible on short records. Falsification or scope exclusion?
- **Both hold but the sign prediction is wrong:** this is the clean falsification case and should be stated as such.

Without this, the v3 has no decision rule for half its possible outcomes.

### 4.4 The "close to definitional" argument for B is too strong

The memo says `m > 0` for institutional thresholds is "close to definitional, and a v3 whose content is 'the near-definitional case recurs across pools' generalizes little." That is true *if* institutional thresholds are always set above the natural drought equilibrium. It is not true *if* a jurisdiction ever sets an institutional threshold below it — which happens when regulators set thresholds at levels the system can actually hold. The qualifier "almost" is doing a lot of work.

**Strengthening:** state B as a *testable* half rather than a near-definitional corollary. If the relation is right, B's half should hold for pools where `m > 0`, and fail where `m < 0` — including institutional thresholds. That is falsifiable and worth pre-registering.

### 4.5 The fallback to v3-B on the same pool is weak

If Lovelady coverage fails and v3-C falls back to v3-B on Barton Springs, the fallback is *not* a strong test, for two reasons:

1. Per §4.4, v3-B's content is close to definitional.
2. Per §5.1 of the memo, the BSEACD family equivalence is unresolved.

The memo should either (a) name a *different* fallback pool for v3-B, or (b) accept that if the gate fails, the right response is to *not write a v3 yet*, rather than to write a weak one.

## 5. What's missing

### 5.1 The E3 half of the program is unaddressed

The memo's v3-C generalizes E4. It declines v3-A. It says nothing about what a strengthened E3 generalization looks like. That leaves half the stated goal ("strengthen and generalize the two papers") without a path.

The honest options for E3 are:

- **E3 does not generalize in verdict form** (the Uvalde record's finding). State this as the paper's scope limit.
- **E3 generalizes in mechanism form** (recharge whiteness + head persistence as moderator). This is v3-A's content but not its confirmatory test. It can be stated as a *theoretical* extension, tested only where a pool is genuinely naive.

The memo should say which of these it accepts. Silence implies the E3 generalization is deferred indefinitely, which may be correct but should be explicit.

### 5.2 The margin lemma's derivation is not given

The memo cites the lemma ("0 violations / 15 cases") and uses `m = K − H*₀`. It does not restate the derivation. For a decision memo this is fine; for a v3 it is not. The v3 should either restate the lemma with proof or cite the derivation precisely. The memo should flag this as a required v3 element.

### 5.3 The multiplicity count is not enumerated

The memo says "multiplicity across pools × thresholds × floors × definitions." With:

- 1 primary pool (Barton Springs) + fallback(s)
- 3 thresholds (per the Uvalde pattern: 6.5, 49.7, plus one)
- 3 floors (drought, q05, q10)
- 2–4 driver definitions
- 2 signs of the margin

that is roughly 36–72 comparisons before horizons. The memo should state the correction *and the count*, pre-registered. Otherwise "multiplicity stated" is a gesture, not a control.

### 5.4 The erratum is under-utilized

The M1-loss mechanism (AR(1) converges on persistence as AC(1) → 1, so M1's edge shrinks toward estimation noise) is a real finding. It is currently filed as "a note to add to §3.2." It is stronger than that:

- It **predicts** the direction of the retention flip without fitting the flip.
- It is **monotone** in AC(1), so it is testable on a third pool.
- It is **mechanistic**, not statistical, so it does not have the underpowering problem of D1–D3.

This should be elevated to a candidate *secondary* prediction for v3-C's pool, or at minimum to a standalone note in the E3 paper. It partially rehabilitates the two-pool comparison even though the point-rule flip is noise.

## 6. The internal tension

The memo's §2 argues that **power, not existence, is the decisive criterion**, and that v3-C is chosen because the margin sign has "effectively deterministic power." This is right.

But §6 then says **Lovelady coverage is probably ~35 usable years**, which is short enough to interact "badly with the underpowering problem." If the pool is underpowered *on the head side*, the deterministic-power argument for the *margin* side is weakened — not because the lemma fails, but because estimating `(a, α, β, γ)` to locate `H*₀` is itself a statistical problem on 35 years.

The memo's mitigation (state the v3 in springflow units, which has a long record) is good — but it presupposes that the *springflow* dynamics are also well-described by a linear stock-flow map with a computable fixpoint. That presupposition is untested. **The v3 should pre-register a diagnostic for it: fit the same map form to springflow and check H1/H2 before testing the sign prediction.** If the diagnostic fails, the springflow specification is not a valid port either.

---

## 7. Completed recommendation

Restating the memo's verdict with the gaps closed:

**Pursue v3-C as the margin specification, conditional on:**

1. A demonstrated R04 case — *no judgment is transferred; a scalar relation is tested* (§4.1).
2. A demonstrated family equivalence between the BSEACD declared family and E4's `P ∈ [0, K_PHYS]` family (§4.2).
3. Pre-specified decision rules for H1/H2 failure, sign-prediction failure, and both-sign outcomes (§4.3, §4.4).
4. A long-record springflow specification with its own H1/H2 pre-check (§6).
5. The multiplicity count enumerated, not gestured at (§5.3).

**The feasibility gate is the binding constraint.** If Lovelady cannot be retrieved or is too short, and if springflow cannot be fit to the map form with H1/H2 holding, then the correct action is **not to write a v3**. The fallback to v3-B on Barton Springs is too weak to justify the versioning cost.

**Decline v3-A as a v3. Preserve its mechanism as an E3 scope note** — recharge whiteness as the mechanism, head AC(1) as the moderator, M1's convergence as the proximate cause of the retention flip. This is a real contribution to E3 even if it does not generalize E3's verdict.

**Uvalde remains the motivating case, never the test.** The memo's discipline on this is correct.

---

## 8. The one thing the memo should add to its own discipline note

The memo says it verified existence without computing statistics. That is right. It should add one more sentence:

> The same discipline applies to v3-A's moderator check: no candidate pool's AC(1) may be inspected to determine whether it is "in the untested range." Any such check spends the pool. Only a *directional* pre-registration ("AC(1) > X ⇒ M2 retained") avoids the trap.

The memo already says this in §4; it should be elevated to the discipline note, because it is the general form of the trap that burned Uvalde.
