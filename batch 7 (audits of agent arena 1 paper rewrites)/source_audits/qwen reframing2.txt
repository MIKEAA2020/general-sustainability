## 2.1 v5’s abstract still risks the very conflation it warns against

v5 correctly says:

> avoid implying that the measured non-cropland book is the model’s ecological-capital book.

But its corrected abstract ends with:

> “…do not establish a measured global decline in ecological capital, which the data show remained essentially flat…”

That phrasing can still be read as saying:

> the data measured ecological capital and found it flat.

That is too close to the conflation. The safer formulation is:

> “…do not establish a measured decline in the model’s ecological-capital book; the measured non-cropland book remained essentially flat…”

This is a small wording change, but it is epistemically important.

---

## 2.2 The title still contains “Collapse,” which may re-trigger the referee’s concern

The reviewer already flagged the paper as potentially **“overly speculative”** or **theoretical**. A title containing:

> “Identifiability of Ecological-Capital Collapse”

may sound more alarmist than the paper’s actual evidentiary basis.

The model does contain a collapse-like floor collision, but the measured empirical claim is weaker and more careful:

- cropland grew strongly;
- non-cropland was essentially flat;
- aggregate growth masked per-capita decline;
- the model shows how capital drawdown could be hidden.

Therefore, for the title, **“drawdown”** is safer than **“collapse.”** It preserves the technical signal without sounding like a planetary-collapse claim.

Recommended title:

> **How Aggregation Can Mask Decline: Aggregate Biocapacity, the Composition Illusion, and the Identifiability of Ecological-Capital Drawdown**

If you want to preserve the model-specific term “collapse,” then qualify it:

> “…and the Identifiability of Modelled Ecological-Capital Collapse”

But the safer version is **drawdown**.

---

## 2.3 “Forest-and-soil” and “wetland” glosses may over-specify the NFA object

v5 sometimes uses:

> “forest-and-soil book”

and earlier drafts used:

> “forest, soil, and wetland base.”

Those are rhetorically appealing, but they may be too specific if the manuscript’s non-cropland aggregate is not exactly and exclusively forest and soil, and wetlands may not be represented in the NFA land-type matrix.

The safest manuscript language is:

> **the non-cropland book**

or, if the manuscript explicitly defines it:

> **the non-cropland components**

or:

> **the ecological-capital-side components**

Use “forest and soil” only if that is the exact definition in the manuscript. Otherwise, keep the more general term.

# 6. Revised abstract — final v6 version

This is the strongest abstract version. It is under 300 words, leads with the measurement problem, puts the measured evidence early, keeps the model as mechanism, and ends with the identifiability/scope clause.

Word count: approximately **278 words**.

```latex
\begin{abstract}
Humanity's headline indicator of whether the planet can support us---total biocapacity---is a single
number built from two very different sources: the land we farm and the broader bioproductive lands
that regenerate what we consume. This paper asks whether that single number can conceal the composition
of what is growing. It can, and the concealment is structural, not accidental. From 1961 to 2022,
global biocapacity grew about 23\%, but measured land-type accounts show that this growth came almost
entirely from cropland, whose biocapacity grew 2.85$\times$, while the non-cropland book stayed
essentially flat. Over the same period, per-capita biocapacity fell by about half as population grew.
A deliberately simple two-land model then makes the mechanism precise: conversion from
ecological-capital land to provisioning land can raise the measured total while the model's capital
book falls. In the model, the drawdown is demand-driven and takes the form of an abrupt approach to a
floor rather than a slow precursor. Recovery is not the reverse of decline; it requires an explicitly
gated restoration flow. The deeper point is measurement. Because the headline unit already combines
land areas, yields, and equivalence factors, the aggregate does not identify its own composition. A
rising total can therefore reflect either ecological recovery or substitution toward fast provisioning,
and the two cannot be separated from the total alone. Identifying the composition requires independent
land-cover and yield data. Global sustainability indicators should therefore report components alongside
totals. These results establish an identifiability property of aggregate biocapacity and a measured
composition pattern for 1961--2022; they are not a forecast and do not show that the model's
ecological-capital book declined in the observed data, where the non-cropland book remained essentially
flat.
\end{abstract}
```

# 9. Indicator-implication paragraph

Insert this in the introduction after the empirical grounding insertion, or near the point where the paper transitions to its contribution.

```latex
This has a direct consequence for how sustainability is measured. The composite-indicator literature
warns that in additive aggregation, weights act as trade-offs, allowing a deficit in one dimension to
be offset by a surplus in another (Nardo et al., 2008). Because biocapacity is exactly such a
composite, the same structural risk applies. Reporting only the total therefore risks sending a
misleading policy signal (Becker et al., 2017; Fischer et al., 2022). The aggregate remains useful as a
headline indicator, but only if its components are reported alongside it.
```

# 11. Epistemic-status block for the response letter or SI

This is one of the most useful additions. It makes the paper’s claim structure explicit and directly addresses the referee concern.

## Plain-text version for the response letter

```text
The revised manuscript now explicitly distinguishes four types of claim.

First, the measured land-type attribution shows that cropland biocapacity grew 2.85-fold while the
non-cropland book remained essentially flat, so cropland accounts for effectively all net aggregate
growth across the two NFA vintages.

Second, the population/per-capita decomposition is an exact accounting identity: aggregate biocapacity
can rise while per-capita biocapacity falls if population grows sufficiently fast.

Third, the two-land model provides a structural mechanism: aggregate biocapacity can rise while the
model's ecological-capital book falls, because the aggregate combines provisioning and capital-side
components.

Fourth, the indicator implication is methodological: because aggregate biocapacity is a composite, its
composition cannot be recovered from the total alone. Component reporting is therefore required.

The paper does not claim that the observed data independently demonstrate a measured global decline in
the model's ecological-capital book. The measured non-cropland book is essentially flat; the falling
capital book is the model's mechanism for showing how aggregation can conceal composition.
```

## Slightly shorter version

```text
The revised abstract and introduction separate the measured claim, the accounting identity, the model
mechanism, and the indicator implication. The measured claim is that cropland biocapacity grew
2.85-fold while the non-cropland book remained essentially flat. The accounting identity is that
aggregate biocapacity can rise while per-capita biocapacity falls when population grows. The model
claim is that a rising aggregate can mask a falling ecological-capital book. The indicator implication
is that the aggregate alone is not identifiable without component-level data. We do not claim that the
observed data show a measured decline in the model's ecological-capital book.
```
