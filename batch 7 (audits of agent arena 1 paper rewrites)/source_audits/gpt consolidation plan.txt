# Consolidated v33 Revision and Adjudication Plan

## Executive adjudication

Scope-A is the right conceptual direction: the manuscript should replace the non-identifiable scalar environmental stock with two physical land components:

- \(A_f\): fast provisioning land;
- \(A_c\): slow capital/ecological land.

This directly addresses the deepest problem identified in the audits: the prior model placed productive flow, ecological regeneration, and stock depletion in different accounting “books.” A two-land model can make the composition change physically explicit.

However, the proposed scope-A equations are not yet mathematically closed. Several claims imported into the plan—especially the persistence of the \(P=B/e\) equilibrium family, the exact zero determinant on the no-conversion face, the \(V_{\rm crit}=20\)-yr regime boundary, and the universality of the \(\tau_g\approx18\)–20 yr cliff—do not follow from equations (1)–(9) as currently written.

The correct revision strategy is therefore:

1. **Adopt the two-land ontology.**
2. **Repair the land-accounting closure before retaining any legacy stability claim.**
3. **Treat the unified one-stock model as a didactic comparator, not automatically as an exact special case.**
4. **Re-derive all equilibrium, Jacobian, delay, fold, and recovery results under the repaired two-land system.**
5. **Downgrade every claim not yet re-established from theorem to hypothesis, numerical result, or programme of work.**

The title can be retained only after the two-land model becomes the actual analysed object rather than a proposed replacement.

---

# I. Non-negotiable model repairs

## 1. The fixed-land constraint requires an equation for \(A_r\)

The plan declares

\[
A_f+A_c+A_r=A_{\rm tot},
\]

but equations (5)–(6) imply

\[
\frac{d}{dt}(A_f+A_c)
=
G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))-L_c-\eta_fA_f.
\]

Therefore the declared fixed-total constraint requires

\[
\dot A_r
=
-G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))
+L_c+\eta_fA_f.
\]

Without this equation, the model creates or destroys total land. The algebraic constraint is not satisfied dynamically.

The repaired land ledger should be:

\[
\dot A_c
=
G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))
-\frac{S}{\kappa}
-L_c,
\tag{5}
\]

\[
\dot A_f
=
\frac{S}{\kappa}
-\eta_fA_f,
\tag{6}
\]

\[
A_r=A_{\rm tot}-A_f-A_c,
\tag{10}
\]

or, equivalently,

\[
\dot A_r
=
-G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))
+L_c+\eta_fA_f.
\tag{11}
\]

The reserve is therefore not merely a typed floor. It is the residual land compartment that absorbs:

- ecological recovery into \(A_c\);
- abandonment or degradation of \(A_f\);
- optional timber liquidation;
- any other land-class transition.

### Required interpretation

The model must specify whether:

- ecological regeneration converts reserve land into capital land;
- timber harvest converts capital land into reserve land;
- fast-land retirement converts provisioning land into reserve land;
- reserve land can be converted back into \(A_f\) or \(A_c\).

The current equations imply one-way conversion

\[
A_c\longrightarrow A_f
\]

through shortfall, while regeneration and retirement exchange land with the reserve. That is acceptable, but it must be stated.

---

## 2. The current equations do not preserve the claimed \(P=B/e\) equilibrium family

This is the most important unresolved conflict in the plan.

On the no-conversion face with \(\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1\),

\[
S=[E-Y_f-Y_c]_+=[E-B]_+.
\]

At a positive population equilibrium,

\[
P^*=\frac{B^*}{e},
\qquad
E^*=eP^*=B^*,
\]

so \(S^*=0\).

But equations (5)–(6) then become

\[
\dot A_c=G_c(A_c^*)-L_c^*,
\]

\[
\dot A_f=-\eta_fA_f^*.
\]

If \(L_c=0\) and \(\eta_f>0\), an equilibrium requires

\[
G_c(A_c^*)=0,
\qquad
A_f^*=0.
\]

For the logistic regeneration function, this generally gives only boundary points:

\[
A_c^*=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
A_c^*=A_{c,\max},
\]

not a continuum of interior equilibria.

Therefore:

> The \(P=B/e\) family does not survive as an exact equilibrium family of equations (1)–(9) unless additional accounting or transition terms are added.

The legacy statement

\[
\det J\equiv0
\]

must consequently be removed from the adopted two-land model unless it is re-established after a revised closure.

### Two coherent options

#### Option A — Recommended: retain the physically closed two-land model

Keep the current land dynamics, add \(\dot A_r\), and acknowledge:

- the one-stock neutral continuum is not an exact property of scope-A;
- equilibria are generally isolated or boundary equilibria;
- the Jacobian determinant must be recomputed;
- neutral stability is not assumed.

This is the cleaner physical model.

#### Option B — Preserve a deliberate accounting degeneracy

If retaining the equilibrium family is essential, the model must explicitly define the capital-growth contribution as an accounting flow that is harvested or transferred in the equilibrium balance. The stock equation would then need an additional term representing the use of the sustainable increment.

That would be a different model and should not be introduced implicitly. If this option is chosen, the manuscript must display the revised stock ledger and show algebraically why \(E=B\) makes the stock equation vanish.

### Adjudication

Adopt **Option A** for the main manuscript. Retain the neutral-continuum result only in the unified one-stock comparator or in a clearly labelled degenerate limit.

---

## 3. The no-conversion face is not the same as the capital-land MSY

The plan states that at the interior capital-land MSY:

\[
S=0,
\qquad
\lambda=-r.
\]

But the capital-land MSY is defined by

\[
\frac{dY_c}{dA_c}=0,
\]

where

\[
Y_c=b_cA_c+b_{G,c}G_c(A_c).
\]

That condition is

\[
b_c+b_{G,c}G_c'(A_c^*)=0.
\]

It does not imply

\[
G_c(A_c^*)=0.
\]

Conversely, \(S=0\) is a demand/accounting condition, not an MSY condition.

With the present equations, an interior equilibrium additionally requires a balance such as

\[
G_c(A_c^*)=L_c^*
\]

if \(A_c^*\) is stationary. If \(L_c=0\), the interior MSY is not an equilibrium merely because it maximises \(Y_c\).

The revised manuscript must distinguish:

1. **capacity maximum**:
   \[
   Y_c'(A_c^*)=0;
   \]

2. **no-conversion condition**:
   \[
   S^*=0;
   \]

3. **land-stock equilibrium**:
   \[
   G_c(A_c^*)-\frac{S^*}{\kappa}-L_c^*=0;
   \]

4. **population equilibrium**:
   \[
   P^*=K^*;
   \]

5. **debt equilibrium**:
   \[
   [E^*-B^*]_+-\eta D^*=0.
   \]

Only their intersection defines a full equilibrium.

---

## 4. The \(V_{\rm crit}\) statement must be corrected

The condition for an interior maximum of

\[
Y_c(A_c)=b_cA_c+b_{G,c}\rho_cA_c
\left(1-\frac{A_c}{A_{c,\max}}\right)
\]

is

\[
b_{G,c}\rho_c>b_c.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\frac{b_{G,c}}{b_c}>\frac1{\rho_c}.
\]

Thus, if

\[
V_{\rm eco}:=\frac1{\rho_c},
\]

the condition is

\[
V_{\rm eco}<\frac{b_{G,c}}{b_c}.
\]

It is not generally correct to state

\[
V_{\rm crit}=\frac1{\rho_c}=20\ {\rm yr}.
\]

The value \(1/\rho_c=20\) yr is the regeneration timescale for \(\rho_c=0.05\,{\rm yr^{-1}}\), not by itself the regime boundary. The boundary depends on the ratio \(b_{G,c}/b_c\).

Use the following notation:

\[
V_{\rm eco}:=\rho_c^{-1},
\]

\[
V_{\rm regime}:=\frac{b_{G,c}}{b_c}.
\]

Then:

\[
\text{interior capacity maximum}
\iff
V_{\rm eco}<V_{\rm regime}.
\]

If the baseline is intended to have a 20-year boundary, choose parameters satisfying

\[
\frac{b_{G,c}}{b_c}=20\ {\rm yr},
\]

and state that this is a parameter calibration, not an identity.

The secondary boundary \(1/(\rho+r)\) should be removed from the main results unless it is derived from the repaired full Jacobian. It is not automatically a universal second threshold.

---

## 5. Correct the capital-land share formula

At the interior maximum,

\[
A_c^*
=
\frac{A_{c,\max}(b_c+b_{G,c}\rho_c)}
{2b_{G,c}\rho_c}.
\]

The flow share is

\[
\psi_c^*
=
\frac{b_cA_c^*}{Y_c(A_c^*)}
=
\frac{2b_c}{b_c+b_{G,c}\rho_c}
=
\frac{2}{1+b_{G,c}\rho_c/b_c}.
\]

This formula is correct.

It follows that:

\[
\psi_c^*=1
\iff
b_{G,c}\rho_c=b_c.
\]

At the interior maximum, where \(b_{G,c}\rho_c>b_c\),

\[
0<\psi_c^*<1.
\]

There is no general \(\psi_c=1/2\) threshold. In particular,

\[
\psi_c^*=\frac12
\iff
b_{G,c}\rho_c=3b_c.
\]

The manuscript should not use \(\psi_c\) as a global constant. It is state-dependent:

\[
\psi_c(A_c)
=
\frac{b_cA_c}{Y_c(A_c)}.
\]

# II. Adopted model after repair

## 6. State and unit conventions

Use one notation throughout:

| Symbol | Meaning | Units |
|---|---|---|
| \(A_f\) | fast provisioning land | ha |
| \(A_c\) | capital/ecological land | ha |
| \(A_r\) | reserve land | ha |
| \(A_{\rm tot}\) | total land | ha |
| \(P\) | population | persons |
| \(D\) | accumulated productivity debt | gha·yr |
| \(b_f\) | fast-land productivity | gha ha\(^{-1}\) yr\(^{-1}\) |
| \(b_c\) | capital-land flow productivity | gha ha\(^{-1}\) yr\(^{-1}\) |
| \(b_{G,c}\) | conversion from capital-land growth to accounting capacity | gha ha\(^{-1}\) |
| \(\rho_c\) | capital-land regeneration rate | yr\(^{-1}\) |
| \(e\) | footprint per person | gha person\(^{-1}\) yr\(^{-1}\) |
| \(\kappa\) | flow-to-land conversion coefficient | gha ha\(^{-1}\) |
| \(\eta_f\) | fast-land retirement rate | yr\(^{-1}\) |
| \(\eta\) | debt repayment rate | yr\(^{-1}\) |

Set

\[
\kappa=b_f
\]

only if the model explicitly intends a shortfall of one gha·yr\(^{-1}\) to convert one hectare per year at current fast-land productivity. Because \(b_f\) depends on \(D\) and technology, \(\kappa\) is then state-dependent:

\[
\kappa(t)=b_f(t).
\]

The manuscript must discuss whether this is intended. If a fixed institutional conversion coefficient is preferable, use a separate parameter \(\kappa_0\) and do not identify it with \(b_f\).

Keep the distinction:

\[
\gamma_{\rm timber}:=\frac1{b_{G,c}},
\]

where \(\gamma_{\rm timber}\) belongs only to the optional timber-liquidation channel, while

\[
V_{\rm eco}:=\frac1{\rho_c}.
\]

Never write

\[
\gamma=\frac1{V_{\rm eco}}.
\]

## 7. Governing equations

The main model should be written as:

\[
G_c(A_c)=\rho_cA_c
\left(1-\frac{A_c}{A_{c,\max}}\right),
\]

\[
b_f(t)=
\left(b_{f0}+T_b(t)\right)e^{-\alpha D(t)},
\]

\[
Y_f=b_fA_f,
\]

\[
Y_c=b_cA_c+b_{G,c}G_c(A_c),
\]

\[
B=Y_f+Y_c,
\]

\[
E=eP,
\]

\[
S=
\left[
E-\sigma_fY_f-\sigma_cY_c
\right]_+,
\]

\[
\dot A_c
=
G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))
-\frac{S}{\kappa}
-L_c,
\]

\[
\dot A_f
=
\frac{S}{\kappa}
-\eta_fA_f,
\]

\[
\dot P
=
rP
\left[
1-\frac{P}{K(t-\tau_p)}
\right],
\]

\[
K(t)=\max\left\{K_{\min},\frac{B(t)}e\right\},
\]

\[
\dot D
=
[E-B]_+-\eta D.
\]

The reserve is

\[
A_r=A_{\rm tot}-A_f-A_c.
\]

The timber channel is optional:

\[
L_c=
\frac{[H_c-\sigma_cY_c]_+}{b_{G,c}}.
\]

However, \(L_c\) must be interpreted consistently. If it removes capital land and transfers it to reserve land, that is represented by \(\dot A_r\). If it represents permanent physical destruction, the fixed-total constraint must be modified.

# III. Accounting and ratio results

## 8. Distinguish three different boundaries

The manuscript must stop treating these as interchangeable:

### Aggregate accounting balance

\[
R_B:=\frac{E}{B}.
\]

At a constant equilibrium with \(P>0\),

\[
R_B=1
\]

because \(P=K=B/e\) implies \(E=eP=B\), provided the carrying-capacity floor is inactive.

### Fast-land conversion trigger

\[
R_A:=\frac{E}{Y_f}.
\]

With \(\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1\), the conversion trigger is not simply \(R_A=1\), because capital-land capacity also contributes:

\[
S>0
\iff
E>Y_f+Y_c
\]

for the unrestricted system.

If the policy rule allocates capital-land capacity separately, then the relevant fast-land trigger may be

\[
E-\sigma_cY_c>\sigma_fY_f.
\]

This must be stated as a policy-dependent condition.

### Trajectory-level capital decline

From equation (5),

\[
\dot A_c<0
\iff
\frac{S}{\kappa}+L_c
>
G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g)).
\]

This is the actual stock-decline condition.

It is not equivalent to

\[
R_B>1
\]

along delayed trajectories, because current \(B\) uses current \(G_c(A_c(t))\), whereas recruitment uses

\[
G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g)).
\]

Define the delayed accounting capacity if needed:

\[
\widetilde B(t)
=
Y_f(t)+b_cA_c(t)
+b_{G,c}G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g)).
\]

Then any trajectory-level comparison must identify whether it uses \(B(t)\) or \(\widetilde B(t)\).

## 9. The productivity illusion becomes a composition result

The robust claim is not that total \(B\) must rise for a fixed five-year interval. The stronger and more defensible two-land statement is:

\[
\dot A_c<0
\quad\text{while}\quad
\dot B>0.
\]

The contribution of conversion to total capacity is approximately

\[
\left.
\frac{\partial B}{\partial t}
\right|_{\rm conversion}
=
\left(b_f-b_c-b_{G,c}G_c'(A_c)\right)
\frac{S}{\kappa}.
\]

Define the effective capital-land productivity:

\[
b_{c,\rm eff}(A_c)
=
b_c+b_{G,c}G_c'(A_c).
\]

Then conversion raises current accounting capacity when

\[
b_f>b_{c,\rm eff}(A_c).
\]

The conversion-loop gain is therefore controlled by

\[
\mathcal G_{\rm conv}
=
\frac{b_f-b_{c,\rm eff}(A_c)}{\kappa},
\]

subject to the delayed population and debt feedbacks.

This is the new headline mechanism. It replaces the old claim that the entire system is governed by a universal \(+0.625\) eigenvalue.

The manuscript should distinguish:

- **illusion onset**: \(\dot A_c<0\), \(\dot B>0\);
- **illusion duration**: time until either \(\dot B\le0\), the arable ceiling binds, debt degradation dominates, or the trajectory reaches a policy floor;
- **masking state**:
  \[
  R_B<1
  \quad\text{while}\quad
  \dot A_c<0;
  \]
- **conversion crisis**: loss of viability of \(A_c\) or activation of a typed floor.

The claim that the illusion is “generic and historically long” should be presented as a hypothesis to be demonstrated numerically and empirically, not as an algebraic theorem.

# IV. Stability and bifurcation programme

## 10. Do not import the one-stock Jacobian into scope-A

The one-stock result

\[
\det J=0
\]

belongs to the degenerate closed-loop structure of the unified model under particular assumptions. It does not automatically apply to the two-land equations.

For scope-A, derive the full linearisation in the smooth interior region:

\[
z=(A_c,A_f,P,D).
\]

The delayed system has the form

\[
\dot z(t)=J_0z(t)+J_gz(t-\tau_g)+J_pz(t-\tau_p),
\]

with characteristic matrix

\[
\Delta(\lambda)
=
\lambda I-J_0-J_g e^{-\lambda\tau_g}
-J_p e^{-\lambda\tau_p}.
\]

The characteristic equation is

\[
\det\Delta(\lambda)=0.
\]

The derivation must be performed separately for:

1. no conversion, \(S=0\);
2. active conversion, \(S>0\);
3. active timber liquidation, \(L_c>0\);
4. boundary states where the positive-part function is nonsmooth;
5. carrying-capacity floor active, \(K=K_{\min}\).

No determinant sign or eigenvalue value should be carried forward until this calculation is complete.

---

## 11. The conversion-loop result should replace the universal instability claim

The legacy result that a single transverse eigenvalue is universally positive—previously reported as approximately

\[
\lambda_+\approx0.625\ {\rm yr^{-1}}
\]

—must not be transferred to scope-A.

In the two-land model, the local effect of active conversion depends on:

- the productivity difference between fast and capital land;
- the conversion coefficient \(\kappa\);
- the slope \(G_c'(A_c)\);
- fast-land retirement \(\eta_f\);
- debt sensitivity \(\alpha\);
- population response \(r\);
- both delays;
- the active policy coefficients \(\sigma_f,\sigma_c\);
- proximity to the reserve and arable ceilings.

For fixed \(P,D\), no timber liquidation, and active conversion,

\[
S=eP-\sigma_fb_fA_f
-\sigma_c\left[b_cA_c+b_{G,c}G_c(A_c)\right].
\]

The relevant partial derivatives are

\[
\frac{\partial S}{\partial A_f}
=
-\sigma_fb_f,
\]

\[
\frac{\partial S}{\partial A_c}
=
-\sigma_c
\left[b_c+b_{G,c}G_c'(A_c)\right],
\]

\[
\frac{\partial S}{\partial P}=e,
\]

and, because \(b_f=(b_{f0}+T_b)e^{-\alpha D}\),

\[
\frac{\partial S}{\partial D}
=
\alpha\sigma_fY_f.
\]

These terms enter the full Jacobian. They show that the conversion loop can be:

- locally stabilising;
- locally destabilising;
- weak near a productivity crossover;
- amplified by debt degradation;
- altered qualitatively by delayed capital regeneration.

The revised result should therefore be stated as:

> Active conversion creates a composition feedback whose sign and strength are state- and parameter-dependent. Instability is a derived regime property, not a universal constant.

A useful diagnostic is

\[
\Delta b_{\rm conv}(A_c,D)
=
b_f(D)-b_c-b_{G,c}G_c'(A_c).
\]

Then:

- \(\Delta b_{\rm conv}>0\): conversion can increase current accounting capacity;
- \(\Delta b_{\rm conv}=0\): first-order capacity neutrality;
- \(\Delta b_{\rm conv}<0\): conversion immediately lowers current accounting capacity.

This diagnostic is not itself an eigenvalue. It identifies the direct capacity effect before demographic, debt, retirement, and delay feedbacks are included.

---

## 12. Delay analysis must be re-derived rather than inherited

For a capital-land perturbation \(a_c(t)\), delayed regeneration contributes

\[
G_c'(A_c^*)a_c(t-\tau_g).
\]

A scalar reduction of the form

\[
\dot x(t)=ax(t)+bx(t-\tau_g)
\]

has characteristic equation

\[
\lambda-a-be^{-\lambda\tau_g}=0.
\]

At a Hopf boundary, \(\lambda=i\omega\), so

\[
-a=b\cos(\omega\tau_g),
\]

\[
\omega=-b\sin(\omega\tau_g),
\]

and therefore

\[
\omega^2=b^2-a^2.
\]

Such a reduction is valid only if it is derived from the full two-land system under explicit timescale or invariant-subspace assumptions. The actual model generally has both regeneration and population delays:

\[
\det\!\left[
\lambda I-J_0-J_ge^{-\lambda\tau_g}
-J_pe^{-\lambda\tau_p}
\right]=0.
\]

Accordingly:

- \(\tau_g\approx18\)–20 yr must not be called a universal cliff;
- any critical delay must be reported as a parameter-dependent stability boundary;
- multiple stability switches are possible;
- the critical delay may depend strongly on \(\eta_f,\rho_c,r,\alpha,\kappa\), and the equilibrium composition;
- nonsmooth switching at \(S=0\) requires separate treatment.

The original 18–20 year value may remain as a baseline numerical finding only if it is reproduced under the repaired equations and accompanied by a continuation diagram.

---

## 13. Fold and tipping claims require branch continuation

A fold cannot be inferred merely from a sharp trajectory change or a failed numerical integration. A genuine saddle-node condition requires an equilibrium problem

\[
F(z,\mu)=0
\]

with

\[
\det D_zF(z,\mu)=0
\]

and the appropriate transversality and nondegeneracy conditions.

For the nonsmooth model, folds must be sought separately on each active set:

- \(S=0\);
- \(S>0\);
- \(L_c=0\);
- \(L_c>0\);
- \(K=B/e\);
- \(K=K_{\min}\);
- reserve floor inactive or active.

The continuation programme should vary, at minimum:

\[
A_r^{\min},\quad
\rho_c,\quad
b_f,\quad
b_c,\quad
b_{G,c},\quad
\eta_f,\quad
\alpha,\quad
\tau_g,\quad
\tau_p.
\]

The manuscript must distinguish among:

1. **smooth saddle-node bifurcation**;
2. **Hopf bifurcation**;
3. **border collision or active-set transition**;
4. **constraint activation**;
5. **rate-induced tipping**;
6. **irreversible loss caused by a one-way land transition**;
7. **numerical termination**.

Only the first is properly called a fold.

# V. Constraints, floors, and admissibility

## 14. All land floors must be typed

The following are different constraints and should not share one symbol:

### Reserve floor

\[
A_r\ge A_r^{\min}.
\]

Since

\[
A_r=A_{\rm tot}-A_f-A_c,
\]

this implies

\[
A_f+A_c\le A_{\rm tot}-A_r^{\min}.
\]

### Capital-land viability floor

\[
A_c\ge A_c^{\min}.
\]

This represents a minimum ecological-capital stock, not merely unallocated land.

### Fast-land ceiling

\[
A_f\le A_f^{\max}.
\]

This may represent soil, climate, slope, water, or institutional constraints.

### Carrying-capacity floor

\[
K\ge K_{\min}.
\]

This is a demographic or humanitarian floor and should not be interpreted as physical land production.

### Debt or degradation ceiling

If used,

\[
D\le D_{\max}
\]

must be justified independently.

The shortfall-to-conversion rule must be constrained by available convertible land. A projected or complementarity formulation is preferable:

\[
0\le u_c\le \frac{S}{\kappa},
\]

\[
\dot A_c=G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))-u_c-L_c,
\]

\[
\dot A_f=u_c-\eta_fA_f,
\]

with

\[
u_c=0
\]

when further conversion would violate the reserve or capital-land floor.

A simple implementation is

\[
u_c=
\begin{cases}
S/\kappa,
&
A_r>A_r^{\min}
\ \text{and}\
A_c>A_c^{\min},\\[4pt]
0,
&
\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]

though a smoothed or complementarity-based formulation will be more suitable for continuation.

---

## 15. Non-negativity and forward invariance must be checked

The admissible state space is

\[
\Omega=
\left\{
(A_f,A_c,A_r,P,D):
A_i\ge0,\;
A_f+A_c+A_r=A_{\rm tot},\;
P\ge0,\;
D\ge0
\right\}.
\]

The model should demonstrate that trajectories remain in \(\Omega\).

Potential failures include:

- \(A_c\) becoming negative under unrestricted conversion;
- \(A_f\) exceeding available land;
- delayed regeneration remaining positive after capital land has reached zero;
- debt becoming negative because of numerical error;
- \(K\) becoming undefined if \(B\le0\);
- \(P\) overshooting a rapidly collapsing delayed carrying capacity.

The numerical implementation should use:

- event detection for land boundaries;
- nonnegative state enforcement;
- constrained conversion rates;
- explicit delay-history functions;
- active-set logging;
- convergence checks under reduced step size and tighter tolerances.

A delayed regeneration term also requires care near extinction. If \(A_c(t)=0\) but \(A_c(t-\tau_g)>0\), recruitment may still arrive from prior investment. This is physically reasonable only if the delayed term represents maturing cohorts. If it represents current ecological growth, the delayed formulation should instead be revised.

---

# VI. Recovery and reversibility

## 16. Recovery cannot be inferred from reversing the forcing

The model is structurally asymmetric:

\[
A_c\rightarrow A_f
\]

occurs through conversion, while the proposed equations contain no direct term

\[
A_f\rightarrow A_c.
\]

Fast-land retirement sends land to \(A_r\), not automatically to \(A_c\). Therefore reducing population, footprint, or shortfall does not reverse prior conversion.

Recovery of capital land requires an explicit restoration flow, for example

\[
R_{fc}
=
\chi A_f\,\Phi(B-E),
\]

or a reserve-to-capital flow

\[
R_{rc}
=
\rho_rA_r\,\Psi(\text{restoration policy}),
\]

with the land equations modified consistently:

\[
\dot A_c
=
G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))
-u_c-L_c+R_{fc}+R_{rc},
\]

\[
\dot A_f
=
u_c-\eta_fA_f-R_{fc},
\]

\[
\dot A_r
=
-G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))
+L_c+\eta_fA_f-R_{rc}.
\]

Without such terms, “recovery” can mean only:

- cessation of further capital loss;
- repayment of \(D\);
- demographic adjustment;
- recovery of \(A_c\) from reserve through \(G_c\), if that is the intended interpretation.

The manuscript must specify which meaning is used.

---

## 17. Hysteresis must be demonstrated, not assumed

A return trajectory different from the outward trajectory is not sufficient to establish equilibrium hysteresis. The revised analysis should distinguish:

- **dynamic lag** caused by \(\tau_g\) or \(\tau_p\);
- **path dependence** caused by irreversible land conversion;
- **active-set hysteresis** caused by floors or switching;
- **bistable hysteresis** caused by multiple stable equilibria and folds.

The recovery experiment should report:

\[
\Delta A_c,\quad
\Delta A_f,\quad
\Delta A_r,\quad
\Delta D,\quad
\Delta P
\]

after the forcing returns to baseline.

A useful recovery metric is

\[
\mathcal R_c(T)
=
\frac{A_c(T)-A_c^{\rm degraded}}
{A_c^{\rm initial}-A_c^{\rm degraded}},
\]

with:

- \(\mathcal R_c=0\): no capital-land recovery;
- \(\mathcal R_c=1\): full recovery to the initial stock;
- \(\mathcal R_c<0\): continued decline;
- \(\mathcal R_c>1\): overshoot.

---

# VII. Empirical and numerical programme

## 18. Parameter identification must follow the two-land ontology

The model should not calibrate \(A_f\) and \(A_c\) as arbitrary latent shares if empirical claims depend on their interpretation.

Possible proxies include:

### Fast provisioning land

- cropland;
- intensively managed pasture;
- built or industrial land assigned direct provisioning output;
- irrigated production systems;
- high-input plantation area.

### Capital/ecological land

- mature forest;
- wetlands;
- long-rotation ecosystems;
- high-carbon soils;
- habitat or watershed land;
- low-intensity regenerative systems.

### Reserve land

- currently unallocated or nonproductive land;
- degraded or abandoned land;
- restoration pool;
- land protected from conversion but not counted in \(A_c\).

Because classifications are contestable, the manuscript should include a sensitivity analysis over alternative mappings.

The parameters

\[
b_c,\quad b_{G,c},\quad \rho_c
\]

are not independently identifiable from a single cross-sectional observation of \(Y_c\). Identification requires:

- time-series changes in \(A_c\);
- regeneration or growth data;
- observed provisioning flows;
- independent ecological valuation or accounting weights.

Similarly, \(\alpha\) and \(D\) are confounded unless debt is linked to observable soil, infrastructure, nutrient, or productivity indicators.

---

## 19. Baseline simulations must be reproducible

Each simulation should report:

1. parameter values and units;
2. initial state;
3. delay-history functions on
   \[
   [-\max(\tau_g,\tau_p),0];
   \]
4. active policy regime;
5. numerical solver and version;
6. tolerances and maximum step;
7. event and boundary handling;
8. whether \(\kappa\) is fixed or equals \(b_f(t)\);
9. whether \(L_c\) is enabled;
10. whether restoration is enabled;
11. all floor and ceiling values.

Minimum verification tests should include:

- land conservation:
  \[
  A_f+A_c+A_r=A_{\rm tot};
  \]
- non-negativity;
- debt balance;
- convergence under step refinement;
- agreement between direct simulation and equilibrium continuation where both apply;
- recovery of the ordinary differential equation limit as
  \[
  \tau_g,\tau_p\to0;
  \]
- recovery of the no-debt limit as
  \[
  \alpha\to0
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  D\to0.
  \]

---

## 20. Required sensitivity analyses

At minimum, perform global or structured sensitivity analysis over:

\[
\rho_c,\quad
b_f,\quad
b_c,\quad
b_{G,c},\quad
\kappa,\quad
\eta_f,\quad
\eta,\quad
\alpha,\quad
r,\quad
\tau_g,\quad
\tau_p,\quad
A_r^{\min},\quad
A_c^{\min}.
\]

Report effects on:

- minimum \(A_c\);
- maximum \(A_f\);
- time to conversion onset;
- duration of the productivity illusion;
- peak \(B\);
- peak \(D\);
- population overshoot;
- probability or occurrence of floor activation;
- recovery fraction \(\mathcal R_c\);
- dominant characteristic root.

A local sensitivity table alone is not sufficient near active-set transitions. Use parameter sweeps, Latin hypercube sampling, Sobol indices, or continuation as appropriate.

# VIII. Role of the unified one-stock model

## 21. Retain it only as a comparator

The previous unified-stock system remains useful for explaining why aggregate accounting can hide composition. It should be moved to:

- an early conceptual section;
- a boxed limiting model;
- or an appendix.

It should not be presented as automatically nested inside scope-A.

To claim that the one-stock model is an exact special case, the manuscript must provide a mapping such as

\[
A=A_f+A_c
\]

and show that the aggregated equation obtained from the two-land system is identical to the one-stock equation.

But here,

\[
\dot A_f+\dot A_c
=
G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g))
-L_c-\eta_fA_f,
\]

which is not generally a function of \(A_f+A_c\) alone. Therefore exact aggregation fails unless restrictive closure assumptions are imposed.

The correct statement is:

> The one-stock model is a didactic aggregation that suppresses land composition. It is not generally dynamically equivalent to the two-land model.

Legacy findings may be retained under labels such as:

- “one-stock benchmark”;
- “degenerate accounting limit”;
- “aggregation artefact”;
- “hypothesis tested in scope-A.”

---

# IX. Revised claim hierarchy

## 22. Claims that can be stated analytically

Subject to the repaired equations, the manuscript can state:

1. The capital-land capacity function has an interior maximum iff
   \[
   b_{G,c}\rho_c>b_c.
   \]

2. That maximum occurs at
   \[
   A_c^*
   =
   \frac{A_{c,\max}(b_c+b_{G,c}\rho_c)}
   {2b_{G,c}\rho_c}.
   \]

3. At that maximum,
   \[
   \psi_c^*
   =
   \frac{2b_c}{b_c+b_{G,c}\rho_c}.
   \]

4. Capital land declines when
   \[
   \frac{S}{\kappa}+L_c
   >
   G_c(A_c(t-\tau_g)).
   \]

5. Conversion can increase current accounting capacity when
   \[
   b_f>b_c+b_{G,c}G_c'(A_c),
   \]
   before indirect feedbacks are included.

6. The fixed-total land constraint requires a reserve equation or equivalent algebraic closure.

7. Reversing shortfall does not restore converted capital land unless a restoration pathway exists.

# XI. Final publication decision

The scope-A revision is publishable in principle, but not with the current inheritance of one-stock results.

The minimum acceptable revision is:

1. add the reserve-land balance;
2. decide whether regeneration is a land transition or merely an ecological growth flow;
3. resolve the incompatibility between fast-land retirement and the claimed equilibrium continuum;
4. correct the \(V_{\rm eco}\) and \(\psi_c\) formulas and interpretations;
5. re-derive equilibrium and stability conditions;
6. replace the universal \(+0.625\) claim with the state-dependent conversion-loop diagnostic;
7. recompute all delay thresholds;
8. demonstrate folds through continuation;
9. impose admissible land constraints;
10. define an explicit restoration pathway before making recovery claims;
11. relabel the one-stock model as a comparator rather than an exact nested case;
12. provide reproducible simulations and parameter sensitivity.

The central conclusion should be reformulated as follows:

> Aggregate biocapacity can rise while slow ecological capital is being converted into faster provisioning land. Whether this produces transient growth, instability, overshoot, or irreversible loss depends on land-productivity contrasts, regeneration and demographic delays, debt feedbacks, retirement and restoration pathways, and binding land constraints.

That conclusion is stronger, more physically interpretable, and more defensible than the legacy claim of a universal instability or universal delay cliff.
