main text:
The linearization, Jacobian blocks, characteristic matrix and quasi-polynomial of the constant-parameter subsystem remain internally consistent with one another and with the earlier derivation (the two delayed edges share a vertex, so no mixed \(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(\tau_m+\tau_p)}\) term appears). Equilibrium condition (10), existence criterion (11) and the numerical values of \((M^*,P^*)\) likewise follow directly from setting the right-hand sides to zero. Units throughout Eqs. (1)–(8) and (13) are consistent once \(\gamma\) is recognized to be dimensionless. Scenario F’s Half-Earth numbers (\(M=0.970\), \(P=0.243\), \(\Omega=0.575\)) are the exact algebraic consequence of replacing \(K\) by \(0.5\,B/r_{\mathrm{opt}}\) while retaining the overshoot value \(e=1.15\,r_{\mathrm{opt}}\).

The remaining statements contain several errors and internal contradictions.

- Non-delayed Routh–Hurwitz criteria are mis-stated. The matrix \(A+B+C\) always has positive determinant whenever an interior equilibrium exists; the only additional requirement is \(\operatorname{tr}<0\), i.e. \(a_{11}<r\). Because \(r=0.02\) is two orders of magnitude smaller than \(\rho\), this is numerically almost the same as \(a_{11}<0\) (i.e. \(M^*>M_{\max}/2\)), but the paper presents the latter as a necessary condition and never writes the actual trace/determinant pair. The same misconception reappears when \(M_{\max}/2\) is later called a “logistic regeneration threshold from which recovery is impossible.” Isolated logistic dynamics recover from every \(M>0\); the point of no return is an artefact of persistent delayed depletion, not of the hump of the logistic.

- The explicit one-delay polynomials offered as an “analytic proof” that no Hopf bifurcation exists for Scenario A are only half-correct. With the baseline numbers the \(\tau_p=0\) polynomial is \(\omega^2(\omega^2+0.2504)=0\) (exactly the displayed multiple of \(10^4\)). The \(\tau_m=0\) polynomial must be of degree 4 and likewise factors as \(\omega^2(\omega^2+c)=0\); the displayed degree-6 expression \(2500\omega^4+1199\omega^2+143.5\) does not follow from the characteristic equation. (The conclusion that only \(\omega=0\) occurs happens to be true, but the algebra that is claimed to prove it is not.)

- A truncated sentence survives in the Scenario D discussion: “when *ted near the notional equilibrium \ldots”.  

- Regeneration rate \(\rho=1.5\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\) is two to three orders of magnitude faster than any terrestrial process that could be aggregated into \(M\). The choice is internally consistent with the desire to place both equilibria on the “stable” side of \(M_{\max}/2\) while keeping delays of 25–30 yr, but it makes the environmental lag a pure delay in the sink term while regeneration itself is essentially instantaneous—an implausible separation.

- Half-Earth is implemented as an exogenous cap on the reference carrying capacity \(K=B/r_{\mathrm{opt}}\). Because the actual per-capita demand remains \(e=1.15\,r_{\mathrm{opt}}\), the realized overshoot ratio is 0.575 rather than 0.5, so more than half of total biocapacity is still appropriated. The text never remarks on the discrepancy.

All other numerical claims (Hopf location \(\tau_m\approx83\,\mathrm{yr}\) for Scenario B, successive crossings spaced by \(2\pi/\omega\approx242\,\mathrm{yr}\), grid-scan threshold \(\tau_m+\tau_p\approx80\,\mathrm{yr}\) for Scenario A, Euler time-step, etc.) are consistent with the characteristic equation and with the stated parameter set. The qualitative distinction drawn between debt-driven collapse (B–C) and delay-induced loss of basin of attraction (D) is also compatible with the local stability results, once the erroneous “\(M_{\max}/2\) threshold” language is ignored.

improvements:
The paper’s core ambitions—a minimal two-dimensional DDE in which carrying capacity is an emergent state, two distinct lags interact, ecological debt is irreversible, and technology enters as bounded logistic waves—are worth preserving. The following changes restore internal consistency and mathematical accuracy while leaving the narrative, the six stylized scenarios, the weak/strong-sustainability reconciliation, and the qualitative conclusions intact.

**Stability of the delay-free system (Sections 3.1 and 3.2).**  
The matrix \(A+B+C\) has determinant \(\gamma e a_{21}-a_{11}r\). Whenever an interior equilibrium exists this determinant is automatically positive, so the Routh–Hurwitz criterion reduces to the single inequality \(a_{11}<r\). Because the demographic rate \(r\) is two orders of magnitude smaller than the regeneration rate \(\rho\), the inequality is numerically almost identical to \(a_{11}<0\) (i.e., \(M^*>M_{\max}/2\)). State the exact condition, note that the paper’s parameter choices satisfy it comfortably, and replace the claim that the upper logistic branch is required with the milder statement that it is sufficient (and, for realistic \(r\ll\rho\), essentially necessary).

**One-delay Hopf polynomials.**  
The \(\tau_p=0\) elimination is already correct: \(\omega^2(10^4\omega^2+2504)=0\). The \(\tau_m=0\) calculation contains an algebraic slip. Repeating the same \(\sin^2+\cos^2=1\) procedure with the baseline numbers yields
\[
\omega^2(40000\omega^4+19184\omega^2+2296)=0,
\]
whose remaining quadratic in \(\omega^2\) has positive coefficients and therefore no positive real roots. Insert the corrected polynomial (or simply say “the analogous even polynomial of degree 6 likewise has strictly positive coefficients”). The supporting inequality \(r^2a_{11}^2\ge(\gamma e a_{21})^2\) remains valid and can stay.

**“Point of no return” language.**  
Isolated logistic growth recovers from every \(M>0\). The irreversible collapse observed in Scenarios D and E is produced by the combination of delayed depletion and a vanishing basin of attraction, not by the hump of the logistic. Rephrase every occurrence of “logistic regeneration threshold \(M_{\max}/2\) from which recovery is impossible” accordingly. The same correction removes the truncated sentence in the Scenario D paragraph.

**Parameter realism.**  
Keep the numerical values (they already produce the advertised 80-year two-delay threshold and the 83-year one-delay Hopf). Add one sentence in Section 4 noting that \(\rho=1.5\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\) is deliberately large so that regeneration is fast relative to the lags; the qualitative delay-interaction and debt-versus-technology results survive under any \(\rho\) that still places both equilibria on the locally attracting side of the delay-free linearization.

**Half-Earth implementation.**  
The exogenous cap \(K\leftarrow\frac12 B/r_{\mathrm{opt}}\) together with the retained overshoot factor \(e=1.15 r_{\mathrm{opt}}\) produces a realized ratio \(\Omega=0.575\). Mention this explicitly: the policy still keeps total demand below total biocapacity (hence \(D\equiv0\)) while illustrating a simple reservation of roughly half the regenerative flow. No change to the simulation or to the theoretical equilibrium \((M,P)=(0.970,0.243)\) is required.

**Minor clean-ups that do not alter the picture.**  
- Replace the Euler integrator remark with a parenthetical that any standard DDE solver (e.g., dde23) reproduces the same long-term regimes.  
- In the stability-map paragraph, keep the 41\times41 grid and the “preliminary estimate” caveat; they already match the characteristic equation.  
- Fix the obvious typographical fragment “when *ted near\ldots”.

With these revisions the characteristic equation, the two-delay interaction theorem, the debt-driven versus delay-driven collapse distinction, and the productivity-illusion argument remain exactly as written. The paper is then free of internal contradictions while retaining its original scope and message.

reviewer response:
**Units, accounting convention, and a dedicated subsection.** Retain the stock–flow distinction that is the model’s conceptual core (orchard \(M\) versus annual harvest \(B\)), but make the convention explicit and internally consistent so that it no longer conflicts with GFN reporting practice. Insert a short “Units and GFN convention” paragraph (or table) immediately after the first appearance of \(M\), \(B\) and \(E\):

- \(M(t)\): stock of biologically productive area (gha).
- \(B(t)\) and \(E(t)\): annual regenerative flow and annual demand, both gha yr\(^{-1}\) (the quantities that GFN reports as “gha” for a given year). The reported GFN figures are therefore the time-integrated annual equivalents; our \(\gamma\) (dimensionless) converts a flow demand into a permanent stock-loss rate.
- Ecological debt \(D\): \(\int\max(E-B,0)\,dt\) therefore has units gha (total unrepaid hectare-years of regeneration). \(\alpha\) has units gha\(^{-1}\).
- \(r_{\mathrm{opt}}\) and \(e\): gha cap\(^{-1}\) yr\(^{-1}\); \(K=B/r_{\mathrm{opt}}\) remains a population.

This matches the existing footnote and the characteristic-equation units check while answering the reviewer directly. If a referee still prefers the “Footprint in gha, debt in gha·yr” convention, a one-line rescaling (\(\gamma\leftarrow\gamma/\Delta t_{\mathrm{ref}}\), \(\alpha\leftarrow\alpha\Delta t_{\mathrm{ref}}\)) leaves every qualitative result unchanged; the paper can note the equivalence.

**Parameters that can vary.** The general formulation already writes \(b(t)\), \(r_{\mathrm{opt}}(t)\) and \(e(t)\). Keep the constant-parameter subsystem for the analytic results (existence, Routh–Hurwitz, two-delay characteristic equation). In the model-development section add one sentence: “In the full system these quantities may be prescribed functions of time (affluence, efficiency, dietary change, etc.); the constant case isolates the \(M\)–\(P\)–debt–technology feedbacks.” Simulations can then include a slow exogenous trend in \(e\) or \(r_{\mathrm{opt}}\) as a robustness check without raising the state dimension. The same modular structure already used for \(T(t)\) covers this.

**References, data limitations, and the 1961–2022 sentence.** Expand the GFN citations (Borucke et al. 2013 is already present) with Wackernagel & Rees, the National Footprint Accounts methodology papers, and the most recent NFA documentation. Add an explicit limitations paragraph:

National accounts rest on UN (FAO, etc.) data and are known to under-record soil degradation, biodiversity loss, water-quality decline and below-ground carbon. Consequently reported biocapacity is likely overstated and overshoot understated. The modest rise in global biocapacity 1961–2022 is therefore consistent with, but does not demonstrate, a “technology-outpaces-degradation” narrative; the data gaps themselves are part of the motivation for an explicit debt term.

Re-write the offending sentence as a qualified observation rather than a causal claim, or move it to the discussion with the caveats attached. This preserves the productivity-illusion argument while removing the over-interpretation.

**Empirical status and justification of functional forms.** State clearly (Introduction and Limitations) that the paper is a minimal mechanistic thought-experiment, not a calibrated Earth-system model. Parameters \(\rho\), \(\gamma\), \(\alpha\), the two lags, etc., are chosen illustratively so that interior equilibria exist, the delay-free linearization is attracting, and the two-delay Hopf threshold and debt-driven versus basin-loss collapse regimes appear on multi-decadal time-scales. They are lumped coefficients; constancy is a starting-point assumption that can be relaxed (e.g., \(\rho=\rho(M)\), \(\gamma=\gamma(D)\)). The logistic regeneration, exponential damage and logistic technology waves are selected for positivity, boundedness and continuity with the existing literature (Kuang, Meyer & Ausubel, Dasgupta). Linear depletion \(\gamma E\) is the simplest closure; nonlinear alternatives are noted as future work.

For the overuse–degradation link, add a handful of empirical citations (soil-carbon loss, fisheries, deforestation, IPCC land-degradation chapters) while acknowledging that a global, quantitatively unique mapping does not exist—hence the phenomenological debt term. The model does not “assume the dynamics then reproduce them”; it postulates two well-documented slow processes (environmental inertia, demographic inertia) plus irreversible debt, then derives the interaction theorems and the qualitative scenarios. Sensitivity and the already-mentioned debt-repayment tests remain the appropriate substitute for point estimation at this stage. A future calibration exercise against historical \(P\), reported \(B\) and \(E\) series (with uncertainty) is listed as an extension.
