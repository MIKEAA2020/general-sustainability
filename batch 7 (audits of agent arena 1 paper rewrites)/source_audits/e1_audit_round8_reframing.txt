The single highest-leverage reframing is to turn the paper from a **case-study negative result** into a **methods-and-practice contribution**: a general, reusable protocol for deciding whether added model structure improves out-of-sample forecasts of assessment outputs, validated by simulation, with northern cod as the worked application. That change would address the paper’s main impact-limiting weaknesses—small \(n\), point-rule ranking, non-identifiability, conditional-hindcast caveats—while preserving everything already done.

## Bottom line

**Primary recommendation:** Reframe as:

> **“A retention rule and information-set framework for evaluating added structure in stock-assessment forecasting: protocol, diagnostics, and a northern cod application.”**

Add a **simulation study** that validates the retention rule’s operating characteristics. Move most identification and per-origin detail to supplementary material. Make the headline finding: *Even when supplied with future catch, no structural module beats persistence; simulation shows this is due to non-identifiability and weak power, not necessarily to the irrelevance of the added processes.*

This would fit **ICES Journal of Marine Science**, **Fish and Fisheries**, or **CJFAS** and would be a genuine contribution to assessment practice, not just a stock-specific result.

## Why this reframing elevates impact most

1. **It generalizes the contribution.**  
   Currently the paper says: “For northern cod, under these two specifications, persistence wins.”  
   Reframed, it says: “Here is a transparent rule for testing whether added structure earns its keep; here is how to report the information set; here is what happens when you apply it to a canonical hard case.”

2. **It answers the “so what?” and the “low power” objections.**  
   A simulation study can show:
   - How often the rule retains a truly better module.
   - How often it rejects a module because the data cannot identify it.
   - How the 5% tie band, horizon pair, and RMSE choice affect outcomes.
   - Whether the northern cod non-retention is a power problem or a genuine failure.

3. **It turns the conditional-hindcast caveat into a feature.**  
   The paper already has an excellent **information-set table** (Table 2b). Make that a central methodological proposal: forecast evaluations should explicitly distinguish *available* from *supplied* quantities. Then the negative result becomes stronger: even with future catch supplied—an advantage no operational forecast has—the structural modules still lose to persistence.

4. **It makes the identification diagnostics reusable.**  
   The flat-valley analysis, boundary Allee estimate, parameter compensation, and floor-binding log-RMSE are currently buried in caveats. In a methods framing, they become a **diagnostic toolkit** for why added structure fails.

5. **It justifies the protocol disclosure.**  
   The paper admits the tie band and comparator declarations were post hoc. In a methods paper, that becomes part of the contribution: here is a fixed computational protocol, here is what was pre-specified, here is what was completed after scoring, and here is a simulation showing the rule’s properties.

## Concrete changes to make

### 1. New title, abstract, and introduction
- **Title:** Emphasize the rule, the framework, and the application. Example:  
  *“When does added structure improve stock-assessment forecasts? A retention rule, information-set framework, and the northern cod case.”*
- **Abstract:** Lead with the methodological problem, not the stock. State the rule, the simulation validation, and the headline negative result.
- **Introduction:** Position the paper against MASE, hindcast cross-validation, and the diagnostic cookbook. Make the gap: no one has a graded, comparator-declared retention rule applied to assessment output with an explicit information-set audit.

### 2. Add a simulation study (highest-value addition)
Design:
- Generate synthetic data from known surplus-production maps (Schaefer, depensatory, with/without AR residuals, with/without catch regimes).
- Apply the same ladder and retention rule.
- Vary: series length, catch contrast, observation error, process noise, true \(\mathfrak s\), true \(\phi\).
- Measure:
  - Probability of retaining each module when it is true.
  - Probability of retaining persistence when persistence is true.
  - False-retention rates.
  - Sensitivity to the 5% tie band and horizon pair.
- Report operating characteristic curves.

This directly addresses the reviewer concern: “Your negative result may be due to low power.” If the rule has good power in simulation but fails to retain on northern cod, that strengthens the conclusion that the data are uninformative for these modules, not that the rule is broken.

If a full simulation is too much, do a **parametric bootstrap power analysis** using the fitted northern cod models: simulate alternative histories where M1 is true, apply the rule, and see how often it retains M1. That is lighter but still very valuable.

### 3. Formalize the retention rule as a decision protocol
- Justify RMSE, both horizons, and the 5% tie band—or at least discuss sensitivity.
- State the rule as a general algorithm, not a one-off.
- Move the protocol-status disclosure to a clearly labeled subsection: “What was pre-specified, what was completed after scoring.”

### 4. Promote the information-set table to a central contribution
- Rename it “Table 1. Information set at forecast origin.”
- Propose it as a standard reporting template for any hindcast evaluation.
- Use it to frame the conditional-hindcast issue: “M2–M4 receive future catch; M1–M1b do not. This is why their scores respond differently to catch treatment.”

### 5. Streamline the main text
Move to supplementary material:
- The full M1b profile and curvature measures.
- The M4 decomposition against stale-start persistence.
- The catch-treatment reconciliation (264 vs 303, flat valley).
- Per-origin floor-binding counts.
- The long M1/M2 recovery-window identity explanation.

Keep in main text:
- The ladder and retention rule.
- The headline rolling-origin RMSE table.
- The simulation validation.
- The information-set table.
- The key identification diagnostic (one figure or table).
- The conclusion and scope.

Target a 30–40% reduction in main-text length.

### 6. Reframe the negative result
- Headline: “Even with future catch supplied, no structural module beats persistence at both horizons on either specification.”
- Explain: simulation shows the rule can detect real improvements when the data identify them; northern cod does not.
- Clarify: non-retention is a statement about these implementations on these series, not a claim that surplus production is useless.

### 7. Fix remaining terminology and consistency issues
- Replace “delay” with “lagged initialisation” or “stale-start” throughout, including the retention rule, ladder ordering, and conclusions.
- Correct all stale numbers in Section 3.5 “Readings” and Section 4’s Table 9 discussion.
- Fix M1b boundary language: “approaches an unattained lower bound,” not “at its lower bound.”
- Correct section citations (Proposition 4.1, Table 10) and table references (264 vs 303 kt).
- Clarify the 1992 catch-drop description: model treatment vs historical policy.

## Alternative reframings, ranked

1. **Methods/practice paper with simulation** — highest impact, most feasible given existing code.
2. **Forecast-evaluation-practice paper** centered on the information-set table and conditional hindcast. Feasible with editorial changes only; strong fit for *Fish and Fisheries* or *ICES JMS*.
3. **Identifiability paper** on why surplus-production modules cannot be identified from these data. Strong fit for *Ecological Modelling* or *Fisheries Research*, but narrower.
4. **Cross-domain framework paper** combining northern cod with the Edwards Aquifer case. Higher impact if coordinated with Abaee (2026a), but requires additional framing and coordination.
5. **Stock-specific negative result** — current form. Lowest impact; risks being read as “persistence wins for northern cod” rather than a general lesson.

## Target journals after reframing

- **ICES Journal of Marine Science** — best fit for methods/practice with simulation.
- **Fish and Fisheries** — if framed as a broad synthesis of forecast-evaluation practice.
- **Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences** — strong fit for the northern cod case plus methodological contribution.
- **Fisheries Research** — good fit for the surplus-production model evaluation and retention rule.
- **Ecological Modelling** — if the identifiability and simulation components are emphasized.

## What not to do

- Do not simply add more caveats. The paper is already over-caveated. Impact comes from a clear, generalizable contribution, not from defending every corner.
- Do not claim operational forecast skill. The conditional-hindcast framing is correct; make it a feature, not an apology.
- Do not treat M4 as a true delay. Rename it and explain why it is a stale-start experiment.
- Do not leave the retention rule as an ad hoc set of hypotheses. Formalize it and validate it.

**The single most impactful feasible change:** add a simulation study validating the retention rule and reframe the paper around that rule plus the information-set framework. That turns a defensive case study into a reusable methodological contribution and gives the negative result a credible, generalizable interpretation.
