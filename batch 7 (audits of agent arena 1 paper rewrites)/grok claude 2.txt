## 1. What “elevated” has to mean in this literature

In viability theory, obstruction is a **one-sided certificate**: \(B\notin\mathrm{ERViab}\). Achievability is the complementary one-sided certificate: \(B\in\mathrm{ERViab}\) together with a **regulation map**. Construction is a procedure that outputs that map (or an observation structure that makes the map exist).

Veliov and the estimation-tube programme already occupy the sufficiency high ground at the level of *existence in estimation space*. They do **not** occupy:

- a **common pointwise selection** (your Theorem 3 is exactly the gap);
- a **finite, checkable** witness of existence dual to Farkas;
- a **quantitative inverse problem** (choose \(O\), \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\), bias correction so that the kernel becomes nonempty);
- an **algorithm** that returns \(u=\pi(B)\) rather than a set-valued feedback on compact subsets.

Those four items are the elevation. Do not re-derive Veliov. Cite it as the existence envelope, then construct the objects his theorem does not hand over.

A useful slogan for the sequel:

> Obstruction says the predecessor is empty. Achievability exhibits a nonempty predecessor and a measurable selection. Construction computes both from data of \((f,U,D,\mathcal{V},O,T_{\mathrm{obs}})\).

## 2. Invert each mechanism: the constructive dual

### 2.1 Theorem 1 (exit) → constructive invariance

You already have the dual in spirit: the robust Nagumo condition \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{V}}(x)\neq\varnothing\) on \(\partial\mathcal{V}\) plus a measurable selection. Elevation is to **produce the selection and a margin**.

- **Achievability theorem.** If on a strip \(\{0\le q\le a\}\)
  \[
  \inf_{x}\sup_{u}\inf_{d} D^+q(x;f(x,u,d))\ge\varepsilon>0,
  \]
  then \(\{q\ge 0\}\) is robustly invariant, and any measurable \(u(x)\in\arg\max_u\inf_d D^+q\) is a regulation map. Exit time of Theorem 1 dualizes to a **minimum dwell / recovery time** \(q/\varepsilon\).
- **Construction.** Polyhedral / SOS / linear-program selection of \(u(x)\); or a barrier function whose Lie derivative supplies \(\varepsilon\). This is classical — keep it short, as the base case that needs **no** observation theory.

This theorem should stay the “dynamics-only” pole: if it fails, stop; if it holds, observation theory begins.

### 2.2 Theorems 2–3 (common action) → one-step synthesis

This is the centre of gravity. Emptiness of \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{V}}^B(B)\) is Farkas. Nonemptiness is an LP. That is already a construction, and you under-sell it.

**Achievability (one-step).**  
If \(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{tube}}(B,\Delta)\neq\varnothing\), then there exists a hold-\(\Delta\) observation-based action that keeps \(\mathrm{Reach}_{[0,\Delta]}(B,a)\subseteq\mathcal{V}\). Any \(a\) in that set is a constructive witness. Instantaneous \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{V}}^B(B)\neq\varnothing\) is the \(\Delta\downarrow 0\) inner approximation; the tube is the official object (your own remark).

**Construction.**

| Data | Procedure | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Polyhedral \(U,D,\mathcal{V}\), finite \(B=\{x_1,\dots,x_N\}\) | LP: find \(a\) s.t. \(A_i a\le b_i\) for all \(i\) (stack the safe-action inequalities); or the Farkas dual to **certify emptiness** | \(a^\star\) or a multiplier \(\lambda\) |
| Compact \(B\), Lipschitz \(f\) | discretize \(B\), over-approximate \(\mathrm{Reach}_{[0,\Delta]}(B,a)\) (interval, zonotope, support function), search \(a\) | inner/outer certificates |
| Convex \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{V}}(x)\) | set-valued intersection + measurable selection (Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski) | regulation map \(\pi(B)\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{V}}^B(B)\) |

**Recursive achievability (the real kernel construction).**  
Define the **epistemic predecessor** on information states:
\[
\mathrm{Pre}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{K})
=\bigl\{B:\ \exists a\in U^B(B)\ \text{s.t. every compatible one-step successor }B^+\in\mathfrak{K}\bigr\}.
\]
Then \(\mathrm{ERViab}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{V})\) is the largest fixed point of \(\mathrm{Pre}_{\mathcal{I}}\) inside \(\{B:B\subseteq\mathcal{V}\}\). That is Saint-Pierre’s algorithm, moved to beliefs, with the **common-action constraint baked into Pre**. Obstruction = “this \(B\) is outside the first Pre.” Construction = iterate Pre until convergence on a finite partition of \(Y\) (or a grid on compact subsets).

This single object does three things you currently split across theorems: it contains Theorem 3 as \(\mathrm{Pre}(\{B\subseteq\mathcal{V}\})=\varnothing\), it contains the tube form, and it is the constructive counterpart of the estimation-tube value function **with a single-valued selection**.

Write one theorem:

> **Theorem (constructive one-step).** \(B\in\mathrm{Pre}_{\mathcal{I}}(\{B'\subseteq\mathcal{V}\})\) iff \(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{tube}}(B,\Delta)\neq\varnothing\). If the data are polyhedral, this is decidable by linear programming, and any feasible \(a\) is an observation-based one-step policy.

That sentence is the elevation of Theorem 3.

### 2.3 Theorem 4 (delay) → review-interval synthesis

Obstruction: \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}>\inf q/\varepsilon\) ⇒ failure.  
Achievability is **not** the negation (the bound is only necessary). Construction is to compute the **maximal safe hold**.

**Design problem.**
\[
\Delta^\star(B)=\sup\bigl\{\Delta\ge 0:\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{tube}}(B,\Delta)\neq\varnothing\bigr\}.
\]
Then any review interval \(\Delta<\Delta^\star(B)\) is a constructive schedule at that information state; \(\Delta^\star=0\) recovers Theorem 3; \(\inf q/\varepsilon\) is a cheap inner bound on \(\Delta^\star\) under uniform outward drift.

**Achievability theorem (checkable special case).**  
If \(O\) is constant on \([0,\Delta]\), \(B_0\) compact, and there exists a **single** \(a\) such that
\[
\sup_{x\in B_0}\sup_{d(\cdot)}\ \text{exit-time}(x,a,d)\ge\Delta,
\]
then hold-\(a\) is robustly viable on that interval. Compute the exit-time by Dini comparison or by reachability.

This is the natural joint with your sample-and-hold governance paper: \(T_r\) becomes a **computed** spectral/viability design parameter, not only a crossing of a monodromy. One constructed object — \(\Delta^\star\) — would serve both manuscripts.

### 2.4 Theorem 5 (fibres) → observer / indicator synthesis

You already have a construction you did not name as such:

- **Conservative certifier (always exists):** \(C(y)=1\) iff \(y\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mathrm{safe}}\). Sound, incomplete. This is achievability of *verification*, not of *control*.
- **Exact certifier exists** iff fibres do not cross \(K\). Construction of \(C\) is \(C=\mathbf{1}_{O(K)}\).

**Inverse problem (the elevation).**  
Given floors \(K\) (or \(\mathcal{V}\), or \(\mathrm{RViab}\)), **design** \(O:X\to Y\) of bounded complexity such that fibres lie in one class:
\[
\min_{O\in\mathcal{O}}\ \mathrm{cost}(O)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\forall y,\ O^{-1}(y)\subseteq K\ \text{or}\ O^{-1}(y)\subseteq X\setminus K.
\]
Special cases that are theorems, not slogans:

1. **Disaggregation.** If \(K=\{q_1\ge 0,\dots,q_m\ge 0\}\), the map \(O(x)=(q_1(x),\dots,q_m(x))\) (or their signs) is a finite exact certifier. Per-floor measurement is a **constructed** sufficient design, which you currently only mention.
2. **Linear sensors.** \(O(x)=Hx\). Fibre-crossing iff some fibre of \(H\) meets both \(K\) and its complement; for polyhedra this is LP. Sensor placement = choose rows of \(H\) to kill those fibres.
3. **Refinement.** If \(O\) fails Theorem 5, a partition of the bad fibres (one extra bit per crossing) restores exactness. The **minimal number of extra bits** is the log of the number of mixed fibres — a quantitative coarseness theorem, dual to “separate safety classes, not states.”

Lead Section 4 with Corollary 6 and this inverse problem; keep Theorem 5 as a proposition.

### 2.5 Remark 1 (certainty equivalence) → observer construction

This is the easiest construction in the paper, and should be promoted from a remark to a short constructive theorem.

- If \(O\) is known and invertible, \(\pi(y)=k(O^{-1}(y))\) recovers \(\mathrm{Viab}\). Construction: invert.
- If \(O\) is biased but the bias class is known (\(\hat S=S+b\), \(b\in[b_{\min},b_{\max}]\)), two constructive routes:  
  (i) **worst-case correction** \(u=k(\hat S-b_{\max})\) (or the corresponding eroded kernel \(K^{-c\varepsilon}\));  
  (ii) **identifier + CE** (online estimate of \(b\), then invert).
- If \(O\) is noisy, the observer-and-buffer transfer you sketch in 5(c) **is** the construction — but it needs a cited theorem and an explicit buffer width, not “standard arguments.” Write: Lipschitz \(k\), observer decay \(\lambda\), margin \(\varepsilon\) ⇒ \(K_\delta\) viable under \(u=k(\hat x)\) for \(\delta=\delta(L_k,M,\lambda,\varepsilon)\). That is achievability with a formula.

The monitoring moral then becomes an obligation with two constructed objects: an error bound **and** a correction map. Without the second, you have Remark 1; with it, you have a theorem.

## 3. Theorems the sequel actually needs

Five results would change the paper’s type from obstruction calculus to a small synthesis theory.

**A. Common-selection theorem (the missing interface with estimation tubes).**  
Let \(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{est}}(B)\) be the set-valued regulation map of the estimation-space problem (Cardaliaguet–Quincampoix–Saint-Pierre). Then:

- \(\mathrm{ERViab}_{\mathcal{I}}\) is nonempty at \(B\) iff \(B\) is viable for that set-valued system **and** there exists a **jointly admissible** selection \(a\in\bigcap_{x\in B}U(x)\) in \(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{est}}(B)\);
- the second conjunct fails iff Theorem 3 fires.

That single statement positions your obstruction as the **selection obstruction inside a solved estimation problem**, and makes achievability = “estimation kernel nonempty + LP/selection feasible.”

**B. Finite-horizon constructive criterion.**  
For sampled review with horizon \(N\) reviews, existence of an observation-based policy is a tree of tube-feasibility problems (dynamic programming on a finite partition of \(Y\), or on a grid of compact sets). Polyhedral data ⇒ decidable. This is the “finite, checkable” claim the abstract currently misapplies to Theorems 1 and 4.

**C. Maximal review interval.**  
\(\Delta^\star(B)\) as above, with inner bound \(\inf q/\varepsilon\) and outer bound from a conservative reachability tube. Governance output: a number.

**D. Observation synthesis for exact certification.**  
Existence of a map \(O\) in a declared class (linear, coordinate projections, thresholded indices) such that fibres do not cross \(K\), plus the certainly-safe relaxation when exactness is impossible. Governance output: which indicators to add.

**E. Completeness in a restricted class.**  
You cannot close the middle ground in general (and should not claim to). You **can** close it for:

- finite \(Y\), finite \(U\), sampled hold; or  
- linear-polyhedral systems.

Statement: in that class, either a Farkas/tube certificate of nonviability exists, or the DP/LP returns a policy. That is a genuine necessary-and-sufficient theorem, which the present paper correctly refuses in the general nonlinear setting.

## 4. Computational layer (what “construction” looks like on the page)

Without at least one computed object, “construction” remains rhetorical. Three levels, increasing commitment:

1. **Worked examples with numbers** (minimum). Example 1: compute \(\mathcal{R}^B=\varnothing\), then add one bit of observation (\(\mathrm{sign}\,\theta\)) and exhibit \(u=\mathrm{sign}\,\theta\). Theorem 2: exhibit the unique common action \(u=0\) and the exit time. Remark 1: plot \(S(t)\) under uncorrected vs corrected CE. Theorem 4: one numerical \(\inf q/\varepsilon\) vs \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\). This already converts “checkable” from a claim into a figure.

2. **A Saint-Pierre-type scheme on a finite observation alphabet.** Discrete \(Y\), discrete \(U\), interval arithmetic on \(X\). Iterate Pre. Report the computed epistemic kernel for the hidden-mode example and for a 2-D stock-and-flow sketch. This is the viability-theory house style (Saint-Pierre 1994) and will be recognized as construction.

3. **A design optimizer.** Decision variables: review interval, which coordinates are observed, bias-correction on/off. Constraint: computed Pre nonempty. Objective: monitoring cost. Even a tiny instance (two stocks, three candidate indicators, two cadences) turns Section 6.4 from a list of morals into a table of designs.

Code in an appendix, not “available on request.”

## 6. The middle ground, handled honestly

The open set “neither Veliov nor obstruction” should become a **research programme with a first theorem**, not a limitation paragraph.

Three constructive attacks, in order of difficulty:

1. **Strengthen the observer** until a Veliov-type condition holds (buffer + convergent estimate). Construction: observer + eroded kernel. This is 5(c) done properly.
2. **Refine \(O\)** until mixed fibres disappear or \(\mathcal{R}^B\neq\varnothing\). Construction: the extra-bit / extra-sensor problem of §2.4.
3. **Shrink the policy class in the other direction** — not CE, but **set-valued** or **randomized** actions when \(U^B=\varnothing\) is an admissibility clash rather than a safety clash (switching, dithering, probing). Probing is the interesting one: spend a short interval on an identifying control that splits \(B\), then apply the common action on each atom. That is a constructed policy that **uses** indistinguishability rather than being defeated by it. It is also the first place your calculus would need a new object (information-value of a control). Flag it; one example is enough.

Do not claim (3) unless you write it. Mentioning it as the canonical occupant of the middle ground is already an elevation of Section 6.5.

- Do not promote the linear-substitution Farkas of 5(d) into the construction half; at most one sentence: the same alternative (pathways vs multipliers) is the material dual of common-action vs Farkas.

## A. Major problems

**A1. Theorem 4 is circular as stated.** Hypothesis (H2) asserts: *for every observation-based policy* there is a compatible initial state and admissible disturbance under which drift ≤ −ε holds "along the realized trajectory from x\* *under the policy's actions*". That is already the statement that every policy admits a violating compatible branch; the conclusion B₀ ∉ ERViab follows from (H2) alone, and (H1) and the timing bound (4) contribute nothing to the proof. The proof's sentence "Until t\*, the record contains no informative observation … so the policy cannot have altered its action" is inert, because (3) has already been assumed *under whatever actions the policy takes*. The timing bound only becomes load-bearing if the drift hypothesis is verified for *open-loop controls on [0, T_obs)* — which is what observation-equivalence buys: before T_obs the policy's actions are a fixed function of time common to all branches. Restate (H2) as a condition on open-loop controls over [0, T_obs) from a state x\* ∈ B₀ (a robust tube-emptiness condition), and then the timing bound is genuinely what makes the obstruction epistemic rather than dynamic. As written, the theorem is a hypothesis-restatement.

**A2. Theorem 1's proof does not establish existence of the adversarial closed loop.** The selection d(t) ∈ D_ε(x(t), u(t)) is a *state-dependent* selection; Aubin–Frankowska Thm 8.1.3 (measurable selection in t of a measurable set-valued map) does not give it, and a merely measurable feedback σ(x,u) yields a right-hand side f(x,u(t),σ(x,u(t))) that is discontinuous in x, for which Carathéodory existence fails. The parenthetical "convexity of D … is needed only for Filippov-style closure arguments, which play no role here" is exactly backwards: closure/relaxation is the standard tool for this step. Since the constraint ∇q·v ≤ −ε is *convex in v*, the convexified inclusion ẋ ∈ co f(x,u(t),D_ε(x,u(t))) preserves the drift inequality and admits solutions under Marchaud conditions; state that, or assume D lower semicontinuous (or constant) so that a locally constant adverse disturbance suffices. The same gap recurs in Theorem 3's proof ("measurable local selection d(x) with d(x̄) = d̄").

**A3. Theorem 2's emptiness is manufactured by the state-dependent control set, not by hidden modes.** With U(S) = {0, r(S)}, the control r(S) is admissible *only* at S; the obstruction is an admissibility artefact (U^B(B₀) = {0}). With the natural constant control set U = [0, ū], the constant control u = r(1.5) drives every state of the fibre [1,2] to the stable point r⁻¹(u) ∈ (1,2), so the fibre *is* epistemically viable and the theorem's system is not an example of the mechanism the section heading announces ("hidden modes"). Example 1 (θ = ±1) is the genuine hidden-mode example. Either drop Theorem 2, or replace its plant by one where the incompatibility is in the *safe* sets under a fixed U (Example 1 generalised), and justify any state-dependent U(x) as a modelling primitive with the convention "inadmissible action = failure" stated explicitly (it is currently implicit in both Theorem 2 and Theorem 3's admissibility case).

**A4. The novelty framing is inconsistent with §6.3 and with what the estimation-tube programme provides.** Abstract and §1.1 say the necessity side "has lacked a comparable instrument", but the Cardaliaguet–Quincampoix–Saint-Pierre reduction gives a Dini characterization of the belief-space kernel, i.e. a *necessary and sufficient* condition; §6.3 then concedes the calculus is "the local certificate layer" of that programme. The defensible claim is narrower: finite, mechanism-specific *sufficient* certificates for belief-space nonviability. Scale the Abstract, §1.1 and §7 accordingly.

**A5. §5(b) and §6.3 contradict each other on the same point.** §5(b): the estimation-space policy "is a set-valued feedback, and its pointwise selections need not be jointly admissible." §6.3: "correctly carried out, its belief-state controls are common controls applicable to all compatible states." Pick one; the second is correct for the set-membership semantics adopted.

**A6. Theorem 3 without the dwell hypothesis is false for nonconvex U, and the paper does not say why.** A measurable control's value at a single instant is irrelevant, and a chattering control realises any relaxed action in co U(x) when f is affine in u; if the state-specific safe sets are disjoint but their convex hulls meet, a common *relaxed* safe action exists. (H1) rules this out. Say so explicitly, and note the case in which (H1) is unnecessary (f affine in u, U(x) convex ⇒ safe sets convex, chattering adds nothing; also the boundary case of Example 1, where any positive-measure hold exits).

**A7. "Linearly bounded" appears for the first time inside Theorem 1's proof. State Marchaud-type standing assumptions once.

**A8. Definition 1 is not well posed.** (i) ("there exists … an admissible disturbance realization compatible with the record such that every trajectory compatible with the record remains in V") is murky even with the "coupling caveat", and EViab is then never used ("We use ERViab throughout"). Also the *domain* of admissible initial beliefs is never specified (Theorem 2 silently assumes observation fibres), and the sanity lemma ERViab = RViab for singleton beliefs under injective O is neither stated nor proved.

## 1. Reframe: the kernel as a fixed point, obstructions as complement certificates

The single move that turns the paper around is to define the **belief-space predecessor operator** and prove that the obstructions are exactly the finite witnesses of its emptiness.

For a review interval Δ (or, in the continuous case, in the limit), define on the hyperspace 𝒦(𝒱) of compact information sets

  Pre_Δ(𝔎) = { B : ∃ a ∈ 𝒜_tube(B, Δ) such that every successor belief B′ of (B, a) lies in 𝔎 },

where successors range over the observations y that can arrive after Δ under some compatible (x, d), and B′ = Reach_Δ(B, a) ∩ O⁻¹(y). Then

- ERViab_𝓘(𝒱) is the greatest fixed point of Pre_Δ contained in {B ⊆ 𝒱} (this is the estimation-space theorem stated for your sampled setting; prove it, don't cite it);
- Theorem 3 is exactly "𝒜_tube(B, Δ) = ∅ ⇒ B ∉ Pre_Δ(anything)";
- Theorem 4 is "𝒜_tube(B₀, T_obs) = ∅ certified through a drift bound";
- Theorem 1 is "𝒜_tube(x, Δ) = ∅ for every singleton, for every Δ > q(x)/ε".

Once this is in place, **achievability is the same computation read the other way**: any measurable selection a(B) ∈ 𝒜_tube(B, Δ) on the fixed point is a viable observation-based policy. The paper's title can honestly change from "obstruction calculus" to "epistemic viability: kernel, certificates, and synthesis".

Two theorems to state here:

- **Monotonicity/lattice theorem.** If O′ refines O (O′ ≼ O as partitions), then proj_∃ ERViab_O ⊆ proj_∃ ERViab_O′ ⊆ RViab(𝒱), with equality on the right when O is injective and Δ ↓ 0. Kernel is antitone in coarseness, antitone in Δ, antitone in delay.
- **Squeeze theorem.** For each mechanism, an outer bound (obstruction) and inner bound (achievability); the gap is the "open middle ground" of §6.5, now with both edges computable.

## 2. One constructive twin per mechanism

**(1) Exit certificate ↔ inward-margin invariance, with the band quantified.**
Prove the mirror statement: if sup_u inf_d ∇q·f ≥ +ε on the strip, then a measurable selection (or, under convexity, a continuous one via Michael) makes {q ≥ 0} robustly invariant. Then characterise the band where sup inf ∈ (−ε, +ε): show that on it the minimal time-to-violation function

  T(x) = sup_u inf_d inf{ t : q(x(t)) < 0 }

is the viscosity solution of a Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs equation, and the robust kernel is {T = +∞}. The band is then not "unresolved" but numerically computable. That is the achievability side of Theorem 1: a computable margin function m(x) := sup_u inf_d ∇q·f whose sign field delimits the kernel boundary.

**(2)/(3) Common-action obstruction ↔ common-action achievability, including relaxation.**
State the belief-space Nagumo theorem: if ℛ^B(B_t) ≠ ∅ along every belief reachable under some selection, the belief is viable, and the selection is the policy. Then add the **relaxed-control achievability**: if f is affine in u and the state-specific safe sets have intersecting convex hulls, a chattering (or averaged, in the sampled case) control achieves viability even when the instantaneous intersection is empty. This converts A6 from a caveat into a theorem: *the obstruction is sharp exactly when co ℛ_𝒱(x) ∩ co ℛ_𝒱(x′) = ∅*. Example 1 then becomes a theorem instance: at z = 0, co{+1} ∩ co{−1} = ∅, so no relaxation helps; move the constraint to z ≥ −η and show a dithered control does achieve viability for η > 0 with an explicit dwell — the "tube" version yields a computable minimal buffer for coarse information.

**(4) Delayed information ↔ a sampling theorem for viability.**
This is the highest-value construction and it links directly to your sample-and-hold governance paper. Prove: if the perfect-information kernel K has an inward margin m > 0 on a buffered boundary layer of width ρ, f is Lipschitz with constant L on 𝒱, and disturbances are bounded by ‖d‖ ≤ δ, then sample-and-hold output feedback with review interval

  Δ < Δ*(m, ρ, L, δ)  (explicit; of the form ρ /(sup‖f‖ · e^{LΔ}) solved for Δ, or the tighter Isaacs bound ρ/ε_drift),

keeps a buffered subset K^{−ρ} viable with the observation taken only at review instants. Theorem 4 says T_obs > inf q/ε kills viability; the sampling theorem says T_obs < ρ/ε′ preserves it; between them lies the review-interval design problem, and both bounds are computable from the same drift data. This is the analogue of Nešić–Teel for viability rather than stability, and it is exactly the missing constructive content of your first paper's "review interval as design parameter".

**(5) Fibre criterion ↔ the coarsest sufficient observation.**
Theorem 5 is static; make it dynamic and constructive. Define an observation O to be **viability-sufficient** for a target set K₀ ⊆ RViab(𝒱) if K₀ ⊆ proj_∃ ERViab_O. Then prove a **minimal-observation theorem**: the coarsest viability-sufficient observation is the quotient by the coarsest equivalence ≡ such that (i) ≡ is finer than the safe-action partition on the kernel boundary (x ≡ x′ ⇒ co ℛ_K(x) ∩ co ℛ_K(x′) ≠ ∅), and (ii) ≡ is preserved by the closed-loop predecessor (a bisimulation-type condition). Give the **partition refinement algorithm**: start from the safe-action partition, iteratively split classes whose successors under a common safe action land in different classes, terminate at the greatest fixed point. In the finite-mode setting (hidden θ ∈ Θ finite) the algorithm is finite. This is the continuous analogue of Lin–Wonham observability / supremal normal sublanguages in supervisory control under partial observation — cite that literature; it is the closest existing "achievability under partial observation" theory and it has exactly your structure (controllability = viability, observability = fibre condition, normality = certain-safety).

The governance payoff: instead of "your index cannot certify the floor", the theorem says "here is the minimal set of distinctions the index must make", i.e. which pairs of states must be separated, and nothing more.

**(6) Certainty-equivalence trap ↔ a separation theorem for viability.**
Replace Remark 1 with the positive result: **robust certainty equivalence** — apply the feedback to the worst-case member of the belief, u(t) ∈ ℛ^B(B_t), where B_t is produced by a set-valued (interval, zonotopic, or polytopic) observer — is viable on the buffered kernel whenever the observer's belief diameter is ≤ ρ within time T_conv and the margin absorbs ρ. Prove the separation structure: observer design (contracting belief) and controller design (selection from the common safe set) can be done independently, with the buffer as the coupling constant. Bias then becomes a special case: a known bias is absorbed into the observer; an unknown bounded bias enters as a hidden parameter and the belief lives in X × [−b̄, b̄], where your Example 1 machinery applies.

## 3. Observation design as synthesis, not diagnosis

**Farkas multipliers as design instructions.** In the polyhedral common-action case the certificate λ ≥ 0 with λᵀA = 0, λᵀb < 0 has support on a specific set of (state, constraint) rows. Prove: the compatible states whose rows carry the support of λ form a *minimal separating set* — refining the observation to distinguish them (in the sense of §2, item 5) restores nonemptiness of the common action set at that belief. So the same LP that certifies infeasibility outputs the information that must be acquired. This is the "shadow price of information" reading, and it is entirely constructive.

**Value of information as kernel expansion.** Define VoI(O → O′; K₀) = the increase in the volume (or in the robust margin function) of proj_∃ ERViab when refining O to O′, and show it is monotone and submodular-like under refinement in the finite-mode case. Monitoring design becomes: choose (Δ, O, delay) subject to cost to make K₀ ⊆ proj_∃ ERViab. The certificates supply the constraints; the sampling theorem and minimal-observation theorem supply the feasible set.

**Three design theorems worth stating explicitly**, each with the paper's current obstruction as its converse:

- minimal review frequency (from Δ*),
- minimal observation resolution (from the coarsest sufficient partition),
- maximal tolerable bias/delay (from the buffer arithmetic of the separation theorem).

## 4. Computability — where the fixed point is actually tractable

The belief-space kernel lives on a hyperspace; you need classes where it collapses:

- **Finite hidden modes.** Θ finite, x observed exactly: beliefs are X × 2^Θ, and Saint-Pierre's viability kernel algorithm runs on a finite product of copies of X. Example 1 generalised (θ ∈ {recovering, collapsing}, multiple stocks) is fully computable; this should be the paper's worked example, with the kernel plotted, the coarsest sufficient observation derived, and a policy synthesised.
- **Linear dynamics, polytopic D and U, box/zonotope beliefs.** Beliefs stay in a finite-parameter family under set-valued observers; the predecessor is a polytope operation; the epistemic kernel is approximated by a finite lattice of polytopes. This also makes the tube-safe action set an LP for real, with hypotheses stated.
- **Scalar and planar sustainability models** (Schaefer/logistic with hidden r or K): interval beliefs, closed-form predecessor, everything explicit — and directly relevant to the fisheries setting of the companion paper (unknown natural mortality M is literally a hidden mode).
