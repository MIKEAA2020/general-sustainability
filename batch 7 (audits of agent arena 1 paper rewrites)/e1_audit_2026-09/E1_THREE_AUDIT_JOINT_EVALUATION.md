## 2. Prop 4.1 — UPHELD. My earlier pass was wrong.

The proposition claims: *if the observed path is non-monotone (crash then partial recovery), no trajectory of
`F` reproduces it exactly.*

Using the paper's own printed fit, `F(S) = S + 1.935·S(1−S/1032.7) − 240`:

```
S0 = 950 → 857.2 → 899.1 → 884.2 → 890.2 → 887.9
```

**That is a decline followed by a recovery, generated by the very map the proposition invokes.** GPT's
counterexample reproduces exactly. I swept starting values 150–1040 kt: **89 of 90 produce a non-monotone
trajectory that never goes below the repeller.** These are generic, not knife-edge — the attractor has
`F′(S*) ≈ −0.39 < 0`, so *damped oscillation is the normal local behaviour*, and every oscillation is
non-monotone.

**The proof proves something narrower than the statement.** It rules out *crash below the lower repeller
followed by recovery*. It does not rule out non-monotonicity, and the proof's own parenthesis concedes
"damped oscillations around it — not a monotone approach," which **contradicts the proposition's conclusion
in the same breath**. This is why it survived my line-level read: the proof text is locally correct and the
error is a quantifier mismatch between statement and proof.

**Fix:** restate as — *no trajectory that falls below the lower repelling equilibrium can subsequently
recover; the observed path does so, hence it is not a trajectory of F.* Then show the observed path actually
crosses 144 kt (it does — SSB falls to single digits by 1995). This preserves the paper's conclusion while
making the claim true. Note the claim must also be restricted to the **autonomous, noise-free** case: Def 2.1
includes `ε_t`, and M3/M4 are not autonomous scalar maps (qwen 3.2 — also upheld).

## 3. Lemma 3.2 — UPHELD. Premise fails on the window used.

The lemma is stated "on a training window along which `S_t` rises monotonically" and is then applied to the
recovery window 1995–2007. Actual NCAM SSB on that window:

```
1995  9.68 → 1999 34.59 → 2000 34.42 → 2001 29.50 → 2002 23.87
      → 2003 22.07 → 2004 20.07 → 2005 25.18 → 2006 40.83 → 2007 81.10
```

**Five consecutive annual declines (2000–2004), a drawdown of 14.5 kt — 42% of the 1999 level.** The window
is emphatically not monotone. The lemma's hypothesis is not satisfied by the data it is applied to.

Separately, GPT's objection stands on its own: boundary attainment is not proof of non-identifiability, and a
threshold below the observed range is not necessarily "below any biologically meaningful threshold."

**Fix:** GPT's suggested replacement is right and costs nothing — *the fitted Allee parameter approached its
lower bound; because the training observations do not constrain behaviour near a putative threshold, we do
not read this as evidence for a biological Allee threshold.* **The empirical fact survives** — I confirmed
`s → 0` numerically (2.1×10⁻²³ coarse, 9.4×10⁻⁶ annual). Only the theorem-packaging fails.

## 4. K lower bound — UPHELD, but all three audits get the direction backwards

All three treat this as an unresolvable contradiction and demand reconciliation. Having pulled
`src/run_ladder.py`, it **is** resolvable, and the resolution is not what any of them guessed:

```python
x0     = [0.3, max(np.max(S) * 1.5, 500.0)]          # 500.0 = INITIALISER
bounds = [(1e-3, 2.0), (np.max(S0) + 10.0, 5000.0)]  # lower bound = 50.83 kt here
```

**§2.2 is correct; §3.2 and Table 10 are wrong.** 500.0 is the multi-start initialiser sitting **449 kt above**
the true bound — L-BFGS-B never left `x0` because the valley is flat.

**And there is a further error none of the three audits caught: the two catch treatments are swapped.**
Re-running both fits:

| reproduced | paper attributes to |
|---|---|
| C=5.00 (**coarse**): r=0.458, K=500.0, MSE=128.35 | annual landings ❌ |
| C=3.19 (**annual**): r=0.370, K=5000.0, MSE=127.84 | coarse regime ❌ |

Same swap for M1b (coarse K=105.8, annual K=129.8). Table 10's "neither is reconciled here" should be
deleted, not softened — it grants false parity to a section that is simply wrong.

**This correction strengthens the paper:** a *stalled optimiser start* is better evidence of a flat objective
than a bound solution, since the gradient was too weak to move it 449 kt.

## 5. Table 9 — the audits are right to demand a definition, wrong that it is an error

GPT is careful here ("I am **not** saying this necessarily proves a computational error"); qwen states flatly
that the row is "clearly inconsistent." **GPT's caution is vindicated.**

`campaign_e1_dm_uncertainty.py` computes two different things: `DM_z` is Diebold–Mariano on the per-origin
**squared-loss difference**; `ci95`/`p` come from a Künsch moving-block bootstrap of the **RMSE difference**
(20,000 reps, seed 0). Table 9 reproduces `e1_dm_uncertainty.csv` exactly, and CI and *p* are **internally
coherent in all 32 rows** (CI excludes 0 ⟺ p<0.05, zero exceptions).

They diverge in 5 of 32 rows because the loss differential is dominated by a few collapse origins that inflate
its variance, while the square root in RMSE compresses exactly that tail. **Both correct; different estimands.**
So the fix is qwen's *stated* remedy (define the column) rather than its *diagnosis* (a wrong number).

GPT's deeper point stands and is the more valuable one: **a bootstrap interval procedure does not by itself
specify a valid bootstrap hypothesis test.** The `p` here is a percentile-interval inversion, not a null-centred
test. That should be stated.

## 9. Conceptual items — upheld, and they are the ones that matter for resubmission

**GPT §3 (SSB−catch accounting)** is the strongest conceptual criticism in all three audits and has no
counterpart in my line-level pass. `S` is *spawning-stock* biomass; `C` is *total landings*. `ΔSSB + landings`
is not an exact stock balance — maturation, weight-at-age and selectivity all intervene. So "the model-free
accounting bound that precedes every fit" and "Regular et al.'s M≈2.5 peak is the same residual in assessment
clothing" are both too strong. This affects the paper's *explanation of why* the models fail. **Recommend
narrowing to "apparent net production," an explicitly model-dependent construct.**

**Brier (qwen 3.4)** — upheld as wording. The event is degenerate (LRP 884.6 kt above all rolling states), but
the *score* separates strongly (persist 0.00 vs M1/M2/M3/M4 1.00). "Degenerate event frequency" ≠ "little
separation." My earlier pass verified the 0.00 justification and missed this distinction.

**"Independent" certificate (gpt 8, qwen 3.8)** — upheld. Same stock, overlapping years, related assessment
products. "Second specification-level" is the honest phrase.
