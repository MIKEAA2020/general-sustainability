grok:

**Line-level review: flaws, inconsistencies, and improvements**

The paper’s design is unusually disciplined for a fisheries forecast comparison: a locked retention rule, two unpooled assessment specifications, origin-matched controls, an explicit negative certificate scoped to estimator and ladder, and a freeze-discipline caveat that most authors would hide. Those choices are the contribution. The same caution, however, coexists with a structural obstruction that the ladder cannot beat (and that the paper already knows), several numerical and origin-mix disclosures that a referee will treat as residual risk, a “machine-verified certificate” that verifies arithmetic not class-incompatibility, and a cluster of companion-paper / future-citation / freeze-asymmetric issues that weaken the claim of a fixed protocol. The empirical result — persistence wins — is robust to the checks you ran; the writing still asks the reader to treat a designed-to-fail class comparison as a scored discovery.

Issues are grouped by severity.

---

### 1. The result is largely predetermined (state this earlier and more sharply)

**Proposition 4.1 and Lemma 2.2 already say the class cannot reproduce the path.**  
A monotone one-dimensional autonomous map cannot crash and recover. The collapse window is therefore not a fair skill contest between persistence and M1/M1b/M2; it is a demonstration that the class is misspecified for this series. You say this in §4. The abstract and Highlights still read as if “no structural model beats persistence” were an empirical surprise. Move the obstruction to the front of the abstract (one sentence) and of §1, and recast the scored test as *how large the penalty is*, which is already the language of paragraph 3 of the introduction. Otherwise a referee will write: you fitted a model you proved cannot generate the data, then certified that it lost.

**The 1992 catch drop is endogenous.**  
§3.1 states this clearly: prescribing the drop in a constant-\(r\) map forces a rebound. That is the right diagnosis. It also means M2 is not a “stock-flow improvement” on collapse; it is a counterfactual that the paper itself rejects. Say in Table 2’s caption that M2 on 1991–95 is a *policy-as-exogenous* run, not a candidate for retention on that window.

**Persistence inherits the smoother.**  
§4 already notes that the predictand is NCAM/xteNCAM SSB, not a raw observation, and that persistence inherits the assessment’s autocorrelation. That caveat belongs in the Highlights and abstract. Forecasting a filtered state with last-value persistence is a high bar; losing to it is informative about *this ladder versus this assessment*, not about surplus production versus nature. A vintage-based or survey-only predictand would be a different (and harder) paper; you correctly say vintages are unavailable.

### 3. Mathematical / modelling notes

**Definition 2.1 and Lemma 2.2.**  
Correct: \(\mathfrak{s}\to 0\) is cubic, not Schaefer; Schaefer is \(a\equiv 1\). The same point was made in the sample-and-hold / obstruction papers. Keep the lemma; it prevents a standard referee comment.

**Lemma 3.2 (Allee unidentified on a recovery window).**  
Correct and useful. Scope: it applies to *monotonic recovery* training windows. On a window that dips below a candidate \(\mathfrak{s}\) the penalty argument fails. State the hypothesis (“training path strictly above a candidate threshold”) in the lemma statement, not only in the proof.

**Proposition 3.1 (M1 = M2 when \(C\equiv 5\)).**  
Trivial but worth having so Table 3’s “M1 = M2” is not mistaken for a bug.

**Proposition 4.1.**  
“Monotone parameter regime” is doing a lot of work. Define it: \(F\) increasing on \((0,S^\star)\) and on \((S^\star,K)\), unique positive equilibrium, no overshoot. Well scoped. Do not let it migrate into “the autonomous scalar class is falsified.” The class is obstructed by 4.1; the certificate is non-retention of *this implementation*.

**M4 delay decomposition.**

## A. Major problems

**A1. Proposition 4.1 does not apply to the fitted models, and does not apply to the window it is attached to.**
(i) It assumes a "monotone parameter regime" (undefined). For the discrete map $F(S)=S+rS(1-S/K)-C$, $F'(S)=1+r-2rS/K$, which is negative for $S>K(1+r)/(2r)$; at the fitted $r\approx 2$ (the reported saturation value) $F$ is decreasing above $0.75K$ and trajectories overshoot and oscillate — a discrete logistic at $r=2$ *can* produce a crash followed by partial recovery. The fits are outside the regime the proposition needs. (ii) "Unique positive equilibrium" is false for every member with $C>0$ (two positive roots below MSY) and for the Allee branch. (iii) The collapse test window 1991–1995 is monotone decreasing, so an obstruction against *non-monotone* paths says nothing about "M1's collapse failure"; M1 fails on collapse because the training window is flat and the plugged 240 kt catch forces a 235 kt yr⁻¹ mechanical rebound when catch drops. (iv) M2–M4 are non-autonomous (time-varying $C_t$) and the proposition is silent on them. Either drop it or restate it correctly for increasing $F$, apply it only to M1/M1b, and delink it from the collapse score.

**A2. Lemma 3.2's hypothesis fails on the actual training window.** The NCAM 1995–2007 series is not monotone (2000 = 34.4 kt, 2004 = 20.1 kt per Table A2). Moreover the "proof" only shows that $\mathfrak s>\min S_t$ incurs a penalty at some points; it does not show the objective is monotone in $\mathfrak s$, and the conclusion it draws ($\mathfrak s\le\min S_t\approx 10$–25 kt) is weaker than the reported $\mathfrak s\to 0$. The real reason $\mathfrak s$ is unidentified is designed in: the only years that could identify a threshold (a decline from states below it, 1992–1996) are excluded from every training window. State that; demote the lemma to a remark.

**A3. The declared parameter box is violated by the reported fits.** $K\in[500,5000]$ kt, yet §3.2 reports M1b $K=105.9$ and $129.8$ kt. Either M1b has different bounds (undeclared) or a bug. Conversely, the lower bound $K\ge 500$ kt exceeds every post-1992 SSB value (max 299), so on every recovery window the bound — not the data — dictates that the stock is far below carrying capacity and forces growth; §3.3's "carrying capacity extrapolated far beyond the training range" is the bound doing the extrapolating. This prior is nowhere acknowledged.

**A4. M1/M1b catch dependence is internally inconsistent.** Table 2 says M1 plugs the training-mean catch and M1b *estimates* $C$. Then: (a) Tables 4 and 5 report identical M1 and M1b rows (121/289, 115/289) under coarse and annual catch, although §3.2 documents that M1's recovery-window fit changes from 120 to 264 kt with the catch file — the rolling rows cannot both be unchanged; (b) if M1b estimates $C$, its fit cannot depend on the catch file, yet its recovery RMSE (90 vs 78) and $K$ (105.9 vs 129.8) differ between treatments. One of Table 2, Table 5, or §3.2 is wrong.

**A5. The flat-objective disclosure undermines the ranking claim.** §3.2 shows that changing the plugged constant by 1.8 kt moves the M1 minimiser from $K=500$ to $K=5000$ (SSE 128.35 vs 127.84 kt²) and the test RMSE from 120 to 264 kt. M1's score is therefore determined by which box edge the optimiser lands on, origin by origin. This is a two-parameter model nesting persistence ($r=0$) overfitting 7–30 transitions; say so, and either regularise/profile the fit or report the score range across the valley. As it stands "suffice to rank models" (§4) is not supported.

**A6. The blanket "no structural model beats persistence" is true only on rolling origins.** On the fixed recovery window M1b (90) beats persistence (104); on Spec B's recovery-stall window M1 (254) and M1b (178) beat persistence (262). Highlights, Abstract and §5 elide this; §4 even calls the stall-window result "the same failure on the forecast side" when the structural models win there. Qualify everywhere with "on rolling-origin RMSE", and report the fixed-window wins as what they are.

**A7. The M4 delay decomposition has its labels swapped.** With $p_1=$ persistence from $S_t$, $p_0=$ persistence from $S_{t-1}$: $p_0-p_1$ (184−98 = 86) *is* the cost of the one-year delay; $M4-p_0$ (196−184 = 12) is the structural model's cost given the delay. The text calls 86 "information loss" and 12 "the delay itself", then at $h=5$ on Spec B calls 694 (= M4 − $p_0$, the model cost) "the delay's persistence of stale information". Rewrite with those two quantities defined once. The constructive finding is being buried: a one-year-stale persistence (184) loses to every structural model at $h=1$, so the value of a timely assessment (86 kt) exceeds the value of any structure tested.

**A8. The retention rule is underspecified and has no noise margin.** Definition 2.4 does not say which horizon ($h=1$, $h=5$, both) decides; "next-simpler model on the ladder" is undefined for a non-nested ladder (is M2's comparator M1 or M1b?); and "reduces" is a raw comparison with no tie band or test, while §4 concedes $n$ "does not suffice to certify a small skill difference" (M1b 115 vs M1 121; M1 289 vs persist 265). Fix the horizon, list the comparison pairs, and add a Diebold–Mariano or block-bootstrap interval.

**A9. "Negative certificate … machine-verified … distinct from a statistical null result" inverts the evidential order.** A non-retention under a deterministic rule with no uncertainty is *weaker* than a statistical null, not a different and stronger object. "Machine layer"/"machine-verified" are never defined, and there is no code or data availability statement (Fisheries Research requires both). Definition 4.2 belongs in Methods.

**A10. Catch-source statements conflict.** Schijns et al. (2021) is called a "reconstruction" and "the best available public catch reconstruction", yet it is said to match STATLANT and Regular et al. Table 1 *exactly* on 1983–1993 — reconstructions include unreported catch and would not match reported landings to the tonne. Clarify whether Schijns is reported landings only; if so, the "unreported catch" hypothesis is untested by the annual-landings pass, which weakens §3.2's inference. Also "the STATLANT-versus-Schijns sensitivity is closed for the collapse window" leaves 1994–2015 and 1954–1982 open, both used.

**A11. Freeze-discipline claims are inconsistent.** Abstract: retention rule "fixed in the analysis scripts before execution." §4: passes evolved, no dated protocol file, only the primary pass is defensibly pre-fixed. Qualify the Abstract.

**A12. Three uncited, unpublished "companion" studies (Edwards Aquifer, governance, methodological template) carry claims in the text.** Cite or remove.
