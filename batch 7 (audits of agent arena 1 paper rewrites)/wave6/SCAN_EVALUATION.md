# Wave-6 scan evaluation — sentence-level normalised diff of the five preceding versions of each paper vs its final

**Task 76 (owner directive):** "conduct deep, systematic, granular sentence-level normalised diff scan; anything from any of the preceding 5 versions of each paper worth implementing in the final versions?"

**Method.** `wave6/scan_sentence_diff.py` (same splitter/normaliser on both sides of every comparison, so splitter quirks cancel; markdown table rows and display-math lines atomic; the per-version `*Version log` paragraph excluded as meta and quoted per transition; a sentence counts as dropped only if absent from the whole final file — relocation inside the paper is not a drop; P1/P3/P4/P5 drops are additionally checked against every supplementary file of the family, with relocations flagged). Full per-transition reports with per-sentence pairing (similarity ratio to the replacement sentence added in the same transition) in `wave6/scan/<paper>.md`; the per-transition counts in `wave6/scan/SUMMARY.csv`. Every low-pairing drop (r < 0.55, 355 of the 1,322) was then classified by hand against the recorded version logs, the wave-4/wave-5 records, the paper's own current text, the committed data and results files, and the audit files.

**Headline verdict.** Across the nine papers and 45 version transitions, **exactly two unregistered drops were worth implementing**, and both are now implemented (E2 v19, E3 v13). Everything else dropped over the last five versions of each paper is one of: (a) a recorded docket edit (wave-2/4/5 items with per-item records), (b) a superseded pre-correction number — restoring it would restore an error, (c) a recorded restructure or rename, (d) content preserved elsewhere in the final file or relocated to a supplementary, or (e) an abstract-cap or tightening edit with the replacement present.

## The two implemented restores (details and wording decisions)

### E2 v19 — the critical-zone supply gloss (Result 3.4's Reason)

* **The drop:** v13/v14 carried "S1 gives 10.0 kt (the critical-zone cut is active in 83% of observed years — the stock was below the LRP for almost the entire history, a fact about the collapsed-era estimation window rather than about the rule's post-recovery supply properties, and the two regimes are not mixed)" — §3.3's supply-replay paragraph. The v14→v15 source-year restructure kept the supply-replay values (now in Result 3.4's Reason: "BAU 5 kt, S1 10.0 kt, the cascade 16.3 kt …") but lost the explanation. No version log exists for v13–v16; the v17/v18 logs and the wave records do not mention it. Unregistered drop.
* **Worth implementing?** Yes — the final prints S1's mean (10.0 kt) against its 60-kt above-LRP cap with no explanation, and the explanation is a checkable presentation-layer fact of the window (the class Task 75 re-opened). The arithmetic is convention-robust (both 1983–2006 and 1984–2007 state conventions give 4 above-LRP states: 1985, 1987, 1988, 1989; 60 × 4/24 = 10.0; 20/24 = 83%).
* **Wording decision:** upgraded the v13 "83% of observed years" to the exact "20 of the 24 replay years" with the four states named; kept v13's scoping caveat ("a fact about the collapsed-era estimation window rather than about the rule's post-recovery supply properties, and the two regimes are not mixed") verbatim in substance; placed as one sentence inside Result 3.4's Reason immediately after the supply-replay values it explains; house percent style ($83\%$); the build script re-verifies the arithmetic against the committed series.

### E3 v13 — the post-2007 secondary-score record (§5.3, before §5.3.1)

* **The drop:** v9/v10's §5.3 carried the post-2007 paragraph ("On post-2007 origins only (n = 16, h = 1): persist 13.09, M1 12.16, M2 13.31, oracle 8.03 ft. The ranking is unchanged. The 660-ft Brier scores are 0.31 (persist), 0.25 (M1), and 0.19 (oracle). At h = 5 (n = 12): persist 25.10, M1 17.16, M2m 17.64, mean 16.41, M2/M3/M4 34.9–35.0, oracle 8.69 ft — the post-2007 h = 5 reversal of the M1–persistence ordering is reported without changing the one-year retention statement. …"). The v11 restructure dropped it wholesale; v11's log lists seven items, none of which is this. The result: Definition 4.1 *declares* the Brier as a secondary score (twice updated per the audits: "for deterministic 0/1 forecasts this score is a misclassification rate"), but no version since v10 prints its values — a declared score left dangling, and the audits had verified exactly those values.
* **Worth implementing?** Yes — the strongest class of restore: a declared quantity whose recorded values exist in the committed frozen results (`wave_e_edwards/results/rolling_modern_2007.csv`: 0.3125/0.25/0.1875; 13.087/12.163/13.314/8.031; 25.097/17.156) and in the frozen print the audit verified. No new computation anywhere.
* **Wording decision:** one paragraph with the definition's own scoping ("origins at or after 2007, where the 660-ft Stage I rule is in force"); the three Brier values in the v10 rounding (0.31/0.25/0.19); the one-year ranking row including M2's 13.31 (faithful to the frozen print); the h = 5 reversal with only the M1–persistence pair (17.16 vs 25.10) and v10's exact honest rider "reported without changing the one-year retention statement"; claude's small-n caveat ("a small subsample … not over-read") and the definition's misclassification-rate naming; the pointer to the committed CSV in the Data-Availability house style; the full h = 5 row left to the archived record (minimalism — the load-bearing content is the reversal, and every value remains retrievable from the committed file).
