grok:

**Line-level review: flaws, inconsistencies, and improvements**

The paper’s real contribution is a **scored, frozen protocol** on a single fitted map: kernels, a constructive catch bound, an expansion obstruction that empties certified kernels, and an honest split between geometric, identified, and form-sensitive findings. That discipline is rare in fisheries viability papers. The same caution is not yet matched by internal consistency. The abstract’s lead claim (“the LRP is protected by good years rather than by catch management”) is a property of *this map and these floors*, not of Northern cod; \(K\) is pinned at 5000 kt on a series whose maximum is 941 kt; the retention rule is structurally unable to retain any harvest at the boundary; freeze date 2026-08-26 is in the future if submitted now; and several “certificates” are arithmetic identities or rule-geometry, not empirical findings. A referee who grants the scoping sentences will still ask why the object is a forecast-losing, bound-pinned Schaefer map.

Issues grouped by severity.

### 1. Direct contradictions and overclaims (fix first)

**Abstract vs §4 on what the result is about.**  
Abstract last sentence: “The certificate is scoped to this fitted map… The paper asserts no such conclusion for Northern cod.”  
Abstract body and §3.2: “The reference point is protected by good years, not by demand management.”  
§5(5) repeats the slogan.  
Keep the slogan *only* after the scope clause, or a skimming reader (and a press office) will quote it as a stock finding. Put the scope sentence first in the abstract.

**“Protocol frozen before any kernel was computed” vs post-freeze layers.**  
Abstract: protocol frozen before any kernel. Then: “The stochastic, finite-duration, carrying-capacity, and bootstrap layers are post-freeze.” §2.1 repeats both. That is honest. It also means the abstract’s numbered results (3)–(5) and most of Tables 3–5 were *not* under the freeze. Either:

- restrict the abstract’s numbered list to frozen objects (Table 1, Props 3.1–3.4, 57.6 kt), and label 3.6–3.11 as post-freeze sensitivities, or  
- stop saying the protocol covers “the” results.

**Freeze date 2026-08-26.**  
Same anachronism as the obstruction paper’s reconstruction plan. If the manuscript is submitted before that date, the freeze is a future event. If after, lock the bibliographic status of Regular et al. (2025), Rose (2026) in the companion, and this date. A referee will treat an undated “frozen protocol” as a claim they cannot audit unless the archive is public at submission.

**Retention rule cannot retain anyone (Prop 3.1).**  
H1: kernel at least as protective as BAU at every reading. On a single lower threshold, any \(C>5\) at or above the LRP strictly raises the kernel boundary. S1 and the cascade are *defined* to harvest 60 kt above the LRP. They fail H1 by construction. The paper says this (“declared geometry… not an empirical verdict”). Then why run them through a retention rule whose only possible survivors are \(C\le 5\)? Either:

- drop “retention” and report a dominance partial order (Table 1 already is one), or  
- change H1 to a *trade-off* (protection vs mean catch) so a 60 kt cap can win on H3 without having to match the moratorium’s kernel.

As written, Prop 3.1 is a tautology of the rule, and comparing it to the groundwater companion that “retained reactive rules” is misleading: that companion’s constraint was apparently not a single fatal floor.

**Vacuous emptiness (Prop 3.2) is an identity.**  
\(|e| > g_{\max}\) ⇒ every trajectory falls, every \(C\ge 0\). Calling this a “negative certificate” inflates an arithmetic check. The abstract already says “vacuous robustness rather than a productivity finding.” Lead with that; do not number it as finding (2) on a par with the 57.6 kt bound.

**Expansion vs §4 limitation paragraph.**  
Prop 3.4 / Prop 3.6: expansion is “data-selected,” \(F'\ge 1\) at every \(K\ge 2K^*\), contraction only where certificates collapse.  
§4: “the expansion obstruction is therefore conditional on the bound-pinned carrying capacity, not on the identified \(r\).”  

Those two sentences fight. Table 3 supports the first: SSE falls toward \(K=5000\), \(F'=1\) exactly at \(2K^*\). The limitation paragraph restates the *algebra* \(F'>1\Leftrightarrow K>2K^*\) as if it were a defect of the pin. Rewrite §4: the pin is a real defect of the *fit* (series max 941 kt, \(K=5000\)); the expansion classification is nonetheless the minimum-SSE regime inside the declared box. Do not say “not on the identified \(r\)” when \(r\) is re-estimated at each \(K\).

**Companion forecast paper’s negative certificate.**  
This map *lost* to persistence out of sample in the companion. Using it as the governed object for viability is defensible only as “the same misspecified map, now closed-loop.” Say that in sentence 1 of the abstract, not only in §1. Otherwise you are doing viability on a model the previous paper told the reader not to use for forecasts.

### 2. The object is a strained fit (state this as a result, not a footnote)

**\(K=5000\) kt vs training max 940.75 kt.**  
Declared. The LRP is 884.6 kt, so almost the entire observed series sits in a thin band just around/below the LRP, while \(K\) is 5.3× the maximum observation. Local slope at the LRP is then \(F'=1+r(1-2K^*/K)\approx 1+r\), i.e. expansion is nearly guaranteed once \(K\gg 2K^*\). That is why Table 3’s \(K=1000\)–1500 rows (closer to the data) restore contraction *and* empty or cap the kernels. A one-panel figure of the 1983–2007 series against \(K=5000\) and \(K=2K^*=1769\) would make the pin visible. Without it, 57.6 kt looks like a stock number.

**\(r=0.2369\) on 1983–2007.**  
This window includes the collapse and the early moratorium. One-step LS on a non-monotone path with a monotone map is the same obstruction as the forecast paper’s Prop 4.1. Residual mean \(-20.4\), SD 135, min \(-460\) (1992). The “productivity floors” are mostly the 1992 residual recycled as a perpetual climate. That is a modelling choice, not a productivity regime. Say: floors are *residual-based*, and the 1992 draw is one observation.

**Defect \(\varepsilon=460\) vs out-of-sample max 47.1.**  
Declared defect is the 1992 residual; 2008–2015 does not exceed it. The certified layer then uses \(\varepsilon=460\) in an *expansive* geometric series and empties kernels by \(T=5\) at 4005 kt. The stochastic layer (Table 4) is the honest counterpart; the certified layer is a stress test. Abstract item (3) should say “certified conversion under the 1992 residual as perpetual defect,” not “model error.”

**Allee off in the primary object, Allee preferred in-sample.**

Fine — but then the primary object is not the best-fitting member of the family you entertain. **Fox worse than Schaefer, Allee best.**

### 3. Mathematical / definitional notes

**Closed-loop kernel vs Aubin kernel (Def 2.5).**  
You flag the difference. Good. Then stop using “viability kernel” without “policy-fixed” in table captions. Referees from the Aubin school will otherwise read Table 1 as existential kernels.

**Safe set \([K^*,\infty)\).**  
No upper constraint. Surplus \(g(S)=rS(1-S/K)\) is negative for \(S>K\), so large \(S\) crash downward under a floor. Table 3 at \(K=1000\)–1769 shows upper caps. Table 1’s “\([884.6,10^4]\)” for BAU under q10 is an artefact of \(K=5000\) and a floor milder than \(g(K^*)\). Mention the missing upper bound in Def 2.2.

**Constructive bound 57.62 kt (Prop 3.3).**  
Arithmetic: \(g(K^*)-|e_{q10}|=172.47-114.85=57.62\). This is the catch that makes the *equilibrium* of the floor-shifted map sit at \(K^*\), not a viability-kernel computation. For a monotone scalar map they coincide at \(T=\infty\). One sentence: “for this 1-D monotone map the maximal robust constant catch equals surplus at the LRP minus the floor.” Otherwise “constructive boundary” sounds like a kernel algorithm.

**Certified conversion (Def 2.7).**  
Contracting case \(r_T=\varepsilon(1-a^T)/(1-a)\) is a standard geometric bound on accumulated error. Expansive case \(r_T=\varepsilon(a_{\max}^T-1)/(a_{\max}-1)\) with \(a_{\max}=\sup|F'|\) is a crude Lipschitz bound, not a viability conversion. It uses a global \(\sup|F'|\) on the whole safe domain; since \(F'\) is largest at the *left* endpoint (the LRP), \(a_{\max}=F'(K^*)=1.153\), and the bound is driven by the most expansive point. That is conservative to the point of emptying. Either:

- compute the bound along the actual closed-loop trajectory, or  
- call it a Lipschitz stress bound, not a “certified kernel.”

\(r_5=3120.5\), \(K^*+r_5=4005\): check \(460\cdot(1.153^5-1)/(0.153)\). \(1.153^5\approx 2.04\), \((2.04-1)/0.153\approx 6.80\), \(\times 460\approx 3128\). Close enough; rounding of \(a_{\max}\) should be specified.

**Expansion \(F'(S)=1+r(1-2S/K)\).**  
Correct for unharvested Schaefer. With constant catch \(C\), the map is \(F_C(S)=[S+rS(1-S/K)-C]_+\), so \(F_C'(S)=1+r(1-2S/K)\) still (catch is additive). At the LRP this does not depend on \(C\). Hence “every declared policy” in Prop 3.4 is correct for the *slope*, but the certified kernel of \(K^*+r_T\) still depends on the nominal kernel, which does depend on \(C\). The emptiness-beyond-5 claim is that even the zero-catch nominal kernel of \(4005\) is above the observed range, hence empty *for practical states*. State that: empty on the observed range, not empty as a set.

**Prop 3.3 proof is not a proof.**  
It states the number and the arithmetic. That is a calculation, not a proof. Replace “Proof” with “Calculation,” or prove that the infinite-horizon kernel’s lower edge equals \(K^*\) iff \(C\le g(K^*)-|e|\).

**Prop 3.1 proof.**  
H1 failure of S1/cascade is geometric. H3 is never reached. Flat caps with \(C>5\) fail H1. Zero catch: is it “non-BAU”? Def 2.6 says non-BAU. Zero catch’s kernel matches BAU under q10 (both 884.6) and is slightly better under harsh \(T=1\) (1136.6 vs 1141.0). H1: at least as protective — yes under q10; H2: improves somewhere — yes at harsh \(T=1\); H3: mean allowed catch exceeds every at-least-as-protective flat cap — zero catch’s mean catch is 0, which does *not* exceed BAU’s 5. So zero catch also fails. The proposition is true but the proof only discusses S1/cascade. Finish the cases.

**Stochastic viability, i.i.d. vs blocks.**  
Lag-1 residual acf 0.65; blocks of 4 are a start. 20 000 trajectories, seed fixed, shared draws: good. \(P=0.87\) at zero catch from the LRP includes re-drawing 1992 every year with probability 1/24. That is why no-1992 jumps to 0.949 and why the 0.9-bar catch is 48.6 kt only in that scheme. The Chow \(F=3.68\), permutation \(p=0.062\) is correctly “not a proven break.” Do not let Figure 5’s no-1992 curve be read as the main result.

**xteNCAM row (3.11).**  
Different LRP (276 vs 884.6), different scale, own residual floors. Constructive bound *negative*: \(g(\mathrm{LRP})=130.7 < 178.7=|e_{q10}|\). Zero-catch does not hold the LRP from the LRP. This is the most important sensitivity in the paper and is buried. If the 2023 40% \(B_{\mathrm{MSY}}\) LRP is the current DFO object, then the registered paper’s “moratorium holds the LRP” is an artefact of the 2016 884.6 kt definition (1980s mean) sitting near the top of the observed series. Promote 3.11: the two specifications agree on expansion and on policy ordering, and **disagree on whether the reference point is self-viable**. That is a finding, not a labelled row.

**Finite-duration floors, backward recursion on a monotone map.**  
Credible. \(n=5\) worst BAU = 1956.4 matches certified \(T=5\) nominal — good check. Table 5’s “empty” at long duration + high catch is informative in a way perpetual vacuity is not. Keep; it is post-freeze and should be tagged as such.

## A. Major problems

**A1. The catch appears to be misaligned by one year, and the "defect" ε = 460 kt inherits the misalignment.**
The replay (§3.5) applies the −460 residual to the 1991→1992 transition (906.5 → 622.3 under zero catch: 906.5 + g(906.5) − 460 = 622.3 ✓). The observed transition is 735 → 382 kt. The residual is then 382 − [735 + g(735) − C] = −501.5 + C, which equals −460 only if C ≈ 41.5 kt — the *1992* landing. With the 1991 landing (~170 kt) the residual is ≈ −330 kt. The same pattern holds one step earlier: the 1990→1991 residual implied by the replay (−124.4) requires C ≈ 171.5 kt, the *1991* landing. Unless Table A2 SSB is end-of-year (which should then be stated and reconciled with NCAM's convention), the fit uses C_{t+1} against S_t → S_{t+1}. Every downstream number — r, the residual SD, both harsh floors, the "defect", the replay — would change. State the timing convention and the exact residual formula.

**A2. The residual statistics are mutually inconsistent.** SD 135.0 kt, mean −20.4 kt, n = 24 imply SSE ≈ 429,000 kt²; §3.7 reports SSE 306,532 kt² (MSE 12,772, RMS 113 kt). One of these is wrong. §3.6 also labels 12,772.2 as "SSE" once and "MSE" once.

**A3. The Limitations paragraph contradicts the paper's own §3.7, Abstract and Conclusion (4).** §3.7/Prop 3.6(i): the expansion "is the data-selected regime, not a bound artifact". §4, seventh paragraph: "the expansion obstruction is therefore conditional on the bound-pinned carrying capacity, not on the identified r." Both cannot stand. The truth is between them: the SSE profile is nearly flat for K ≳ 1800 kt (ΔSSE/σ² ≈ 1.9 from K = 2000 to 5000, inside a 95 % profile interval), so F′(K*) ranges over [1.00, 1.17] within the data-consistent set, and the bootstrap interval [1.001, 1.177] is computed with K *fixed*. Expansion is marginal, not "data-selected".

**A4. The "expansion obstruction" is a tautology about limit reference points, not a finding about cod.** For the discrete Schaefer map, F′(S) > 1 ⇔ S < K/2 = B_MSY. An LRP is by construction below B_MSY, so the closed loop is "expansive at the LRP" under every Schaefer fit of every stock, and the certified layer (Definition 2.7) can never certify anything at any LRP. That is a defect of the certification machinery (a sup-Lipschitz tube), not "the first scored instance in which the contraction form is provably inapplicable" (§4). Say so plainly, and drop the claim that this is a contribution to the viability-methods record.

**A5. Definition 2.7's "two forms" are algebraically the same expression.** ε(1−aᵀ)/(1−a) ≡ ε(aᵀ−1)/(a−1). There is no "contraction form that fails"; there is one geometric-series bound that is bounded when a < 1 and grows when a > 1. Further: (i) the tube should require the nominal path to clear K* + r_t at each t ≤ T, not K* + r_T at all t; (ii) ε and the worst floor are declared to be "the same measured quantity in two roles", so the certified layer double-counts the 1992 shock on top of the robust kernel; (iii) no theorem is stated or cited for the conversion.

**A6. "Empty beyond T = 5" appears to be wrong at T = 6.** r₆ = 460(1 + 1.153 + … + 1.153⁵) ≈ 4057 kt, so the threshold K* + r₆ ≈ 4942 kt < K = 5000, and K is a zero-catch fixed point (and near-fixed under 5 kt), so the certified set at T = 6 is nonempty; emptiness begins at T = 7 (threshold ≈ 6023 kt > K). Also, the T = 5 set [4005, 10⁴] depends entirely on the arbitrary 10⁴ kt upper edge.

**A7. The reactive-rule comparison is degenerate by construction.** S1 and the cascade switch only *at or below* the LRP, and any state below the LRP is already a violation, so on the kernel domain both rules are identically the 60-kt flat cap (Table 1 row "flat 60 / S1 / cascade"; Prop 3.7(ii) admits this). The retention verdict against reactive rules therefore tests nothing about reactive management; the family contains only flat caps. A meaningful comparison needs rules that switch *above* the LRP (USR-based ramps, F-based rules). "Declared geometry of the rule" is not a defence of a null design.

**A8. The retention rule cannot retain zero catch even though it strictly dominates BAU on protection.** Flat 0 kt passes H1 and H2 (1136.6 < 1141.0 at worst T = 1; 1012.1 < 1016.5 at q05) and fails only H3 (0 kt does not "exceed" its own catch). So "no non-BAU policy is retained" includes the most protective policy tested. State that the rule seeks catch expansion at no protection cost, and that Prop 3.1 is therefore about supply, not protection.

**A9. Prop 3.5 misreports the depensatory refit.** With r = 2, K = 1671.7, s₀ = 642.3, g(K*) ≈ 196 kt, so the constructive bound becomes ≈ 81 kt, not 57.6 — the number moves by 40 %, yet the text says the constructive boundary is "unaffected". The worst-floor T = 5 emptiness is attributed to g_max = 356 < 460, but the Schaefer form also has g_max < 460 and a nonempty T = 5 kernel; the real cause is the refit's negative surplus above K = 1672 with r = 2 (states above K crash). With r = 2 the refit map is non-monotone on the safe domain, so the "exact backward recursion on the monotone map" (§3.9) and sup|F′| over [884.6, 10⁴] (astronomical for a cubic) do not apply to it. Calling a post-freeze refit a "co-equal primary specification" also breaks the freeze discipline the Abstract advertises.

**A10. The xteNCAM row reverses the headline result and is absent from the Abstract.** On the current assessment and current LRP (276 kt), zero catch does *not* hold the reference point from itself under that fit's own informative class (T = 1 boundary 309 kt; T = ∞ 387 kt). The paper's conclusion (1) — moratorium holds the entire safe set — is therefore specification-specific, and the specification on which it holds is the superseded one. The Abstract's five items omit §3.11 entirely.

**A11. "Protected by good years, not by catch management" is contradicted by §3.8.** Moving from 0 to 120 kt drops 20-year survival from 0.87 to 0.58; from 0 to 57.6 kt, from 0.87 to 0.77. Catch matters a great deal on this map; the slogan follows only from the perpetual-floor construction (in which either everything survives or nothing does). Remove it from Abstract, §3.2 and §5.

**A12. The disturbance classes are percentiles of 24 numbers.** The "5th percentile" (−318.8) is an interpolation between the two lowest residuals, one of which is −460; the "10th percentile" between the second and third lowest. State the percentile convention, report the sensitivity to it, and rename the classes — a *perpetual* 10th-percentile floor is not a 10 % event, and calling it "informative" is a misnomer.

**A13. Post-1992 residual bias is disclosed and then ignored.** §3.8: 1992–2007 residual mean −55.8 kt. The fitted map over-predicts post-collapse growth by ≈ 56 kt yr⁻¹ — a third of g(K*) — in exactly the regime the LRP question concerns. The residual resampling in §3.8 (mean −20.4 overall; centred or not is unstated) and the deterministic kernels (zero-mean nominal) are both optimistic for that regime.

**A14. Three uncited "companion" studies carry numbers and claims** (forecast-evaluation, groundwater, methodological template), including inside the "proof" of Prop 3.1. The claim that this is "the same fitted map by construction" as the companion's needs checking: the companion (as I read it) fitted on 1983–1990, 1995–2007 and rolling windows with box K ∈ [500, 5000]; here the window is 1983–2007 and the box starts "just above the training maximum" (≈ 941 kt).
