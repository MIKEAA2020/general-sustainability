# 1. What this paper establishes

Depletion indicators can carry the same unit while answering different questions. Reserve-life ratios, trend-persistence indices and removals-only pressure scales are read as "time to depletion", though they measure different objects. And compensatory aggregation hides a deficit in one component behind a positive scalar. This paper builds the accounting layer in which both failures become structurally impossible rather than merely discouraged, and it states each result with the predicate it actually establishes.

**1.3 Background, kept to what is needed.** Two literatures supply the failure modes this paper is about, and one supplies the machinery.

*Compensatory aggregation.* The composite-indicator and weak-versus-strong-sustainability literatures document the failure and its non-compensatory remedies (Munda and Nardo, 2009; Ekins et al., 2003; Neumayer, 2013). Its public flagship object is Earth Overshoot Day, which aggregates component flows into a single calendar date, so a severe deficit in one component can coexist with a date that still falls late in the year (Lin et al., 2018; Wackernagel and Beyers, 2019; Blomqvist et al., 2013); the systems-dynamics overshoot models raise the same question in dynamic form (Meadows et al., 1972). The replacements built here are component-resolved by construction: the per-asset, per-pool horizons of §6.5 are each reported beside the pool they draw on and never summed into a scalar date.

*Classification drift.* Reserve-life ratios are arithmetic, and because reserves are an economic classification that changes with prices, technology, exploration and regulation, the ratio is not an exhaustion forecast; the critique has been made forcefully for phosphate (Illakwahhi, Vegi and Srivastava, 2024) and is standard in mineral economics, where the reserves-versus-resources distinction has been central at least since Tilton (2003), with copper reserves documented growing through a century of rising production (Tilton and Lagos, 2007). The same conflation runs through accounting itself: material flow analysis supplies the bookkeeping of a society's material throughput (Brunner and Rechberger, 2004; Eurostat, 2001; Fischer-Kowalski et al., 2011), and its incidence structure is shared with reaction-network theory, where the sign pattern of the stoichiometric matrix is a conservation object (Feinberg, 2019). Bookkeeping balance, stoichiometric conservation, thermodynamic admissibility and sustainability safety are four predicates, and the literature slides between them. A mass-balanced ledger can be chemically impossible; a chemically consistent ledger can violate every declared barrier; a ledger satisfying all declared barriers can fail conservation. Separating the layers and proving their relationships so that each claim carries the predicate it establishes is §3's task.

*The productivity illusion.* A third failure mode is the appearance that a system delivers adequately while the base sustaining delivery is reduced. Its arithmetic sense is the aggregation failure above, formalised as the aggregation obstruction of §7.1. Its dynamical sense is yield inflation: measured yield can exceed true sustainable yield when it is held up by drawing down a support pool — groundwater, soil carbon, available nutrients — rather than by that pool's regeneration. A support pool is anything that receives the current draw and is not renewed as fast; it may be read as natural capital, as a stock, or as a slowly regenerating flow of services, and these are overlapping readings of one pool. Every such pool regenerates on some timescale — a crop within a season, an aquifer within years to decades, a mineral deposit over geological time — and what decides the case is the renewal rate of the pool relative to the rate of use: a draw that falls on regeneration is sustained, a draw that falls on the pool is liquidation. **The size of the pool is a property of the resource; whether a drawdown is recoverable is a property of the rate.** (The elevator picture is the source's own framing device, kept because it is the paper's worked analogy for the wear phase, and stated in one sentence.) An elevator rated for ten people carries fourteen for a long time; the cable parts later, under accumulated wear the passenger count never shows, and the snap is sudden and total. Yield inflation is the wear phase, and the ledger's job is to make the wear measurable while the cable still holds — not to predict the snap (§9 states what the passage-time results do and do not condition on).

The paper's own non-claim list governs this paragraph. Where the yield-inflation reading calls for an explicit two-pool model — a resource beside a slow-regenerating support pool — no such model is claimed as established: the groundwater two-pool model is a **registered open gap** whose admitted applied object is the one-pool affine approximation (§8.2), and spawning biomass is explicitly not an abiotic support pool (§6.5.4). What §6.5 actually reports is per-resource, per-pool horizons at each object's own status, not a supporting-pool column the applied data do not carry.

*Two design choices follow.* A measured yield is booked against its own generating flux, never against a stock level — which is why support drawdowns cannot be traded against revenue anywhere in the ledger. And the two-pool architecture exists to detect the wear phase, not more.

**1.4 Weak and strong sustainability as two regimes of one ledger.** The weak-comparability thesis of ecological economics holds that values relevant to environmental decisions may not be commensurable in a single metric (Martinez-Alier, Munda and O'Neill, 1998). At the level of material accounting it has a precise algebraic form, stated and proved in §7.1–7.2: no non-negative weighting of component balances certifies that every component satisfies its floor.

Weak and strong sustainability are not competing hypotheses but two regimes of one dynamic system, distinguished not by whether substitution alone keeps pace with depletion but by whether the material cycle can be closed at the rate of use — a reading developed for the ledger, not the received distinction of the literature, which turns on the substitutability of natural capital (Neumayer, 2013; Ekins et al., 2003). Weak sustainability is the idealised regime: consumption and population grow slowly enough that technological substitution and natural regeneration together redistribute matter so it is used as it arises. On human-relevant timescales substitution is the dominant term; regeneration acts far more slowly — often on deep-time scales — and is included for physical completeness rather than as a co-equal mechanism (Daly, 1990).

The deep-time clause is **scoped to the slow compartments** — geological donors and mineral stocks, whose renewal runs on deep time. The regenerative compartments the applied record tabulates renew on human timescales (a crop within a season, an aquifer within years to decades, a fish stock within years), so for them regeneration is a co-equal or dominant term and the regime question is decided by the rate of use relative to that renewal, not by any deep-time regeneration. In the idealised closure, the by-products of use — carbon drawn from the atmosphere, chemical substances released to air, water and soil — are returned to use in time and are therefore not waste. **Waste is a relational status, not an intrinsic property of any material**: it is matter that, under the present relation and circumstances, accumulates because it cannot be put to immediate use for lack of knowledge, technology or timely redistribution. A substance that is a product in one context is waste-in-waiting in another, and no substance is waste by its nature.

Strong sustainability is the regime in which that closure fails — either because no identified physical pathway re-routes the extracted matter, or because depletion outruns the rate at which such a pathway could be deployed. Substitution is admissible only where an identified physical pathway exists and does not draw down a different critical stock; no substitute is admitted merely because an aggregate production function or a weighted index permits it. In ledger terms a substitute is either a recycled or recovered flux returned to the regenerating pool, or a non-renewable drawdown on a second compartment — different entries, carrying different statuses (§2.3, §6).

The two regimes are two readings of one object. The scalar reading $B=b\cdot M$ — aggregate regeneration flow, $b$ the per-unit-mass regeneration rate and $M$ the natural-block mass; letters local to this section, since from §2.2 on $B$ names gross turnover $R+T$ while $M$ keeps the natural-block reading — against consumption is the weak-sustainability flow check: does the cycle close in aggregate? The multi-compartment ledger is the vector reading: does it close only by drawing down a critical compartment or over-filling the waste one, i.e. are local depletions or waste accumulation merely pushed off-book? Both readings are required, neither demotes stocks, and both are instantaneous: the checks must also not drift into overshoot over time, which is why the scenario-conditioned hitting time of §6.5 exists — to give the drift a horizon. The reserve classification itself encodes the point: a horizon built on a reserve figure carries the substitution and technology premises of the classification, not a physical forecast.

# 2. The ledger, in the minimum form the results need

> Read this section for four rules: one material identity, one boundary and one unit per compartment; no flow without a named primitive; no outflow larger than its donor; no sum across types without an explicit coefficient. Everything else in the paper is a consequence.

**Notation discipline.** One letter carries one sort wherever a computation is displayed; section-local aliases are declared where they occur, and the incidence operator is never written $N$ (reserved for the living stock). The source prints the full alias table at the head of §2 so that every alias is declared before use; it is reproduced verbatim in `humanized/v1/part_02_s2_typed_ledger.md` and is not repeated here, because the results do not depend on it. What the compressed ledger needs is the short list: $N$ living stock (nutrient stock locally in §2.4) · $S^{\top}$ the typed stoichiometric (incidence) operator · $v$ the non-negative primitive flux vector · $S=Cx$ the moiety readout · $s$, $\sigma$ the support and donor fractions · $M$ the natural-block mass $N+A_{\mathrm{act}}+A_{\mathrm{geo}}+U$ · $\hat M$ the demand-coverage matrix · $R$ net regeneration, $T=\kappa_A Ns$ gross uptake, $B=R+T$ gross turnover · $r$ the intrinsic growth rate · $K$ carrying capacity, or the maintainability kernel when subscripted · $C$ the composition matrix, the extraction-law readout, or mining intensity when subscripted · $G$ the geological pool, or reserves locally in §6.5.3 and §9.6 · $I$ the inert sink · $U$ detritus · $P$ product (or production rate in the mining columns) · $W$ the absorbing stock · $E\ge0$ extraction effort · $\alpha$ the harvest routing fraction, $\rho_P$ the retirement fraction · $\mu,\nu,\rho$ the product, waste and price parameters of the unreduced ledger (zero in the specialization) · $\varepsilon$ the drift bracket of Prop. 17, the probabilistic level of §6.4, the resource-threshold fraction of §6.5.2 and §9.6, the donor-draw diagnostic $\varepsilon_G$, and the slack in §7.1 · $\tau$ hitting and exit times · $\zeta$ surrogate noise scale (§9) · $\lambda,\nu$ the inverse-Gaussian shape and mean (§9.3). Each of these carries exactly the scope the source gives it; where a letter is local to one statement, the statement says so.

**2.1 Four concepts, separated.** A **moiety** is a conserved substance class (an element, or a declared conserved combination) — the only object a conservation law attaches to. A **species** is a chemical or biological form of a moiety. A **compartment** is a spatial or functional location holding a stock. A **stock** is a compartment's current amount of a species, with a physical unit. "Carbon in the atmosphere" is a location-specific stock, not a moiety: carbon is the moiety, the atmosphere is the compartment. Nothing is conserved merely by being a compartment.

A ledger state $x\in\mathbb R^m_+$ collects compartments, each carrying a material identity, spatial support and physical unit. Internal dynamics use non-negative primitive fluxes:

$$\dot x=S^{\top}v(x,y,\theta)+Bu_\partial(t)+dx(t),\qquad v\ge0. \tag{1}$$

Entries are added within a row only when their types and units agree; a conversion between types is an explicit stoichiometric coefficient, never an implicit sum. If $L^{\top}S^{\top}=0$ then $\tfrac{d}{dt}(L^{\top}x)=L^{\top}Bu_\partial+L^{\top}dx$ — one conservation law per conserved moiety and boundary — and **the identity does not create a scalar sustainability mass across incommensurable systems**. Three clarifications are part of the statement: $dx$ must itself be typed (a physical disturbance on represented material is a different object from a structural discrepancy term); $S^{\top}$ may contain signed entries even though $v\ge0$ (sign pattern and non-negativity are separate declarations); and forward invariance is a separate requirement — every primitive outflow must vanish or be limited when its donor is empty, and a target-relaxation flux from a finite donor is admissible only after donor limitation is made explicit.

**2.2 The closed finite-donor ledger.** Let $x_L=(N,A_{\mathrm{act}},A_{\mathrm{geo}},U)$ with $s=A_{\mathrm{act}}/(A_{\mathrm{act}}+A_0)$, $\sigma=A_{\mathrm{geo}}/(A_{\mathrm{geo}}+A_{g0})$, and constitutive laws $R(N,A_{\mathrm{act}})=rN(1-N/K)s$, $T=\kappa_A Ns$, $B=R+T$. The four geo-interface and closure primitives are $e_{GA}=\omega_AA_{\mathrm{eq,intrinsic}}\sigma$, $e_{AG}=\omega_AA_{\mathrm{act}}$, $C_{A,\mathrm{lim}}=C_A\sigma$, and the detritus return $\gamma_UU$. These instantiate one of **three recharge laws**, which are distinct objects and are tabulated once so that none is silently substituted for another:

| Recharge law | Form | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Primitive donor-limited exchange | $e_{GA}=\omega_AA_{\mathrm{eq,intrinsic}}\sigma$ | the closed block's law: forward rate depends on the donor alone, not on how empty the receiver is — linear donor-limited exchange whose rest is $A_{\mathrm{act}}=A_{\mathrm{eq,intrinsic}}\sigma$ (Thm. 13) |
| Target-relaxation | $\omega_A(A_{\mathrm{eq}}-A)$ | **Banned unless donor-limited** (§4.4): it runs backward at an empty donor; admissible only with the source declared an effectively infinite external reservoir, which makes the system open |
| Working derived target | $A_{\mathrm{eq},W}=A_{\mathrm{eq,intrinsic}}+\kappa_AK/\omega_A$ | the delay-dynamics analysis's working completion: not a closed-block law, and the reason the two systems do not reduce. **In the closed block no derived target appears** |

Recharge is donor-limited and cannot run backward ($e_{GA}=0$ at $A_{\mathrm{geo}}=0$); the positive-part convention $[\cdot]_+$ — read as a one-way valve at a non-positive target — never binds here, because the registered intrinsic target is positive. Mining $C_{A,\mathrm{lim}}$ is donor-limited the same way extraction is. With $A_{g0}>0$, $\sigma$ is smooth and strictly increasing in the donor level. Under the institutional-failure specialization ($\mu=\nu=\rho=0$ and $C_A=0$ — the product, waste and price parameters of the unreduced ledger, its macroeconomic-feedback, recycling and price-response channels, set to zero together with mining intensity; the parameters are glossed at their §5.4 site) the closed natural block is

$$\dot N=R-qEN,\qquad \dot A_{\mathrm{act}}=-B+e_{GA}-e_{AG}+\gamma_UU,\qquad \dot A_{\mathrm{geo}}=-e_{GA}+e_{AG},\qquad \dot U=T-\gamma_UU, \tag{2}$$

together with a memory–effort pair $(Z,E)$ driven by $qEN-R$ (never by mining; $q$ is the per-effort extraction coefficient of the harvest law). The pair is the registered object of the companion delay-dynamics analysis (Author, D., et al., *in review*; eq. (1) and §2.4 of that analysis) and **is not analysed in this article**. Net regeneration is the difference of two non-negative primitives — gross regeneration $rNs$ (support → stock) and density-dependent return $rN^2s/K$ (stock → support) — so (2) stays within the primitive-flux discipline despite the signed entry. Harvest routing is the $\alpha=0$ corner of §2.4: harvest $qEN$ exits the natural block entirely as product, and a positive detritus-routed fraction $\alpha>0$ would add $\alpha qEN$ to $\dot U$ and reduce the block export to $(1-\alpha)qEN$; the mass identity of Theorem 7 is stated **for the declared routing**.

The **registered parameterization** is $r=0.02$, $K=100$, $q=0.001$, $\kappa_A=0.05$, $\omega_A=10^{-3}$, $A_0=1$, $A_{\mathrm{eq,intrinsic}}=50$, $\gamma_U=0.2$; $A_{g0}$ is declared positive (smoothness of $\sigma$) under the separation-of-scale condition $A_{\mathrm{geo}}\gg A_{g0}$, in which regime $\sigma\approx1$; the scale separation is **registered rather than a numerical value**, and the $A_{g0}=0$ corner is the discontinuous-perturbation limit, not the registered regime. With $A_0>0$ and $A_{g0}>0$ the right-hand side of (2) is locally Lipschitz on the closed orthant, and the comparison $\dot N\le rN(1-N/K)$ (with $\dot N\le0$ once $N\ge K$) bounds the stock by $\max\{N(0),K\}$; classical solutions therefore exist globally and stay in the orthant by Theorem 10 — the existence clause behind every "classical solution" statement below.

The four-row block incidence, rows $(N,A_{\mathrm{act}},A_{\mathrm{geo}},U)$ against the columns gross regeneration $rNs$, density-dependent return $rN^2s/K$, harvest $qEN$, uptake $T=\kappa_A Ns$, detritus return $\gamma_UU$, recharge $e_{GA}$, return $e_{AG}$, mining $C_{A,\mathrm{lim}}$, is

```
  N       1  −1  −1   0   0   0   0   0
  A_act  −1   1   0  −1   1   1  −1   0
  A_geo   0   0   0   0   0  −1   1  −1
  U       0   0   0   1  −1   0   0   0
```

Every column is a two-compartment transfer or a block-boundary export: the six internal columns sum to zero, the two exports (harvest, mining) carry column sums $-1$ each, so summing the rows reads off $\dot M=-qEN-C_{A,\mathrm{lim}}$ directly from the display (under the specialization, $C_A=0$ and mining is inactive). Restoring product, waste and the inert sink with the same donor-limited routing closes the full ledger $N+A_{\mathrm{act}}+A_{\mathrm{geo}}+U+P+W+I$ (§5.2). **The geological donor is an internal state throughout — no infinite reservoir is declared** — and the block boundary is crossed only by harvest (to product) and, when restored, mining (to product or waste). Uptake $T=\kappa_A Ns$ never enters $\dot N$: it is an $A_{\mathrm{act}}\to U$ throughput with the living stock entering as a catalytic factor, not stored biomass — legitimate in a monomaterial projection, and the masses below are defined on this routing.

**2.3 Mechanism typing: routing is never determined by diagnostic labels.** Extraction has at least three distinct physical meanings — standing-stock culling (present extraction removes reproductive stock directly), recruitment suppression (present use prevents future recruits without immediate adult removal), and weak viability coupling (use has limited or indirect effect on reproduction). In the ledger, standing-stock culling enters as an outflow from the standing-stock compartment. **The typing is the physical module's, not the diagnostic's: a diagnostic label such as "unsustainable portion" never determines physical destination.** Material routing is determined by the typed physical module alone, and the diagnostic threshold that flags a flow has no standing in the incidence matrix.

Where an application splits extraction between standing-stock removal and recruitment suppression — $C_{\mathrm{stock}}=\psi qEN$, $C_{\mathrm{recruit}}=(1-\psi)qEN$ for $\psi\in[0,1]$ — the assignment requires evidence **per channel**; the dominant physical mechanism sets it, never a diagnostic label. Illustrative assignments through a logistic two-channel proxy (stated as **illustrative, calibrated examples, not constitutive claims for the named domains**): soil zinc under crop export, an existing-unit removal, at $\psi=0.85$, against impaired mineralisation, a replenishment degradation, at $\psi=0.25$; pollinators under adult mortality at $\psi=0.70$ against brood failure at $\psi=0.20$ — and across such mechanism pairs the trough depth varies by a factor of about **1.5** from mechanism alone. The mass-routing discipline is the typing made explicit: literally harvesting pre-recruit stages is a harvest of existing units and routes to the product and waste fractions; habitat-induced failed recruitment is a *prevented inflow* — routing it into product or waste would create mass that was never in the stock; and damage to the capital stock itself (aquifer compaction, severe soil loss) is not a split-assignment channel at all but a slow drift in capacity or a transfer to the inert sink.

**2.4 Scaffold variants, in two compact paragraphs.** For one conserved limiting material the scaffold can be instantiated as six compartments ($X,U,A,G,P,W$) with eight non-negative primitives (assimilation $g$, mortality $m$, harvest $h$, decomposition $d_U$, $e_{GA}$, $e_{AG}$, direct mining $c_G$, retirement $r_P$) and the incidence matrix $S(\alpha,\rho_P)$ (displayed in full in the source at §2.3 and in `humanized/v1/part_02_s2_typed_ledger.md`), whose zero column sums are the incidence statement of mass conservation; the constant splits $\alpha,\rho_P$, the compartment set and the absorbing-sink convention are **declared choices of the example**, the construction is a monomaterial projection, recovery claims require $U$ and $P$ split by material, location and grade with declared yields and residual routes, and the four-block system (2) is **not** a specialization of that scaffold — the two are different timescale lumpings of the same physical story and no incidence specialization maps one onto the other. A second exact specialization closes a resource–sink system with a nutrient and a product stock, $(\mathsf S,\mathsf K,\mathsf N,\mathsf P)$, giving $\tfrac{d}{dt}(\mathsf S+\mathsf K+\mathsf N+\mathsf P)=I_{\mathsf N}-Q_{\mathsf P}$: mass is conserved exactly when both boundary transfers vanish, and every internal transfer cancels in the column sum. That balance carries two **empty-kernel mechanisms** that operate whatever the resource stock does: with sink loading $w(H)$, assimilation $\delta(K)$ and a harvest floor $H\ge H_{\min}>0$, under no assimilation ($\delta\equiv0$) $\dot{\mathsf K}\ge w(H_{\min})>0$ so any finite ceiling $K_{\max}$ is exceeded in finite time, and under weak assimilation ($\delta(K_{\max})<w(H_{\min})$) the sink still exits above the ceiling in finite time — the explicit negation of the ceiling condition $\delta(K^\dagger)=w(H_{\min})$, $K^\dagger\le K_{\max}$ — so in both cases the viability kernel of $\{\mathsf S\ge\mathsf S_{\min},0\le K\le K_{\max}\}$ (Aubin, 1991) **is empty**. The obstruction needs $\theta_K>0$: if $\theta_K=0$ harvest never loads the sink and emptiness would have to come from the resource constraint or an undeclared ceiling on the product stock. In ledger language, a closed ledger without recycling has the sink rising monotonically against the finite total mass, and which constraint fails first depends on $M$, $K_{\max}$ and the remaining stock. Which constraint fails first depends on the total mass $M$, the ceiling $K_{\max}$ and the remaining stock: a ceiling below the sink's reachable mass share is crossed in finite time, while a ceiling at or above the total mass is unreachable and the violated constraint is the resource floor (the finite-budget bound of Theorem 14). **This is the precise sense in which a "balanced" mass ledger can be physically inadmissible: the bookkeeping is exact, but no harvest schedule respects both the resource floor and the sink ceiling.**

**2.5 Support saturation: the logistic equation is a readout.** Two results control what the stock equation becomes when its support pool saturates; both are singular reductions with explicit scope, and neither is a full-system reduction. **Theorem 1 (Support-saturated logistic stock limit).** *Fix $T<\infty$ and non-negative $\mu,\delta,c,q$; assume (H1) $A_\kappa$ measurable with $A_\kappa(t)\ge a_0>0$ and $0\le X_\kappa(t)\le X_{\max}$, (H2) $E\in L^\infty([0,T])$; let $X_\kappa$ solve $\dot X_\kappa=\mu X_\kappa A_\kappa/(\kappa+A_\kappa)-\delta X_\kappa-cX_\kappa^2-qE(t)X_\kappa$ and $X_0$ the limiting equation with the same initial value. Then $\sup_{t\le T}|X_\kappa(t)-X_0(t)|=O(\kappa)$; if $\mu>\delta$ and $c>0$ the limit is $\dot X_0=rX_0(1-X_0/K_{\log})-qEX_0$ with $r=\mu-\delta$, $K_{\log}=(\mu-\delta)/c$.* *Proof.* With $e(t)=|X_\kappa(t)-X_0(t)|$, the saturation defect is $\big|\tfrac{A_\kappa}{\kappa+A_\kappa}-1\big|=\tfrac{\kappa}{\kappa+A_\kappa}\le\tfrac{\kappa}{a_0}$, so $|\dot X_\kappa-\dot X_0|\le L_1\kappa+L_2e(t)$ with $L_1=\mu X_{\max}/a_0$, $L_2=\mu+\delta+2cX_{\max}+q\|E\|_\infty$, using $|\mu-\delta|\le\mu+\delta$ and $c(X_\kappa+X_0)\le2cX_{\max}$; Gronwall gives $e(t)\le(L_1/L_2)\kappa(e^{L_2t}-1)\le C_T\kappa$. The bound $X_\kappa\le X_{\max}$ is satisfiable in the registered family ($\dot X_\kappa\le X_\kappa(\mu-\delta-cX_\kappa)$ keeps $X_\kappa\le\max\{X(0),(\mu-\delta)/c\}$). $\blacksquare$ **Remark 2 (Registered-family identity).** *In the primitive-flux core with $g=\mu XA/(K_A+A)$, $m=dX+cX^2$, $h=qEX$, the saturated stock equation is, for each fixed interior $A>0$ as $K_A\to0$, $\dot X=(\mu-d)X-cX^2-qEX=rX(1-X/K)-qEX$ with $r=\mu-d$, $K=(\mu-d)/c$, requiring $\mu>d$, $c>0$; the identity is pointwise on the interior support region and **not uniform through the depleted-pool boundary**, since $A/(K_A+A)=0$ at $A=0$ for every $K_A>0$.* The scope note is load-bearing: the limit does not eliminate $U$, does not make $A$ constant near its boundary, does not transform the memory or effort laws — **an ecological stock-equation identity, not a full-system reduction and not a transfer principle for bifurcation thresholds** — and bifurcation numbers of the two families do not transfer. For material-flow accounting: a logistic stock equation is a saturated support-pool readout, not a primitive physical law, and substituting it for stock-support dynamics is legitimate only at interior saturation and only on timescales where the approximation holds.
