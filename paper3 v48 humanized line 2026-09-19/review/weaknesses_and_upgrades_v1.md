# Weaknesses & Upgrade Programme — `paper3_material_ledgers_v32.pdf`

**Self-contained.** Everything about the article below was checked this session against the full extracted text of all 40 pages (`/home/user/work/paper3.txt`, 140,225 chars); each claim carries its evidence (line number in that extraction, or a counted frequency). External comparators carry sources. Nothing here assumes the reader has the PDF open.

**What the article is (for standalone reading).** Abaee (6 Sep 2026, 40 pp., 11 sections, 19,862 prose words, 761 sentences). It builds a *typed stock–flow ledger* in which a conservation law attaches only to a declared **moiety**, and uses it to separate three predicates that sustainability accounting normally fuses — conservation, positivity, barrier safety — and three quantities that "time to depletion" normally fuses — gross turnover, frozen net-depletion ratio, scenario-conditioned hitting time. It proves 15 theorems + propositions/lemmas/definitions on a closed finite-donor block (registered parameters: r = 0.02, K = 100, q = 0.001, ω_A = 10⁻³, A_eq,intrinsic = 50, γ_U = 0.2), classifies three published indicator families at exact status (G3P persistence = record-relative statistical index; phosphate reserve-life = arithmetic on an economic classification; RAM Legacy ADH = removals-only pressure scale), states seven explicit non-claims, refuses any reduction between the closed ledger and a companion open-donor system (five reasons), and ends with an impossibility theorem: **no non-negative weighting can certify componentwise safety** (Universality of Failure of Weighted Certification, §10.1). Its two motivating failures are "compensatory aggregation" and "classification drift"; its one metaphor is an elevator rated for ten that holds fourteen (line 95).

**Headline assessment.** The rigour is unusual and is the article's brand; the *exposure* is that everything that would make it citable, checkable and adoptable is currently deferred, disclaimed, or absent — and the closest prior-art tradition is not cited at all. Below: 21 weaknesses (N1–N6 novelty, E1–E8 evidence/artifact, S1–S7 structure, I1–I6 impact/positioning — note the lettering runs to I6; totals 27 items), each with the check that found it and the specific upgrade, then a ranked programme and predicted referee reports.

Severity: ●●● blocks a higher bar · ●● hurts it · ● cosmetic-but-cheap.

## A. Novelty exposures

**N1 ●●● The article never states its delta.** Frequency counts in the text: "novel" 0, "for the first time" 0, "to our knowledge" 0, "in contrast" 0, "differs from" 0, "compared with/to" 0, "prior work" 0, "existing framework" 0, "the closest" 0 — across 40 pages and 11 sections, with a reference list of 28 entries (counted from the author-list + year pattern in the extracted reference section), of which **three are the author's own companion preprints**. A reviewer is left to infer the contribution, and will infer the *smallest* reading of it (packaging of compartmental bookkeeping). *Upgrade:* one "Positioning" subsection (~700 words) with an explicit five-line delta table — object | what the standard literature certifies | what this ledger additionally certifies | which theorem does the work | which limitation remains.

**N2 ●●● The nearest prior art — green national accounting / resource-rent conventions — is entirely uncited.** The bibliography (28 entries, extraction lines 2072–2143) contains no El Serafy, Hartwick, Hamilton, Arrow, Vincent, Lange, SEEA, or national-accounts item; MFA appears only via Eurostat (2001), Brunner & Rechberger (2004) and Fischer-Kowalski et al. (2011). That tradition has spent four decades doing exactly this paper's job — deciding what counts as depletion versus income — and it contains the very object the paper attacks: the World Bank / **Hamilton–Ruta and El Serafy–Wei "resource-asset-pricing methods" compute depletion with T − t = S/R**, i.e. the stock-over-flow horizon the paper calls a dependency measure, not a time. Meanwhile SEEA's physical supply-and-use tables already ground themselves on "the conservation of mass and the conservation of energy", and the SEEA Central Framework (adopted 2012 as the first international statistical standard) is under formal revision — 2025 SNA has already adopted its depletion treatment. *Upgrade (two parts, both high-yield):* (a) cite and locate: the ledger's contribution is not the identities (official statistics already has them) but the **certification layer** — donor-limited positivity, declared barriers, and the non-compensation theorem, none of which SEEA/CWON provides; (b) exploit the standard's vocabulary in reverse: SEEA defines **residuals** as flows "discarded, discharged or emitted" — i.e. waste as a relational status, the paper's §1.2 claim — so the paper can claim compatibility with an adopted statistical standard *and* a novel extension, which is a much stronger novelty story than "a typed ledger".

**N3 ●●● Theorem depth is uneven; several results are standard.** The conservation theorems are zero-column-sum algebra on an incidence matrix (the paper's own citation, Jacquez & Simon 1993, is the classic treatment); positivity is a face-by-face Nagumo tangent-check; Prop 18's inverse-Gaussian first-passage law is admitted in the text as "a standard fact, stated for notation"; Prop 17 is two integrated inequalities; the weighted-certification impossibility is two-dimensional linear algebra with an explicit witness. Individually none will read as new to a mathematical referee, and the paper's own §4 theorems are mostly well-posedness bookkeeping. *Upgrade:* add **one** deep result that only the typed machinery can produce. Three candidates already implicit in the text: (i) a quantitative non-compensation bound — for a given index (e.g. a footprint or a persistence score), the *maximum* componentwise deficit concealable per unit of aggregate slack (a tight version of §10.1's witness family, with the extremal ledger characterized); (ii) a **viability-kernel computation** for the closed block — §3 currently declares barriers but never computes the maximal viable set, and §2's empty-kernel result would generalise into a computable certificate; (iii) a recovery theorem: given donor-limited recharge, an explicit bound on the drawdown rate below which the compartment returns, sharpening the §1.1 claim that recoverability is a property of the rate. Any one of these converts "architecture" into "architecture plus results".

**N4 ●● The taxonomy of depletion quantities is close to known conventions and is not benchmarked against them.** "Gross turnover / frozen ratio / hitting time" is a cleaner statement of the R-P vs. "depletion time" vs. model-extrapolation distinction that resource economics already argues about — but the paper never maps its three objects onto that debate (0 occurrences of "Tilton 1985"-style critiques beyond Tilton 2003 and Tilton & Lagos 2007, no Barnett–Morse/Michot-line depletion-time literature, no backstop model). *Upgrade:* a half-page correspondence table (each convention → which of the three objects it is → which predicate it certifies → its failure mode), which also gives the paper a citable "three-quantity taxonomy" that other indicator authors can adopt as vocabulary.

**N5 ●●● The negative results — the genuinely new content — are unquantified.** The non-reduction section lists five *qualitative* obstructions with point values (targets 50 vs 397.87 vs 5050; field difference 5.000; recharge 4.652 vs donor −0.348); the weighted-certification theorem is existential. There is no measure of *how wrong* the aggregate is, no distribution of concealable deficits, no case where the framework changes a published verdict by a stated amount. *Upgrade:* quantify the two flagship negatives. Report, e.g., "over N published composite indices, the aggregate certifies safety in k% of country-years while ≥1 component violates its declared barrier in p%", and give the extremal concealment from N3(i). Numbers of this kind are what get cited.

**N6 ●● No long-time characterisation of the object it builds.** §4 proves there is no interior rest at positive effort and that extraction is L¹, but supplies no attractor, no stability/instability analysis of the boundary rest sets, no phase portrait, no numerics of trajectories. The paper explicitly defers dynamics to companions (whose periodic/Hopf results it says do not transfer) and leaves a slow-passage question open. *Upgrade:* one numerical section (a page, five figures) integrating the registered closed block under registered scenarios — persistence diagrams of τ_exit, the depletion-vs-recovery boundary in (E, q)-space, and where the aggregate sign flips. Cheap, and it is what makes an applied-math reader stay.

## B. Evidentiary and artifact gaps

**E1 ●●● Zero figures, zero numbered tables, zero worked examples.** In the extracted text: "Figure n" 0 occurrences (the single hit for "figure" is prose); no "Table n." labels at all — §6.5.2's three evidence tables appear as bare tabulars introduced by "are tabulated below"; and the statement inventory contains Definitions 1–6, Theorems 1/5/7–15, Propositions (incl. 17, 18, 20), Lemma 3, Corollary 19, Remarks 2/16, Non-example 1 — but **no numbered Example** — a case-sensitive search for `Example [0-9]+` returns 0 hits, the only example-class labels being the two mentions of Non-example 1 — **and no Counterexample label**, even though §3's Ṡ = −kS and §4.9's cancellation are exactly those. **E2 ●●● The motivating object is never actually put on trial.** Earth Overshoot Day is introduced as the "public flagship object" (lines 59–62) and the ecological-footprint literature is cited (Lin et al. 2018; Blomqvist et al. 2013; Wackernagel & Beyers 2019) — but "recompute/recomputed" appears 0 times: no published composite index is ever run through the componentwise test. The one aggregation on display, the equal-weight inverse-horizon score ≈0.130 yr⁻¹, is built by the author from four basins plus world reserves and labelled Non-example 1. So the paper's central empirical claim — that scalar certification misleads on real data — rests on a synthetic witness. *Upgrade (this is the single highest-impact change available):* take a public, live index with components (National Footprint Accounts by country-year; or the Beyond Planet Boundaries indicators; or the SEEA water/material-flow tables), declare component barriers, and report how many country-years pass the aggregate and fail the vector, with the largest concealment found. One figure plus one table turns a positioning paper into a methods paper with a finding.

**E3 ●●● No uncertainty machinery, in a paper that includes a section on stochastic semantics.** Counts: "bootstrap" 0, "Monte Carlo" 0, "confidence" 0, "credible" 0, "cross-valid" 0, "held out" 0, "error bar" 0, "p-value" 0, "significance" 0, "estimator" 0, "validation" 0, "sensitivity" 2, "uncertaint*" 4. §7 is devoted to what the surrogates do *not* certify ("no calibrated predictive distribution is claimed", line ~1765), and §6.5's horizons are printed as point values. The disclaimer is rigorous; the absence is also the reason nothing here can be believed numerically. *Upgrade:* a minimal UQ pass — trend-standard errors from the G3P series, a block bootstrap for the ADH cohort medians, and a two-parameter sweep (q, ω_A) with τ_exit contours — plus tighten Prop 17's bracket from a declared ε to an estimated one. Then §7's honesty becomes a baseline ("we did the uncertainty and here is why it does not license a date") rather than a gap.

**E4 ●● An undeclared label on the most quotable row — not an arithmetic error.** §6.5.2 prints "World (resources, ε = 0.10) | > 300,000,000 | > 1,125 | 240,000", and the text asserts "the resource-based world horizon (≈ 1,125 yr at ε = 0.10) is more than three times the reserve-based figure (≈ 309 yr)". Check the arithmetic: 0.10 × 300,000,000 / 240,000 = **125 yr**; 0.90 × 300,000,000 / 240,000 = **1,125 yr**. The printed value is (1−ε)·R/P — **and the article does declare that convention**: §6.5.3 writes "a resource-threshold calculation T_resource,10% = 0.9 G_resource/C_G" and §7.6 "a threshold fraction εG₀ giving (1−ε)G₀/P". So no number needs recomputing. What is missing is the label where the number is read. The table prints "ε = 0.10" beside a row whose factor is 0.9 and whose declaration sits a page away; a reader who takes ε as the extractable share gets 125 yr, which is *shorter* than the reserve horizon and inverts the "three times" argument. Compounding it, one letter ε carries five different objects in the notation table — drift bracket (Prop. 17), probabilistic level (§6.4), resource-threshold fraction (§6.5.2, §7.6), the donor-draw diagnostic ε_G, and slack (§10.1). For an article whose thesis is that a number must carry the semantics of its construction, a convention that is declared elsewhere but not at the row is the detail a referee circles. *Upgrade:* keep every number; add a column note "horizon = (1−ε)·resources / production, with ε = 0.10 the share left unextracted", and split the letter (ε_drift for Prop. 17, keep ε for the probabilistic level, θ_res or 1−β for the threshold fraction, ε_G unchanged).

## D. Positioning and impact

**I1 ●●● No addressee.** The paper cites hydrology (G3P, GRACE/Tapley et al.), fisheries (RAM Legacy), indicator methodology (Munda & Nardo 2009; Nardo; Blomqvist; Lin), resource economics (Tilton ×2; Daly; Clark), viability theory (Aubin), stochastic processes (Øksendal; Redner; Chhikara & Folks) and chemical reaction network theory (Feinberg) — but addresses none of them. There is no sentence telling any community what to *do* differently on Monday, and "we" appears once, so nothing is recommended in the first person. *Upgrade:* add a "For …" paragraph in the abstract's wake and a Closing Recommendations section with three community-specific imperatives (statisticians: barriers belong in the accounts; modellers: declare donor limitation or lose positivity; indicator authors: three quantities, never one).

**I2 ●● No benchmark table for its own claims.** "benchmark" 0 occurrences; no head-to-head comparison against any alternative framework besides the §9 interface with its own companion. *Upgrade:* the five-row comparator table (SEEA CF physical accounts; EW-MFA/Eurostat; CWON/Inclusive Wealth; ecological footprint/EOD; composite-indicator quality guidance) with columns: units, production boundary, does-it-prove-conservation, does-it-certify-barriers, does-it-forbid-compensation, and the paper's addition in each row.

**I3 ●●● The live empirical conversations it could join, and one it must answer.** Not cited anywhere (0 hits each): Jasechko et al. (2024, *Nature*, ~170,000 wells in ~1,700 aquifers; rapid declines >0.5 m/yr widespread, with recovery cases) — the natural empirical frame for §6.5.1's persistence index; the 2026 follow-up review of 67 aquifer-recovery cases (over 80% involving an alternative water source) — direct evidence for the paper's own "recoverability is a property of the rate"; Gustafson et al. (2024, *Nature*) on extinction risk of exploited marine animals and Free et al. (2020, *Science*) on rebuilding — the closest thing to a published "time to depletion" claim in fisheries, and the right foil for the ADH verdict; and **Neubauer et al. (2019, *Science*)**, which reports that recovery is slower than predicted and that prolonged intense overexploitation erodes resilience — an *empirical counterexample* to the paper's rate-only slogan, since hysteresis makes recoverability a property of history and state as well as rate. A 2026 demographic study of 77 stocks further finds life-history traits are poor predictors of recovery potential, which bears on §6.5.3's taxon-group medians (elasmobranchs/sebastids/pleuronectids) as a diagnostic. *Upgrade:* engage all five; then extend the axiom to "recoverability is a property of the rate *and of the path taken*", with the ledger's capacity-drift entry (already in §2.5) as the mechanism that carries it. This is a genuine scientific improvement, not just a citation fix, and it pre-empts the strongest referee objection.

**I4 ●● No decision object.** §6 supplies exit times and a maintainability set but no protocol: nothing an agency can run. §8.3 shows instrument parameters ≠ physical floors, and stops there. *Upgrade:* publish a screening protocol — "declare barriers → freeze donor-limited fluxes → compute τ_exit → require τ_exit > review interval and x(T) ∈ K_maint → otherwise the aggregate index is inadmissible as a certification" — as a numbered checklist with one worked pass on one basin, plus the machine-readable config schema from E7.

**I5 ●●● A real policy window is open and unaddressed.** The SEEA Central Framework, adopted by the UN Statistical Commission in 2012 as the first international standard for environmental-economic accounting, is in its revision cycle (drafting of revised text 2026–27, consultation 2027, adoption slated for the Commission's 2028 session), and the 2025 SNA has already taken up SEEA's treatment of depletion as a cost of production. A componentwise-certification annex — barriers declared alongside flows, non-compensation made explicit, positivity guaranteed by donor limitation — is a concrete, small, on-topic contribution with a named recipient and a date. *Upgrade:* alongside the journal submission, produce a 6-page technical proposal in SEEA submission style, plus a 1,200-word comment for the revision's consultation. This is the difference between a framework cited 15 times and one adopted.

**I6 ●● Packaging undersells the one genuinely striking result.** The title leads with "Typed Flux Ledgers"; the impossibility theorem — *no* non-negative weighting of component balances can certify componentwise safety, for every weight vector and any number of components — is the paper's most citable claim and sits in §10.1, ~34 pages in. *Upgrade:* lead with it. Candidate title: "Aggregates Cannot Certify: Typed Flux Ledgers, Donor Limitation, and the Semantics of Depletion Horizons". Abstract order: impossibility result → the three-quantity semantics → empirical flip count (from E2) → what changes for practice.

## F. Predicted referee reports and what neutralizes them

**R1, ecological-economics referee (strong-sustainability school).** "The weak/strong reframing restates Neumayer and Daly with bookkeeping; Hartwick's rule and the El Serafy user-cost debate are never engaged, though the paper's 'recycled flux returned to the regenerating pool' *is* the reinvestment rule, and the 'unreduced deficit' *is* a rent/depletion accounting question." → Neutralized by N2 + the delta table: the ledger's addition is that reinvestment is a *typed routing*, so "the cycle closes at the rate of use" becomes a decidable predicate with donor limitation, which rent accounting cannot express. Currently this is exactly what the paper says in §1.2 but never connects to the literature it is answering.

**R2, environmental-indicator / composite-index referee.** "Non-compensation is a known result in this literature (Munda & Nardo 2009 is in your own bibliography: additive aggregation is inadmissible for non-substitutable components). Your Theorem in §10.1 is the trivial part of that story, and you never apply your test to an index anyone compiles." → Neutralized only by N5 + E2: a quantitative extremal result plus a real-index flip count. Without them, this objection is close to decisive.

**R3, applied-math / control referee.** "Conservation from zero column sums and positivity from Nagumo are textbook; there is no stability or reachability analysis, no numerics, and the stochastic part is a known IG identity. Where are the figures?" → Neutralized by N3(ii)/N6 (a computed viability kernel, trajectory figures) and by moving the standard results to a clearly-labelled appendix so the new content stands alone.

**R4, hydrology/fisheries data referee.** "The groundwater table contains a number you forbid people to use; the fisheries cohort matches neither public release; the ε convention is undeclared and changes a horizon ninefold; no uncertainty at all." → Neutralized by E3–E6, and only by them: these are the four sentences that make the applied section look careless in a paper that is otherwise obsessive about status.
