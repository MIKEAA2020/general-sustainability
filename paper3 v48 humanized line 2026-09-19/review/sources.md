## Official statistics / accounting standards (N2, I2, I5)
- UNECE/UN Statistics Division, *SEEA Central Framework* Chapter 3 (physical flow accounting), incl. ¶3.3–3.38 — the PSUT's "fundamental physical identities … conservation of mass and conservation of energy", and the natural inputs / products / residuals trichotomy. https://unstats.un.org/unsd/statcom/43rd-session/documents/SEEA-Central-Framework-Ch3-E.pdf
- UN Statistics Division, background document to the Statistical Commission's 52nd session (SEEA framework history; Central Framework "adopted … at its forty-third session in March 2012" as the initial international standard; definitions of natural inputs, products, residuals). https://unstats.un.org/unsd/statcom/52nd-session/documents/BG-3f-SEEA-EA_Final_draft-E.pdf
- IPBES policy-support entry: SEEA CF adopted 2012 as the first international standard for environmental-economic accounting; scope (assets, extraction, use, return as residuals), compatibility with the SNA. https://www.ipbes.net/policy-support/tools-instruments/system-environmental-economic-accounting
- UNCEA slide deck, *SEEA Central Framework update* (Di Matteo): revision timeline — proposal 2023, mandate March 2024, issue papers and global consultation 2025, drafting 2026–27, chapter consultation 2027, "Adoption of the SEEA CF by the UNSC: March 2028". https://unstats.un.org/unsd/classifications/Meetings/UNCEISC2023/Session09_Pres3_SEEA_CF_update_rev.pdf
- OECD/UN, *Measuring natural resources in the national accounts* (Dec 2025): the 2025 SNA adopts SEEA's treatment of depletion as a cost of production plus the split-asset approach; the three historical proposals for depletion (vanoli/"gifts from nature" full-rent withdrawal; Repetto/BEA netting discoveries; El Serafy user cost splitting rent into income and depletion); note that monetary asset valuation "requires SEEA-style physical asset accounts". https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/measuring-natural-resources-in-the-national-accounts_420c7c2a-en/full-report/measuring-natural-resources_a0accc5d.html (draft text also at https://unstats.un.org/unsd/nationalaccount/RAdocs/DraftNatResGuide15022025.pdf)
- *Non-renewable natural capital and the social cost of carbon in wealth accounting* (2026, prepublications): the resource-asset-pricing-method table — El Serafy (1989) / Wei (2015) user cost with T − t = S/R; Hamilton & Ruta (2009) weighted-average pricing; Arrow et al. (2003, 2012) efficient pricing; Hamilton (2016)/SEEA with ϕ > 0. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0095069626000240
- FAO guidance chapter summarizing Hartwick's rule (rents reinvested so that net investment ≥ 0; Pearce et al. 1989 framing as weak sustainability), Vincent & Hartwick (1997) net-price method, and the El Serafy conversion factor. https://www.fao.org/4/ab603e/AB603E04.htm
- Lange et al. on "Weak and strong sustainability in the SEEA: concepts and measurement" (Hartwick 1977; Hamilton 1994; El Serafy's lower depletion estimates than the net-price method; critique of genuine-savings coverage of renewables). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4842155_Weak_and_strong_sustainability_in_the_SEEA_Concepts_and_measurement

## Groundwater (E2, E5, I3)
- Jasechko, Seybold, Perrone et al., "Rapid groundwater declines in many aquifers globally but cases of recovery", *Nature* 2024, doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06879-8 — 170,000 wells in ~1,700 aquifers from 1.5 million level measurements; declines of half a metre per year widespread; Central Valley and Indian basins among the fastest; reversals documented. Summaries: https://www.zmescience.com/science/groundwater-resources-depleted/ and https://insideclimatenews.org/news/24012024/world-groundwater-levels-dropping-quickly/
- Jasechko's 2026 *Science* review of 67 aquifer-recovery cases: most successes combined multiple interventions, >80% (81%) involved an alternative water supply; recharge and policy/market instruments the other two strategies; recovery speed varies widely across basins; "groundwater depletion is not inevitable". https://news.ucsb.edu/2026/022469/why-some-regions-are-winning-fight-against-groundwater-depletion (also https://phys.org/news/2026-03-regions-groundwater-depletion.html)

## Fisheries (I3, E3)
- Neubauer, Peterson et al., "Resilience and Recovery of Overexploited Marine Populations", *Science* 2019, doi:10.1126/science.1230441 — recovery slower than predicted; exploitation history itself predicts recovery; "even extreme limitations in harvest pressure may not allow for the timely recovery of collapsed stocks"; current harvest and low biomass render recovery improbable for most depleted stocks. https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1230441
- Worm et al., "Rebuilding Global Fisheries", *Science* 2013, doi:10.1126/science.1173146 — 63% of assessed stocks worldwide still require rebuilding; exploitation rates at or below MSY in seven systems. https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1173146
- "Demographics and recovery potential of exploited marine teleosts", *PLOS ONE* 2026, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0340369 — 77 assessed stocks; 88% moderate-to-high recovery potential, 62 of them likely to double within 20 years absent fishing; median λ ≈ 1 and only slightly higher with fishing removed; λ < 1 in 56.8% of years; life-history characteristics poor predictors, regional factors stronger; stock-specific (not species-specific) parameters needed. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0340369
- Context for the extinction-risk comparator the article does not cite: Gustafson et al. (2024, *Nature*) on extinction risk across ~450 assessed fished stocks; the search results above surfaced the follow-on literature rather than the article itself, so treat the *specific headline fraction* as unverified and check the source before citing it.
