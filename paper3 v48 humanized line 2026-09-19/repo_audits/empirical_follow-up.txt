### Concerns and advice

1. **The coarse grid is suggestive, not decisive.**  
   The grid uses `τ_g∈{10,15}` vs `{20,30}` and `τ_p∈{20,25,30}`. This shows insensitivity to `τ_p` at those coarse points, but it does not prove “entirely τ_g-controlled.”  
   **Advice:** Run a finer one-dimensional sweep for `τ_g` in 1-yr steps from 15 to 25, while holding `τ_p=25`. If possible, also test a few more `τ_p` values (e.g., 10, 40) at the cliff edge to confirm flatness. This would turn “coarse grid” into a solid robustness statement.

2. **The 50 % threshold is a modeling choice that should be justified.**  
   The phrase “≈50 % of pre-disturbance productive capacity” is explicit but still arbitrary.  
   **Advice:** Add one sentence citing why 50 % is a reasonable proxy for “begins restoring” — e.g., it avoids counting early transient “green shoots” and matches the time to meaningful biomass/stock recovery in the cited field studies. If no direct support exists, state that it is a conservative definitional threshold.

3. **`τ_p` insensitivity may mask interactions with other parameters.**  
   The grid shows `τ_p` does not move the cliff for the chosen values, but other parameters (e.g., `b`, `D`, initial conditions) may interact with `τ_g`.  
   **Advice:** If feasible, add a brief note that the result is conditional on the other parameters remaining at baseline. A full global sensitivity analysis is not necessary, but a one-sentence acknowledgment prevents overstatement.

4. **The manuscript still needs the “where it flips” statement clearly linked to field data.**  
   The description says the field data place `τ_g` in the decades range, but the figure should visually connect the shaded field band to the cliff.  
   **Advice:** Ensure the figure caption explicitly states: “The field-supported band lies entirely in the collapse regime for the baseline parameter set,” so readers see the policy implication immediately.

### Overall assessment

The v14 changes are a clear improvement and move the paper toward a defensible, empirically grounded position without overclaiming. The next incremental step should be **a finer `τ_g` sweep near the cliff** and **a one-line justification of the 50 % definition**, not a full calibration.

## 2. Is a full calibration feasible?

### Short answer

**In principle, yes, but it is probably not necessary — and may not be worth the effort for a conceptual/stylized model.**

### What “full calibration” would require

A full calibration would mean estimating the model’s free parameters from empirical data, typically by:

- collecting time-series or cross-sectional data on the relevant stocks (`A`, `P`), flows (`b·A`, `D`), and delays (`τ_g`, `τ_p`),
- fitting the model via maximum likelihood, Bayesian inference, or simulation-based methods,
- reporting posterior distributions, credible intervals, and model-checking diagnostics.

### Feasibility constraints

1. **Data availability.**  
   The model is intentionally simple, but even so, you would need reliable time series for `A` and `B` (or `b`), and ideally independent observations of the degradation term `D`. Many of these are not directly measured; proxies are often noisy and inconsistent across studies.

2. **Identifiability.**  
   With delays, nonlinearities, and unobserved fluxes, parameters may be only weakly identifiable. A full calibration might produce wide posterior intervals that do not sharpen the qualitative conclusions.

3. **Scope of the paper.**  
   The manuscript is framed as a conceptual/stylized model with policy implications, not a detailed empirical forecasting tool. The claims are about regime behavior, not quantitative predictions. A full calibration would shift the paper’s emphasis and increase length, complexity, and review burden.

4. **Time and reproducibility.**  
   Digging through per-study curves, extracting data, and performing a defensible calibration is a substantial undertaking. It may be better as a separate follow-up paper.

### What to do instead

- **Keep the current approach:** literature-banded parameter sweeps + sensitivity analysis + honesty about limitations.
- **Add a short “calibration outlook” paragraph** stating that a formal calibration is possible but requires structured data extraction (Step 1a), and that the current robustness results are sufficient for the paper’s qualitative claims.
- **If you want one quantitative anchor,** calibrate only `τ_g` (and maybe `b`) using a small number of well-documented case studies, while keeping the rest as sensitivity parameters. This gives a middle ground without a full model fit.

### Bottom line

**Full calibration is feasible with substantial effort, but it is not required for the current manuscript’s claims and may distract from the paper’s core contribution.** A finer sensitivity analysis and a clear statement that calibration remains future work is the strongest next step.
