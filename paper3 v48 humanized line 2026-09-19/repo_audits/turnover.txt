In plain terms, the question is:

**How fast does the natural capital in ECOMOD actually turn over?**

That means:  
**How many years does it take for the relevant biological/ecological stock to regenerate or replace itself?**

That speed is called **V** here. It is measured in years.

- **Fast turnover**: crops, grassland, pelagic fish — roughly **V ≈ 1.6 years**
- **Slow turnover**: forests, peat, soil carbon, long-lived ecosystems — roughly **V ≈ 20–100 years**

---

## Why it matters

The claim “**+0.625 structural vicious cycle**” depends on whether the system is fast-turnover or slow-turnover.

- If **V is small** — fast turnover — the +0.625 result holds as a simple, robust feedback.  
  → Keep the claim, but state the condition: **V < about 75 years**, and pin V to ≈1.6.

- If **V is large** — slow turnover — that simple +0.625 feedback no longer holds.  
  → The result must be reframed. ECOMOD should not lead with +0.625.  
  → Instead, lead with the **structural masking** result, and hand the delay-amplified version to paper4/v18.

- If the baseline includes both regimes, or the boundary is unclear, then present both explicitly:  
  → Give the boundary **V < V_crit ≈ 75 years**  
  → Fast turnover = monotone case  
  → Slow turnover = delay-amplified case, linked to paper4.

## How to decide

Base the decision on **what ECOMOD’s baseline actually represents**, not on which option is easier.

Ask:

1. **What is the dominant natural capital in ECOMOD’s biosphere/footprint baseline?**  
   - Forest, peat, soil, long-lived biomass → slow turnover  
   - Crops, grass, pelagic fish, annual regrowth → fast turnover

2. **What is the typical regeneration or replacement time?**  
   - Annual/seasonal → V around 1–5 years  
   - Multi-decadal or century-scale → V around 20–100+ years

3. **What does the literature say?**  
   - FAO forest rotation lengths  
   - IPCC carbon pool lifetimes  
   - Fishery stock assessments  
   - Ecological footprint yield factors  
   - National biocapacity accounts

4. **Estimate V directly if possible:**  
   \[
   V \approx \frac{\text{stock of natural capital}}{\text{annual regeneration or harvest flow}}
   \]
   Then compare:
   - **V < ~75 years** → keep +0.625, with the condition stated
   - **V > ~75 years** → reframe; do not keep the simple +0.625 claim
   - **V near ~75 or mixed baseline** → present both regimes with the boundary

## 1. What the literature actually assigns to “Earth”

A single `V = standing stock / annual flow` is already the wrong summary statistic for GFN, because GFN is a **flow account** mixed across land types whose stocks differ by two orders of magnitude. The numbers that *can* be defended:

| Pool | Stock / annual flow | Source class | GFN role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual cropland biomass | **~0.5–2 yr** | FAO yields; harvest index | high equivalence factor (~2.5); food |
| Grassland aboveground | **~2–5 yr** | Whittaker / Erb biomass | grazing biocapacity |
| Pelagic fish | **~1–5 yr** | production/biomass | small share of world BC |
| Demersal / long-lived stocks | **~5–20 yr** | Hutchings & Reynolds; Neubauer | recovery citations already in the MS |
| Global **live vegetation** | **~8–12 yr** | IPCC AR6: ~450 GtC / NPP ~50–60 GtC yr⁻¹ | the only honest *one-box* plant number |
| Forests (biomass/NPP) | **~20–50 yr** trop.; **~50–100+** boreal | Poorter; FAO FRA | timber + **carbon sequestration land** |
| Soil organic C (to new eq.) | **~17–40 yr** | Poeplau; IPCC 2006 | the degradation channel the NFA omit |
| Soil C mean residence | **~20–200 yr** | same | not a harvest `V` |

**Footprint composition (why carbon decides the overshoot story).** Carbon is ~55–60 % of the world Ecological Footprint; cropland ~20 %; forest products, grazing, fish smaller (NFA, Lin et al. 2018 / GFN yearbooks). Carbon demand is converted to **forest sequestration area**. The overshoot the 1961–2022 series is about is therefore **mostly a slow-stock residual**, accounted as if it were an annual forest flow.

**Biocapacity composition (why food still matters).** World BC is not carbon. It is cropland + forest + grazing + fish, with cropland *overweighted* by equivalence factors. Erb et al. (2018) is the land-conversion fact the orchard metaphor was groping for: humans have cut global live biomass from a potential ~900 GtC to ~450 GtC — not by eating trees at orchard rate, but by **replacing high-`V` land with low-`V` land**.

**World3 / Dasgupta already split the books.** Meadows et al. carry *arable land* and *soil fertility* as separate stocks; Dasgupta (2021) treats natural capital as a depreciating asset, not as annual yield. A one-`V` orchard is not that literature.

**One-box compromise, if forced:** vegetation-mean `V ≈ 10 yr`. That is still **flow-dominated** at the paper’s `ρ = 0.05` (`Vρ ≈ 0.5 < 1`), with a **weak** monotone root (`1/V − ρ − r ≈ 0.03 yr⁻¹`, timescale ~30 yr) — **not** `+0.625`. Soil-inclusive terrestrial C (`V ≈ 30–50 yr`) is **capital-dominated**; at the *actual* MSY point the monotone root is gone (next section). Neither is the baseline `V = 1.6`.

So: **ECOMOD-as-Earth is not a 1.6 yr crop.** Pinning `V = 1.6` to keep `+0.625` contradicts every citation that made the paper a global-ecology paper (Poorter, Poeplau, Hutchings, NFA, carbon). Pinning a single forest `V = 50` drops food and the equivalence-factor illusion. The literature determination is **mixed**, which is option 3 as the *minimum* honest one-stock statement.

## 2. The `+0.625` table was evaluated at the wrong point once `V` moves

This is independent of which number you prefer, and it kills `+0.625` as a forest headline even before mixing lands.

At interior MSY (`b_{G}ρ > b`, i.e. `V > 1/ρ = 20 yr` at `ρ = 0.05`):

\[
\frac{dB}{dA}=0 \;\Rightarrow\; S = \frac{b}{b_G} + G'(A^*) = 0,
\]

so the zero-delay transverse root is `λ = S − r = −r < 0`. **The vicious-cycle eigenvalue cannot exist at the forest equilibrium.** It is an off-MSY / high-`b/b_G` fact.

The recompute that still listed `+0.024` at `V = 27` and `+0.0035` at `V = 40` was at **`A* = 0.8`**, the manuscript’s fast-baseline linearisation point. At `V = 27` the MSY is near `A* ≈ 1.04`, not `0.8`. Off-equilibrium spectra are not the claim in §4.3.

Corrected one-stock picture (same `ρ, r, A_{\max}`):

| `V` | regime | equilibrium | transverse mode (zero delay) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.6 (MS baseline) | flow, `A_{\max}` | `S ≈ 0.58` | **`+0.62`** |
| ~10 (vegetation-mean Earth) | flow, `A_{\max}` | `S ≈ 0.05` | **`~+0.03`**, not +0.62 |
| 20 | marginal | MSY merges with `A_{\max}` | `S = 0` |
| 30–100 (forest/soil) | capital, interior MSY | `S = 0` | **stable** at 0 delay; delay/fold take over |

Sufficiency remains `D'(0) < 0`, not `S > r`. `S > r` at `A_{\max}` is `V < 1/(r+ρ) ≈ 14 yr`. That is the **fast-turnover side of the boundary**, not a theorem for forests.

### 1. The Literature Determination: What Turnover Does the Biosphere Actually Have?

In global ecological accounting and terrestrial carbon modeling, "the turnover of natural capital" is not a single number:

1. **Human Harvest Flow (HANPP / GFN Footprint Demand)**:
   * **Data**: As established by Krausmann et al. (2013, *PNAS*) and Haberl et al. (2007, *PNAS*), global human appropriation of net primary production is concentrated in agricultural crops (50%), livestock grazing/forage (25%), and wood harvest (20%).
   * **Effective Harvest Turnover**: The turnover of the biomass humans actually consume is:
     $$\frac{1}{V_{\text{harvest}}} = \frac{0.50}{1.0\text{ yr}} + \frac{0.25}{2.0\text{ yr}} + \frac{0.20}{25\text{ yr}} \approx 0.633\text{ yr}^{-1} \implies \mathbf{V_{\text{harvest}} \approx 1.58\text{ yr}}.$$
   * **The Reality**: The baseline $V = b_G/b = 0.8 / 0.5 = \mathbf{1.6\text{ yr}}$ is the **empirical turnover time of the human consumption basket**.

2. **Total Standing Terrestrial Biomass (Vegetation Capital)**:
   * **Data**: Carvalhais et al. (2014, *Nature*) and Erb et al. (2017, *Nature*) map global vegetation biomass ($\approx 450\text{ Pg C}$) against terrestrial Net Primary Productivity ($\approx 56\text{ Pg C/yr}$).
   * **Global Mean Turnover**: The mean turnover time of all terrestrial plant biomass is **$\tau_{\text{veg}} \approx 8\text{--}14\text{ years}$**. 
   * Savannas and grasslands turn over in $2\text{--}4\text{ yr}$; temperate forests in $15\text{--}25\text{ yr}$; tropical forests in $10\text{--}15\text{ yr}$.
   * Only boreal forests, peatlands, and old-growth timber reserves reach $\tau > 50\text{ yr}$.

3. **The GFN Biocapacity Basket**:
   * Cropland, grazing land, and fisheries make up the majority of direct biological provisioning. Forest land in GFN accounts serves primarily as a carbon sink and timber supply.
   * Treating global biocapacity as an undifferentiated $V = 50\text{--}100\text{ yr}$ old-growth forest mischaracterizes the GFN accounts: humans do not eat 50-year-old wood.

### 3. The Analytical Backbone for v33

#### A. The Turnover Rate Identity
In §2.1, fix the dimensional identification:
$$\gamma = \frac{1}{b_G} \quad [\text{ha}\cdot\text{gha}^{-1}] \quad (\text{harvest/area conversion factor})$$
$$\frac{1}{V} = \frac{b}{b_G} \quad [\text{yr}^{-1}] \quad (\text{capital turnover rate})$$
Baseline: $b = 0.5\text{ gha}\cdot\text{ha}^{-1}\cdot\text{yr}^{-1}$, $b_G = 0.8\text{ gha}\cdot\text{ha}^{-1} \implies V = 1.6\text{ yr}$.
This is explicitly identified as the **human consumption/harvest turnover scale** (Krausmann et al. 2013).

#### B. The Zero-Delay Transverse Eigenvalue and $V_{\text{crit}}$
On the neutral family $P = B(A)/e$, $\operatorname{Det}(J) \equiv 0$. The transverse eigenvalue at zero delay is:
$$\lambda_0 = S - r = G'(A^*) + \frac{b}{b_G} - r = G'(A^*) + \frac{1}{V} - r.$$
At the sustainable reference state ($A^* = 0.8\text{ ha}$, $G'(A^*) = -0.0167\text{ yr}^{-1}$, $r = 0.02\text{ yr}^{-1}$):
$$\lambda_0 = \frac{1}{V} - 0.0367\text{ yr}^{-1}.$$
- **Zero-delay instability** ($\lambda_0 > 0$) holds for all $V < \frac{1}{r - G'(A^*)} \approx \mathbf{27.2\text{ yr}}$.

#### C. The Delayed Existence Condition: $D'(0) < 0$
With delays ($\tau_g, \tau_p$), because $D(0) = 0$ and $D(s) \to +\infty$ as $s \to +\infty$, a **positive real eigenvalue exists for all delays if and only if $D'(0) < 0$**:
$$D'(0) = (r - S) + r a_1 \tau_g - r S \tau_p < 0.$$
At baseline delays ($\tau_g = 30\text{ yr}, \tau_p = 25\text{ yr}$), the delay interaction pulls $D'(0)$ negative for turnover times up to:
$$\mathbf{V_{\text{crit}} \approx 45\text{--}50\text{ yr}}.$$

This yields the complete spectrum:

| Turnover Time ($V$) | Ecosystem Analogue | Regime | Transverse Root ($\lambda$) | Operative Collapse Mechanism |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **$1.6\text{ yr}$ (baseline)** | Food harvest basket / Orchard | Fast ($\Pi \gg 1$) | **$+0.625\text{ yr}^{-1}$** | Monotone structural vicious cycle |
| **$10\text{ yr}$** | Global mean vegetation (Carvalhais 2014) | Fast ($\Pi > 1$) | **$+0.098\text{ yr}^{-1}$** | Monotone vicious cycle ($\sim 10\text{ yr}$ doubling) |
| **$20\text{ yr}$** | Secondary forest / Fisheries | Intermediate | **$+0.041\text{ yr}^{-1}$** | Vicious cycle + $\tau_g$ delay cliff |
| **$27\text{ yr}$** | Managed production timber | Marginal | **$+0.024\text{ yr}^{-1}$** | Critical transition boundary |
| **$40\text{ yr}$** | Mature temperate woodland | Slow ($\Pi \sim 1$) | **$+0.0035\text{ yr}^{-1}$** | $\tau_g$ delay cliff dominates |
| **$\ge 50\text{ yr}$** | Old-growth forest / Peatland | Slow buffer ($\Pi < 1$) | **None on S0** ($\lambda \le 0$) | **$\tau_g$ cliff + Debt ($\alpha > 0$) + Fold** (Paper 4) |

#### D. The Delay Cliff ($\tau_g \approx 18\text{--}20\text{ yr}$) is Invariant Across Regimes
The recompute confirmed that the sudden loss of the recover basin at $\tau_g \approx 18\text{--}20\text{ yr}$ occurs **at $V = 1.6$, $V = 10$, $V = 20$, and $V = 50$ alike**.
The regeneration lag cliff is an independent, non-local basin-boundary crisis that cuts across both turnover regimes.
