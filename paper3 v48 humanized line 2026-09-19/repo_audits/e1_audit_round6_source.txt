# 3. The Allee parameter has an unresolved boundary inconsistency

## 3.1 The stated feasible set excludes zero, but the fitted estimates are effectively zero

You state:

> “\(\mathfrak s \in [0,\max_{\mathrm{train}}S_t]\)”

but also:

> “with the further feasibility restriction \(0 < \mathfrak s < 0.8K\)”

and later:

> “\(\mathfrak s\to0\)”  
> “\(\mathfrak s=2.1\times10^{-23}\)”  
> “the fitted \(\mathfrak s\) approaches its lower bound”

If the actual optimization domain is \(0<\mathfrak s\), then zero is not an attainable lower bound; it is an infimum. If zero is allowed, then the model at \(\mathfrak s=0\) is the cubic production model

\[
g(S)=rS(1-S/K)(S/K),
\]

not a positive-threshold Allee model.

### Required decision

Choose one of these formulations:

#### Option A: zero allowed

State:

\[
\mathfrak s\in[0,\min(\max S_t,0.8K)).
\]

Then explicitly say:

> “At \(\mathfrak s=0\), M1b is not a positive-threshold Allee model; it is the cubic zero-threshold branch.”

#### Option B: zero excluded

State:

\[
\mathfrak s\in(0,\min(\max S_t,0.8K)).
\]

Then do not call the numerical value \(2.1\times10^{-23}\) a boundary estimate. It is a numerical approximation to an unattained infimum. Report the optimization tolerance and whether the objective is minimized at the smallest permitted value.

The present text uses both conventions.

# 4. The catch–SSB accounting problem is more serious than the current wording suggests

That caveat is necessary but not yet strong enough. The model equation itself is

If \(S_t\) is SSB and \(C_t\) is total landings, then \(C_t\) is not generally the removal from the SSB state. SSB depends on:

- survival;

# 7. The uncertainty analysis is useful but overinterpreted

## 7.1 DM tests are not well calibrated for this design

The manuscript acknowledges several problems:

> “origins overlap at \(h=5\), the estimation window expands, the predictand is a smoothed reconstruction and the sample straddles a structural break”

but still presents DM statistics and \(p\)-values in a way that invites inferential interpretation.

Additional issues are:

1. forecasts are generated by recursively estimated models;
2. loss differentials are dependent because training samples overlap;
3. models are sometimes nested or nearly nested;
4. the target is a revised smoother rather than a real-time observation;
5. the sample is very small;
6. multiple model/horizon/specification comparisons are reported.

The DM test with HAC lag \(h-1\) is not automatically valid for expanding-window recursively estimated forecasts. For nested models, a standard DM test can also have nonstandard behavior.

### Recommended change

Call these:

> “descriptive loss-differential diagnostics”

rather than ordinary hypothesis tests.

If the \(p\)-values are retained, state:

> “The reported DM \(p\)-values are exploratory and should not be interpreted as calibrated evidence of equal predictive ability.”

A Clark–West-type adjustment could be considered for nested comparisons, but with this design and small samples it may not solve the broader problem.

## 7.2 The bootstrap is conditional on fixed forecast files

The bootstrap is computed from archived forecast errors without re-estimating the model in each bootstrap sample. Therefore it measures uncertainty in the empirical loss sequence conditional on the already fitted forecast paths. It does not incorporate:

- parameter-estimation uncertainty;
- uncertainty in the assessment target;
- uncertainty in catch reconstruction;
- uncertainty caused by origin-specific model selection;
- uncertainty in the future covariate path.

This should be stated explicitly.

### Recommended wording

> “The moving-block intervals quantify sampling variation in the archived origin-level loss sequence conditional on the fitted forecast paths. They are not full predictive intervals and do not propagate parameter, assessment-revision, catch, or covariate uncertainty.”

# 12. The Proposition 4.1 needs mathematical tightening

## 12.1 The assumptions are not sufficient as currently written

The proposition assumes:

> “at most two positive equilibria ... and a one-step map monotone below its maximum”

Then the proof says:

> “below the lower equilibrium \(F(S)<S\), and \([0,S_-)\) is forward-invariant because \(F\) is monotone there”

The conclusion requires more explicit assumptions:

1. \(F(S)<S\) for every \(0\le S<S_-\);
2. \(F\) is nondecreasing on \([0,S_-]\);
3. \(F(S_-)=S_-\);
4. \(F(S)\ge0\) after clipping;
5. the lower interval does not map across \(S_-\).

“Monotone below its maximum” is not the clearest condition, and for M1b it may not hold in the same way.

### Better proposition

> Let \(F:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) be the clipped deterministic map. Suppose there exists \(S_->0\) such that \(F(S_-) = S_-\), \(F(S)<S\) for all \(0<S<S_-\), and \(F([0,S_-])\subseteq[0,S_-]\). Then any trajectory entering \([0,S_-)\) is nonincreasing and cannot later exceed \(S_-\).

This is nearly tautological but correct. Then separately verify the conditions for the fitted Schaefer map.

## 12.2 The clipping convention is inconsistent inside the proposition

Definition 2.1 uses:

\[
[\cdot]_+,
\]

while the forecast implementation uses:

\[
[10^{-3},10^6].
\]

The proposition says:

> “the interval being closed at the left because the implementation clips the state at a non-negative floor.”

But the actual floor is \(10^{-3}\), not zero, and the mathematical map uses a zero floor.

Use one notation:

\[
[x]_{\ell}^{u}=\min\{u,\max\{\ell,x\}\}.
\]

Then distinguish:

- analytical map:
  \[
  \ell=0;
  \]
- forecast implementation:
  \[
  \ell=10^{-3},\quad u=10^6.
  \]

Do not mix the two in the proof.

## 12.3 The proposition should not imply anything about the historical cause

> “This proposition is a property of the fitted deterministic forecast map. It is not a causal statement about why the observed assessment series crossed the fitted lower equilibrium.”

That should appear immediately after the proposition.

# 13. The productivity inversion is useful but needs stronger dimensional and domain qualifications

The text defines:

\[
\hat r_t=\frac{P_t}{S_t(1-S_t/K)}.
\]

This is fine for a Schaefer increment, but the denominator can be:

- zero at \(S_t=0\);
- zero at \(S_t=K\);
- negative for \(S_t>K\);
- very small near \(S_t=0\) or \(K\).

You acknowledge some of this, but the reported mean comparisons can be highly sensitive to denominator magnitude and to \(K\).

### Recommended additions

Report:

- the range of the denominator;
- whether any transitions are excluded because the denominator is near zero;
- the exact rule for handling \(S_t>K\);
- uncertainty or sensitivity to \(K\);
- whether the mean is arithmetic in \(\hat r_t\) or weighted by production scale.

Also avoid:

> “no constant \(r\) reproduces a sequence that changes sign.”

A constant positive \(r\) cannot reproduce negative **implied production** while \(0<S<K\), but a full model with \(S>K\), negative residuals, or time-varying catch could behave differently. Say:

> “Under the Schaefer branch with \(0<S_t<K\), a constant positive \(r\) cannot reproduce the observed sign reversal in the reconstructed apparent-production diagnostic.”
