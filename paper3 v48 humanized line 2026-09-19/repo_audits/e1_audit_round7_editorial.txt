This revision is substantially cleaner than the earlier version. Several prior problems are fixed: M1b is now correctly described as a separate branch rather than a strict nesting of M1; the M3 wording is corrected; Table 9 is now tied to the primary coarse-regime pass; catch timing is stated; the five-module count is consistent; and the protocol status is disclosed. What remains are mostly stale numbers, terminology drift, and a few internal contradictions that should be fixed before submission.

## 1. Remaining internal inconsistencies

**1.1 Stale numbers in Section 3.5 “Readings.”**  
The paragraph still uses old Table 9 values:

- “At \(h=1\) the deficits against persistence (16.7--108.2 kt on \(n=25\)) carry DM \(z\) from 0.98 to 1.51…”  
  Current Table 9 gives \(h=1\) persistence gaps of 16.8–97.5 kt, with \(z\) up to 1.53 (M4).
- “At \(h=5\) (23.9--221.7 kt on \(n=21\)) \(z\) runs from 1.10 to 1.84 with \(p\) from 0.27 to 0.76.”  
  Current Table 9 gives \(h=5\) persistence gaps of 23.9–223.5 kt; for persistence comparisons the \(p\)-range is roughly 0.078–0.421, not 0.27–0.76.
- “M4’s delay cost against M3 … not on Specification A (\(p=0.90\)).”  
  Table 9 now reports \(p=0.707\) for Spec A \(h=5\) M4 vs M3, not 0.90.

These should be updated to match Table 9 exactly.

**1.2 Section 4 still says Table 9 Spec A rows are the annual-landings pass.**  
The sentence:

> “Table 9’s Specification A rows are the annual-landings pass, where M4 reads 206.3 kt, so the two sets of M4 figures are not directly comparable.”

is now wrong. Section 3.5 and the Table 9 caption state that both specifications are scored on their primary pass: Spec A rows are the coarse catch regime, so M4 reads 195.6 kt, matching Table 4. This sentence should be deleted or rewritten.

**1.3 M4 terminology remains inconsistent.**  
Table 2 now calls M4 “lagged initialisation,” and Section 4 correctly says it is “a stale-start experiment rather than a fully delayed information set.” But the retention rule still says “M3 for M4 (the module the delay acts on),” the ladder ordering in Section 1 says “residual, then delay,” and the Conclusions refer to “delayed information.” Since the paper itself argues M4 is not a genuine delay, it should consistently call it “lagged initialisation” or “stale-start” and note that “delay” is only a shorthand.

**1.4 M1b lower-bound wording is inconsistent.**  
Observation 3.2 correctly says zero is excluded by \(0<\mathfrak s<0.8K\), so the fitted values \(2.1\times10^{-23}\) and \(9.4\times10^{-6}\) are “a numerical approach to an unattained infimum rather than an attained boundary estimate.” But Section 3.1 still says “The estimate sits at the boundary,” and Table 10 says “\(\mathfrak s\) at its lower bound.” Both should be changed to “approaches the lower boundary to numerical tolerance” or similar.

**1.5 “Estimation range” vs “profile range.”**  
Section 3.1: “The grid for \(\mathfrak s\) spans the estimation range \([0,\min_{\mathrm{train}}S_t]=[0,9.68]\) kt.” This is the **profile** range, not the estimation range. The scored fit range is \((0,S^{\mathrm{pred}}_{\max}]=(0,40.83]\). The text later distinguishes them, so this phrase should be corrected.

**1.6 Wrong table citation for 264 vs 303 kt.**  
Section 3.1 says: “Under annual landings they do not (264 against 303 kt, Table 5).” Table 5 is rolling-origin RMSE with annual landings; it does not contain 264 or 303. Those numbers are the fixed recovery-window scores reported in Section 3.2 text. The citation should be to the text or to a new fixed-window table, not Table 5.

**1.7 “M1 fit” should be “M1b fit.”**  
Section 3.2, in the M1b pair discussion: “the dependence of the M1 fit on the catch file…” In context this is about M1b. Change to “M1b fit.”

**1.8 Proposition 4.1 cites the wrong section for M2 collapse evidence.**  
It says: “for M2 the argument is motivating rather than binding, and the collapse-window result of Section 3.3 is the relevant evidence.” For Specification A, the M2 collapse-window result is in Section 3.1. Section 3.3 is Specification B. Change to Section 3.1.

**1.9 Table 10 section citation for M1 collapse.**  
Table 10 lists M1 collapse as “(\S{}1, \S{}4).” It should be “(\S{}3.1, \S{}4).”

**1.10 “34% of old LRP” ambiguity.**  
Section 3.3: “The same late-period biomass is 34% of the old LRP and above the new one after 2016.” 2015 SSB is 33.8% of the old LRP; 2024 SSB is 342/884.6 = 38.7%. The sentence should specify which year is meant, or say “the 2015 value is 34%…”

## 2. Remaining gaps and methodological issues

**2.1 M4 is still not a clean delay experiment.**  
The manuscript now states this clearly, but the retention rule and conclusions still treat M4 as the “delay” module. Either rename it everywhere to “lagged initialisation” or add a standing note that M4 tests only a stale start state, not a full information delay.

**2.2 The 1992 catch drop is described as endogenous in one place and policy-driven elsewhere.**  
Section 3.1: “The 1992 catch drop is an endogenous response to depletion rather than an exogenous policy lever.” Section 4: “The moratorium is a change in implementable catch and is already in \(C_t\).” The 1992 moratorium was a policy action, even if prompted by depletion. The model treats it as a prescribed exogenous catch path. The text should say: “In the model the 1992 drop is supplied as an exogenous catch path; in reality it was largely a policy response to depletion. Because production is stationary, lowering \(C\) raises the predicted increment.”

**2.3 The prey module’s degrees-of-freedom problem is acknowledged but not fully quantified.**  
It says three parameters are fitted on as few as eight training years, i.e. seven one-step transitions. That is correct. But the paper does not report how many transitions are actually available at the shortest origins after dropping missing index years. A small table or sentence giving the minimum transition count would strengthen the claim that overfitting is a plausible cause of non-retention.

**2.4 The M1b alternative comparator for M2 is never printed.**  
Section 3.5 says the M2-vs-M1b rows are held in the archive. Since the retention rule declares M1b an alternative comparator for M2, the paper should either print those rows or explicitly state that they are immaterial to every verdict, with the archived values available.

**2.5 The uncertainty layer is still descriptive, not a formal test.**  
This is now clearly disclosed. But the paper repeatedly uses phrases like “separates” and “within noise.” These are fine if read as descriptive summaries, but the manuscript should avoid language that sounds like formal hypothesis testing. The current disclosure mostly does this; a few remaining phrases (“separating only on Specification B”) could be softened to “the bootstrap interval excludes zero on Specification B.”

**2.6 The log-RMSE floor binds often, but the paper does not show whether it affects any ranking.**  
The raw-RMSE column is the retention score, so no verdict depends on the floor. But the log-RMSE column is reported as a secondary diagnostic. The paper should state explicitly whether any secondary ranking would change if the floor were removed or set lower.

## 3. Smaller line-level fixes

- Section 2.1: “The two objects differ in five specification fields: the state equation (xteNCAM versus NCAM M-shift; start year 1954 versus 1983), the reference threshold…” The list is still awkwardly punctuated. Number the five fields explicitly: (1) state equation, (2) start year, (3) reference threshold, (4) catch treatment, (5) horizon.
- Section 2.3: “The sign-hit rate is conventionally 0.00 for persistence… persistence is excluded from that ranking by declaration.” But Table 3 reports NA for persistence. Clarify that some conventions assign 0.00, but this paper reports NA and excludes persistence.
- Section 3.2: The sentence beginning “One mechanism unifies both faces of the catch-dependence…” is very long and contains a confusing parenthetical: “though the training-mean catch does change with treatment and can move their fitted parameters (121 and 115 kt at \(h=1\)).” The 121 and 115 kt are the rolling scores, not fitted parameters. Rewrite.
- Section 3.2: “The recovery-window \(K=500\) kt fit therefore cannot be interpreted this way over the pre-collapse states.” The preceding calculation uses \(K=1032.7\) and \(K=5000\). Clarify which fit is meant and why \(K=500\) is mentioned.
- Section 3.5: Missing article: “persistence baseline is not archived per-origin” should be “The persistence baseline…”
- Section 4: “Accordingly, it is therefore a perturbed initialisation…” Remove one of “Accordingly” or “therefore.”
- Section 4: “The delay evaluated here is a one-year information delay…” Change “delay” to “lagged initialisation” if that is the new terminology.
- Section 5: “delayed information” should be “lagged initialisation” or “stale-start information.”
- Table 10: M1b recovery coarse says “\(\mathfrak s\) at its lower bound.” Change to “approaches lower bound to numerical tolerance.”
- Table 10: M1 collapse section cites “\S{}1, \S{}4”; should be “\S{}3.1, \S{}4.”

## 4. Priority improvements

1. **Update all stale numbers** in Section 3.5 “Readings” and Section 4’s Table 9 discussion to match the current Table 9.
2. **Fix the M4 terminology** globally: use “lagged initialisation” or “stale-start,” and reserve “delay” only for the conceptual discussion of why M4 is not a true delay.
3. **Correct the M1b boundary language** everywhere: it approaches an unattained lower bound, not an attained boundary.
4. **Fix the Section 3.1 Table 5 citation** for the 264 vs 303 kt comparison.
5. **Clarify the 1992 catch-drop description** to distinguish model treatment from historical policy.
6. **Correct minor citations** (Proposition 4.1, Table 10) and typographical issues (“M1 fit” → “M1b fit,” missing “The,” redundant “Accordingly, it is therefore”).

The core analysis and retention verdict appear sound within the stated scope. The remaining problems are largely editorial and consistency-related, but several of them—especially the stale Table 9 numbers and the M4 terminology—could confuse readers about what was actually tested and what the uncertainty layer shows.
