### 2. The Journal Abstract (Strictly ≤300 words, A1–A6 compliant)
*This leads with the evidence, separates the definitional arithmetic from the measured land-type data, frames the model as a mechanism demonstrator, and ends on the methodological/identifiability contribution.*

```latex
\begin{abstract}
Humanity's headline indicator of whether the planet can support us---total biocapacity---is a single number built from two very different things: the land we farm, and the ecosystems that regenerate what we consume. This paper asks whether that single number can conceal the composition of what is growing. It can, and the concealment is a property of how the number is built. From 1961 to 2022, global biocapacity grew about 23\%, yet measured land-type data show that growth came almost entirely from cropland (whose biocapacity grew 2.85$\times$) while the forest-and-soil book stayed essentially flat. Furthermore, per-capita biocapacity fell by half as population grew. A two-land model makes the mechanism precise: converting capital land into farmland raises the measured total while the capital book falls, and the drawdown is demand-driven, an abrupt approach to a floor rather than a slow precursor. Recovery is not the reverse of the decline: it needs an explicitly gated restoration flow. The deeper point is about measurement, not only ecology. Because the headline unit already mixes farmland yield with ecological-capital value, its composition cannot be read off the total; any aggregate that offsets a falling capital book with a rising yield can hide a decline. Identifying the composition therefore requires independent land-cover and yield data, and global sustainability indicators should report the components, not only the total. These results establish that an aggregate can conceal its composition; they are not a forecast and do not establish a measured global decline in ecological capital, which the data show remained essentially flat while the fast book grew.
\end{abstract}
```
*(Word count: 258 words. Safely inside the hard limit.)*

**The Indicator Implication Paragraph (Strictly A5 compliant):**

This has a direct consequence for how sustainability is measured. The composite-indicator literature warns that in additive aggregation, weights act as trade-offs, allowing a deficit in one dimension to be offset by a surplus in another \citep{Nardo2008}. Because biocapacity is exactly such a composite, the same structural risk applies. Reporting only the total therefore risks sending a misleading policy signal \citep{Becker2017, Fischer2022}, as an aggregate can conceal the very composition it is meant to summarize.
