> “A 1 M4 M3 … Gap +52.7 … DM z 0.99 … 95% CI [+4.7, +144.7] … p < 0.001.”

**Problems:**

1. A DM \(z\)-statistic of 0.99 is not compatible with a \(p\)-value below 0.001 under the usual normal approximation.

2. A 95% confidence interval that just excludes zero, `[+4.7, +144.7]`, is more consistent with marginal significance, not \(p<0.001\).

3. The text later says:

   > “On Specification A no non-retention margin separates from zero…”

   but then also says:

   > “M4's delay cost against M3 separates at \(h=1\) on both specifications (\(p\) below 0.001)…”

   These statements conflict if the M4-versus-M3 margin is a non-retention margin.

4. Some rows have bootstrap \(p\)-values that are hard to reconcile with the reported DM \(z\)-values, e.g. \(z \approx 1.02\) but \(p=0.927\). This may be possible if the \(p\)-value comes from a skewed bootstrap distribution, but the table does not make clear whether the \(p\)-value is a DM \(p\)-value, a bootstrap \(p\)-value, or something else.

**Fix needed:** define exactly what the \(p\)-value is, check the M4-versus-M3 Specification A row, and correct the text if some Specification A margins do separate.

### 1.8 Table 2 lists `C` as free for M1b, but the text never explains whether M1b estimates catch

### 1.9 “Unidentified modules increase error” is too broad and is contradicted by some table comparisons

**Locations:** Abstract, Section 4.

Abstract:

> “… and unidentified modules increase error.”

Section 4:

**Problem:** This is not uniformly true relative to the next-simpler model.

Examples:

- Specification A, Table 4, coarse regime:
  - M2: 144, M3: 135 at \(h=1\). M3 improves on M2.
  - M2: 398, M3: 366 at \(h=5\). M3 improves on M2.

Similarly, M1b improves on M1 on Specification A at \(h=1\) and on the fixed recovery window, although it fails overall retention.

**Fix needed:** say “failed to beat persistence” or “did not satisfy the retention rule,” not “increase error” without qualification.

## 3. Logical and methodological flaws

### 3.1 Definition 2.4 does not specify an H1 comparator for M1

**Location:** Definition 2.4.

H1 requires a module to reduce RMSE relative to its declared comparator. Comparators are given for:

- M1b vs M1,
- M3 vs M2,
- M2 vs M1,
- M4 vs M3.

But no comparator is specified for M1 itself.

**Problem:** M1 is the first structural rung. Does H1 apply to it? If so, what is its comparator? Persistence? Mean? If H1 does not apply to M1, the definition should say so.

Because M1 fails H2, the omission does not change the verdict, but the rule is incomplete as written.

---

### 3.2 The role of \(\varepsilon_t\) in Definition 2.1 is unclear

**Location:** Definition 2.1.

The map is:

\[
S_{t+1}=\bigl[S_t+g(S_t)-C_t+\varepsilon_t\bigr]_+.
\]

But the forecasting models in Table 2 are deterministic, and Proposition 4.1 analyzes \(S_{t+1}=F(S_t)\) without \(\varepsilon_t\).

**Problem:** Is \(\varepsilon_t\):

- a regression error used only in estimation?
- a process noise used in forecasting?
- a residual modeled by M3?
- set to zero for forecasts?

This is not stated. If forecasts are deterministic, Definition 2.1 should either define the forecast as the conditional mean or remove \(\varepsilon_t\) from the forecast map. If \(\varepsilon_t\) is stochastic, Proposition 4.1’s deterministic impossibility claim needs qualification.

---

### 3.3 Lemma 3.2 is applied to the recovery window without establishing monotonicity

**Location:** Lemma 3.2 and surrounding text.

Lemma:

> “On a training window along which \(S_t\) rises monotonically…”

Then it is applied to the recovery window:

> “The identification failure is topographically forced.”

**Problem:** The paper does not show that the 1995–2007 training window is monotonically rising. If there are dips, the lemma’s premise is not strictly satisfied. The broader point about weak identification may still be true, but the lemma as stated depends on monotonic recovery.

---

### 3.4 Brier score is called “near-degenerate” and said to separate little, but table values show separation

**Locations:** Section 2.3, Table 3, Section 3.3.

Section 2.3:

> “On Specification A the Brier indicator is near-degenerate … so the score separates little.”

Table 3 collapse window:

- persist: 0.00
- M1: 1.00
- M1b: 0.80
- M2/M3/M4: 1.00
- mean: 0.00

That is strong separation: persistence is perfect, several structural forecasts are maximally wrong.

Section 3.3 also says Specification A persistence Brier is 0.00 and structural scores are positive, “unbeatable there.”

**Problem:** The outcome may be degenerate because all observed values are below LRP, but the score is not separation-poor in this sample; it separates persistence from several structural models.

**Fix needed:** distinguish “degenerate event frequency” from “little separation.”

---

### 3.5 The claim that catch-source sensitivity is “closed” for the collapse window is not fully supported

**Location:** Section 2.2.

Text:

> “Regular et al. (2025) Table 1 matches Schijns exactly on 1983--1993 … STATLANT matches Schijns on 1983--1993, so the STATLANT-versus-Schijns sensitivity is closed for the collapse window (they are the same column there).”

**Problem:** The collapse test window is 1991–1995. Matching only through 1993 does not establish that 1994–1995 are identical. If catches in 1994–1995 are very low, the practical effect may be negligible, but the statement “closed for the collapse window” is stronger than the stated matching range.

---

### 3.6 The 1956 catch discrepancy is said to be unused, but Specification B includes 1956

**Location:** Section 2.2.

Text:

> “A 1956 discrepancy between those sources … lies outside 1983--2015 and is unused.”

**Problem:** Specification B is 1954–2024. Therefore 1956 lies inside Specification B. If the discrepancy is irrelevant because Specification B uses a different catch source, say that. As written, “unused” conflicts with the later use of the extended 1954–2024 specification.

---

### 3.7 The prey-index module definition conflicts with the “pre-1991 values not carried across 1991” rule

**Locations:** Section 2.2, Section 3.4.

Section 2.2:

> “Pre-1991 acoustic values are not carried across 1991.”

Section 3.4:

> “\(I_{\mathrm{known}}(t)\) is the last observation at or before \(t\).”

**Problem:** If \(I_{\mathrm{known}}(t)\) is simply the last observation at or before \(t\), then pre-1991 values would be carried across 1991 when early-1990s observations are missing. The text says missing survey years are not interpolated, but it does not say what value is used when the last observation is pre-1991. The two statements need reconciliation.

---

### 3.8 “Independent negative certificate” may overstate independence

**Location:** Section 3.3.

> “That is the second independent negative certificate…”

**Problem:** Specification B uses overlapping years, related assessment products, the same ladder, and the same scoring rule. It is a second specification, but “independent” may be too strong unless independence is defined.

**Fix needed:** “second specification-level negative certificate” would be safer.

---

### 3.9 The claim that rolling scores are insensitive to catch treatment is too broad

**Location:** Section 3.2.

> “… so the rolling scores (refit at every origin) are insensitive to the catch treatment while the single fixed-window fit is not.”

**Problem:** This flat-valley explanation applies to M1/M1b with constant catch. But M2’s rolling RMSE does change with catch treatment:

- Specification A M2 \(h=1\): 144 kt coarse, 160 kt annual.
- Specification A M2 \(h=5\): 398 kt coarse, 394 kt annual.

So rolling scores are not generally insensitive to catch treatment across the ladder.

**Fix needed:** limit the sentence to M1/M1b or clarify that different modules behave differently.

---

### 3.10 The recovery-stall fixed window is described as showing “the same failure,” even though M1b beats persistence there

**Location:** Section 4.

> “… the ladder's single-origin comparison on that window (M1 254 kt, M1b 178 kt against persistence 262 kt, with no retention on the rolling score) shows the same failure on the forecast side.”

**Problem:** On that single fixed window, M1 and especially M1b outperform persistence. The rolling retention verdict still fails, but calling this single-origin result “the same failure” is misleading.

**Fix needed:** say that the rolling score still fails retention, even though the single fixed-origin comparison favors M1/M1b.

## 4. Definition and scope issues

### 4.1 “Schaefer/Allee ladder” understates the full model set

**Locations:** Abstract, Discussion.

Abstract:

> “… negative certificate for the scored Schaefer/Allee ladder…”

But the ladder includes:

- M1 Schaefer,
- M1b Allee,
- M2 stock-flow,
- M3 residual,
- M4 delay,
- plus prey/index variants in Section 3.4.

**Problem:** The certificate is for a broader ladder than “Schaefer/Allee.” If the certificate is only for M1/M1b, that should be stated; otherwise use a broader phrase.

---

### 4.2 “Primary predictand” and “safe set” are clear, but the Brier indicator uses a threshold that makes the primary Brier uninformative on Specification A

**Location:** Section 2.3, Section 3.3.

The LRP 884.6 kt is above all rolling origin states. Therefore persistence’s Brier score is perfect by construction. This is disclosed, but it means the Brier score on Specification A cannot assess early-warning skill. The paper says it is secondary and not retention-deciding, which is good, but the phrase “secondary diagnostic” should be repeated wherever Brier values appear to avoid overinterpretation.

---

### 4.3 Fixed windows overlap at 1995

**Location:** Section 2.3.

Fixed windows:

- Collapse: train 1983–1990, test 1991–1995.
- Recovery: train 1995–2007, test 2008–2015.

**Problem:** 1995 appears in the collapse test window and as the first recovery training year. This is not necessarily invalid because the windows are separate exercises, but it should be acknowledged if the paper wants to avoid any suggestion of leakage between fixed-window exercises.

---

### 4.4 Catch timing is not explicit

**Location:** Definition 2.1 and catch regime.

The map uses \(C_t\) to produce \(S_{t+1}\). The coarse regime says:

- \(C_t=240\) for \(t\le 1991\),
- \(C_t=120\) for \(t=1992\),
- \(C_t=5\) for \(t\ge 1993\).

**Problem:** It is not explicit whether a forecast issued from origin year \(t\) to \(t+1\) uses catch in year \(t\), year \(t+1\), or a known catch assumption. This matters for the 1992 drop. The likely convention is clear, but it should be stated.

---

### 4.5 Estimation procedure for M3’s AR term is not fully specified

**Locations:** Section 2.2, Table 2, Section 3.1.

The paper says parameters are estimated by one-step least squares. M3 adds an AR(1) residual. It is not clear whether:

- \(r,K\) and \(\phi\) are jointly estimated,
- residuals are computed from a first-stage M2 fit and then \(\phi\) is estimated,
- one-step least squares includes lagged residuals as covariates.

For reproducibility and for interpreting “one-step least squares,” this should be clarified.

---

## 5. Statistical interpretation issues

### 5.1 The negative certificate is described as machine-verified, but the class-level incompatibility is not machine-verified

**Locations:** Definition 2.5, Section 2.3, Abstract, Section 4.

The text correctly says:

> “The machine layer verifies the recorded arithmetic and its byte-level reproducibility, not the class-level incompatibility.”

But Definition 2.5 says:

> “A negative certificate is a machine-verified finding of non-retention of a model class…”

**Problem:** The phrase “model class” may imply a broader mathematical conclusion than the machine verification supports. The machine can verify that this implementation/ladder/series fails the rule; it cannot verify all possible implementations of the class. The scope is later narrowed, but the definition itself should be more careful.

**Fix needed:** define the negative certificate as machine-verified non-retention of the scored implementation, not the entire class unless the class is precisely restricted to that implementation.

---

### 5.2 Small-sample caution is present, but the point-rule retention verdict is still strong relative to the uncertainty

**Locations:** Section 3.5, Section 4.

Section 3.5 says for Specification A:

> “… the negative certificate on Specification A is a point-rule ranking whose margins are within noise…”

This is an important caveat. It should appear near the Abstract and Conclusions as prominently as the non-retention verdict itself. In the Abstract, the non-retention result is stated more forcefully than the uncertainty caveat.

---

### 5.3 The DM layer uses archived annual-landings per-origin forecasts for Specification A, while the coarse-regime per-origin forecasts are not archived

**Location:** Section 3.5.

This creates a weird status:

- primary coarse-regime summary is frozen,
- primary coarse-regime per-origin rows are not archived,
- uncertainty is computed from the annual-landings per-origin rows.

The text says verdicts coincide, but for auditability this is a weakness. If the paper claims machine-verified reproducibility, the absence of archived per-origin rows for the primary pass should be stated more prominently.

## 7. Summary of the most important corrections needed

The most consequential internal problems are:

1. **Ladder versus baselines count is inconsistent.**
2. **The `K` lower-bound statement is directly contradicted by the 500-kt pinned fit, and the paper admits the conflict without resolving it.**
3. **Proposition 4.1 is mathematically overstated; the proof only rules out recovery after crossing the lower repeller.**
4. **Table 9 has at least one clearly inconsistent row: Specification A \(h=1\) M4 vs M3 with \(z=0.99\), positive 95% CI, and \(p<0.001\).**
5. **Table 9 uses the annual-landings pass for Specification A while being framed as uncertainty on the primary retention margins.**
6. **The retention rule is described as pre-frozen, but comparator declarations and the tie band were added after scoring.**
7. **M4’s parameter status and M1b’s treatment of `C` are ambiguous or contradictory.**
8. **The claim that unidentified modules increase error is too broad and conflicts with M3 improving on M2 in several tables.**
9. **Origin-matching arithmetic in Specification B is mixed across controlled and mixed-origin baselines.**
10. **Catch-source matching claims do not fully cover the collapse window or Specification B’s extended period.**

Addressing these would substantially tighten the manuscript’s internal coherence and prevent reviewers from questioning the reproducibility claims even where the substantive conclusions may be sound.
