astra:
## Scope and reporting

The results below are **established**, subject to the explicitly stated modelling conventions. Additional numerical ledgers are **illustrative**; their exact LP optima are reported as **bounds conditional on those declared inputs**.

I retain the manuscript’s quoted “Verified instance” labels. Where a conclusion does not follow, I mark a **disagreement**, rather than changing that label or repairing the statement.

Several requested conclusions are false or under-specified, rather than open mathematical problems. In particular, \(G_i,\lambda_i,B_1+B_2,T,L\) do **not** specify a joint flux LP.

### Two conventions needed for the review

**Quotient incidence.** The phrase

> “whose incidence is \(\operatorname{diag}(S_1,S_2)\) extended by the identification rows”

has a specification gap: adding equality rows and quotienting state coordinates are different operations.

For the quotient results below, use this explicit computational convention. Let \(R\) be the class-incidence matrix of the equivalence relation generated by \(J\):
\[
R_{Ca}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\in C.
\]
For additive pooling,
\[
x_C=Rx_0,\qquad
S_C=R[S_0\ E],\qquad B_C=RB_0,
\]
where \(S_0=\operatorname{diag}(S_i)\), and an exchange \(a\to b\) has column
\(E_\varphi=-e_a+e_b\). Identifications are computed by connected components or union–find. Types and units must agree throughout every class; capacities retain their declared flux identifiers.

This establishes a quotient ledger when those checks pass. It does **not** automatically establish that component margins or component certificates descend to that ledger.

**LP convention.** All LPs below use
\[
\tag{P}
\max c^\top z
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
Az=d,\quad Hz\le h,\quad z\ge0,
\]
with dual
\[
\tag{D}
\min d^\top p+h^\top q
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
A^\top p+H^\top q\ge c,\quad p\ \text{free},\ q\ge0.
\]
Boxes are rows of \(H\); stationarity, declared \(\ell\)-rows, and interface couplings are rows of \(A\). Adding nonnegative slacks gives equality standard form.

A dual witness proves the bound through
\[
d^\top p+h^\top q-c^\top z
=p^\top(d-Az)+q^\top(h-Hz)
 +(A^\top p+H^\top q-c)^\top z.
\]
For feasibility, \(A^\top p+H^\top q\ge0\) and \(d^\top p+h^\top q<0\) is a Farkas infeasibility witness. Failure of a certificate search is never interpreted as evidence of danger.

## 1. The lift statement: injectivity is automatic; surjectivity is not

**Status: established under the quotient-incidence convention.**

Let
\[
K_0=\ker S_0^\top=\bigoplus_i\ker S_i^\top,
\]
and let \(N\) have columns forming a basis of \(K_0\). Let \(H_J\) have rows
\(e_a^\top-e_b^\top\) for the identifications. Define
\[
C=\begin{bmatrix}H_J\\E^\top\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Then pullback by the quotient map is \(R^\top\), and
\[
\boxed{
R^\top\ker S_C^\top
=K_0\cap\ker H_J\cap\ker E^\top .
}
\]
Indeed, \(\operatorname{im}R^\top=\ker H_J\), and
\(S_C^\top\ell=0\) is exactly
\(S_0^\top R^\top\ell=0,\ E^\top R^\top\ell=0\).

Consequently:

* \(R^\top\) is always **injective**, because \(R\) has full row rank.
* It is **surjective onto the full direct sum \(K_0\)** iff
  \[
  \boxed{\operatorname{rank}(CN)=0.}
  \]
  Equivalently,
  \[
  \operatorname{rank}
  \begin{bmatrix}S_0^\top\\H_J\\E^\top\end{bmatrix}
  =\operatorname{rank}S_0^\top.
  \]
* The exact dimension is
  \[
  \boxed{\dim\ker S_C^\top
  =\dim K_0-\operatorname{rank}(CN).}
  \]

For **conservation as defined in the question**, replace \(S_0\) by
\([S_0\ B_0]\), include inherited boundary columns in the composition, and require nonnegativity.

Thus:

> “every left-null vector of each part lifts to a left-null vector of the composition”

**Disagreement with the unrestricted statement.** A qualitative prohibition on merging “distinct moieties” is not the exact condition. Moieties need not have a unique basis, and exchange columns impose additional compatibility conditions even when \(J=\varnothing\).

There is also an important ambiguity in “lifts”:

* Preserving an arbitrary tuple of part vectors requires \(\operatorname{rank}(CN)=0\).
* Extending one prescribed part vector while allowing the other parts’ vectors to change is weaker.

Writing \(N=\operatorname{diag}(N_i)\), every vector of part \(i\) has an unrestricted linear extension iff
\[
\operatorname{rank}[CN_i\ \ CN_{-i}]
=\operatorname{rank}(CN_{-i}).
\]
Nonnegative extension of a specified moiety is checked by the LP
\[
\ell_C\ge0,\qquad S_C^\top\ell_C=0,\qquad
B_C^\top\ell_C=0,
\]
together with the prescribed restriction equations.

### Two donors, one identification

**Illustrative declared instance.** Each part has a donor and a receiving pool:
\[
S_1=S_2=\begin{bmatrix}-1\\1\end{bmatrix},\qquad B_i=0,
\]
with unit boxes. Identify the two receiving pools. The composition, ordered as
\((d_1,p,d_2)\), has
\[
S_C=
\begin{bmatrix}
-1&0\\
1&1\\
0&-1
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The two proposed inherited totals have coefficients
\[
(1,1,0)S_C=(0,1),\qquad
(0,1,1)S_C=(1,0).
\]
Both original, separately named totals cease to be conserved observables. Only their compatible common extension survives:
\[
\ker S_C^\top=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1,1)^\top\}.
\]

**Important limit on the requested witness.** One identification alone removes at most one linear degree of freedom. It cannot make a two-dimensional direct-sum nullspace zero. Moreover, if both parts have nonnegative conserved vectors, one identification alone leaves some nonnegative compatible combination. Thus “both conservations destroyed” cannot mean “no conservation remains” in this one-identification, no-exchange example.

### Complete destruction with one identification and one declared exchange

If the permitted \(\Phi\) is included, complete destruction is possible.

Take
\[
S_1=\begin{bmatrix}-1\\2\end{bmatrix},
\quad \ell_1=(2,1)^\top,\qquad
S_2=\begin{bmatrix}-1\\1\end{bmatrix},
\quad \ell_2=(1,1)^\top.
\]
Identify the receiving pools and add a unit-box exchange \(d_1\to d_2\). Assign the donors the same declared type and unit, and likewise the receiving pools. All of Def 34(i)–(iii) pass. Then
\[
S_C=
\begin{bmatrix}
-1&0&-1\\
2&1&0\\
0&-1&1
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad \det S_C=1.
\]
Both part conservations are lost, and there is no nonzero composition left-null vector.

For the normalized conservation-feasibility LP
\[
S_C^\top\ell=0,\qquad \mathbf1^\top\ell=1,\qquad \ell\ge0,
\]
the equality-row Farkas witness is
\[
p=(3,-5,-4,-1)^\top:
\quad
\begin{bmatrix}S_C^\top\\\mathbf1^\top\end{bmatrix}^{\!\top}p=0,
\qquad
(0,0,0,1)p=-1.
\]

This example uses exactly the supplied formal admissibility rules. A fixed elemental-composition map attached to each material type would be an additional hypothesis, not something available here.

## 2. Is \(\oplus\) a calculus?

### Ledger-level algebra

**Status: established conditional on coherent global interface bookkeeping.**

For a fixed global collection of identifications, exchanges, and shared-capacity rows:

* **Associativity:** yes, up to the canonical relabelling isomorphism.
* **Unit:** the empty ledger, with empty interface.
* **Commutativity:** yes, up to relabelling, while retaining each physical exchange’s named source and destination.
* **Order of imposing identifications:** no dependence.

The proof is that successive quotient maps produce the quotient by the equivalence relation generated by the **union** of all identifications. Thus both parenthesizations have the same normal form
\[
R_{\rm global}
\bigl[\operatorname{diag}(S_1,S_2,S_3)\ \ E_{\rm global}\bigr].
\]
Readout, boundary, and capacity declarations are carried by their identifiers, exactly once.

This does **not** establish an associative binary operation on bare ledgers without interfaces. Different interface declarations, dropped capacity rows, or duplicated exchange variables define different objects.

### Interface costs

Write \(w_i=\lambda_i^\top G_i\). For an exchange \(a\to b\), the coefficient in the derivative of the total potential is
\[
a_\varphi=w_b-w_a.
\]
For independently selectable boxes,
\[
\max_{0\le f_\varphi\le\bar v_\varphi}
\sum_\varphi a_\varphi f_\varphi
=\sum_\varphi a_\varphi^+\bar v_\varphi.
\]
There is **no additional many-sided algebraic residual** for two-endpoint exchanges with fixed compatible potentials.

But:

1. Couplings can make that separable bound strict.
2. Arbitrary identifications can invalidate the inherited potential altogether.
3. Changing the certificates during sequential composition can change the reported bound.

### Three-part coupling witness

**Illustrative declared LP.** Let
\[
w=(1,2,4)^\top,\qquad
E=
\begin{bmatrix}
-1&0&1\\
1&-1&0\\
0&1&-1
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
a=E^\top w=(1,2,-3)^\top.
\]
These are exchanges \(1\to2\), \(2\to3\), \(3\to1\), each with box \([0,1]\).

The independent-box programme has value \(3\), with primal witness
\(f=(1,1,0)\) and box dual \(q=(1,2,0)\).

With steady-state coupling,
\[
\max a^\top f
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad Ef=0,\quad 0\le f\le1,
\]
the value is \(0\). The dual witness is \(p=w,\ q=0\), since \(E^\top p=a\). The coupling forces all three flows equal.

Thus the exact joint contribution can be strictly smaller than the sum of positive parts. That is a coupling saving, not a new many-sided residual.

### Three-part identification witness

Identifications require an additional compatibility check.

Take three scalar pools, each with \(G_i=1\), drain \(S_i=-1\), certificate and service coefficient
\[
\lambda_i=c_i\in\{1,2,4\}.
\]
Identify all pools, use no exchange columns, start at zero, and declare boundary input \(b_1=1,\ b_2=b_3=0\). Over unit time, the part budgets sum to \(1\).

The composed stationary LP is
\[
\max v_1+2v_2+4v_3
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
v_1+v_2+v_3=1,\quad 0\le v_i\le1.
\]
Its exact value is \(4\), attained by \(v_3=1\). The equality dual \(p=4\), with zero box multipliers, certifies it.

There are no \(\Phi\)-prices to charge, yet the proposed inherited budget is \(1\). The weights \(1,2,4\) do not descend to the identified pool. This is a missing compatibility condition, not something repaired by adding a universal three-sided price term.

## 3. Orientation and sign

**Status: established counterexample and conditional budget theorem.**

Def 35 must first name both endpoints. For disjoint compartment sets, the second occurrence of \(c\) is undefined unless an endpoint correspondence is declared.

Suppose, as stated, the paying side is the **source** \(a\), and the recipient endpoint is \(b\). Then
\[
\pi_\varphi=w_a-w_b.
\]

Let \(U=\sum_i\lambda_i^\top m_i\), and let \(b_i\) exclude the newly added exchange. Along a trajectory on which the margins hold,
\[
\sum_i y_i+\dot U
\le \sum_i w_i b_i
+\sum_\varphi(w_b-w_a)f_\varphi.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\int_0^T\sum_i y_i
\le B_1+B_2+
\sum_\varphi(-\pi_\varphi)^+\bar v_\varphi T .
}
\]
This theorem also requires the summed readout to have a declared common unit and, for a quotient, a well-defined nonnegative total potential.

Hence:

> “The composition is certified for \(y_1+y_2\) with budget  
> \(B_1+B_2+\sum_\varphi\pi_\varphi^{+}\bar v_\varphi T\)”

**Disagreement: the sign is reversed under the stated draining-side convention.**

### Admissible trajectory counterexample

Use two stock-to-waste ledgers, with stock margins, common declared service units, and
\[
\lambda_1=c_1=1,\qquad \lambda_2=c_2=2.
\]
Start with one unit in stock 1 and zero in stock 2. Transfer \(f=1\) from stock 1 to stock 2, and drain stock 2 at \(v_2=1\), for unit time. All stocks remain nonnegative; total stock plus waste is conserved.

Then
\[
B_1+B_2=1,\qquad \pi=1-2=-1,\qquad
\int(y_1+y_2)=2.
\]
The quoted bound gives \(1\), and is violated.

The corresponding integrated LP is
\[
\max V_1+2V_2
\]
subject to
\[
V_1+F\le1,\qquad V_2-F\le0,\qquad
0\le V_1,V_2,F\le1.
\]
Its value is \(2\), with primal \((0,1,1)\) and dual multipliers \(2,2\) on the first two rows, zero on the box rows.

### Reversing the direction

> “the cost \(\pi_\varphi^+\bar v_\varphi T\) is invariant under reversing the declared direction”

**Disagreement.** Reversing the physical direction while retaining a nonnegative box changes \(\pi\) to \(-\pi\). In general,
\[
\pi^+\bar vT\ne(-\pi)^+\bar vT.
\]
For the quoted weights \(2,1\), \(L=0.2\), \(T=30\), these two expressions are \(6\) and \(0\).

An **orientation-covariant convention** is:

* store an oriented incidence column \(e_\varphi\);
* store its signed feasible flux set \(F_\varphi\);
* use the coefficient \(w^\top e_\varphi\).

A coordinate reversal is
\[
e_\varphi'=-e_\varphi,\qquad
f_\varphi'=-f_\varphi,\qquad
F_\varphi'=-F_\varphi.
\]
Then the support value is invariant:
\[
\sup_{f'\in-F}( -w^\top e)f'
=\sup_{f\in F}(w^\top e)f.
\]
Replacing \([0,L]\) by \([0,L]\) after reversing the column is instead a different physical interface.

Finally:

> “the interface is free iff all \(\pi_\varphi\le0\)”

**Disagreement under Def 35’s source-minus-recipient convention.** For independent positive boxes, the derived zero-charge condition is \(\pi_\varphi\ge0\). With coupling or zero boxes, even the corresponding componentwise converse need not hold.

> “with one exchange of box \([0,L]\), certification holds iff  
> \(L\le L_{\max}=(B_1+B_2)/(\pi T)\)”

**Disagreement.** A finite-box upper bound remains a certificate for every finite \(L\); increasing \(L\) can merely loosen it. A certification cutoff requires an additional declared target or permitted budget. The products \(6,15\) and ratio \(0.30\) are correct arithmetic, but they do not establish the stated iff.

## 4. Tightness and the missing joint LP

**Exact optimum of the quoted scalar instance: not established from the supplied inputs.**

Missing inputs include \(S_i\), service coefficients \(c_i\), primitive boxes, boundary inputs, initial stocks, and any coupling or conservation rows. Moreover, a stationary programme \(Sv=0\) is not automatically an exact finite-horizon trajectory programme.

Even the correctly signed box bound need not be attained. Equality requires, among other things, zero terminal potential, tight used-flux certificate inequalities, and simultaneous attainment of the relevant interface-box extrema.

For the quoted direction \(w_1=2\to w_2=1\), however, the exchange is dissipative:
\[
\int(y_1+y_2)\le 9-\int f\le9.
\]
Thus, conditional on that direction and ordinary exchange accounting, \(9+30L\) cannot be attained when \(L>0\). The exact gap still needs the missing ledger data.

### Two illustrative completions with the same quoted scalar data

Declare
\[
a=\frac3{20},\qquad T=30,\qquad
\dot x_1=a-v_1-f,\qquad \dot x_2=f-v_2,
\]
with \(x(0)=0\), \(G_1=G_2=1\), \(\lambda_1=2,\lambda_2=1\), and
\[
0\le v_1,v_2\le a,\qquad 0\le f\le L.
\]
The declared boundary contribution gives \(B_1+B_2=2aT=9\).

The stationary fibre-product LP is
\[
\tag{P_L}
\max c_1v_1+c_2v_2
\]
subject to
\[
v_1+f=a,\qquad v_2-f=0,\qquad
0\le(v_1,v_2,f)\le(a,a,L).
\]
Equivalently, include a boundary variable \(q=a\) and impose
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1&-1&0&-1\\
0&0&-1&1
\end{bmatrix}
(q,v_1,v_2,f)^\top=0.
\]
The \(\ell\)-rows are empty in these particular open scalar ledgers.

* **Completion A:** \(c=(2,1)\).

  \]
  Primal: \((a,0,0)\). Equality dual: \(p=(2,1)\); box dual zero.

* **Completion B:** \(c=(0,1)\).
  \[
  V_B(L)=\min(a,L).

  If \(L\le a\), primal \((a-L,L,L)\), equality dual \(p=(0,1)\), and \(q_f=1\).  
  If \(L\ge a\), primal \((0,a,a)\), equality dual \(p=(1,1)\), box dual zero.

These stationary fluxes are admissible constant trajectories with \(x(t)=0\), so the integrated values are attained.

## 5. Completeness of the certificate search

### Affine margins

**Status: established counterexample to completeness as stated.**

The search
\[
\lambda\ge0,\qquad \lambda^\top GS+c^\top\le0
\]
is complete for finding certificates **of exactly that restricted algebraic form**. It is not complete for all valid budgets in systems having affine margins.

Declare
\[
\dot x=-v,\qquad x\ge0,\qquad 0\le v\le1,\qquad
y=v,\qquad x(0)=10,\quad T=1.
\]
The \(\lambda\)-search requires \(\lambda\ge1\), and hence gives a budget at least \(10\).

But the exact integrated programme is
\[
\max V\quad\text{s.t.}\quad V\le10,\quad V\le1,\quad V\ge0.
\]
Its value is \(1\), attained by \(v=1\). The dual witness is \(q=(0,1)\): the box row, omitted by the restricted search, proves the sharp bound.

**What is complete:** for a nonempty, fully specified polyhedron with finite optimum, the full LP dual certifies every valid linear upper bound, in the sense that some dual objective is no larger than that bound. This does not imply completeness for trajectory budgets unless the LP is an exact representation of the trajectory problem.

### Quadratic barriers and SOS

**Status: established tractability distinctions.**

There is no single boundary “affine is tractable, quadratic is intractable”:

* Rational LPs are polynomial-time solvable in their input bit size.
* Global nonnegativity of a quadratic polynomial is an exact positive-semidefinite matrix condition.
* Quadratic nonnegativity on a general polytope is already hard. For example, maximizing a convex quadratic over a box includes weighted maximum cut: a maximum occurs at a vertex.
* Some special quadratic implications are lossless SDPs—for example the single-quadratic-constraint S-lemma under its strict-feasibility hypothesis.
* Fixed-degree SOS representations with coefficient equations affine in the unknowns give SDPs. Their Gram matrices have size \(\binom{n+d}{d}\) for degree \(2d\). Approximate tractability requires the usual conditioning/strict-feasibility qualifications; exact SDP feasibility is not interchangeable with floating-point success.
* Jointly multiplying unknown barriers by unknown multipliers generally creates bilinear decision constraints, not an SDP.

SOS is sufficient, not generally necessary. For example,
\[
M(x,y)=x^4y^2+x^2y^4+1-3x^2y^2\ge0
\]
by AM–GM, but it is not SOS. If it were a sum of squares of cubics, restricting to either coordinate axis forces every summand to have only monomials
\(1,xy,x^2y,xy^2\). Its \(x^2y^2\) coefficient would then be a sum of squares, contradicting the coefficient \(-3\).

On a compact semialgebraic domain with an Archimedean quadratic module, strict positivity has a finite-degree SOS-multiplier representation at some order. This does not give completeness at a fixed order, nor guaranteed finite attainment for nonnegative boundary cases.

Prop 37 uses constant \(G\), so it is **not applicable unchanged** to quadratic barriers. A nonlinear analogue uses
\(\nabla U(x)^\top E_\varphi\), generally a state-dependent coefficient. Chain-rule or SOS-based versions remain sufficient. They do **not** become iff statements merely by enlarging the barrier class.

## 6. The interval hypothesis and endpoint algorithms

### Correct topological statement

**Status: established.**

> “\(\tau\) monotone along the fibre”

**Disagreement as a needed hypothesis.**

For a fixed declared evolution:

* If \(\mathcal F(z)\) is nonempty and connected and \(\tau\) is continuous on it, then \(\mathrm T(z)\) is an interval.
* If additionally the fibre is compact and \(\tau\) is finite, then
  \[
  \mathrm T(z)=
  [\min_{\mathcal F(z)}\tau,\ \max_{\mathcal F(z)}\tau].
  \]

Continuity of an exit-time function is an additional requirement; smooth dynamics alone do not ensure it at grazing or infinite-exit configurations. Connectedness and continuity are sufficient, not logically necessary.

### Precisely when endpoint LPs follow

> “quasi-concavity/convex superlevel sets give the endpoint LPs”

**Disagreement without further representation assumptions.** Convex superlevel sets support level-set feasibility methods, not automatically LPs; the lower endpoint can remain a hard nonconvex minimization.

A useful exact polyhedral case is
\[
\mathcal F=\{x\ge0:Ax=b,\ Hx\le h\},\qquad
\tau(x)=g\!\left(\min_{j=1}^k(a_j^\top x+b_j)\right),
\]
where \(g\) is a declared continuous increasing function.

Compute
\[
r_{\max}=\max_{x,r}r
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad x\in\mathcal F,\quad
r\le a_j^\top x+b_j\quad(j=1,\dots,k),
\]
and
\[
r_{\min}=\min_j\ \min_{x\in\mathcal F}(a_j^\top x+b_j).
\]
Then the endpoints are \(g(r_{\min}),g(r_{\max})\).

This uses \(k+1\) LPs, polynomial in the rational input size. Infeasible fibres, unbounded endpoints, or failure of the stated representation are explicit failure modes. General parameterized polyhedral superlevel sets permit LP-feasibility bisection for the upper endpoint, not necessarily one exact LP.

### The quoted decay instance

Here
\[
\tau(x_1,x_2)=\log\min(x_1,x_2).
\]
The upper endpoint LP is
\[
\max r\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
x_1+x_2=100,\quad x_i\ge1,\quad r\le x_i.
\]
It has value \(50\), with primal \((50,50,50)\). The dual certificate is
\[
r\le \tfrac12x_1+\tfrac12x_2=50.
\]
The lower coordinate minima are \(1\), certified by the lower-bound rows. Thus
\[
\mathrm T(z)=[0,\log50].
\]
Starts \((2,98)\) and \((50,50)\) give \(\log2\) and \(\log50\).

The exact upper endpoint is \(\log50\). The printed \(3.9120\) is a four-decimal display, not a literal outward-rounded upper bound.

### Disconnected exit-time sets

**Illustrative counterexample.** Retain the same decay dynamics and aggregate record, but declare the additional initial-state constraint
\[
(x_1-1)(x_1-2)(x_1-4)(x_1-5)\le0.
\]
Then
\[
x_1\in[1,2]\cup[4,5],\qquad x_2=100-x_1,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm T(z)=[0,\log2]\ \cup\ [\log4,\log5].
}
\]
It is disconnected.

Nonlinear or nonconvex constraints therefore **can**, but need not, produce disconnected \(\mathrm T(z)\). Connected fibres with continuous \(\tau\) still have interval images.

## 7. Stochastic exit times

### What replaces \(\mathrm T(z)\)?

**Status: established conditional on a fully declared stochastic model.**

For an internal diffusion matrix \(\Gamma\), every closed conserved moiety must satisfy
\[
\ell^\top\Gamma=0.
\]
Otherwise the noise must be an explicitly declared boundary contribution. An unbounded Brownian boundary contribution cannot satisfy a finite deterministic residual bound on every path; its stochastic residual reporting rule must also be declared.

For a specified initial state and stochastic model, \(\tau\) has a law \(K_{x_0}\) on \([0,\infty]\). The natural replacement of the deterministic set is generally a **family of laws**
\[
\{K_{x_0}:x_0\in\mathcal F(z)\},
\]
not one law. A single mixture law additionally requires a declared initial-allocation distribution. Random aggregate observations require the corresponding conditional laws under each declared model.

Computationally, under standard well-posedness and boundary regularity, survival probabilities solve the backward equation
\[
\partial_t u=\mathcal Lu,\qquad
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad u(0,x)=1,
\]
and a finite mean \(h(x)=\mathbb E_x\tau\) solves
\[
\mathcal Lh=-1,\qquad h|_{\partial\Omega}=0.
\]
Mean identification is established if the infimum and supremum of the appropriate conditional \(h\) over the admissible fibre coincide finitely. Missing coefficients, boundary conditions, conditioning rules, or infinite means prevent that conclusion.

### The aggregate need not identify the mean

**Illustrative zero-sum-noise counterexample.**
\[
dx_1=\sigma\,dW,\qquad dx_2=-\sigma\,dW,\qquad x_1+x_2=100,
\]
stopped when either pool reaches \(1\), and frozen thereafter. The entire aggregate record is \(100\), independently of the split.

The mean exit time is
\[
h(x_1)=\frac{(x_1-1)(99-x_1)}{\sigma^2},
\]
as verified by \((\sigma^2/2)h''=-1\) and the two boundary conditions.

For declared \(\sigma=1\), starts \((2,98)\) and \((50,50)\) have exact means \(97\) and \(2401\), respectively. Thus the aggregate record does not identify the mean, even with genuinely stochastic, mass-conserving transfer noise.

### Drift brackets do not give deterministic support brackets

> “brackets the passage-time law between the two deterministic bracket horizons”

**Disagreement for nonzero diffusion.**

Let \(A_0=a>0\), the barrier be \(0\), and
\[
dA_t=-b_t\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t,\qquad
0<b_{\min}\le b_t\le b_{\max}.
\]
Use the same Brownian path for the two constant-drift comparisons. Then
\[
A_t^{b_{\max}}\le A_t\le A_t^{b_{\min}},
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\tau_{b_{\max}}\le\tau\le\tau_{b_{\min}}
\quad\text{pathwise}.
}
\]
These are **random** hitting times.

For constant \(b>0\) and \(\sigma>0\),
\[
f_b(t)=\frac{a}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(a-bt)^2}{2\sigma^2t}\right],
\qquad t>0,
\]
and
\[
F_b(t)=
\Phi\!\left(\frac{bt-a}{\sigma\sqrt t}\right)
+e^{2ab/\sigma^2}
\Phi\!\left(-\frac{bt+a}{\sigma\sqrt t}\right).
\]
Thus
\[
F_{b_{\max}}(t)\ge F_\tau(t)\ge F_{b_{\min}}(t),
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\frac{a}{b_{\max}}
\le \mathbb E\tau
\le \frac{a}{b_{\min}}.
}
\]
The deterministic horizons bracket the **means**, not the support of the noisy law. Since \(f_b(t)>0\) for every \(t>0\), there is positive probability both before the earlier deterministic horizon and after the later one.

For a constant \(b\) strictly between \(b_{\min}\) and \(b_{\max}\), both hitting-time comparisons are strict almost surely, and both mean inequalities are strict. For adapted \(b_t\), a sufficient strictness condition is a uniform positive gap from both endpoint drifts before exit. If \(b_{\min}=0\), the upper mean need not be finite.

Finally, \(A\) in this scalar example cannot be an undeclared noisy **closed total moiety**. It must be an availability coordinate with a valid transfer realization, or have explicitly declared boundary noise.

## 8. Capacity duals do not characterize substitution

### The closure-capacity arithmetic

**Status: established conditional on the declared shared-capacity LP.**

For the two otherwise unconstrained cycles, eliminating the use and return variables gives
\[
\max\mu
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\mu\le1,\quad \mu\le1,\quad 2\mu\le C,\quad \mu\ge0.
\]
Its dual is
\[
\min \alpha_1+\alpha_2+C\eta
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\alpha_1+\alpha_2+2\eta\ge1,\qquad
\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\eta\ge0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Lambda^*(C)=\min(1,C/2).
\]
For \(C=1.5,1.0,2.0\), the quoted capacities \(0.75,0.50,1.0\) follow. The dual witness is \(\eta=1/2,\alpha_1=\alpha_2=0\).

Without a new shared restriction, local closure solutions can simply be combined with \(f=0\). The example’s failure of closure comes specifically from its newly declared shared-capacity row.

But:

> “give \(\Lambda^*=0.75\) and deficit \(0.25\)”

**Disagreement with inferring the stated closure deficit from this LP alone.** The defined \(\delta_m\) uses the fraction returned within a declared lag. Steady-state capacity does not specify that fraction. Return lags entirely within or entirely beyond \(\tau_{\rm use}\) can have the same stationary LP while giving different \(\kappa_m\). The lag-return rule is additional required input.

### What the dual price actually establishes

For a capacity parameter \(k\), a fully specified LP value is the infimum of affine dual objectives. At a finite optimum, an optimal capacity multiplier \(\eta^*\) gives
\[
V(k')\le V(k)+\eta^*(k'-k).
\]
It is a supergradient; it is the derivative where the value function is differentiable.

It need not be unique. In the shared-capacity example at \(C=2\), optimal shared-capacity prices range from \(0\) to \(1/2\); the right and left derivatives differ.

A moiety is a left-null vector, not a capacity row. There is no supplied canonical assignment of a moiety to one capacity multiplier. Calling a finite multiplier “substitutability” would establish only a renamed capacity sensitivity.

### Finite price is not sufficient for compensation

**Illustrative counterexample.** Two noninterconvertible declared use cycles have capacities \(k_1,k_2\):
\[
\max\mu\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\mu\le k_1,\quad\mu\le k_2,\quad\mu\ge0.
\]
At \((k_1,k_2)=(1/2,2)\), the value is \(1/2\), and the optimal capacity prices are \((1,0)\), both finite. Increasing \(k_2\) by any amount cannot replace the missing first use.

The target \(\mu\ge1\) is infeasible. Adding its row \(-\mu\le-1\), the Farkas multipliers
\[
q=(1,0,1)
\]
give zero left-hand side and right-hand side \(-1/2\).

An absent substitution column does not cause a shadow price to become infinite. The “all interface prices non-positive” condition is vacuously satisfied for an empty interface and still does not create a substitute.

### Non-positive interface price is not necessary for admissibility

> “compensation between components is admissible exactly where that price is finite and the interface price is non-positive”

**Disagreement.** Def 34’s admissibility conditions do not involve \(\lambda\). On the same physical drain ledgers with \(c_1=c_2=1\), both choices
\[
(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)=(2,1)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(1,2)
\]
are feasible part certificates. They give opposite interface-price signs for the same type-compatible exchange. Physical admissibility cannot depend on which certificate was chosen.

A positive accounting charge also does not prohibit compensation if adequate stocks, capacities, and budget are available.

### A computational replacement predicate

Given the full declared substitution columns, target \(D\), changed boxes, boundaries, and shared constraints, test
\[
v\ge0,\quad v\le\bar v',\quad
S_Cv+B_Cu_\partial=0,\quad Pv=D,\quad Hv\le h'.
\]
This feasibility programme establishes **steady-rate compensation under those declared inputs** when feasible. A Farkas witness establishes infeasibility of that particular programme; missing columns or constraints leave compensation **not established**. It does not establish dynamic safety.

No Def 21, formal “weak/strong” predicates, or complete substitution hypergraph has been supplied. The requested attribution to “Prop 36/37 plus Def 21” is therefore **not established**, independently of the counterexamples above.

## Conventions used below

For an exchange directed from a donor \(a\) to a receiver \(b\), I use the incidence column

\[
d_\varphi=-e_a+e_b.
\]

This is the convention consistent with “outflow” from the donor and “inflow” to the receiver. If Definition 34 intends the opposite sign, the direction labels must also be reversed. Several conclusions below are conditional on this declared incidence convention.

There is also a dimensional ambiguity in the phrase

> “incidence is \(\operatorname{diag}(S_1,S_2)\) extended by the identification rows”

because quotienting compartments removes or coalesces rows rather than adding identification rows. I use the canonical quotient construction defined in Task 1. Without such a construction, associativity and the lift map are not established.

---

# 1. Lift lemma and conservation

### Status: established, conditional on the canonical quotient interpretation

Let

\[
A_0=\operatorname{diag}\bigl([S_1\ B_1],\ldots,[S_k\ B_k]\bigr)
\]

on the disjoint union of all part compartments. Let \(R_J\) be the \(0\)-\(1\) quotient matrix: each original compartment column has a \(1\) in its quotient class. Thus the local columns in the composition are \(R_JA_0\).

For every exchange \(\varphi:a_\varphi\to b_\varphi\), let

\[
d_\varphi=-e_{a_\varphi}+e_{b_\varphi},\qquad
D_\Phi=[d_\varphi]_{\varphi\in\Phi}.
\]

Let \(C_J\) have rows \(e_a^\top-e_b^\top\) for a generating set of the identifications. Define

\[
C=\begin{bmatrix}C_J\\D_\Phi^\top\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Finally, let \(N\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}\) have columns forming a basis of

\[
\mathcal N_0=\ker A_0^\top
 =\bigoplus_i\ker [S_i\ B_i]^\top .
\]

Then the pullback of the composed left-null space is exactly

\[
R_J^\top\ker [R_JA_0\;\;R_JD_\Phi]^\top
   =\{N\alpha:CN\alpha=0\}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\dim\ker A_{\rm comp}^\top
 =r-\operatorname{rank}(CN).
\]

The pullback \(R_J^\top\) is always injective because \(R_J\) has one row for every nonempty quotient class. It is surjective onto the full direct sum \(\mathcal N_0\) if and only if

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(CN)=0.}
\]

For a particular tuple of part conservation vectors \(\ell=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k)\), the exact lift condition is simply

\[
\boxed{C\ell=0.}
\]

Thus the verbal condition “no identification merges distinct conserved moieties” is neither the exact condition nor sufficient by itself:

* exchange columns impose additional compatibility conditions;
* normalisations of conservation vectors are arbitrary;
* surjectivity onto the full direct sum is extremely strong. Since vectors supported on one part belong to the direct sum, surjectivity ordinarily requires every interface compartment to have coefficient zero in every such part-supported null vector.

The correct codomain is usually the matching space

\[
\mathcal N_0\cap\ker C,
\]

onto which the pullback is an isomorphism.

### Computational conservation test

A nonzero nonnegative composed conservation exists exactly when the LP

\[
CN\alpha=0,\qquad N\alpha\ge0,\qquad
\mathbf 1^\top N\alpha=1
\]

is feasible. Feasibility establishes conservation. Exact infeasibility reports conservation as not established for that composition.

# 2. Algebra of composition and three-sided costs

## 2.1 Associativity, unit and commutativity

### Status: established, conditional on a global interface declaration

For three ledgers, declare all data on the original disjoint union:

1. all identification pairs;
2. all directed exchange columns;
3. all individual and shared capacity rows;
4. all type/unit checks.

Let \(\sim\) be the equivalence relation generated by all identifications. Then both parenthesisations have compartment set canonically isomorphic to

\[
(X_1\sqcup X_2\sqcup X_3)/{\sim}.
\]

Quotient matrices compose, so for every local or interface column,

\[
R_{\rm final}R_{\rm intermediate}d=R_{\rm global}d.
\]

Therefore:

* **Associativity:** established up to canonical relabelling.
* **Unit:** the empty ledger, with empty interface, is a two-sided unit.
* **Commutativity:** established up to swapping the two summands and transporting the entire interface, including exchange orientations and capacity rows.
* **Order of identifications:** the final quotient does not depend on order because it depends only on the equivalence relation generated by \(J_1\cup J_2\).

However, the binary notation by itself is not a total operation. It is a partial operation indexed by an interface. If \(J_2\) is specified only in terms of an intermediate quotient representative, or if a shared capacity row is discarded after the first binary composition, the expression is under-specified and associativity is not established.

## 2.2 Three-part interface correction

Let \(p_i=\lambda_i^\top G_i\), and let \(r_\varphi\) denote the actual coefficient of \(f_\varphi\) in the summed barrier calculation. For a feasible interface set \(\mathcal U\), the exact interface relaxation is its support function:

\[
\boxed{
T\,\sigma_{\mathcal U}(r)
  =T\max_{f\in\mathcal U}r^\top f .
}
\]

If the interface is the Cartesian box

\[
\mathcal U=\prod_\varphi[0,\bar v_\varphi],
\]

then

\[
\sigma_{\mathcal U}(r)
 =\sum_\varphi r_\varphi^+\bar v_\varphi.
\]

There is no additional many-sided term in this rectangular case. If exchanges share capacities or are coupled by balance equations, the support function need not separate, and the edgewise sum is only an upper bound.

### Three-part witness

### Status: illustrative

Take three exchanges in a cycle with coefficient vector

\[
r=(1,1,-2),\qquad 0\le f_{12},f_{23},f_{31}\le1.
\]

Without balance coupling,

\[
\max r^\top f=2,
\]

attained at \((1,1,0)\). The box-dual witness is \(\beta=(1,1,0)\).

If each part is steady and has only the cycle exchanges, balance gives

\[
f_{12}=f_{23}=f_{31}.
\]

Then \(r^\top f=0\) for every feasible circulation, so the exact value is zero. With

\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\\0&1&-1\end{bmatrix},
\]

the equality-dual witness \(\alpha=(1,2)\) satisfies

\[
A^\top\alpha=(1,1,-2)=r,
\]

and therefore \(r^\top f=\alpha^\top Af=0\).

Thus there is no new three-sided “residual” to add. There can instead be a strict many-sided tightening caused by coupling rows.

# 3. Orientation and sign of the interface price

### Status: established under the declared outflow-negative convention

Let an exchange drain donor \(a\) and enter receiver \(b\). Its incidence is

\[
d_\varphi=-e_a+e_b.
\]

Writing \(p_i=\lambda_i^\top G_i\), its coefficient in the summed barrier derivative is

\[
r_\varphi=p_{\rm recv,b}-p_{\rm pay,a}.
\]

Definition 35 defines

\[
\pi_\varphi=p_{\rm pay,a}-p_{\rm recv,b},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{r_\varphi=-\pi_\varphi.}
\]

Consequently, for \(0\le f_\varphi\le\bar v_\varphi\), the interface surcharge supplied by the stated certificate algebra is

\[
\boxed{(-\pi_\varphi)^+\bar v_\varphi T,}
\]

not \(\pi_\varphi^+\bar v_\varphi T\).

> “the interface is free iff all \(\pi_\varphi\le0\)”

**Disagreement — not established.** Under the stated direction convention, the zero-surcharge condition is

\[
\pi_\varphi\ge0.
\]

> “budget \(B_1+B_2+\sum_\varphi\pi_\varphi^+\bar v_\varphi T\)”

**Disagreement — not established under the donor-outflow convention.** The sign is reversed.

## Reversing direction

If a one-way edge \(a\to b\), with a fresh box \([0,L]\), is replaced physically by the edge \(b\to a\), then

\[
\pi'=-\pi.
\]

In general,

\[
\pi^+LT\ne(-\pi)^+LT.
\]

For example, \(p_a=2,p_b=1\) gives a manuscript cost \(LT\) in one physical direction and zero in the reverse direction. Thus physical reversal is not cost-invariant.

### Orientation-covariant convention

Choose an arbitrary reference orientation and use a signed flow \(f\). Define

\[
d=e_{\rm head}-e_{\rm tail},\qquad r=p^\top d.
\]

The interface correction is

\[
\sigma_{\mathcal U}(r)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal U}r^\top f.
\]

Under a mere coordinate reversal,

\[
d'=-d,\qquad r'=-r,\qquad f'=-f,\qquad
\mathcal U'=-\mathcal U.
\]

Then

\[
\sigma_{\mathcal U'}(r')=\sigma_{\mathcal U}(r).
\]

For a physical one-way box \([0,L]\), coordinate reversal produces \([-L,0]\), not another \([0,L]\). Resetting the box to \([0,L]\) changes the physical model.

# 4. Tightness and the exact LP

## 4.1 The supplied numerical instance is incomplete

### Status: established

The displayed data

\[
G_1=G_2=1,\quad \lambda_1=2,\quad \lambda_2=1,\quad
B_1+B_2=9,\quad T=30
\]

do not specify:

* \(S_1,S_2,B_1,B_2\) as matrices;
* the service readout vector \(c\);
* the split of the initial margins;
* primitive service boxes;
* whether exchange is optional or required;
* steady-state, terminal-state or other fibre-product rows;
* shared capacity rows.

Therefore the exact joint value function is not determined by the quoted instance. Different admissible ledgers with all the displayed scalar data can have different exact optima.

The appropriate finite-dimensional standard form is

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize} & c^\top z\\
\text{subject to} & Az=d,\\
                  & Hz\le h,\\
                  & z\ge0,
\end{array}
\tag{P}
\]

where \(A\) contains typed balances and exchange couplings and \(H\) contains boxes, margin budgets and shared capacities. Its dual is

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{minimize} & d^\top\alpha+h^\top\beta\\
\text{subject to} & A^\top\alpha+H^\top\beta\ge c,\\
                  & \beta\ge0,\quad \alpha\ \text{free}.
\end{array}
\tag{D}
\]

A compositional certificate is one feasible point of (D). It is exact only when it is dual-optimal and a complementary primal point exists.

For a finite-horizon stock problem, imposing \(Sv=0\) is an additional steady-state declaration. Normally one instead introduces terminal stock variables and writes the integrated balance as an equality.

## 4.2 What is computable from the printed price and box

With the donor having \(p=2\) and the receiver \(p=1\), the actual interface coefficient is \(-1\). Let \(F=\int_0^T f\,dt\), so \(0\le F\le K=LT\). The exact interface support LP is

\[
\max\{-F:0\le F\le K\}=0.
\]

Its dual is

\[
\min K\gamma\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \gamma\ge-1,\quad \gamma\ge0,
\]

with witness \(\gamma=0\). Thus the interface alone adds zero under the donor-outflow convention.

If one instead inserts the manuscript coefficient \(+1\), the box-only LP is

\[
\max\{F:0\le F\le K\}=K,
\]

with dual witness \(\gamma=1\). This proves only tightness of the rectangular support relaxation, not attainability by a ledger trajectory.

## 4.3 An admissible completion with a strict gap

### Status: illustrative; all reported values are exact for the following declared completion

\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize}&2V_1+V_2\\
\text{subject to}
 &V_1+F\le4,\\
 &V_2-F\le1,\\

\tag{P\(_K\)}

Here

B_1+B_2=2(4)+1(1)=9.

The primal witness \((V_1,V_2,F)=(4,1,0)\) has value \(9\). The dual is

\begin{array}{ll}

 &\alpha,\beta,\gamma\ge0.
\end{array}

The dual witness

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(2,1,0)

has value \(9\). Hence

# 5. Completeness of the certificate search

## 5.1 Exact LP completeness for the terminal affine relaxation

### Status: established, conditional on a nonempty finite-dimensional LP

Let

\[
h=m(x(0))+\int_0^T Gb\,dt,\qquad V=\int_0^T v\,dt.
\]

Retaining only terminal affine margins gives the primal LP

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize}&c^\top V\\
\text{subject to}&-GS_{\mathcal T}V\le h,\\
&V\ge0.
\end{array}
\tag{A}
\]

Its dual is

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{minimize}&h^\top\lambda\\
\text{subject to}
&-S_{\mathcal T}^\top G^\top\lambda\ge c,\\
&\lambda\ge0,
\end{array}
\]

equivalently

\[
\lambda^\top GS_{\mathcal T}+c^\top\le0.
\]

If (A) is feasible and bounded, LP strong duality proves that the \(\lambda\)-search is complete for this particular terminal-margin relaxation.

It is not complete for all valid budgets of the declared ledger because it omits box rows, balance equalities, shared capacities and pathwise reachability.

### Counterexample

### Status: illustrative

Let

\[
\dot x=-v,\quad x(0)=100,\quad 0\le v\le1,\quad T=1,\quad y=v.
\]

The exact cumulative budget is \(1\). The \(\lambda\)-only condition is

\[
-\lambda+1\le0,
\]

so \(\lambda\ge1\), giving a minimum \(\lambda\)-only bound of \(100\). The full LP dual is

\[
\min 100\lambda+\rho
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\lambda+\rho\ge1,\quad \lambda,\rho\ge0,
\]

and \((\lambda,\rho)=(0,1)\) certifies the exact bound \(1\). The missing multiplier \(\rho\) is the box-row multiplier.

Thus:

* infeasibility of the \(\lambda\)-only search remains “not established,” as required;
* completeness is recovered for a finite polyhedral model only by including multipliers for every defining row.

---

## 5.2 Quadratic barriers and SOS

### Status: established as a tractability boundary

1. **Finite rational polyhedra with linear objectives:** LP; complete dual certificates under feasibility and boundedness. Polynomial-time in the ordinary bit-complexity model.

2. **Convex quadratic programmes:** tractable to declared numerical tolerance under standard regularity. Exact multiplier completeness requires the relevant strong-duality assumptions.

3. **One quadratic implication:** the S-lemma can be exact under its Slater hypothesis.

4. **Several nonconvex quadratic constraints:** general QCQP is NP-hard. Scalar quadratic multipliers are only sufficient.

5. **Fixed-degree SOS multipliers:** coefficient matching and positive-semidefinite Gram constraints give an SDP. A feasible SOS certificate is sufficient, but fixed-degree SOS is not complete for polynomial nonnegativity.

6. **SOS hierarchies:** under an Archimedean compactness condition, Putinar-type hierarchies are asymptotically complete for strictly positive polynomials. Finite convergence is not guaranteed, especially where the target polynomial vanishes on the boundary.

7. **Jointly searching for a barrier and its multipliers:** generally introduces bilinear coefficient products and need not remain convex.

Prop. 37 does not become an iff merely by replacing affine certificates with quadratic or SOS certificates. Even when all local searches are complete, the summed certificate is a restricted joint dual and may omit balance or shared-capacity multipliers. An iff is obtained only from the full joint primal-dual pair with strong duality.

## Interval theorem

### Status: established

Let \(\mathcal F(z)\) be nonempty and connected, and let

\[
\tau:\mathcal F(z)\to\mathbb R
\]

be continuous. Then

\[
\mathrm T(z)=\tau(\mathcal F(z))
\]

is an interval because a continuous image of a connected space is connected, and connected subsets of \(\mathbb R\) are intervals.

If \(\mathcal F(z)\) is also compact, then

\[
\mathrm T(z)=[\tau_{\min}(z),\tau_{\max}(z)]
\]

and both endpoints are attained. Monotonicity along the fibre is unnecessary.

---

## Polyhedral endpoint algorithm

Suppose

\[
\mathcal F(z)=\{x:Ax\le b,\ Ex=z\}
\]

is a nonempty rational polytope and

\[
\tau(x)=\min_{i=1,\ldots,r}g_i(a_i^\top x+d_i),
\]

where each declared \(g_i\) is continuous and strictly increasing.

For the lower endpoint, solve \(r\) LPs:

\[
q_i=\min_{x\in\mathcal F(z)}(a_i^\top x+d_i),
\qquad
\tau_{\min}=\min_i g_i(q_i).
\]

For the upper endpoint, feasibility at a proposed \(t\) is the LP system

\[
x\in\mathcal F(z),\qquad
a_i^\top x+d_i\ge g_i^{-1}(t)
\quad\forall i.
\]

Thus \(\tau_{\max}\) is obtained by one-dimensional bisection using LP feasibility. If all \(g_i\) are a common \(g\), one LP suffices:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize}&q\\
\text{subject to}&x\in\mathcal F(z),\\
&q\le a_i^\top x+d_i,\quad i=1,\ldots,r,
\end{array}
\]

followed by \(\tau_{\max}=g(q^*)\).

The rational LPs have polynomial bit complexity. Bisection to declared additive tolerance \(\varepsilon\) requires \(O(\log((U-L)/\varepsilon))\) LP feasibility calls.

Quasi-concavity alone gives convex superlevel feasibility for the maximum, but it does not turn the minimum of a general quasi-concave function into an LP. The finite minimum-of-monotone-affine representation above is the additional computational hypothesis.

For the manuscript instance, the endpoint programmes give

\[
\tau_{\min}=0,\qquad
\tau_{\max}=\log 50,
\]

so

\[
\mathrm T(z)=[0,\log 50].
\]

The decimal \(3.9120\) is illustrative rounding.

# 7. Stochastic extension

## 7.1 Stochastic fibre

### Status: established, conditional on a fully declared stochastic kernel

For an initial state \(x_0\), define the exit time

\[
\tau_{x_0}=\inf\{t\ge0:\text{some declared barrier is violated}\}.
\]

The stochastic analogue of \(\mathrm T(z)\) is the set of exit-time laws

\[
\mathscr T(z)=
\left\{
\operatorname{Law}(\tau_{x_0}):
x_0\in\mathcal F(z)
\right\},
\]

with any additional declared noise scenarios included in the fibre. Law-identifiability means that \(\mathscr T(z)\) is a singleton.

The mean is identified exactly when all laws in \(\mathscr T(z)\) are integrable and

\[
\left\{
\mathbb E_\nu[\tau]:\nu\in\mathscr T(z)
\right\}
\]

is a singleton. This is weaker than identifying the full law, but it is not implied by observing the aggregate record.

The deterministic example already supplies a counterexample: two hidden starts inducing the same aggregate \(100e^{-t}\) give point-mass exit laws at different times and hence different means.

The mean is identified only under an additional condition such as:

* the exit event is measurable from the aggregate process itself; or
* the conditional exit-time law is invariant over the fibre.

---

## 7.2 Transfer-noise discipline

If \(A\) is the total of a closed conserved moiety, then

\[
dA=-b\,dt+\sigma\,dW
\]

is admissible only with \(\sigma=0\). For \(\sigma\ne0\), the diffusion must be:

* an explicitly declared boundary transfer, or
* one side of a zero-sum transfer with a compensating pool.

Otherwise it is a type error. The process should also be stopped or otherwise treated at the nonnegative boundary.

---

## 7.3 Hitting-time comparison

Let

\[
A_t=a-\int_0^t b_s\,ds+\sigma W_t,\qquad
a>0,\qquad
0<b_{\min}\le b_s\le b_{\max}.
\]

Couple it with

\[
A_t^{\max}=a-b_{\max}t+\sigma W_t,\qquad
A_t^{\min}=a-b_{\min}t+\sigma W_t.
\]

Pathwise,

\[
A_t^{\max}\le A_t\le A_t^{\min}.
\]

For the first hitting time of zero,

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{b_{\max}}\le \tau_b\le\tau_{b_{\min}}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
}
\]

Therefore the CDFs satisfy

\[
F_{b_{\max}}(t)\ge F_b(t)\ge F_{b_{\min}}(t).
\]

For constant \(b>0\), the hitting-time density is

\[
f_b(t)=
\frac{a}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(a-bt)^2}{2\sigma^2t}\right],
\qquad t>0,
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E[\tau_b]=\frac ab.
\]

Under the optional-stopping integrability conditions,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{a}{b_{\max}}
\le\mathbb E[\tau_b]
\le\frac{a}{b_{\min}}.
}
\]

The deterministic quantities \(a/b_{\max}\) and \(a/b_{\min}\) bracket the mean, not the support of the law. If \(\sigma>0\), the density is positive for every \(t>0\), so there is positive probability both before \(a/b_{\max}\) and after \(a/b_{\min}\). A claim that the random passage time itself lies between the two deterministic horizons is therefore not established.

The extreme stochastic comparison is strict in law when \(a>0\) and \(b_{\max}>b_{\min}\). A deterministic pathwise horizon bracket is valid when \(\sigma=0\).

# 8. Closure dual prices and substitution

## 8.1 What the closure dual price establishes

### Status: established, conditional on a feasible bounded closure LP

Write a closure LP in standard form as

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize}&q^\top w\\
\text{subject to}&Aw=d,\\
&Hw\le \bar v,\\
&w\ge0,
\end{array}
\]

where \(q^\top w=\mu\), \(A\) contains balances and \(P(v)=\mu D\), and \(H\) selects capacity rows. Its dual is

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{minimize}&d^\top\alpha+\bar v^\top\gamma\\
\text{subject to}&A^\top\alpha+H^\top\gamma\ge q,\\
&\gamma\ge0,\quad \alpha\ \text{free}.
\end{array}
\]

An optimal multiplier \(\gamma_j\) is a supergradient of \(\Lambda^*\) with respect to the declared capacity \(\bar v_j\):

\[
\Lambda^*(\bar v+\Delta)
\le
\Lambda^*(\bar v)+\gamma^\top\Delta.
\]

At a degenerate optimum, \(\gamma_j\) need not be unique. The directional derivative is obtained from the appropriate extremum over the set of optimal dual multipliers.

This is a scenario-conditioned marginal capacity value. It is not by itself a predicate that a moiety is substitutable.

---

## 8.2 Finite price is not sufficient

### Status: established by counterexample

Consider

\[
\max\{\mu:\mu\le C,\ \mu\ge0\}.
\]

At \(C=1\),

\[
\Lambda^*=1,
\]

and the unique capacity dual price is \(1\), which is finite. There is no alternative pathway, so any loss \(C\mapsto1-\varepsilon\) gives

\[
\Lambda^*=1-\varepsilon<1.
\]

Appending an irrelevant second component with an interface having equal barrier potentials gives interface price zero without creating a compensating route. Thus “finite dual price and non-positive interface price” does not imply compensation.

In a finite feasible bounded LP, existence of some finite optimal dual point is routine, so finiteness is nearly vacuous as a structural substitution test.

---

## 8.3 Interface-price sign is not necessary

A positive interface surcharge is a barrier-budget statement, not an admissibility prohibition. Two components can have parallel feasible return routes, so loss of capacity in one is absorbed by spare capacity in the other even when a chosen barrier certificate assigns a positive interface coefficient.

Moreover:

* interface prices depend on the selected, potentially nonunique, barrier certificates;
* closure prices and barrier prices are dual variables of different programmes;
* the corrected no-surcharge sign under Definition 35 is \(\pi\ge0\), not \(\pi\le0\).

Hence the proposed sign condition is neither necessary nor sufficient for closure compensation.

---

## 8.4 Exact computational compensation predicate

Given a declared capacity loss \(r\) and an allowed augmentation polyhedron

\[
\mathcal U=\{\Delta\ge0:R\Delta\le q\},
\]

compensation at the rate of use is established by feasibility of

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{find}&w,\Delta,\mu\\
\text{subject to}
&A w=d,\\
&Pw=\mu D,\\
&\mu\ge1,\\
&0\le Hw\le \bar v-r+M\Delta,\\
&R\Delta\le q,\quad \Delta\ge0,
\end{array}
\]

including all typed interface and lag restrictions. This is an LP for polyhedral declared inputs.

* A feasible point establishes compensation at the declared rate.
* An exact Farkas infeasibility witness establishes the absence of a feasible compensation; the positive predicate is reported as not established.
* Missing interface, lag or capacity data make the predicate not established.

The corresponding Farkas characterization necessarily uses all equality and inequality multipliers, not one capacity price and one interface-price sign.

---

## 8.5 Hypergraph and Definition 21 claim

> “Prop 36/37 plus Def 21 already contain a characterization of weak versus strong readings”

**Disagreement — not established.** Definition 21 is not among the supplied assumptions and cannot be invoked.

Even independently of Definition 21, the same typed substitution hypergraph can have:

* sufficient spare capacity in one declared scenario;
* insufficient capacity in another;
* different lags relative to \(\tau_{\rm use}\);
* different demanded use vectors.

Thus compensation is a property of the fully labelled LP and declared timescale, not of unlabelled hypergraph connectivity alone. Hypergraph reachability can supply a necessary structural path condition, but not the claimed exact characterization.

Also, the capacity calculation does not determine the closure deficit

\[
\delta_m=1-\kappa_m(\tau_{\rm use})
\]

without a declared lag-return model linking \(\kappa_m\) to \(\Lambda^*\).

Accordingly, in Proposition 36 the values

\[
\Lambda^*=0.75,\ 0.50,\ 1.00
\]

are established for the LP

\[
\max\mu\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\mu\le1,\quad \mu\le1,\quad 2\mu\le C,
\]

at \(C=1.5,1,2\), respectively. The accompanying statement “deficit \(0.25\)” is not established from those capacity rows alone because \(\delta_m\) was defined through \(\kappa_m(\tau_{\rm use})\), not as \(1-\Lambda^*\).

# Reproducibility script

All numerical examples above are reproduced by this Python 3 script. It requires `numpy` and `scipy`.

```python
import numpy as np
from math import log
from scipy.optimize import linprog

def max_lp(c, A_ub=None, b_ub=None, A_eq=None, b_eq=None, bounds=None):
    c = np.asarray(c, dtype=float)
    if bounds is None:
        bounds = [(0, None)] * len(c)
    res = linprog(
        -c,
        A_ub=None if A_ub is None else np.asarray(A_ub, dtype=float),
        b_ub=None if b_ub is None else np.asarray(b_ub, dtype=float),
        A_eq=None if A_eq is None else np.asarray(A_eq, dtype=float),
        b_eq=None if b_eq is None else np.asarray(b_eq, dtype=float),
        bounds=bounds,
        method="highs",
    )
    assert res.success, res.message
    return -res.fun, res.x

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Task 1: one identification destroys both separate lifts
# ------------------------------------------------------------
N_simple = np.array([
    [1, 0],  # a
    [1, 0],  # a'
    [0, 1],  # b
    [0, 1],  # b'
], dtype=float)
C_simple = np.array([[1, 0, -1, 0]], dtype=float)
S_q = np.array([
    [-1, -1],
    [ 1,  0],
    [ 0,  1],
], dtype=float)

print("Task 1 simple rank(CN):",
      np.linalg.matrix_rank(C_simple @ N_simple))
print("Task 1 quotient left-null dimension:",
      S_q.shape[0] - np.linalg.matrix_rank(S_q))

# Full destruction: one identification plus one exchange
N_full = np.array([
    [1, 0],  # a:  l1 coefficient
    [1, 0],  # a'
    [0, 2],  # b:  l2 coefficient
    [0, 1],  # b'
], dtype=float)
C_full = np.array([
    [1, 0, -1,  0],  # identify a and b
    [0, 1,  0, -1],  # exchange requires equal endpoint weights
], dtype=float)
S_full = np.array([
    [-1, -1,  0],
    [ 1,  0, -1],
    [ 0,  2,  1],
], dtype=float)

print("Task 1 full rank(CN):",
      np.linalg.matrix_rank(C_full @ N_full))
print("Task 1 determinant of composed S:",
      round(np.linalg.det(S_full)))
assert np.linalg.matrix_rank(S_full) == 3

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Task 2: three-part rectangular support versus circulation
# ------------------------------------------------------------
r = np.array([1.0, 1.0, -2.0])

rect_value, rect_f = max_lp(r, bounds=[(0, 1)] * 3)

A_cycle = np.array([
    [1, -1,  0],
    [0,  1, -1],
], dtype=float)
cycle_value, cycle_f = max_lp(
    r, A_eq=A_cycle, b_eq=[0, 0], bounds=[(0, 1)] * 3
)

alpha_cycle = np.array([1.0, 2.0])
assert np.allclose(A_cycle.T @ alpha_cycle, r)

print("Task 2 rectangular support:", rect_value, rect_f)
print("Task 2 circulation-coupled support:", cycle_value, cycle_f)
print("Task 2 equality-dual witness:", alpha_cycle)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Tasks 3 and 4: interface sign and completed finite-horizon LP
# ------------------------------------------------------------
T = 30.0
B = 9.0
pi = 1.0
print("Quoted Lmax arithmetic:", B / (pi * T))

for L in [0.2, 0.5]:
    K = L * T

    # Correct physical residual for donor potential 2 -> receiver potential 1:
    # max -F subject to 0 <= F <= K
    corrected_support, Fcorr = max_lp(
        [-1.0], A_ub=[[1.0]], b_ub=[K]
    )

    # Manuscript-sign box support:
    quoted_support, Fquoted = max_lp(
        [1.0], A_ub=[[1.0]], b_ub=[K]
    )

    # Completed ledger:
    # max 2 V1 + V2
    # V1 + F <= 4
    # V2 - F <= 1
    # F <= K
    A_ub = np.array([
        [1, 0,  1],
        [0, 1, -1],
        [0, 0,  1],
    ], dtype=float)
    b_ub = np.array([4, 1, K], dtype=float)
    c = np.array([2, 1, 0], dtype=float)

    value, primal = max_lp(c, A_ub=A_ub, b_ub=b_ub)

    # Dual witness (alpha, beta, gamma) = (2, 1, 0)
    dual = np.array([2.0, 1.0, 0.0])
    assert np.all(A_ub.T @ dual >= c - 1e-10)
    dual_value = b_ub @ dual
    assert np.isclose(value, dual_value)

    print(f"L={L}, K={K}")
    print("  corrected interface support:", corrected_support, Fcorr)
    print("  manuscript-sign box support:", quoted_support, Fquoted)
    print("  completed LP value/primal:", value, primal)
    print("  dual value/witness:", dual_value, dual)
    print("  stated total bound and gap:", B + K, B + K - value)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Task 5: lambda-only certificate versus full box dual
# ------------------------------------------------------------
# max V subject to V <= 100 and V <= 1
affine_value, affine_x = max_lp(
    [1.0],
    A_ub=[[1.0], [1.0]],
    b_ub=[100.0, 1.0],
)

# 1. Lift lemma

## Quoted claim marked

> **Proposition 36.** “Conservation composes; closure does not.”

The first clause is **not established** as a statement that every separately named conservation of each part composes unless an additional compatibility condition is imposed.

Let \(\Pi\) be the quotient map from the disjoint union of compartments to compartment classes, and let the incidence of the composition be represented after applying \(\Pi\). For a left-null vector \(y\) of the composition, put

\[
p=\Pi^{TTy}y,
\]

so that \(p_1,p_2\) assign the same value to all compartments in one identification class. For every interface column \(\varphi\), standard endpoint incidence gives the condition

\[
p_{\operatorname{tail}(\varphi)}
-
p_{\operatorname{head}(\varphi)}
=0.
\]

Thus the pullback sends a composite left-null vector into

\[
\mathcal U=
\left(
U_1\oplus U_2
\right)
\cap
\ker D_J
\cap
\ker D_\Phi,
\]

where

\[
U_i=\ker(S_i^\top),\qquad
D_J(u_1,u_2)_{(a,b)}=u_1(a)-u_2(b).
\]

This map is always injective: a quotient vector has one value for every identification class. Its image is the compatible product above, not generally the unrestricted product \(U_1\oplus U_2\).

### Exact rank condition

Every left-null vector of each part lifts independently iff

\[
\operatorname{rank}\left(
[D_J\;D_\Phi]\big|_{U_1\oplus U_2}
\right)=0.
\]

More generally,

\[
\dim\ker\!\left([D_J\;D_\Phi]\big|_{U_1\oplus U_2}\right)
=
\dim U_1+\dim U_2-\operatorname{rank}D,
\]

provided the cone constraints admit the corresponding vectors.

The proposed hypothesis “no identification merges compartments belonging to distinct conserved moieties” is not by itself the exact condition. Two moieties may be deliberately paired, but then they persist as one weighted joint moiety rather than as two independent summands. If endpoint compatibility fails, or if an endpoint functional vanishes on one side but not the other, the simple “same moiety” criterion does not decide the rank question.

### Two-donor witness

For each donor take compartments \((a_i,r_i)\) and one cycle incidence

\[
S_i=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&-1\\
-1&1
\end{bmatrix},
\]

whose positive left-null cone is \((\alpha,\alpha)\), \(\alpha\ge0\). Impose one identification \(J=\{(a_1,a_2)\}\). Compatibility is

\[
\alpha-\beta=0.
\]

The unrestricted product of the two part null spaces has dimension \(2\), while the compatible quotient space has dimension \(1\). Thus neither of the two original conservation summands is preserved independently; a fused common conservation remains.

If “both conservations destroyed” means *no nontrivial nonnegative left-null vector*, then that stronger requested witness is impossible from one pure identification equality. One scalar equality reduces the unrestricted product dimension by at most one. If one endpoint functional is zero, the other side may remain; if both endpoint functionals are nonzero, their positive rays can be scaled to satisfy the equality. Therefore at least one candidate generally remains. The supplied definition does not support the stronger destruction conclusion.

**Predicate status:** established as a counterexample to unrestricted lift; the requested stronger destruction conclusion is not established.

# 2. Is composition a calculus?

## 2.1 Unit and commutativity

Define the canonical neutral interface

\[
\Theta_{\varnothing}=(\varnothing,\varnothing,\varnothing).
\]

The empty ledger is a left/right unit for this neutral interface because the quotient, incidence, boxes, margins, and readouts are unchanged. An arbitrary declared interface is of course not a unit.

Commutativity holds up to relabelling iff swapping the two sides is accompanied by swapping the ordered endpoint roles of every exchange and reversing the corresponding interface incidence columns. If side order is part of the public interface data, commutativity is conditional on that swap isomorphism; otherwise the incidence signs do not match.

## 2.2 Associativity

Unconditional associativity is not established by Definition 34. Interface data designate the scopes in which endpoints are declared. Two nesting orders are isomorphic only after flattening:

1. flatten all identification pairs to original component endpoints;
2. take the same equivalence relation generated by those pairs;
3. require the same exchange endpoint incidence vectors and boxes;
4. require the same declared shared-capacity constraints.

Under this **flattened-interface coherence** predicate,

\[
L_1\oplus_{\Theta_{1,23}}
 (L_2\oplus_{\Theta_{23}}L_3)
\cong
(L_1\oplus_{\Theta_{12}}L_2)
 \oplus_{\Theta_{12,3}}L_3
\]

whenever the flattened interfaces coincide. Without that check, changing the order can change the quotient or the admissible exchange boxes.

A computational **coherence check** takes the nested interfaces as input, expands endpoint classes to original endpoints, and compares the flattened equivalence relation and flux data. Its failure mode is **not established**; it must not be reported as dangerous or invalid.

## 2.3 Three-part interface cost

There is no obligatory many-sided residual. For global certificates and each two-endpoint exchange, let the certificate gradient be \(\chi_\varphi\), with the sign chosen according to the declared orientation. \[
(-\chi_\varphi)^{+}\bar v_\varphi T

# 3. Orientation and sign

## Quoted equation marked

> \[
> \pi_\varphi=(\lambda_1^{\top}G_1)_c-(\lambda_2^{\top}G_2)_c.

This formula is convention-dependent unless the interface endpoint order is stored independently of the labels “first ledger” and “second ledger”.

Let \(p_a-p_b=\delta\). Reversing the orientation gives

\[
p_b-p_a=-\delta.

Now \((-\delta)^+\ne \delta^+\) whenever \(\delta\ne0\). Consequently positive-part cost is preserved under the direction reversal only when \(\delta=0\).

5. physically reversing direction creates a different interface and may change its cost.

An absolute-price convention \(|p_a-p_b|\) would be direction-invariant, but it is not merely a relabelling of Proposition 37; it changes the mathematical content of the certificate inequality and would require reproving that theorem.

# 7. Stochastic extension

Under the transfer-noise discipline, write the diffusion as

\[
dX=(Sv+Bu_\partial)\,dt+H\,dW.
\]

For every conserved \(\ell\), admissibility requires either

\[
\ell^\top H=0
\]

for zero-sum internal redistribution, or the nonzero \(\ell^\top H\) term must be declared as a boundary source/sink. A diffusion with neither property is a type error.

The correct stochastic object is not a single interval of real times. It is

\[
\mathrm T^{\rm stoch}(z)
=
\left\{
\operatorname{Law}\bigl(\tau(x_0,\cdot)\bigr):
x_0\in\mathcal F(z)
\right\}.
\]

For mean identification, define the mean map

\[
\bar T(z)=
\left\{
\mathbb E[\tau(x_0)]:
x_0\in\mathcal F(z)
\right\}.
\]

The aggregate record identifies the mean iff \(\bar T(z)\) is a singleton. Full law identification requires the set of laws to be a singleton.

Mean identifiability does **not** follow merely from zero-sum transfer noise. For example, two pools of aggregate \(100\) and component barrier \(1\) can have allocations \((80,20)\) and \((50,50)\). Transfer noise can preserve their sum, but their first component-barrier times still differ. Hence the aggregate record brackets the candidate means unless a further theorem proves that all candidate trajectories induce the same exit-time mean.

## Hitting-time bracket

Let the remaining amount above the passage boundary be

\[
A(t)=A_0-bt+\sigma W(t),
\]

with boundary distance \(\Delta=A_0-a>0\) and

\[
0<b_{\min}\le b_{\min}(t),b_{\max}.
\]

On one common Brownian coupling,

\[
\tau_{b_{\max}}\le \tau_b\le\tau_{b_{\min}}
\quad\text{a.s.}.
\]

Thus the passage-time law is stochastically bracketed by the high- and low-drift laws; they are not generally deterministic horizons when \(\sigma>0\).

For constant \(b>0,\sigma>0\), the hitting law is inverse Gaussian:

\[
f_b(t)=
\frac{\Delta}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{(\Delta-bt)^2}{2\sigma^2t}
\right],
\]

with

\[
\mathbb E\tau_b=\frac{\Delta}{b}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\frac{\Delta}{b_{\max}}
\le
\mathbb E\tau_b
\le
\frac{\Delta}{b_{\min}}.
\]

The bracket is strict pathwise when \(b_{\min}<b_{\max}\) and the actual drift is strictly interior. If the actual drift is identically one endpoint, equality can occur; if it is stochastic, strictness in expectation holds when its law puts positive mass away from the relevant endpoint used for equality. If \(b_{\min}\le0\), the lower/upper bound can involve infinite passage and is not established without a reaching condition.

**Predicate status:** established as the appropriate stochastic object; aggregate mean identifiability is conditional on singleton mean. The inverse-Gaussian formula is scenario-conditioned.

# 8. Dual prices and substitution

Take \(g=Bu_\partial\) and write the closure LP as

\[
\Lambda(\bar v)=
\max \mu

subject to

\[
Sv+g=0,\qquad
Pv-\mu D=0,\qquad

\[
\min
\rho^\top\bar v-\alpha^\top g

subject to

\[
S^\top\alpha+P^\top\eta\le\rho,\qquad
D^\top\eta=1,\qquad
\rho\ge0,
\]

with \(\alpha,\eta\) free. Under feasibility and qualification, strong duality holds.

For a declared capacity-perturbation direction \(h\),

\[
\left.
\frac{\partial \Lambda(\bar v+r h)}
{\partial r}
\right|_{r=0}
=
\left\{
\rho^\top h:
(\alpha,\eta,\rho)\in\mathrm{DualOpt}
\right\}
\]

when the parametric LP satisfies the usual qualification conditions. With a unique optimal dual solution, this set is a scalar.

A numerical dual coordinate is computationally meaningful only as a **capacity-sensitivity statement**. It is a programme with the closure LP and perturbation direction as inputs; failure modes are infeasibility, degeneracy with multiple duals, or lack of a declared perturbation map. Those failures are reported as “not established”.
