Option 3 is generally acceptable for formal journal publication**, especially for a HESS/EGU-style venue. Formal publication does **not** require an agentless, machine-smooth register. Tiered accessibility—human voice throughout, an accessible front layer in §1 and boxes, and specialist §§2–5—is a legitimate and often effective architecture for interdisciplinary work.

But I would not treat Option 3 as a complete strategy on its own. The strongest version is **Option 3 + Option 4**: tiered accessibility plus referee-proofing. That means:

- **Check journal compliance first.** Confirm that boxes, first-person “we,” and a plain-language/accessible front layer fit the journal’s author guidelines and LaTeX template. If boxes are not supported, use a “Plain language summary,” “Key points,” or clearly marked conceptual subsection instead.
- **Keep the technical core self-contained.** §§2–5 must still stand on their own for specialists. Boxes and §1 should aid interpretation, not carry essential methods, caveats, or evidence.
- **Define “human voice” carefully.** Active voice, authorial judgement, varied rhythm, and fewer contrast frames are fine. Chatty, jokey, rhetorical, or over-opinionated prose is not. Use voice for framing, interpretation, and transitions; keep methods and results neutral and precise.
- **Do not let accessibility dilute caveats.** Put limitations, uncertainty, and scope conditions early and in the main text—not only in boxes or the accessible layer. Referees will check novelty, evidence, reproducibility, and over-claiming.
- **Make boxes few and optional.** They should be genuinely useful, self-contained, and non-essential to the specialist argument.
- **Keep the abstract and conclusions precise.** The accessible front layer can be readable, but the abstract, results, and conclusions still need formal precision and no over-claiming.

So: **yes, Option 3 is publishable and often preferable for HESS-like journals.** But if you want to minimise referee risk, combine it with Option 4. If forced to choose one, Option 3 is acceptable; Option 4 is safer. The best formal-journal answer is usually: **tiered readability + rigorous referee-proofing.** Formal does not mean dull; it means precise, evidenced, and well-judged.
