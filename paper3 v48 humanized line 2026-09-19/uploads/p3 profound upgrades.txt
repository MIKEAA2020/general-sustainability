astra:
The strongest improvement is to move from **a framework that prevents invalid claims** to **a framework that computes the strongest claim the evidence supports—and the action that remains possible**.

That means making three outputs central:

1. **Identifiable depletion horizons:** what can actually be inferred from the observations?
2. **Substitution-impossibility certificates:** can any declared material pathway sustain required service without exhausting a critical margin?
3. **Latest safe intervention deadlines:** how long can action be postponed before a service-preserving transition becomes infeasible?

These additions reuse the existing ledger. They do not require a new Earth-system model.

## 1. Add event identifiability: a correctly calculated number may answer an unidentifiable physical question

The present taxonomy distinguishes mathematical objects. The deeper contribution would establish **when one object can legitimately be promoted into another**.

### Add an observation layer

Separate physical states from reported quantities:

\[
\dot x=S_Tv+b,\qquad v\ge0,
\]

\[
z=\mathcal H(x,\eta)+\varepsilon.
\]

Here:

- \(x\) contains physical stocks;
- \(z\) contains observations or published quantities;
- \(\eta\) contains reference datums, economic classifications, prices, knowledge, or other reporting parameters.

For a smooth observation map,

\[
\dot z
=
\mathcal H_x\dot x+\mathcal H_\eta\dot\eta.
\]

The first term represents physical change. The second represents changing classification or representation. Economic reserve reclassification may require explicit jump terms rather than this smooth formula.

This makes the reserves problem precise: **a change in reported reserves need not be a material inflow**.

### A useful identifiability proposition

Let \(\Omega(D)\) be the set of admissible physical reconstructions compatible with data \(D\). A physical hitting time \(T\) is point-identifiable from those data exactly when

\[
T(\omega_1)=T(\omega_2)
\qquad
\text{for every }\omega_1,\omega_2\in\Omega(D).
\]

The useful result is not this abstract definition alone. It is the construction of **indistinguishable observations with different physical horizons**.

#### Witness A: anomalies do not identify an uncalibrated physical margin

Suppose the observation is

\[
z(t)=A(t)-A_{\mathrm{ref}},
\]

the reference level is unknown, and the declared pre-event future drift is

\[
\dot A=-\delta,\qquad \delta>0.
\]

If no independent constraint removes the translation freedom, then

\[
A_\kappa(t)=A(t)+\kappa,\qquad
A_{\mathrm{ref},\kappa}=A_{\mathrm{ref}}+\kappa
\]

produces exactly the same observations. But the time to a fixed physical barrier \(A_{\min}\) is

\[
T_\kappa
=
\frac{A(0)+\kappa-A_{\min}}{\delta}.
\]

Thus even a perfectly known trend does not identify the physical horizon.

**The practical implication:** a longer or more precise anomaly record cannot resolve an unobserved stock-to-barrier distance. A different kind of measurement is needed.

Anomalies are not intrinsically unsuitable. A physical barrier calibrated in the same anomaly coordinates can supply the missing distance. A record-minimum crossing can also be identifiable—but remains a different event unless that minimum is independently linked to physical failure.

#### Witness B: exact aggregate dynamics do not preserve component failure times

Take two stocks and their receiving sinks:

\[
\dot x_i=-kx_i,\qquad \dot w_i=kx_i.
\]

Each stock–sink pair conserves material and remains nonnegative. The aggregate stock

\[
Z=x_1+x_2
\]

has the exact closed dynamics

\[
\dot Z=-kZ.
\]

Compare initial conditions

\[
(x_1,x_2)=(2,98)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(50,50),
\]

with both critical lower barriers equal to \(1\).

Both systems produce the identical aggregate trajectory

\[
Z(t)=100e^{-kt}.
\]

Yet their first critical-stock hitting times are

\[
T_1=\frac{\log 2}{k},
\qquad
T_2=\frac{\log 50}{k}.
\]

This is stronger than the static noncompensation result:

> Even perfect aggregate observations and exact aggregate dynamics need not identify the time to a componentwise physical event.

For an exact reduced model, **dynamical closure and barrier observability are separate obligations**. This also turns the paper’s particular non-reduction boundary into a reusable test for model interfaces.

### Replace unsupported point forecasts with identification sets

For a declared event and policy \(\pi\), define

\[
T_-(D,\pi)
=
\inf_{\omega\in\Omega(D,\pi)}T(\omega),
\qquad
T_+(D,\pi)
=
\sup_{\omega\in\Omega(D,\pi)}T(\omega).
\]

Then:

- \(T_->H\) certifies that no compatible trajectory reaches the declared barrier by \(H\);
- \(T_+\le H\) certifies that every compatible trajectory reaches it by \(H\), **under that policy**;
- a wide interval identifies unresolved uncertainty;
- an empty compatible set signals model–data inconsistency, not safety.

An algorithm may return certified outer bounds rather than the exact extrema.

**Novelty gain:** the paper becomes a theory of what depletion evidence identifies, not merely a taxonomy of misleading terminology.

## 2. Turn noncompensation into a constructive certificate against substitution escape

The existing noncompensation theorem establishes a limitation. The more consequential next step is to determine whether **any combination of declared substitutions can sustain service within the barriers**.

A useful result follows directly from the ledger.

### A critical-margin budget theorem

Write the fixed affine barrier margins as

\[
m(x)=Gx+a\ge0.
\]

These can include:

- lower-stock margins \(x_i-\ell_i\);
- upper-waste margins \(u_j-x_j\);
- other declared affine constraints.

Let a declared service rate be

\[
y=c^\top v,\qquad c\ge0.
\]

Suppose there exists \(\lambda\ge0\) such that, componentwise,

\[
\boxed{\lambda^\top GS_T+c^\top\le0.}
\]

The coefficients must be typed: \(\lambda_j\) converts margin \(j\) into cumulative service units.

Define

\[
V(x)=\lambda^\top m(x).
\]

Along any admissible trajectory,

\[
\dot V
=
\lambda^\top GS_Tv+\lambda^\top Gb
\le
-y+\lambda^\top Gb.
\]

Consequently, every trajectory remaining inside the declared barriers satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^T y(t)\,dt
\le
V(x_0)+\int_0^T\lambda^\top Gb(t)\,dt.
}
\]

The proof is simply integration, using \(V(x(T))\ge0\).

If required service obeys \(y(t)\ge y_{\mathrm{req}}\), while the declared boundary contribution satisfies

\[
\lambda^\top Gb(t)\le\beta<y_{\mathrm{req}},
\]

then adequate, barrier-respecting operation cannot continue beyond

\[
\boxed{
T\le
\frac{V(x_0)}{y_{\mathrm{req}}-\beta}.
}
\]

### Why this is a substantial upgrade

The inequality holds regardless of how the nonnegative primitive fluxes are mixed. It therefore covers **all substitutions represented by the declared process columns**, not just one selected scenario.

It makes the dynamical productivity illusion quantitative:

> When service exceeds replenishment of a certified resource potential, the excess service is backed by liquidation of critical stock margins or remaining sink capacity.

It also produces an auditable obstruction:

- which support stocks are indispensable;
- which waste capacities are limiting;
- which purported substitutes merely transfer the drawdown;
- how much cumulative service the remaining margins can support.

The multiplier search is a linear-programming problem. It is the dual of a cumulative-service stoichiometric relaxation. Additional capacity constraints can tighten that relaxation.

Two qualifications are essential:

1. **Finding a witness proves a limitation; failing to find one proves nothing about safety.**
2. **Feasibility of an endpoint or stoichiometric relaxation does not prove that a kinetically and thermodynamically realizable trajectory exists.**

This is also **not** a scalar safety certificate. \(V>0\) does not establish that every margin is positive. The argument assumes componentwise safety and derives a limit on how long adequate service can coexist with it.

### Strengthen the finite-donor theorem by stating what is actually finite

Finite total inventory alone does not bound gross cumulative throughput. A closed loop can circulate material indefinitely:

\[
A\rightleftarrows N.
\]

Both gross transfer integrals can diverge while total material remains constant.

Thus the extraction-integrability result needs its specific no-return structure, or a resource potential that decreases with extraction. It should not be generalized from finite inventory alone.

The theorem above supplies that generalization correctly:

> Cumulative activity is bounded when each unit of that activity consumes a finite, nonreplenished resource potential—not merely because the system contains finite mass.

**Novelty gain:** a familiar warning about substitution becomes a computable, pathway-dependent impossibility certificate.

## 3. Make the central decision object the latest safe intervention time

A hitting time describes what happens along a scenario. It does not necessarily describe how much time remains to act.

A system can be:

- currently inside every barrier;
- several years from physical depletion;
- already outside the set from which any admissible transition can preserve both safety and required service.

That is the mathematical version of your elevator image: measure the loss of repair options while the cable still holds, without claiming to predict the snap.

### Distinguish trajectory safety from transition feasibility

The operational question is

\[
\exists\ \text{one implementable policy}
\quad
\forall\ \text{declared compatible disturbances}:
\]

\[
m(x(t))\ge0,
\qquad
y(t)\ge y_{\mathrm{req}}.
\]

The quantifier order matters. It is not sufficient that every scenario has some different successful policy. Nor is it sufficient that each compartment has a separately favorable policy if those policies conflict.

### An exact benchmark linking stock margins, delay, and deployment

Let a critical support stock have current margin

\[
m_0=A_0-A_{\min}>0
\]

and current **net drawdown** \(d_0>0\):

\[
\dot A=-d.
\]

Assume:

1. Required service must be maintained.
2. A verified replacement pathway can eliminate the net drawdown without violating another declared critical barrier.
3. No reduction in drawdown is possible during an unavoidable delay \(\tau\).
4. Subsequently, drawdown can decrease no faster than \(\rho>0\), and that maximum reduction rate is actually achievable.
5. Once \(d=0\), the declared service-preserving terminal regime can continue.

The least possible additional support-stock loss is

\[
\boxed{
m_{\mathrm{needed}}
=
d_0\tau+\frac{d_0^2}{2\rho}.
}
\]

The first term is loss during the delay. The second is the triangular area under the fastest feasible drawdown-reduction path.

Under these assumptions, a transition respecting \(A\ge A_{\min}\) exists exactly when

\[
\boxed{
m_0\ge d_0\tau+\frac{d_0^2}{2\rho}.
}
\]

Use strict inequality if touching the barrier counts as failure.

If action is postponed for an additional time \(L\), the latest feasible start is

\[
\boxed{
L_{\max}
=
\frac{m_0}{d_0}
-\tau
-\frac{d_0}{2\rho}.
}
\]

Compare this with the frozen-rate ratio

\[
H^{\mathrm{loc}}=\frac{m_0}{d_0}.
\]

The difference is the physical cost of response delay and finite deployment speed.

### Numerical illustration—not an empirical estimate

Suppose

\[
m_0=100,\qquad
d_0=10\ \text{units/year},
\]

\[
\tau=2\ \text{years},\qquad
\rho=1\ \text{unit/year}^2.
\]

The local depletion ratio is ten years. But

\[
L_{\max}=10-2-5=3\ \text{years}.
\]

**Ten years of stock coverage can mean only three years left to begin the transition.**

If \(L_{\max}<0\), the stock can still be above its barrier while the declared service-preserving transition is already infeasible.

This is a benchmark, not a universal law. State-dependent regeneration, uncertain disturbances, competing stocks, and nonlinear deployment require corresponding extensions. If the replacement ramp is only an upper bound on capability, rather than an achievable pathway, the margin condition is necessary but not sufficient.

Importantly, \(\tau\) and \(\rho\) can enter as declared institutional-interface assumptions. No exact reduction of the physical ledger to the companion delay system is required.

**Impact gain:** the framework supplies a decision deadline rather than another countdown to depletion.

## 4. Tighten several headline claims before enlarging the novelty claim

These corrections will make the contribution harder to dismiss.

### A. Separate proof obligations without claiming universal logical independence

Distinct predicates can have implication relationships.

For example,

\[
LS_T=0,
\qquad
\dot x=S_Tv+b
\]

imply

\[
L\dot x=Lb.
\]

Similarly, if the declared safe set includes nonnegativity of every stock, safety of a trajectory implies positivity of that trajectory.

The right architecture is therefore a **proof-dependency graph**, not an assertion that every predicate is logically independent of every other predicate.

Keep separate:

- model structure;
- trajectory accounting;
- conservation;
- positivity or orthant invariance;
- thermodynamic admissibility;
- declared barrier safety;
- service adequacy.

A missing thermodynamic certificate should be marked **not established**, not silently inherited from stoichiometric conservation.

### B. Narrow the noncompensation claim—and provide a usable scalar alternative

The defensible universal claim concerns fixed compensatory linear aggregation over a sufficiently unrestricted multidimensional domain.

It is not true that no scalar can certify componentwise adequacy. For positive normalization scales \(s_i\),

\[
S_{\min}(b)=\min_i\frac{b_i}{s_i}
\]

satisfies

\[
S_{\min}(b)\ge0
\iff
b_i\ge0\quad\forall i.
\]

Its sign is independent of the positive normalization choices; its magnitude and bottleneck ranking are not.

Thus the practical replacement is:

- retain the vector for diagnosis;
- publish a noncompensatory minimum-margin summary if needed;
- name the binding component;
- attach the relevant horizon or transition certificate.

This certifies instantaneous adequacy only, not future sustainability.

### C. Treat rate closure as a physical mechanism, not a replacement definition of weak and strong sustainability

Weak/strong sustainability, material-cycle closure, and critical-stock viability are related but different distinctions.

A stronger positioning is:

> The ledger identifies when proposed substitutions are physically available, sufficiently fast, and compatible with critical-stock constraints.

That connects to the established debate without requiring readers to accept new definitions of its principal terms.

Likewise, revise “recoverability is a property of the rate” to:

> Recoverability depends on the current state, accessible restoration pathways, and allowed time. Rate determines whether those pathways can keep pace.

This accommodates hysteresis, irreversible losses, and deployment constraints.

### D. Classify legitimate uses before criticizing stronger interpretations

For example,

\[
\frac{\log(B/B_{\min})}{F}
\]

is an exact hitting time under the declared model \(\dot B=-FB\) with constant \(F\). It is not generally a demographic hitting time once recruitment, growth, and other mortality are included.

Similarly, reserves/production can be a useful coverage statistic without being a geological exhaustion forecast.

The stronger contribution is a **promotion rule**: identify the extra observations and assumptions needed to support the stronger interpretation.

Also keep dimensional types exact: \(g/A\) is an intensity; \(A/g\) is a coverage time.

For Earth Overshoot Day or Meadows, target a specific aggregation rule or unsupported inference—not aggregation or dynamical modeling in themselves.

## 5. Make “proof-carrying” an executable deliverable

Without independently checkable outputs, “proof-carrying” risks sounding like a metaphor.

A useful certificate should contain:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Target | Exactly which physical, statistical, or economic event is being discussed |
| Physical scope | Stocks, species, moieties, boundaries, and omitted compartments |
| Observation map | How reported data constrain physical states and barrier distances |
| Scenario | Policy, disturbances, pathway catalogue, and deployment assumptions |
| Established predicates | Precisely what has been proved |
| Witness | Nullspace identity, flux envelope, dual multiplier, or verified transition |
| Temporal output | Local ratio, conditional event interval, service-duration bound, or intervention deadline |
| Unestablished claims | Explicit unknowns and excluded interpretations |

The checker need not solve every model. It can verify compact witnesses:

- two compatible reconstructions demonstrating non-identification;
- a multiplier satisfying the critical-margin inequalities;
- an interval enclosure establishing barrier avoidance;
- an admissible transition schedule.

Numerical verification should use controlled error bounds where a guarantee is claimed. Model validity, ecological threshold validity, and completeness of the pathway catalogue remain external assumptions.

### Use one full application, not three shallow demonstrations

A groundwater–agriculture application is especially aligned with the productivity illusion:

- observed agricultural service;
- a calibrated groundwater functional barrier;
- recharge and pumping;
- uncertainty in the stock-to-barrier mapping;
- a service-preserving replacement pathway;
- explicit deployment limits;
- accounting for any burden shifted to another compartment.

Show what changes when the missing information is supplied:

1. Does calibrating the physical margin narrow the horizon set?
2. Does a proposed substitute actually remove drawdown, or relocate it?
3. How different is the latest safe intervention time from stock/current decline?
4. Which additional measurement would most improve the decision?

The application should be allowed to conclude **“not identified”**. A justified refusal to publish a physical countdown is itself a result.

Keep the other public indicators as concise classification audits. Move lengthy standard positivity and stochastic first-passage derivations into supporting material unless they directly change one of these decisions.

> **We develop an observation-aware, proof-carrying stock–flow framework that determines which depletion claims are identifiable from available data, certifies limits on adequate service under all declared substitution pathways, and converts critical-stock margins into latest feasible intervention times under finite response speed. ## 0. Where the novelty is currently thin

1. **The noncompensation theorem is linear algebra.** $w^\top b\ge0\nRightarrow b\ge0$, and the "universal failure" construction, will be read by referees as a triviality dressed as a result. It also proves only a negative; it does not say what *is* admissible, and it cannot handle the case the paper itself allows — substitution where a pathway exists.
2. **The fourth predicate (thermodynamic admissibility) is named in §1.1 and never formalized.** The independence claim ("four predicates conflated") is therefore only three-quarters proved.
3. **The paper uses the left null space of $S_T$ (conservation) but not the right null space (steady-state flux cone).** The weak/strong reframing as "closure at the rate of use" is exactly a statement about nonnegative right-null vectors, and it is left verbal.
4. **The classifications of G3P, reserve-life, and removals-only are diagnoses without repairs.** A reader learns what each number is not, but not the minimal declaration that would turn it into a certified object.

## 1. Replace the noncompensation section with an aggregation triad

### T1. Liebig faithfulness theorem (the positive replacement)

Let $b\in\mathbb R^n$ be componentwise balances, $\tau$ declared thresholds, $g:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ continuous. Call $g$ **faithful** if $g(b)\ge0\Rightarrow b\ge\tau$, **complete** if $b\ge\tau\Rightarrow g(b)\ge0$.

- (a) $g$ is faithful and complete iff $\{g\ge0\}=\tau+\mathbb R^n_+$; hence every faithful complete aggregator is sign-equivalent to the Liebig minimum $L_\tau(b)=\min_i (b_i-\tau_i)$.
- (b) For $n\ge2$, **no continuous aggregator strictly increasing in every argument is both faithful and complete.** Every strictly monotone aggregator either conceals a deficit or raises a false alarm.

*Proof of (b).* On the face $\{b_1=\tau_1,\ b_{-1}>\tau_{-1}\}$ completeness gives $g\ge0$ and faithfulness gives $g(b-\varepsilon e_1)<0$; continuity forces $g\equiv0$ on the face, contradicting strict increase in $b_2$. $\square$

Why this is a real upgrade: it is an impossibility theorem with a constructive answer. The admissible scalar summary of a vector ledger is Liebig's law of the minimum (1840) — which lets you say, in one line, that Earth Overshoot Day is not in the admissible sign-class and that its replacement is "the binding component, named, and its margin." It also separates your certification claim from any view on production substitutability, which protects the paper's insistence that weak/strong is about closure, not Cobb–Douglas vs Leontief.

### T2. Substitution-capacity interpolation theorem (weak and strong as endpoints of one predicate)

Declare substitution capacities $\kappa_{ji}\ge0$: the maximal rate at which surplus of component $j$ can be redeployed to cover $i$. Adequacy-after-substitution holds if $\exists\,t\ge0$, $t_{ji}\le\kappa_{ji}$, with $b_i+\sum_j t_{ji}-\sum_j t_{ij}\ge0$ for all $i$.

**Theorem.** Adequacy-after-substitution holds iff for every $U\subseteq[n]$,
$$\sum_{i\in U} b_i \;+\; \sum_{j\notin U,\ i\in U}\kappa_{ji}\;\ge\;0 .$$
(Max-flow/min-cut on a super-source/super-sink network; Gale 1957, Hoffman 1960.)

**Corollaries.** $\kappa\equiv0$ ⇒ the predicate is $b\ge0$ (your vector ledger; strong regime). $\kappa\equiv\infty$ on a connected graph ⇒ the predicate is $\sum_i b_i\ge0$ (your scalar $B=b\cdot M$; weak regime). Every finite $\kappa$ is an intermediate regime, decided by one LP.

This is the theorem the reframing in §1.1 has been asking for. It makes precise three of your verbal claims at once: (i) an *equivalence factor* in any existing index is an undeclared assertion $\kappa_{ji}=\infty$; (ii) a substitute is "a recycled flux returned to the regenerating pool or a non-renewable drawdown on a second compartment" — in the network the second case is an arc leaving a stock compartment, whose drawdown then appears in that compartment's barrier predicate; (iii) "closes at the rate of use" is the requirement that the condition hold per unit time, with $\kappa$ a rate. It also delivers a precise statement about Hartwick's rule and genuine savings: they are the $\kappa=\infty$ corollary, and the cut inequalities are the finite-$\kappa$ correction. Nobody in the weak/strong literature has stated the dichotomy as the two endpoints of a single feasibility predicate; that statement is novel even though the flow theorem is not.

### T3. Closure cone: complete the null-space algebra

The paper derives conservation from left-null vectors of $S_T$. The nonnegative right-null vectors, $K=\{v\ge0:\ S_Tv+B_Tu=0,\ v\le\bar v\}$, are the **elementary closure modes** — flux patterns under which every stock is stationary. Let $P(K)$ be the projection onto use fluxes and $D$ the demanded use vector.

**Theorem.** $D$ closes at the rate of use iff $D\in P(K)$. The closure capacity $\lambda^*=\max\{\lambda:\lambda D\in P(K)\}$ is an LP; $\lambda^*\ge1$ is the weak regime, $\lambda^*<1$ the strong-failure regime, in which every trajectory meeting $D$ has a non-stationary stock and, by conservation, source drawdown equals sink accumulation plus boundary export.

T2 is the special case of a bipartite transfer network. This imports flux-balance analysis (Palsson) into sustainability accounting, turns "circular economy closure" claims into LP-checkable statements, and gives the dynamical productivity illusion a formal home: **yield inflation is exactly demand met outside $P(K)$**, which by T9 below equals the liquidation flux.

## 2. Formalize the fourth predicate

### T4. Exergy admissibility and the relational-waste threshold

For each flux $j$ declare a specific exergy requirement $\omega_j\ge\omega_j^{\min}$ (thermodynamic minimum work), a second-law efficiency $\eta_j$, and a budget $\Omega(t)$. Admissibility: $\sum_j\omega_j v_j\le\Omega$. Adding this to $K$ gives $\lambda^*_\Omega\le\lambda^*$.

For a return flux $\varphi$ from a sink at concentration $c$ to use at $c_{\text{use}}$, $\omega^{\min}=RT\ln(c_{\text{use}}/c)$ per mole (ideal dilute), so return at rate $\varphi$ is admissible only if
$$c\;\ge\;c^*(\Omega,\eta,\varphi)=c_{\text{use}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\eta\,\Omega}{\varphi RT}\right).$$

This does three things. It completes the independence claim: a mass-closing ledger with $c<c^*$ satisfies accounting, conservation, and closure and fails admissibility. It makes "waste is a relational status" quantitative — waste is matter below $c^*$ relative to $(\Omega,\eta,\varphi)$, not an intrinsic property. And it adjudicates the Georgescu-Roegen "fourth law" dispute (Ayres, Bianciardi et al.) without postulating a law: dispersal is a concentration barrier interacting with an exergy budget. Be explicit that $\omega^{\min}$ is a lower bound and that $\eta$ (and processed volume) is where real closure fails; the framework carries both because $\eta$ is declared.

## 3. Depletion arithmetic: from taxonomy to certified objects

### T5. Certified horizon (make "proof-carrying" literal)

Your envelope theorem gives $\underline S_m(t)\le S_m(t)\le\overline S_m(t)$. Define $\underline T=\inf\{t:\underline S(t)\le A_{\min}\}$, $\overline T=\inf\{t:\overline S(t)\le A_{\min}\}$.

**Corollary.** For every trajectory compatible with the declared flux, boundary, and residual bounds, $\underline T\le T\le\overline T$.

Report $[\underline T,\overline T]$ together with the bounds; that pair is the certificate. Under monotone (cooperative) dynamics with box bounds, $\underline T$ is attained (Müller–Kamke comparison). The frozen-rate $H^{\text{loc}}$ lies in the interval iff the constant-drawdown path is compatible with the bounds. This is a *reportable* alternative to the reserve-life ratio that carries a proof, and it unifies §C (envelopes) with §E (horizons), which currently sit apart.

### T6. Sign-definite bias of frozen-rate horizons (sharpen Prop. 17)

Let $\dot A=-f(A)$, $f>0$ on $(A_{\min},A_0]$, so $T=\int_{A_{\min}}^{A_0}dA/f(A)$ and $H=(A_0-A_{\min})/f(A_0)$.

- $f$ nonincreasing in $A$ (drawdown accelerates as stock falls: falling ore grade at constant demand, depensatory regeneration) ⇒ $T\le H$: **frozen-rate is optimistic.**
- $f$ nondecreasing (catch $\propto$ stock, compensatory regeneration) ⇒ $T\ge H$: **frozen-rate is pessimistic.**

One-line proofs; immediately tells a reader which direction each public number errs, given only the sign of $f'$. Prop. 17 becomes the symmetric case when the sign is unknown.

### T7. Anomaly invariance: why the G3P number cannot be a horizon

Any record-relative index $I=F(a)$, $a=S-S_{\text{ref}}$, is invariant under $S\mapsto S+c$; the physical horizon $T(S)$ is not. Hence no $F$ recovers $T$, and $I$ carries no information about the absolute stock. Stronger and more memorable: for a linear-trend-plus-noise series $a_k=-\beta k+\varepsilon_k$, the "fitted distance to record minimum over fitted slope" is $O_p(\sigma/\beta)$ — bounded in probability as the record grows and independent of the stock. **It is an inverse trend-detection statistic in units of years.** That is the rigorous form of "a number in units of years that is not a time to any physical event."

### T8. Positivity unifies the taxonomy

A constant-production flux against a finite reserve is not donor-limited, so its trajectory exits the positive orthant in finite time; **the frozen-rate horizon is precisely the orthant-exit time of a non-donor-limited extrapolation.** This places the reserve-life ratio inside your positivity predicate rather than beside it.

## 4. Stock-side identities that are trivial to prove and nobody states

### T9. Liquidation share — the wear gauge the elevator metaphor promises

Two-pool ledger $\dot A=R(A)-g(X,A)$, yield $Y=Y(g)$. Liquidation flux $\ell=(g-R(A))_+=(-\dot A)_+$; sustainable yield at the current state $Y_s=Y(\min(g,R(A)))$; **liquidation share** $\sigma=(Y-Y_s)/Y$, $=\ell/g$ for linear $Y$. It is identifiable from the support-pool balance and inherits the envelope certificate of T5. This is the discount-rate-free, moiety-typed physical analogue of El Serafy's user cost and of Hicksian income — position it explicitly against both. "Measure the wear while the cable holds" now has a formula.

### T10. Barrier compatibility for a conserved moiety

Closed ledger, total $M$, declared lower barriers $\underline S_k$ and upper barriers $\overline S_k$. A barrier system is realizable iff $\sum_k\underline S_k\le M\le\sum_k\overline S_k$, and the margin identity $(S_1-\underline S_1)+(\overline S_3-S_3)=M-\underline S_1-\overline S_3-S_2$ shows that **depletion and pollution are the same predicate type applied at the two ends of one moiety's cycle.** For phosphorus this makes mine depletion and eutrophication one object; for planetary boundaries it is a joint-feasibility check that compartmental boundaries for the same element are never subjected to.

### T11. Renewability relativity (framing proposition for SEEA)

For any pool, renewability is a predicate on the pair (pool, policy): finite hitting time iff extraction exceeds regeneration on average, infinite iff a positive-extraction policy stays within $\sup R$. # Where the novelty is thin, and the repair strategy

Your self-assessment is honest but it concedes the wrong ground. As written, the paper is exposed to three specific reviewer attacks:

1. **"Formalization of the obvious."** Separation results proved by counterexample (accounting ⇏ conservation ⇏ barrier safety; $w^\top b\ge 0 \nRightarrow b\ge0$) are true, easy, and unsurprising.
2. **"Critique without replacement."** You demote R/P, G3P, removals-only time, and Earth Overshoot Day, but offer as replacement an object (scenario-conditioned hitting time) that requires far more data than any of them. Nobody switches.
3. **"No empirical purchase."** Typed grammar plus standard compartmental theorems plus standard first-passage formulas is methodology, not a finding.

The repair is systematic, and it is the same move in each case:

> **Convert every *separation* into a *directed inference theorem*, every *bracket* into a *signed bound*, and every *typing discipline* into an *identifiability or tractability result*.**

Separations say "these are different." Directed inference says "coarse accounts can refute but never certify." Brackets say "the error is bounded." Signed bounds say "this indicator errs in the unsafe direction exactly when $d\varphi/dA<0$." Typing says "declare your structure." # Upgrade 1. Replace the noncompensation counterexample with a uniqueness theorem plus an endogenous-weight theorem

**Gap.** "No nonnegative weighting certifies componentwise adequacy" is a two-line observation and purely prohibitive. It tells a practitioner nothing about what to do instead, and it leaves the impression that aggregation is *never* admissible — which is false and makes the paper easy to dismiss as anti-quantitative.

**1a. Uniqueness of the limiting-factor aggregator.** Let $\mathcal A:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ act on normalized component adequacies $r=(r_1,\dots,r_n)$ (e.g. $r_i=b_i/d_i$, regeneration over draw). Impose:

* **(M) Monotonicity:** $r\le r' \Rightarrow \mathcal A(r)\le \mathcal A(r')$.
* **(I) Idempotence:** $\mathcal A(c\mathbf 1)=c$ (unit consistency / calibration).
* **(NC) Certification:** $\mathcal A(r)\ge c \Rightarrow r_i\ge c$ for all $i$, for every $c$.

**Theorem.** (M)+(I)+(NC) $\Rightarrow \mathcal A=\min$.
*Proof.* Set $c=\mathcal A(r)$ in (NC): $\min_i r_i\ge \mathcal A(r)$. Since $r\ge(\min_i r_i)\mathbf 1$, (M)+(I) give $\mathcal A(r)\ge\min_i r_i$. ∎

Three lines, but the content is large: **the non-compensatory constraint does not merely exclude weights — it forces Liebig's law of the minimum as the unique certifying aggregator.** You now have a positive prescription, derived rather than asserted, and a quantitative diagnostic:

$$\text{compensation premium } \Pi(r) := \mathcal A(r)-\min_i r_i \ \ge 0,$$

which is exactly the part of any published aggregate produced by cross-component trades. This is the mathematics the paper's §1.1 accuses the literature of never declaring; now it is a number you can compute from other people's published tables.

**1b. The only legitimate weights are endogenous and divergent.** Let $V(x)$ be the value (or maximal sustainable service) attainable from state $x$ subject to barrier safety. The admissible "weights" are $\lambda(x)=\nabla V(x)$ — shadow prices of the binding constraints, not exogenous coefficients.

**Proposition (essentiality ⇒ no fixed weight vector works).** If component $i$ is *limitational* at its barrier — $V(x)\to 0$ (or $-\infty$) as $x_i\downarrow x_i^{\min}$ with other components bounded, which holds for CES-type service functions exactly when the local substitution elasticity $\sigma_i\le1$ — then $\partial V/\partial x_i\to\infty$. Hence for every fixed $w\in\mathbb R^n_+$ there exist states where $w^\top(x-x^{\min})$ increases while $V$ decreases: *fixed-weight aggregates are not even locally order-preserving near a barrier.*

**Corollary (where weak-sustainability accounting is valid — stated generously).** Fixed-weight aggregation is an admissible ordinal proxy precisely on the region $\{x:\lambda(x)\le\Lambda\}$, whose radius is computable from the curvature of the service function. **The paper should certify the domain of validity of footprint-style aggregation rather than only attacking it.** That single change converts the framework from adversarial to enabling, and it is much harder to reject.

**1c. Weak vs. strong sustainability becomes a local regime, not a doctrine.** $\sigma_i>1$ (bounded shadow price) = weak regime; $\sigma_i\le1$ (divergent) = strong regime. And — connecting to Upgrade 5 — $\sigma_i^{\text{eff}}$ is not a property of "natural capital" but of the pathway set at the use timescale. This is your §1 reframing, finally carrying a derivation.

# Upgrade 2. Coarse accounts can refute but cannot certify: a one-directional inference theorem with compositional structure

**Gap.** "Compensatory aggregation is bad" is a critique. What the field actually needs is a rule for *which claims survive a change of scope* — because every real account (facility → sector → nation → globe; Scope 1/2/3; MRIO trade adjustment) is a coarse-graining of a finer ledger nobody has.

**Setup.** Treat typed ledgers as objects and define two classes of morphisms: (i) **coarse-graining** $\pi$ (merging species/compartments/moieties), (ii) **composition along interfaces** (open ledgers glued on matched boundary fluxes — the decorated-cospan setting of Baez–Pollard is directly applicable and gives you a literature to stand on).

**Theorem (predicate transport).**
* **Conservation is preserved** by any coarse-graining that respects the moiety partition (left-null vectors of $S_T$ push forward). It is **reflected** only if the partition is moiety-saturated. ⇒ An explicit condition under which a coarse account can vouch for a fine one.
* **Barrier safety is preserved in one direction only.** Aggregation replaces $\{x_i\ge x_i^{\min}\}$ with $\{\sum_i x_i\ge\sum_i x_i^{\min}\}$, which is strictly weaker. Therefore: *aggregate barrier violation implies fine-grained violation; aggregate barrier satisfaction implies nothing.* **Coarse accounts have refutation power but no certification power.**
* **Composition is conservative iff interface fluxes match pairwise with sign reversal** (a Kirchhoff cut condition). Failure of this condition *is* double counting — so double counting ceases to be a practitioner's worry and becomes a violated algebraic identity with a localizable residual.

**Why this is the paper's best structural result.** It is one theorem, and it subsumes: the compensatory-aggregation critique, the Earth-Overshoot-Day complaint, your non-reduction boundary (physical ledger → institutional model is a morphism that fails to preserve conservation-derived predicates), and the leakage/burden-shifting problem in LCA and consumption-based accounting. Directed inference is also *surprising* in the right way: it licenses aggregate indicators as alarms while forbidding them as clearances, which is a rule nobody currently states and everybody violates in both directions.

**Immediate empirical deliverable (Upgrade 1 + 2, one afternoon of arithmetic).** With component biocapacities $b_i$ and demands $d_i$:

$$\tau_{\text{agg}}=365\frac{\sum_i b_i}{\sum_i d_i}=365\sum_i w_i r_i,\quad w_i=\frac{d_i}{\sum_j d_j},\quad r_i=\frac{b_i}{d_i},$$

so $\tau_{\text{agg}}\ \ge\ \tau_{\min}:=365\min_i r_i$ **always**, with equality iff all component ratios coincide, and

$$\underbrace{\tau_{\text{agg}}-\tau_{\min}}_{\text{compensation premium, in days}}=365\sum_i w_i\,(r_i-r_{\min}).$$

**Earth Overshoot Day is a provably weakly optimistic statistic relative to its own non-compensatory counterpart, the bias is one-signed, exactly decomposable, computable from the published component tables, and unbounded in component dispersion.** Report $\tau_{\min}$, the binding component's identity, and the premium. That is a headline result using data that already exists, it costs nothing, and it is the kind of thing that gets cited outside the methods literature.

# Upgrade 3. A signed-bias calculus for depletion indicators — and R/P rehabilitated as a certified bound

**Gap.** Proposition 17's uniform-drift bracket is symmetric. **Symmetric error bars are useless for precaution.** A decision-maker needs to know the *direction* of the error, not its magnitude.

**3a. The general sign law.** Let $\dot A=-\varphi(A)$ with $\varphi>0$ on $(A_{\min},A_0]$. Then exactly

$$T=\int_{A_{\min}}^{A_0}\frac{dA}{\varphi(A)},\qquad H^{\text{loc}}=\frac{A_0-A_{\min}}{\varphi(A_0)} .$$

* $\varphi$ **nondecreasing in $A$** (decline slows as the stock falls: rising lift costs, rising extraction cost, density-dependent effort) $\Rightarrow\ T\ge H^{\text{loc}}$: **the frozen-rate number is conservative.**
* $\varphi$ **nonincreasing in $A$** (decline accelerates: depensation, well interference, aquifer compaction, soil-structure collapse, cascading infrastructure failure) $\Rightarrow\ T\le H^{\text{loc}}$: **the frozen-rate number is optimistic and unsafe.**

So: **the direction of error in every "stock ÷ decline rate" number in circulation is determined by a single declarable sign condition — the monotonicity of net decline in the stock — which is estimable by regressing $\dot A$ on $A$.** This is a strict, useful upgrade of your own proposition: same effort, one-sided conclusion, and a falsifiable data test attached. Publish the classification table: which real systems sit in the safe class and which in the dangerous class.

**3b. R/P: the crossover criterion.** Let production grow at $g$ and let reserves be replenished by reclassification at elasticity $\eta$ ($\dot R=-(1-\eta)P$, $P=P_0e^{gt}$, $\tau:=R_0/P_0$). Then

$$T=\frac1g\ln\!\Big(1+\frac{g\tau}{1-\eta}\Big),\qquad\text{and}\qquad T\gtrless\tau \iff \eta \gtrless \text{(approximately) } \tfrac12 g\tau .$$

**The R/P ratio is a conservative lower bound on exhaustion time iff the reclassification elasticity exceeds roughly half the growth–horizon product.** Two elasticities, one inequality, a signed verdict. This does three things at once: it stops the paper from merely repeating "reserves are an economic classification" (Tilton; Illakwahhi et al.), it turns the criticized ratio into a *usable certified bound under a declared condition*, and it creates a genuine empirical contribution — estimate $\hat\eta$ from historical reserve revisions and $\hat g$ from consumption series for phosphate, copper, lithium, and nickel, then classify each published R/P horizon as conservative or optimistic. That is a table people will cite, built entirely from public data, and it directly answers the "no empirical purchase" objection.

**3c. Report intensities, not horizons.** Horizons are $+\infty$ in the sustainable regime and therefore discontinuous at the frontier (see Upgrade 6); reciprocals are continuous, and under multiple competing drains they **add**:

$$\frac1{T}\ \approx\ \sum_k \frac1{T_k}\qquad\text{(frozen rates)},$$

whereas horizons neither add nor average — a composition error committed routinely. Recommend hazard-rate-style reporting (per-year depletion intensity), exactly as reliability engineering abandoned mean lifetimes for failure rates.

# Upgrade 4. Yield inflation is a latent-variable identification problem — and output records are provably uninformative

This is the paper's deepest available result and currently it exists only as the elevator metaphor. Make it a theorem and the paper changes category.

**Model.** Output $Y(t)=h(K(t),A(t))$, support pool $\dot A=r(A)-\lambda(t)$, with true sustainable yield defined at $\lambda=r(A)$. Define the **drawdown share** $s=\lambda/(\lambda+r(A))\in[0,1]$: the fraction of delivered yield financed by liquidation.

**Theorem A (non-identifiability).** From the record $\{Y(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}$ alone, $s$ is not identifiable: there is an explicit gauge family — reparametrize $A\mapsto\psi(A)$ with compensating adjustment of $h$ and the growth/drawdown split — producing observationally identical output records with $s$ anywhere in $(0,1)$. **Corollary: rising or stable measured yields carry no information about sustainability.** Not "weak evidence" — zero, within the declared class.

**Theorem B (minimal instrument).** $s$ becomes identifiable iff one of two declared anchors is supplied: (i) the support pool is observed at $\ge2$ times up to a known monotone transform, with a known nonzero coupling $\partial h/\partial A$; or (ii) a conserved moiety tracer ties the liquidation flux to a measurable boundary flux (stoichiometric anchor). **This is exactly what the two-pool typed architecture buys, and it is now a theorem rather than a design preference.**

**Theorem C (detection horizon).** With support-pool measurement noise $\sigma_A$ and regeneration scale $r$, detecting $s\ge s_0$ at size $\alpha$ and power $1-\beta$ requires roughly

$$T_{\det}\ \gtrsim\ \frac{(z_{1-\alpha}+z_{1-\beta})\,\sigma_A}{s_0\,r(A)} .$$

**This is the elevator made quantitative: the wear is detectable in $T_{\det}$ years *if and only if* the support pool is measured at precision $\sigma_A$; with output-only records it is never detectable at any horizon.** It converts the paper's stated purpose ("make the wear measurable while the cable still holds") into a monitoring-design formula with immediate application: GRACE/GRACE-FO precision versus aquifer drawdown share, soil-carbon sampling design, nutrient-budget closure precision.

Three headline sentences fall out, each surprising and each defensible:
- *Yield data cannot certify sustainability — a non-identifiability theorem.*
- *Sustainability claims require a support-pool instrument or a stoichiometric tracer; there is no third option.*
- *Here is the monitoring precision and horizon needed to see depletion before failure.*

# Upgrade 5. Make "closure at the rate of use" a measurable operator: the closure kernel and closure deficit

Your reframing of weak/strong sustainability is the most original prose in §1 and currently carries no mathematics. Give it an estimator.

**Definition.** For moiety $m$, let $\kappa_m(\tau)$ be the fraction of mobilized moiety flux returned to a usable compartment within lag $\tau$ (a renewal/residence-time kernel, estimable from MFA plus residence-time data). Define the **closure deficit at the use timescale**

$$\delta_m \;=\; 1-\kappa_m(\tau_{\text{use}}).$$

**Proposition.** Barrier reachability for the critical compartment holds iff $\delta_m>0$ is sustained, with first-order horizon $\approx (\text{stock margin})/(\delta_m\times\text{mobilization rate})$. Hence:

* **Weak regime** $=\kappa_m(\tau_{\text{use}})\to1$: matter is redistributed as fast as it is used; the barrier is unreachable; shadow prices bounded; aggregation admissible (Upgrade 1c).
* **Strong regime** $=\delta_m>0$: failed closure; barrier reachable in finite time; shadow prices diverge; only $\min$ certifies.

**Three consequences worth the section.**
1. **Weak vs. strong sustainability becomes a measurement, not a position.** $\delta_m$ is estimable; the regime boundary is $\delta_m=0$.
2. **Waste is formalized as a relational status**: $\kappa_m(\tau;\Theta)$ is indexed by the technology/knowledge set $\Theta$, so "unlocking waste" is precisely an increase of $\kappa$ at fixed $\tau$ — exactly your prose claim, now an operator identity.
3. **The circular-economy indicators are diagnosed and fixed.** Recycled-input-rate metrics (including the EU circularity-rate family) are $\kappa$ *without a timescale*, i.e. they report $\kappa_m(\infty)$ and silently claim $\kappa_m(\tau_{\text{use}})$. The closure-deficit form is the corrected object, and the correction is signed and computable. This gives the paper a direct line into circular-economy policy metrology — a large, badly specified, high-visibility target.
4. **Burden shifting gets a theorem.** Substitution is a morphism; admissibility requires non-increase of $\delta$ across all compartments. Any substitution that lowers one $\delta$ by raising another is a predicate violation, not a trade-off — the formal version of "a substitute is either a recycled flux or a drawdown on a second compartment."

# Upgrade 6. Replace the depletion date with a control margin — justified by a defect of hitting times, not a preference

**The defect (state it as a proposition).** $T_A(x_0;\pi,d)=\inf\{t:A\le A_{\min}\}$ is $+\infty$ whenever regeneration exceeds use by any $\varepsilon>0$ and finite for $\varepsilon\le0$. **The hitting time is discontinuous and locally unbounded exactly at the sustainability frontier — the only place decisions are made.** A 1% data revision flips the reported indicator from "48 years" to "never." No robust ranking can be built on such a functional. This is a mathematical disqualification of the entire depletion-date genre, including your own preferred object, and it is far stronger than the semantic critique the paper currently makes.

**The replacement.** Let $\mathcal K(\theta)$ be the viability kernel: states from which barrier-safe trajectories exist under admissible controls $U(\theta)$. Define the **margin**

$$\mu(x_0)=\sup\{\rho: x_0\in\mathcal K \text{ under controls/demand perturbed by } \rho\}.$$

Provable properties: dimensionless and scale-free; non-compensatory (a min over binding constraints, consistent with Upgrade 1a); monotone in stocks; Lipschitz under regularity; **finite and informative in both regimes**; ordinally consistent with $T$ inside the unsustainable regime. $\mu>1$ = viable with headroom, $\mu<1$ = infeasible, $|\mu-1|$ = distance to the frontier in units of a declared control budget. This is "distance to infeasibility" imported into sustainability metrology, with a proof that it repairs a discontinuity that no depletion date can repair.

**Public framing:** *replace the date with a margin.* The date is a discontinuous functional of contested data; the margin is a Lipschitz functional with the same inputs.

# Upgrade 7. Make "proof-carrying" literal: Farkas duals, soundness **and completeness**, and a tractability boundary

**Gap.** "Proof-carrying" is currently a metaphor, and the envelope theorem is only *sound* — a sound-but-slack bound can be vacuous, and a reviewer will ask.

**7a. Sharpness.** Prove the envelope is *tight*: under polytopic declared flux bounds and linear conservation, the componentwise bounds $\underline S_m\le S_m\le\overline S_m$ are attained by extreme admissible flux paths when the flux polytope is a product (or, generally, characterize the reachable set by its support function and bound the envelope–reachable-set gap). Soundness plus sharpness is a theorem; soundness alone is a remark.

**7b. Certificates a third party can check without trusting the auditor.** Envelope safety is an LP feasibility problem; therefore:

* **safe ⇒ a Farkas dual $y\ge0$** verifying infeasibility of violation, checkable in $O(\mathrm{nnz})$ exact rational arithmetic;
* **unsafe ⇒ an explicit violating flux path** (a witness).

That is a **sound and complete** audit with verifiable artifacts, which is what "proof-carrying" should mean. For nonlinear kinetics, escalate to SOS/Positivstellensatz barrier certificates (Prajna–Jadbabaie) with semialgebraic invariance; note decidability for polynomial ledgers.

**7c. Duality unifies three of your contributions.** The dual vector $y$ *is* the endogenous weight vector of Upgrade 1b — barrier-binding, state-dependent, and divergent at criticality. **7d. Typing is a tractability device, not only hygiene.** State the boundary: barrier certification under declared polytopic bounds is an LP (scales); certification with undeclared bilinear couplings and integer scenario choices is NP-hard; yield inflation with unknown coupling is *non-identifiable* (Upgrade 4). **Declaration is what keeps the audit computable.** This is a novel and genuinely persuasive argument for the framework — it appeals to implementers rather than to methodological virtue.

**7e. This yields a partial order of audit states and a progressive-disclosure protocol.

# The rewritten novelty statement

> Sustainability indicators conflate accounting balance, conservation, barrier safety, and depletion horizons. We construct a typed stock–flow ledger in which these are separate predicates with separate proof obligations, and we prove how each transports across scope. Finally, because hitting times are discontinuous at the sustainability frontier, we replace the depletion date with a Lipschitz **control margin**, and we ship barrier claims as Farkas or SOS certificates that a third party verifies in linear time without re-running the model.

# Strongest conceptual upgrade

The paper becomes substantially more novel if it is framed not merely as a synthesis of stock–flow accounting, positivity theory, and first-passage analysis, but as a **theory of predicate-preserving sustainability reports**.

The unifying problem is that sustainability indicators compress a high-dimensional physical system into a smaller object—often one scalar—and then inherit claims that the compression does not preserve.

Let

\[
\phi:\mathcal X_\Gamma\rightarrow \mathcal Y
\]

be an indicator or reporting map from the set of physical states or trajectories consistent with assumptions \(\Gamma\). Let \(S\subseteq\mathcal X_\Gamma\) be the states satisfying a source predicate, such as componentwise adequacy or barrier safety, and let \(A\subseteq\mathcal Y\) be the indicator’s “acceptable” region. The report soundly certifies the physical predicate if and only if

\[
\phi^{-1}(A)\subseteq S.
\]

If a fiber \(\phi^{-1}(y)\) contains both safe and unsafe systems, then no inference based only on \(y\) can distinguish them. This gives one formal explanation for all the paper’s major failures:

- **Compensatory aggregation:** \(b\mapsto w^\top b\) has fibers containing both componentwise-adequate and component-deficient vectors.
- **Classification drift:** economic reserves \(R=\kappa(x,\eta)\) combine physical inventory \(x\) with prices, technology, exploration, and regulation \(\eta\); the same \(R/P\) can correspond to different physical stocks and futures.
- **False depletion horizons:** \((A,\dot A)\mapsto (A-A_{\min})/[-\dot A]_+\) compresses an entire future trajectory into local information; the same ratio can correspond to recovery, finite crossing, or asymptotic non-crossing.
- **Productivity illusion:** reported output suppresses the support-stock balance, allowing current service to appear sustainable while a hidden donor pool is being liquidated.

This **predicate-preservation principle** should be the paper’s central theorem schema. The typed ledger then supplies the source domain; the certification results establish which predicates survive each transformation.

# 1. Upgrade the ledger into a layered semantic architecture

A physical ledger alone cannot fully formalize classification drift. Distinguish four layers.

## A. Physical layer

\[
\dot x=S_Tv(x,\theta)+B_Tu_\partial+d_x,\qquad v\ge 0.
\]

Stocks should be typed at least by:

- moiety;
- species or form;
- compartment;
- quality, concentration, or grade where relevant;
- physical versus boundary status.

The quality dimension matters because global phosphorus conservation, for example, does not imply regeneration of concentrated, accessible phosphate ore.

## B. Observation layer

\[
z(t)=h(x(t),\eta_o(t))+\varepsilon(t).
\]

An anomaly, remote-sensing signal, fitted trend, or index is an observation of a state—not the state itself. A threshold in \(z\)-space becomes a physical threshold only when the observation map and threshold correspondence are established.

## C. Classification layer

\[
R(t)=\kappa\!\left(x(t),\eta_c(t)\right),
\]

where \(\eta_c\) includes prices, technology, exploration, legal rules, and reserve definitions. Then

\[
\dot R
=
\kappa_x\dot x+\kappa_{\eta_c}\dot\eta_c.
\]

Only the first term is connected directly to physical stock change. The second permits reserves to rise during continuing extraction without violating conservation. Reclassification must therefore not be represented as a material flux.

## D. Report and query layer

A reported object should carry a semantic contract:

\[
\mathfrak I=
\left\langle
z,\,
\text{kind},\,
\text{carrier},\,
\text{compartment},\,
\text{temporal operator},\,
\text{event},\,
\mathcal M,\,
\pi,\,
\Gamma,\,
\sigma
\right\rangle .
\]

Relevant kinds include:

\[
\mathsf{Stock},\;
\mathsf{Flux},\;
\mathsf{Observation},\;
\mathsf{Classification},\;
\mathsf{PressureScale},\;
\mathsf{LocalHorizon},\;
\mathsf{EventTime}.
\]

The crucial point is:

> “Years” is a unit, not a semantic type.

A local scale may be promoted to an event time only through a declared model and an accompanying proof.

# 2. Replace one sustainability verdict with a certificate vector

Represent certification as

\[
\operatorname{Cert}(\mathcal L,\Gamma)
=
(\mathsf{Typed},
\mathsf{Balanced},
\mathsf{Conservative},
\mathsf{Positive},
\mathsf{Thermo},
\mathsf{Safe},
\mathsf{Adequate},
\mathsf{Closed})
\in\{\top,\bot,?\}^{8}.
\]

The value \(?\) is essential: “not established” is not equivalent to “false.”

| Predicate | Typical proof obligation | Does not by itself establish |
|---|---|---|
| Type correctness | compatible kinds, units, carriers, and ports | physical truth |
| Bookkeeping balance | ledger identity or bounded residual | moiety conservation |
| Conservation | \(LS_T=0\), with typed boundary terms | positivity or safety |
| Positivity | \(x_i=0\Rightarrow \dot x_i\ge0\) | staying above a functional threshold |
| Thermodynamic admissibility | energy, exergy, or reaction-coupling constraints | barrier safety |
| Barrier safety | invariant set or robust flux envelope | conservation or service delivery |
| Service adequacy | delivered services meet demand | absence of hidden stock liquidation |

Describe these predicates as **separately certified and generally non-equivalent**, rather than claiming blanket pairwise independence. Some definitions generate valid implications—for example, safety in a barrier set contained in the nonnegative orthant implies positivity over that horizon.

# 3. Add theorem-level results that materially deepen novelty

## A. Horizon non-identification theorem

The current taxonomy becomes much stronger with an impossibility result.

Let \(A_0>A_{\min}\), \(d_0>0\), and

\[
H^{\mathrm{loc}}=\frac{A_0-A_{\min}}{d_0}.
\]

For every prescribed \(\tau\in(0,\infty]\), one can construct a sufficiently smooth trajectory satisfying

\[
A(0)=A_0,\qquad -\dot A(0)=d_0
\]

whose first hitting time of \(A_{\min}\) is \(\tau\). Thus local level and slope do not identify, bound, or rank the physical hitting time without additional assumptions.

The positive result then follows cleanly. If, until crossing,

\[
0<d_-\le -\dot A(s)\le d_+,
\]

then

\[
\frac{A_0-A_{\min}}{d_+}
\le T_A\le
\frac{A_0-A_{\min}}{d_-}.
\]

This creates an assumption ladder:

1. units of time;
2. local frozen-rate scale;
3. bounded-drift hitting-time interval;
4. scenario-conditioned event time.

It also provides exact “promotion rules”:

| Expression | Becomes an event time only if |
|---|---|
| \(R/P\) | \(R\) is a fixed physical inventory, \(\dot R=-P\), \(P\) is constant, and there is no discovery or reclassification |
| \(\Delta z/[-\dot z]\) | the observation trend persists and the observation threshold maps to a physical event |
| \(\log(B/B_{\min})/F\) | \(\dot B=-FB\), with constant \(F\) and no growth or recruitment |

This is more useful than simply declaring the quantities “not depletion times.”

## B. A closure hierarchy with primal and dual certificates

Formalize “the cycle closes” at three levels:

1. **Pathway closure:** a stoichiometrically and physically admissible return path exists.
2. **Rate closure:** available pathways have enough capacity to meet use without drawing down critical stocks or overfilling sinks.
3. **Timely safe closure:** capacity is deployed before a declared barrier is reached.

For a linear or polyhedral ledger, define the instantaneous feasible set

\[
\mathcal F(x,t)=
\left\{
v\ge0:
Yv\ge d,\;
(S_Cv+b_C)\ge0,\;
(S_Wv+b_W)\le a_W,\;
v\le\bar v,\;
v\in\mathcal T
\right\},
\]

where:

- \(Yv\ge d\) is service adequacy;
- \(S_Cv+b_C\ge0\) prevents critical-stock drawdown;
- \(S_Wv+b_W\le a_W\) respects sink or assimilation capacity;
- \(\bar v\) contains process capacities;
- \(\mathcal T\) contains declared thermodynamic constraints.

Rate closure holds exactly when \(\mathcal F(x,t)\neq\varnothing\).

After writing the constraints as \(Av\le b,\;v\ge0\), Farkas’ lemma gives either:

- a **primal closure certificate** \(v\), or
- a **dual obstruction certificate** \(y\ge0\) satisfying

\[
A^\top y\ge0,\qquad b^\top y<0.
\]

The dual certificate identifies the stock, sink, process capacity, or service constraint preventing closure. These weights are not arbitrary compensatory weights: they are derived from physical constraints and explain infeasibility.

This makes the paper’s claims about waste and substitution operational:

> A substitute is a feasible, capacity-sufficient, barrier-safe pathway—not a coefficient in an aggregate production function.

> A material is waste relative to a declared process graph, capacity set, demand vector, and time horizon—not intrinsically.

## C. Turn the dynamical productivity illusion into a measurable theorem

Suppose a support stock obeys

\[
\dot A=r-c,
\]

and a declared production relation requires at least \(\alpha\) units of support use per unit of observed service:

\[
c(t)\ge\alpha Y(t).
\]

Then

\[
A(0)-A(T)
\ge
\alpha\int_0^T Y(t)\,dt
-
\int_0^T r(t)\,dt.
\]

Whenever the right-hand side is positive, cumulative output certifies a minimum amount of support-stock liquidation. This converts the elevator metaphor into an auditable result:

- the theorem measures accumulated wear;
- it does not claim to predict the snapping time;
- disagreement with observed stock change exposes a wrong coefficient, missing flux, or measurement inconsistency.

In a multi-pool network, the dual closure certificate can identify a weighted combination of support pools that must decline. This pairs elegantly with the static noncompensation theorem:

- **static illusion:** surplus in one component masks another component’s deficit;
- **dynamic illusion:** current yield masks cumulative support-stock drawdown.

## D. Strengthen and correctly delimit the noncompensation result

The exact claim should be:

\[
n\ge2,\; w\ge0,\;w\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists b:\;w^\top b\ge0
\text{ and }
\min_i b_i<0.
\]

Do not claim that no scalar can certify componentwise adequacy. A noncompensatory scalar such as

\[
C(b)=\min_i\frac{b_i}{s_i}
\]

can certify it, although it loses information about which components fail. The impossibility result applies to additive nonnegative weighting.

A useful practical extension is the worst concealed deficit:

\[
\delta_j^\star(z)=
\max\left\{
-b_j:
w^\top b=z,\;
\ell\le b\le u
\right\}.
\]

This turns the theorem into an audit: given an aggregate score and component bounds, how severe could an unseen deficit be?

## E. Add a compositionality theorem

If two typed ledgers are joined through boundary ports carrying the same moiety and equal-and-opposite fluxes, their conservation certificates compose. Positivity composes under donor-limited interface kinetics. Barrier safety does not automatically compose; it requires an interface contract.

This would make the institutional interface more consequential:

\[
\text{physical ledger}\xrightarrow{D=-\dot N}
\text{institutional model}
\xrightarrow{\pi(t)}
\text{physical ledger}.
\]

The shared observable permits composition, not reduction or theorem transfer.

# 4. Correct the most vulnerable conceptual claims

1. **Do not define strong sustainability as failed closure.**  
   Say instead that weak and strong sustainability are nested tests on one network. The weak test permits every declared physically admissible substitution pathway; the strong test adds componentwise critical-stock and sink constraints. Failure of rate closure shows that the weak-substitution premise is unavailable—it is not itself the definition of strong sustainability.

2. **Replace “every pool is regenerative.”**  
   A better statement is:

   > Every material participates in a larger physical cycle, but a typed stock is regenerative only if matter returns to the same usable species, quality, and compartment at a relevant rate and within the decision horizon.

   Global moiety conservation does not imply regeneration of an ore body, aquifer, fertile soil layer, or viable population.

3. **Refine the recoverability claim.**  
   Recoverability is not only a property of rate. It is a relation among:

   \[
   \text{state, pathway, capacity, threshold, and horizon}.
   \]

4. **Correct the turnover terminology.**  
   Since

   \[
   J_A^{\mathrm{gross}}=\frac{g}{A}
   \]

   has units \(T^{-1}\), it is a turnover intensity. Its reciprocal \(A/g\) is the gross support-coverage time.

5. **Be precise about Earth Overshoot Day.**  
   Avoid claiming that its conversions are literally undeclared; equivalence and yield factors are mathematically specified. The stronger criticism is that an aggregate biocapacity comparison does not certify componentwise ecological adequacy, and its commensuration assumptions should not be interpreted as evidence of physical substitutability.

6. **Do not claim thermodynamic certification unless it is implemented.**  
   If the ledger checks only stoichiometry, report thermodynamic admissibility as \(?\), not as implied by conservation.

7. **Demote standard results from headline novelty.**  
   Finite-donor integrability, inverse-Gaussian passage laws, geometric-Brownian passage formulas, and standard positivity results are valuable supporting tools. The headline novelty should be the new formal object, the predicate-preservation results, and the executable certification procedure.

# 5. Paste-ready novelty and impact statement

> **Novelty and impact.** This paper does not introduce another scalar sustainability index. It develops a typed, proof-carrying calculus for determining what sustainability indicators are entitled to claim. A reported number is treated as the output of a map from a physical stock–flow trajectory, observation process, or economic classification. The report certifies a physical predicate only when that map is proved to preserve the predicate under declared assumptions. This single principle unifies compensatory aggregation, classification drift, hidden support-stock liquidation, and the misuse of quantities expressed in years as physical depletion times.
>
> The framework separates physical stocks and primitive fluxes from observations, reserve classifications, scalar projections, and event-time queries. It consequently produces a vector of certificates rather than one verdict: type correctness, bookkeeping balance, moiety conservation, nonnegative realizability, thermodynamic admissibility, barrier safety, service adequacy, and rate closure are distinct proof obligations. Depletion is treated separately as a first-passage query requiring a declared barrier, model, policy, disturbance, and initial condition.
>
> The paper establishes three complementary classes of result. First, non-preservation theorems show when compressed reports cannot certify their intended predicates: additive aggregates cannot establish componentwise adequacy, and local level–slope ratios cannot identify global hitting times. Second, positive certificates show when stronger claims are valid: incidence structure proves conservation, donor limitation proves positivity, flux envelopes prove robust barrier safety, and bounded drift converts local depletion ratios into hitting-time intervals. Third, closure is made operational as a pathway-, capacity-, and time-dependent feasibility problem, with either a feasible flux certificate or a dual witness identifying the critical stock, sink, or process constraint preventing closure.
>
> The result is a metrology of sustainability claims. Reserve-life ratios, groundwater trend-persistence indices, fisheries pressure scales, aggregate footprint balances, and scenario-conditioned depletion times can be reported in one framework without being treated as interchangeable. The contribution is therefore not a new forecast of aquifer, fishery, or mineral exhaustion; it is a formal and auditable system for preventing an indicator from carrying more physical meaning than its data, model, and proof obligations establish.

## Part I — Four theorem families that change what the paper is

### 1. Invariance under reclassification: turn the reserves critique into an audit criterion

The Tilton observation (reserves grow with prices) is currently a citation. Make it a theorem.

Define an **admissible reclassification** as a compartment-membership morphism \(\mu_p\) parameterized by economic state \(p\) (price, technology, regulation), constrained only to conserve total physical stock: \(S = R(p) + U(p)\). Call an indicator **audit-robust** if its value — or at least its predicate tag — is invariant under all admissible \(\mu_p\).

**Theorem (sketch).** The reserve-life ratio \(R(p)/P\) is not audit-robust: under any \(\mu_p\) with \(R(p)\) strictly monotone in \(p\), the ratio sweeps an unbounded interval while no physical flux changes. Hence it can never carry a hitting-time predicate, only an accounting predicate at frozen \(p\).

**Constructive companion.** Characterize the indicators that *are* invariant: those definable purely from conservation and thermodynamic predicates — cumulative extraction \(Q(t)\), and the marginal exergy cost of extraction \(e(Q)\) as grade declines. These are \(\mu_p\)-invariant because they attach to the physical state of the ore body, not to economic compartment membership.

This converts the paper's best critique into a **design criterion for public statistics**: publish only invariants, or publish with declared morphism-sensitivity. No one in MFA, criticality studies, or SEEA has this criterion. It also retroactively justifies the G3P and fisheries classifications — both fail invariance, under record-relative and effort-relative morphisms respectively.

### 2. Identifiability of hidden liquidation: make the elevator a theorem

The productivity illusion is the paper's best physical intuition and currently has no mathematics. Give it the two-pool model and prove the observation map's failure.

Support pool: \(\dot S = g(S) - \lambda S - h(S,E)\); measured yield \(Y = y(S,E) + \theta\lambda S\). Then:

**Non-identifiability.** For any admissible liquidation profile \(\tilde\lambda(\cdot)\), there exists \(\tilde g\) rationalizing the observed \((Y, E)\). Yield-and-effort data cannot distinguish regeneration from liquidation; apparent yield stability is consistent with arbitrary hidden drawdown. *The cable shows nothing* is a property of the observation map, not a metaphor.

**Minimal-measurement theorem.** Identifiability is restored by exactly one of: (a) a structural prior on \(g\) (e.g., parametric family with generic excitation); (b) two or more direct measurements of \(S\); (c) a tracer carried by the liquidation flux but not the regenerative flux (isotope, moiety tag). Absent these, the envelope theorem applied to the unobserved pool yields the honest object: a **bracket** on liquidation, e.g. \(\lambda S \in [\,Y - \bar g - h,\ \cdot\,]\), and a bound on sustainable-yield overestimate equal to mean liquidation.

This is the paper's strongest potential scientific result: a precise statement of when soil-carbon mining, groundwater-fed yield, or stock-fed catch is *in principle* undetectable from production statistics, and exactly which measurement breaks the degeneracy. Soil science, fisheries, and hydrology all lack this theorem in usable form. It also unifies the paper: the envelope theorem becomes the tool for partially observed support pools, and "making the wear measurable while the cable still holds" becomes a measurement-design problem with a solution.

### 3. Feasibility as a cut condition: polytime certificates and the audit target

The envelope theorem bounds trajectories; it does not yet say certification is *cheap*. Under declared piecewise-constant flux bounds and the ledger's linear stoichiometry, build the time-expanded network (compartment × time nodes, flux-bound arcs, barrier lower bounds on cumulative sums, service demands as required sinks).

**Theorem (sketch).** Barrier safety + service adequacy over a declared horizon is decidable in polynomial time (LP/max-flow). The dual identifies the critical cut — the specific compartment-time pair that binds. Noncompensation reappears constructively: the binding constraint is always componentwise, never aggregate, because min-cuts are componentwise.

Three payoffs:

- **Auditability**: a national ledger is certifiable in seconds, not by simulation;
- **Value of information**: LP sensitivity ranks candidate flux measurements by how much they relax the critical cut — a "what to measure next" output for statistical agencies, which no sustainability framework currently offers;
- **Nonlinear honesty**: nonlinear \(v(x)\) is handled by conservative linear envelopes, which is exactly the paper's existing epistemic stance.

### 4. From non-reduction to deadlines: the delay budget and the last responsible moment

The institutional interface currently proves a negative (non-reduction). Add two positives.

**Budget invariance.** For any delay/feedback structure preserving \(D = -\dot N\): \(\int_0^\infty D\,dt \le M(0)\). Delays reschedule extraction; they never enlarge the budget. One line to prove, and it is the precise content of "you cannot negotiate with the ledger."

**Last responsible moment.** With adjustment bound \(|u| \le \bar u\) and observation delay \(\tau\), compute the capture basin backward from the barrier (dynamic programming on the time-expanded network of §3 — the machinery is already there) and define \(t^*(\tau, \bar u)\): the latest time at which barrier safety remains feasible. Then the governance-facing pair:

- \(T_A\): the physics deadline (scenario-conditioned hitting time);
- \(t^*\): the physics-plus-institutions deadline; \(T_A - t^*\) is the **decision window**; \(t^* < 0\) means overshoot is already unavoidable and the honest report is the *minimal achievable overshoot*.

Conjectured comparison theorem (\(\partial t^*/\partial \tau \le 0\), monotone in delay) needs hypotheses — linear dynamics, common Lyapunov structure — and should be stated with them. But even the computable \(t^*\) alone is a quantity no limits-to-growth descendant has ever produced formally, and it directly operationalizes the Meadows lineage the paper cites.

## Part II — Architecture hardening

### 5. Certificate calculus: make composition a theorem

Ledgers are built sector-by-sector and audited jointly. Define ledger morphisms (moiety-preserving compartment maps with flux pushforward) and prove transport rules: accounting and conservation transport covariantly along pushforward; barrier safety and service adequacy transport only along barrier-reflecting morphisms. Noncompensation becomes the failure of the terminal morphism (aggregation to one dimension) to reflect positivity — the same theorem, now as a corollary of the calculus, and the calculus additionally tells ministries *which* certificates survive composition and with what error budget. Keep the categorical language minimal; the payoff is compositional audit, not abstraction.

### 6. Sharpen noncompensation into a characterization

The current theorem says no nonnegative weighting works. Strengthen to: no *additively separable* continuous certificate exists; any continuous \(s\) with \(s^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_+) = \mathbb{R}^n_+\) must have the bottleneck structure of \(\min_i b_i\) (locally dictatorial at the argmin); the canonical refinements are min and leximin. Economic corollary: certification-compatible aggregation has zero elasticity of substitution — tying the ledger result to the CES/weak-sustainability literature with a precise statement. Dual form: the adequacy cone is not generated by any single price vector, so no numéraire prices componentwise adequacy — a clean impossibility for natural-capital pricing schemes, with barrier-constraint shadow prices as the Hotelling connection.

### 7. Make the fourth predicate real

The paper names thermodynamic admissibility but (apparently) does not formalize it. Assign species their Gibbs energies; declare a flux pattern admissible only if every conversion has nonpositive affinity under compartment conditions. With tabulated potentials this is again LP feasibility — so "mass-balanced but chemically impossible" ledgers are caught mechanically, and the exergy indicator of §1 becomes a typed quantity rather than a suggestion.

### 8. Quantify the closure reading of weak/strong

"Does the cycle close at the rate of use" deserves units. For each moiety, the ledger induces a circulation network; take its circulation time \(\tau_m^{circ}\) (mean recurrence under the restricted rate matrix) and use rate \(u_m\). Closure predicate: \(u_m \tau_m^{circ} \le 1\). The vector of gaps \(g_m = u_m - 1/\tau_m^{circ}\) is a typed circularity gap; scalar \(B\) is its weighted norm. This gives the two readings one generator, explains how ~9% global circularity coexists with local closure, and hands the circular-economy metrics community a rigorous object.

### 9. Extend Proposition 17 into the stochastic section

The uniform-drift bracket and the inverse-Gaussian passage times are currently neighbors, not relatives. SDE comparison theorems give: for \(dA = -b\,dt + \sigma\,dW\) with \(b \in [b_{min}, b_{max}]\), the hitting-time law is sandwiched between two inverse Gaussians, with the deterministic bracket as the \(\sigma \to 0\) limit. One short proof, and the stochastic surrogates inherit ledger discipline instead of floating beside it.

### 10. One fully worked case with real data — non-negotiable

Phosphorus is ideal: contested depletion claims, clean moieties, an economically reclassifying stock (USGS reserves — shows §1), a support pool (soil P — shows §2 with the identifiability bracket on liquidation), an upper barrier (aquatic P, eutrophication — shows two-sided barriers), and active recycling policy (shows §3's certificate and §8's gap).
