# Comprehensive Line-Level Audit and Cross-Manuscript Consistency Report

**Scope:** `papers/paper1_general_theory/manuscript.md`, `papers/paper2_theorem_atlas/manuscript.md`, `papers/paper3_material_ledgers/manuscript.md`, `papers/paper4_delay_dynamics/manuscript.md`, and `papers/paper5_sampled_governance/manuscript.md` within `https://github.com/MIKEAA2020/general-sustainability`.  
**Audit Methodology:** Line-by-line textual analysis, algebraic/numerical re-derivation, verification script execution (`typed_false_positive_instantiation.py`, `verify_consistency.py`, `verify_review_packet.py`), cross-paper concordance validation, and citation matching against source bibliographies.

## 1. Executive Summary

A comprehensive line-level audit of all five manuscripts in the core sustainability suite reveals a tightly architected, mathematically sophisticated framework spanning typed viability theory, stock-flow material conservation, retarded delay differential equations (RFDEs), and sampled governance. The overarching modular division of responsibilities across the series is maintained consistently.

However, detailed line-level scrutiny identified several concrete **mathematical inversions, structural numbering defects, topological imprecisions, notational collisions, and extensive bibliographic disconnects** that require remediation:
1. **Paper 1 (Critical Verbal Inversion):** Theorem B(6) verbally inverts the policy licensing of FAST and SLOW actions relative to $s_2$-prices, directly contradicting its underlying verified mathematical derivation and code instantiation.
2. **Paper 2 (Structural & Numbering Defects):** Multiple sections exhibit severe out-of-order numbering (e.g., Remark 5.9 before Def 5.4; Remark 6.16 before Thm 6.3; Remark 8.15 before Def 8.5; Remark 13.4 before Programme 13.3), undefined parameters in Theorem 5.2 ($\mu_A$), and duplicate object headers (`Hypothesis object 12.3` assigned to two distinct rows).
3. **Paper 3 (Parametric & Notational Collisions):** An essential half-saturation parameter $A_{g0}$ is omitted from the numerical baseline specification in §2.2, and state symbols $K$ and $N$ are overloaded in §2.4 in direct conflict with standard carrying-capacity and biomass conventions.
4. **Paper 4 (Delay Subscript Inversion & Peak Precision):** Proposition 5.2 shifts fundamental delays such that the shifted lower delay $\tau_-$ exceeds the shifted upper delay $\tau_+$ ($\tau_- = 128.374 > \tau_+ = 70.697$), creating a confusing subscript inversion.
5. **Papers 1–5 (Systemic Bibliographic Disconnect):** Following the addition of camera-ready bibliographies, over 50 bibliography entries across the series (16 in Paper 3, 22 in Paper 4, 14 in Paper 5) are never cited in their respective manuscript bodies, and Paper 1 retains raw editorial placeholder tags (`[cite: ...]`).

### Paper 1: General Theory (`papers/paper1_general_theory/manuscript.md`)

#### Finding 1.1 — Mathematical / Verbal Inversion in Theorem B(6)
* **Location:** Line 207 (Section 3.3)
* **Severity:** **High (Mathematical / Interpretive Inconsistency)**
* **Quoted Text:**
  > `"Low-$s_2$-price assessors license FAST only; high-$s_2$-price assessors license SLOW only; assessors at intermediate prices license both."`
* **Mathematical Derivation & Proof of Inversion:**
  In the two-indicator budget framework of Section 3.3, let $w = (w_1, w_2) > 0$ with relative price ratio $r = w_2 / w_1$ representing the price of indicator $s_2$ in units of $s_1$.
  The net change vectors for actions FAST and SLOW are:
  $$\Delta_1 = \Delta_{\rm FAST} = (-1, +2), \qquad \Delta_2 = \Delta_{\rm SLOW} = (+1, -1)$$
  An assessor with weight vector $w$ licenses an action $\Delta$ if and only if $w \cdot \Delta \ge 0$:
  1. For **FAST**:
     $$w \cdot \Delta_1 = -w_1 + 2w_2 \ge 0 \iff 2w_2 \ge w_1 \iff r = \frac{w_2}{w_1} \ge \frac{1}{2}$$
     Thus, FAST is licensed when $s_2$ is relatively **expensive** ($r \ge 0.5$).
  2. For **SLOW**:
     $$w \cdot \Delta_2 = +w_1 - w_2 \ge 0 \iff w_1 \ge w_2 \iff r = \frac{w_2}{w_1} \le 1$$
     Thus, SLOW is licensed when $s_2$ is relatively **cheap** ($r \le 1.0$).
  3. Consequently:
     - When $r < 0.5$ (Low $s_2$-price): Only SLOW is licensed ($w \cdot \Delta_2 > 0$, $w \cdot \Delta_1 < 0$).
     - When $0.5 \le r \le 1.0$ (Intermediate price): Both FAST and SLOW are licensed.
     - When $r > 1.0$ (High $s_2$-price): Only FAST is licensed ($w \cdot \Delta_1 > 0$, $w \cdot \Delta_2 < 0$).

  The text at line 207 states the exact opposite ("Low-$s_2$-price assessors license FAST only; high-$s_2$-price assessors license SLOW only"). This was further confirmed against the companion execution witness `research_program/paper1_instantiation/typed_false_positive_instantiation.py` (line 62: $r=2.0$ licenses FAST only; $r=0.5$ licenses SLOW only).
* **Recommended Fix:** Change line 207 to:
  > `"Low-$s_2$-price assessors ($r \le \rho_2$) license SLOW only; high-$s_2$-price assessors ($r \ge \rho_1$) license FAST only; assessors at intermediate prices ($\rho_2 \le r \le \rho_1$) license both."`

#### Finding 1.3 — Topological Imprecision in Definition 4.1 ($C = \mathbb R^n_+ \setminus \{0\}$)
* **Location:** Line 148 (Section 3.1)
* **Severity:** **Minor (Topological Rigor)**
* **Quoted Text:**
  > `"$C = \mathbb R^n_+ \setminus \{0\}$ (the closed nonnegative cone)"`
* **Analysis:**
  The punctured nonnegative cone $\mathbb R^n_+ \setminus \{0\}$ does not contain its limit point $0 \in \mathbb R^n$, so it is not a closed subset of $\mathbb R^n$. In convex geometry, $\mathbb R^n_+$ is the closed nonnegative cone, while $\mathbb R^n_+ \setminus \{0\}$ is the punctured nonnegative orthant.
* **Recommended Fix:** Change to:
  > `"$C = \mathbb R^n_+ \setminus \{0\}$ (the punctured nonnegative cone)"` or `"$C = \mathbb R^n_+$ (the closed nonnegative cone)"` depending on whether origin extinction is admissible.

#### Finding 2.5 — Imprecise Quantifier Scope in Adversarial Theorem 3.4
* **Location:** Line 139 (Section 3.2)
* **Severity:** **Minor (Mathematical Precision)**
* **Quoted Text:**
  > `"every trajectory entering $\mathcal S_a$ exits $\mathcal S$ within time at most $a/\varepsilon$"`
* **Analysis:**
  Under an adversarial disturbance system $\dot x \in f(x, \mathcal D)$, if the disturbance acts benignly (e.g. $d(t) = 0$), the trajectory may remain in $\mathcal S$ indefinitely. The mathematical property proved is that there *exists* an admissible adversarial disturbance trajectory $d(t)$ under which the state is forced out within time $a/\varepsilon$. Stating that *every* trajectory exits overstates the result before the proof restricts it to the worst-case disturbance.
* **Recommended Fix:** Change to:
  > `"for every initial condition in $\mathcal S_a$, there exists an admissible adversarial disturbance under which the trajectory exits $\mathcal S$ within time at most $a/\varepsilon$"`

### Paper 4: Delay Dynamics (`papers/paper4_delay_dynamics/manuscript.md`)

#### Finding 4.1 — Subscript Label Inversion in Shifted Delays ($\tau_- > \tau_+$)
* **Location:** Line 399 (Section 5.3, Proposition 5.2)
* **Severity:** **Moderate (Notational Confusion)**
* **Quoted Text:**
  > `"shifts the fundamental delays by $\pi/\omega$ on each family: $\tau_- = 128.374$ yr and $\tau_+ = 70.697$ yr, with both shifts strictly positive ..."`
* **Analysis:**
  The unshifted fundamental Hopf delays for Candidate A are:
  - Crossing 1: $\omega_1 \approx 0.02519$ rad/yr, $\tau = 3.666$ yr (originally labeled $\tau_-$)
  - Crossing 2: $\omega_2 \approx 0.03944$ rad/yr, $\tau = 150.358$ yr (originally labeled $\tau_+$)
  
  When applying the half-period shift $+\pi/\omega$:
  $$\Delta \tau_1 = \frac{\pi}{0.02519} \approx 124.708\text{ yr} \implies \tau_1' = 3.666 + 124.708 = 128.374\text{ yr}$$
  $$\Delta \tau_2 = \frac{\pi}{0.03944} \approx 79.661\text{ yr} \implies \tau_2' = 150.358 - 79.661 = 70.697\text{ yr}$$
  
  Because the manuscript preserved the original crossing index labels ($\tau_-$ from crossing 1, $\tau_+$ from crossing 2), it outputs $\tau_- = 128.374$ yr and $\tau_+ = 70.697$ yr. This violates standard mathematical notation where the subscript $-$ denotes the lower bound and $+$ denotes the upper bound ($\tau_- \le \tau_+$), making $\tau_- > \tau_+$ highly disorienting.
* **Recommended Fix:** Clarify the branch mapping explicitly:
  > `"shifts the fundamental delays by $\pi/\omega$ on each family, yielding shifted crossings $\tau_1' = 128.374$ yr (from the $\omega_1$ branch) and $\tau_2' = 70.697$ yr (from the $\omega_2$ branch); re-ordered on the positive delay axis, the lower crossing is $\tau_-' = 70.697$ yr and the upper crossing is $\tau_+' = 128.374$ yr."`
