## 1. Executive Summary

The four Wave E empirical manuscripts document rigorous out-of-sample forecast evaluations and viability-kernel intervention tests across two real-world systems: Northern cod (*Gadus morhua*, NAFO 2J3KL) and the Edwards Aquifer (San Antonio Pool, index well J-17). Both systems cleanly demonstrate the programme’s model-ablation doctrine: additional mathematical complexity is rejected unless it strictly improves preregistered out-of-sample prediction or intervention selection.

However, line-level auditing revealed several **concrete mathematical formulation errors, narrative and simulation contradictions, inverted figure-file references, model identifier collisions, and missing structural sections**:
1. **Mathematical Inconsistency in Cod Surplus Formula (`wave_E_cod_forecast_ladder.md` §3):** The surplus equation specifies an Allee factor $\frac{S_t - \mathfrak s}{K - \mathfrak s}$ and states `"with $\mathfrak s=0$ unless an Allee term is active"`. Setting $\mathfrak s = 0$ yields a cubic growth model multiplied by $S_t/K$, rather than the standard quadratic Schaefer logistic model $r S_t (1 - S_t/K)$ actually implemented in code.
2. **Simulation Contradiction & Terminology Leak (`wave_E_cod_intervention.md` §2.5):** The text states that `"under the critical-period rule, the path is below the LRP already in 1991"`. This introduces a terminology leak from the Edwards Aquifer paper (where CPM is Critical Period Management; in Cod it is the DFO Precautionary Approach "critical zone" or "cascade") and is contradicted by the simulation replay: under the critical-zone rule S1, catch is cut to 0 in 1991, keeping 1991 SSB at **906.5 kt** (above the 884.6 kt LRP). Only under the cascade (cpm) does 1991 SSB fall below the LRP (876.5 kt).
3. **Inverted Figure Filenames (`wave_E_edwards_forecast_ladder.md` §5.4 & §5.5):** Figure 4 (climate-informed recharge) embeds `fig5_pass2.png`, whereas Figure 5 (Comal Springs service fibre) embeds `fig4_fibre.png`, creating an inverted file-to-caption mapping.
4. **Model Nomenclature Collision (`wave_E_edwards_forecast_ladder.md` §5.4):** Table 6 designates models as `M2_enso` and `M2_precip`, whereas the companion discussion in line 207 designates them as `M2_Renso` and `M2_Rprecip`.
5. **Structural Omissions in Edwards Intervention (`wave_E_edwards_intervention.md`):** Unlike the other three manuscripts (including its direct analogue `wave_E_cod_intervention.md`), the Edwards intervention paper completely omits an **Abstract** and a **References** section.
6. **Bibliographic Defects:** Schijns et al. (2021) has an incomplete dangling page number (`2675--.`) in both cod papers, and multiple references in the cod ladder bibliography are uncited in the text.

### 1. `wave_e_cod/manuscript/wave_E_cod_forecast_ladder.md`

#### Finding 1.1 — Mathematical Formulation Inconsistency in Surplus Equation
* **Location:** Lines 58–62 (Section 3)
* **Severity:** **High (Mathematical / Conceptual Error)**
* **Quoted Text:**
  ```markdown
  \[
  S_{t+1}=\bigl[S_t+rS_t\bigl(1-S_t/K\bigr)\tfrac{S_t-\mathfrak s}{K-\mathfrak s}-C_t+\varepsilon_t\bigr]_+
  \]

  with $\mathfrak s=0$ unless an Allee term is active.
  ```
* **Analysis:**
  If $\mathfrak s = 0$ is substituted into the stated formula, the Allee factor becomes:
  $$\frac{S_t - \mathfrak s}{K - \mathfrak s} \;\xrightarrow{\mathfrak s = 0}\; \frac{S_t - 0}{K - 0} = \frac{S_t}{K}$$
  This results in an effective growth term:
  $$g(S_t) = r S_t \left(1 - \frac{S_t}{K}\right)\left(\frac{S_t}{K}\right)$$
  which is a cubic model with a pseudo-Allee threshold at zero, multiplying the standard surplus by $S_t/K$. This is **not** the standard autonomous Schaefer logistic surplus production model $r S_t (1 - S_t/K)$.
  
  In the Python implementation (`wave_e_cod/src/run_ladder.py`, lines 42–47), the Allee factor is conditional:
  ```python
  g = r * S * (1.0 - S / K)
  if s_allee is not None:
    gap = max(K - s_allee, 10.0)
    g = g * (S - s_allee) / gap
  return g
  ```
  When Allee is inactive (`allee=False`), `s_allee` is `None` and $g = r S (1 - S/K)$. Furthermore, Paper 5 (§6.2, line 328) correctly defines this: `"the Schaefer model is the Allee-factor-1 specialisation"`. Stating that $\mathfrak s = 0$ deactivates the Allee term is mathematically invalid.
* **Correction:** Replace lines 58–62 with:
  $$\begin{aligned}
  S_{t+1} &= \bigl[S_t + g(S_t) - C_t + \varepsilon_t\bigr]_+, \\
  g(S_t) &= r S_t \left(1 - \frac{S_t}{K}\right) \times \begin{cases} 1 & \text{(Schaefer, Allee inactive)} \\ \dfrac{S_t - \mathfrak s}{K - \mathfrak s} & \text{(Allee active, $\mathfrak s > 0$)} \end{cases}
  \end{aligned}$$

### 2. `wave_e_cod/manuscript/wave_E_cod_intervention.md`

#### Finding 2.1 — Simulation Contradiction and Terminology Leak in Stress Replay
* **Location:** Lines 115–118 (Section 2.5)
* **Severity:** **Moderate (Simulation Consistency & Domain Terminology)**
* **Quoted Text:**
  > `"under every flat cap of 60 kt and deeper, and under the critical-period rule, the path is below the LRP already in 1991; zero catch and the small-removals policies hold 1991 above the limit (906.5 kt) yet every policy falls below it by 1992 ..."`
* **Analysis:**
  There are three distinct flaws in this sentence:
  1. **Terminology Leak:** The phrase *"the critical-period rule"* imports terminology from the Edwards Aquifer Critical Period Management (CPM) framework. In Northern cod, DFO operates under the Precautionary Approach framework designating the **Critical Zone** (policy S1), or the multi-stage **cascade** (policy cpm), as defined in Section 1 (lines 36–39).
  2. **Contradiction with Replay Data for S1:** Inspection of the verified simulation results (`results/intervention_results.json`, key `stress_replay_1990s`) reveals the actual 1991 trajectory values:
     - `flat_0`: 1991 SSB = **906.52 kt** (holds $\ge \mathrm{LRP} = 884.6$)
     - `BAU` ($C=5$): 1991 SSB = **901.52 kt** (holds $\ge \mathrm{LRP}$)
     - `S1` (critical-zone rule): In 1990, SSB was 861.92 kt $< \mathrm{LRP}$. Therefore, S1's critical-zone trigger activated, cutting catch to 0 kt. Consequently, under S1, 1991 SSB was **906.52 kt** (identically holding above the LRP!).
     - `cpm` (the cascade): In 1990, SSB was in $[0.75\text{LRP}, \text{LRP})$, prescribing $C = 30$ kt. This resulted in 1991 SSB = **876.52 kt** (falling below the LRP).
     
     Therefore, stating that *"under the critical-period rule, the path is below the LRP already in 1991"* is factually false for the critical-zone rule S1 (which stays *above* the LRP) and holds only for the cascade (cpm).
  3. **Ambiguous Phrasing ("60 kt and deeper"):** In harvest management, "deeper" typically denotes a deeper harvest reduction (i.e. smaller catch, such as flat 0 kt). Here, flat 0 kt holds 1991 above the limit, while higher catch levels (60, 120, 180, 240 kt) drop below. The intended meaning was "flat caps of 60 kt and higher removals".
* **Correction:** Revise lines 115–118 to read:
  > `"under every flat cap of 60 kt and above ($C \in \{60, 120, 180, 240\}$ kt) and under the multi-stage cascade (cpm), the path falls below the LRP already in 1991 (reaching 876.5 kt under cpm); zero catch, business-as-usual (5 kt), and the critical-zone rule S1 (which cuts catch to zero upon detecting $S_{1990} < \mathrm{LRP}$) hold 1991 above the limit (906.5 kt for flat-0 and S1; 901.5 kt for BAU), yet every policy falls below the LRP by 1992 ..."`
