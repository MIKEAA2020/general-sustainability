### 1. Arithmetic Inconsistency in the Concordance Audit (Papers 1, 3, and 5)

**The Flaw:** This is a blatant arithmetic contradiction.
*   If there are 409 total rows and 354 are closed, then **55 rows** must remain open ($409 - 354 = 55$).
*   If only 27 rows remain open, then **382 rows** must be closed ($409 - 27 = 382$).
*   The sum of the stated figures ($354 + 27 = 381$) leaves exactly **28 rows** of the concordance entirely unaccounted for in the audit trail.

### 1. Mathematical Flaw: Existential Quantification in Probabilistic Viability
**Location:** `general_theory_of_sustainability_v0.2_comprehensive.md` (§5.3) and `ms_part1.md` (§7, Operator I).

The manuscripts correctly define deterministic robust sustainability using a rigorous alternating quantifier structure:
> "A system is robustly sustainable under $\Omega$ if $\exists \pi \in U \quad \forall w \in W: \quad z^{\pi,w}(t) \in K^* \quad \forall t \in T$."

**The Flaw:** When defining the probabilistic analogue, the text weakens the criterion to:
> "For probabilistic disturbances, a weaker criterion is $\Pr[z(t) \in K^* \ \forall t \in T] \ge 1 - \varepsilon$."

This is formally incomplete and mathematically flawed. By dropping the existential quantifier ($\exists \pi \in U$) outside the probability measure, the statement fails to define a **viability kernel**. A probability bound on a trajectory is meaningless unless it asserts that *there exists a policy* capable of achieving that bound against the random disturbance $w$. The correct formulation must anchor the policy existence: $\exists \pi \in U : \Pr_w[z^{\pi,w}(t) \in K^* \ \forall t] \ge 1 - \varepsilon$. Without this, the definition describes a property of a specific unstated open-loop trajectory rather than the robust capacity of the control system.
