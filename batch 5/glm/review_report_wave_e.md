## 1. Executive summary

These four manuscripts are in substantially better numerical shape than the core papers reviewed previously. Every score table in all four papers — cod Tables 3–8, Edwards Tables 3, 4, 6, the in-text post-2007 subsets, the fixed-window climate numbers, and the Comal fibre scores — reproduces **exactly** from the committed artifacts at the printed precision, and the intervention papers' kernel/attractor/supply/stress numbers reproduce to the last displayed digit, including values I re-derived analytically from the fitted maps (the cod kernel T=1 row, the Edwards attractor table with its uniform 3.16-ft spacing, the certified boundaries 662.2/697.8/706.7, the 7.22% minimal cut, the replay paths 906.5→622.3 / 611.5→366.3, the 1950s open-loop diagnostic). Both intervention runners and all Edwards runners regenerate their outputs **byte for byte** in this environment.

The defects that remain are concentrated in four places: (a) one results paragraph in the cod forecast paper that presents the **Ω_2016 Brier scores inside the Ω_xte section** — a specification-mixing error in a paper whose most-repeated invariant is "the two SSB columns are not mixed", and whose concluding sentence ("No model improves the Brier score over persistence either") is **false** for the specification it appears to describe (W01); (b) a silently under-converged infinite-horizon kernel boundary in the cod intervention table, whose correct value sits in the same results file (W02); (c) a wrong horizon constant in the Edwards intervention paper ("empty beyond T ≈ 14 years"; the true crossover is 12.7, and the paper's own convention used two sentences later gives ≈ 13) (W03); and (d) a reproducibility claim ("every archived result file was regenerated byte for byte") that is environment-fragile: in this environment 13 of 19 cod result files regenerate with differences, and one Table 6 row (M1b) lands on a different local optimum, moving 152/446 → 153/463 kt (W04).

## 2. HIGH-severity finding

### W01 — Cod forecast §5.3: the Brier paragraph reports the Ω_2016 scores as Ω_xte scores, and its conclusion is false for Ω_xte

**Location:** `wave_e_cod/manuscript/wave_E_cod_forecast_ladder.md`, line 192 (inside §5.3 "Alternative assessment specification", the Ω_xte section; the preceding and following paragraphs are xte-only).

**Verbatim:**
> "On the rolling Brier score for $\mathbf{1}\{\hat S<\mathrm{LRP}\}$ (the secondary score declared above): persistence 0.00 at both horizons, M1 0.04 ($h{=}1$) and 0.05 ($h{=}5$), M1b 0.00 and 0.05, M2 0.08 and 0.14, M3 0.08 and 0.10, M4 0.08 and 0.14. No model improves the Brier score over persistence either."

**Problem.** The six quoted numbers are the **Ω_2016** Brier scores, digit for digit. Committed evidence:

- `wave_e_cod/results/rolling_summary.csv` (Ω_2016, primary pass): persist 0.000/0.000; M1 0.040/0.0476; M1b 0.000/0.0476; M2 0.080/0.1429; M3 0.080/0.0952; M4 0.080/0.1429 — i.e. exactly "0.00/0.00, 0.04/0.05, 0.00/0.05, 0.08/0.14, 0.08/0.10, 0.08/0.14".
- `wave_e_cod/results/xte_rolling_summary.csv` (the specification §5.3 actually describes; LRP = 276 kt, n = 63/59 naive origins): persist **0.063/0.271**; M1 0.051/0.455; M1b 0.153/0.473; M2 0.068/0.364; M3 **0.017**/0.309; M4 0.068/0.382.

Two consequences:

1. **Every number in the paragraph is wrong for the object it appears to describe.** "Persistence 0.00 at both horizons" is true only on Ω_2016 (where every target year 1991–2015 is below the 884.6-kt LRP); on Ω_2016's xte counterpart the rolling Brier is 0.063 (h=1) and 0.271 (h=5).
2. **The concluding sentence is false for Ω_xte.** On the committed xte scores, M3's h=1 Brier is 0.0169 (1 misclassification in 59) versus persistence's 0.0635 (4 in 63) — M3 improves the secondary score by roughly a factor of four. The paragraph's claim "No model improves the Brier score over persistence either" is true for Ω_2016 (0.000 is unbeatable) and false for Ω_xte.

This matters beyond the numbers themselves: the paper's central methodological invariant — stated in the abstract ("The series are not mixed"), §2 ("The two SSB columns are not mixed"), §6, and the data-availability section — is that nothing from one specification leaks into the other. This paragraph is a leak in the printed results. The retention verdicts are unaffected (retention is decided on primary RMSE, where persistence wins on Ω_xte at both horizons, 88 vs 120–206 kt), but a reader auditing the secondary score is given wrong values and a wrong summary conclusion.

**Suggested fix.** Replace the paragraph with the actual xte values and correct the conclusion, e.g.:

> "On the rolling Brier score for 1{Ŝ<LRP} on Ω_xte: persistence 0.06 (h=1) and 0.27 (h=5); M1 0.05/0.45; M1b 0.15/0.47; M2 0.07/0.36; M3 0.02/0.31; M4 0.07/0.38. M3 improves the one-year Brier score over persistence (0.02 versus 0.06); no model improves it at h=5, and no model improves the primary RMSE score at either horizon, so the retention verdict is unchanged."

(The Ω_2016 Brier values, if the authors want to keep them, belong in §5.1 with an explicit "on Ω_2016" label — they are currently printed nowhere else.)

## 3. MODERATE-severity findings

### W02 — Cod intervention §2.1: the flat-180-kt infinite-horizon boundary is an unconverged iterate, and the correct value is in the same results file

**Location:** `wave_e_cod/manuscript/wave_E_cod_intervention.md`, line 49 (Table, §2.1 "Kernels (nominal)").

**Verbatim:**
> "| flat 180 kt | 1297.1 | 1171.0 | 991.2 | 2335.4 |"

**Problem.** The T=∞ column entry 2335.4 is not the lower boundary of the maximal invariant set; it is the **300th backward-iteration iterate** of an iteration that needs 1,456 steps to converge. Three mutually confirming computations:

1. **Fixed-point algebra.** With the committed fit (r = 0.2368694, K = 5000, C = 180, UC-q10 floor e = −114.8477), the invariant set's lower boundary is the smaller root of rS(1−S/K) = 294.8477, namely **S = 2338.27**.
2. **The results file itself.** `wave_e_cod/results/intervention_results.json` → `steady_states.flat_75.UC_q10.worst_case_fixed_points = [2338.27, 2661.73]`. The same JSON's `kernels.flat_75.UC_q10.inf.nominal` says `[2335.415…, 10000]`. The file therefore contradicts itself by 2.86 kt.
3. **Extended iteration.** Running the kernel recursion with the convergence test the code itself specifies (1e-9) converges to 2338.2734 in 1,456 steps; the committed 300-step cap (`src/run_intervention.py`, line 201: `max_iter = 1 if T == 1 else (300 if T == "inf" else int(T))`) returns the 300th iterate **silently** — the non-convergence is not flagged anywhere.

The convergence is slow precisely for this policy because its fixed point has F′(2338.3) = 1.0155, so each preimage step shrinks the residual gap by only ~1.5%; flat-120 (F′ = 1.108) and flat-60 (F′ = 1.152) converge within the cap, which is why only this one entry is affected.

No decision depends on the value: flat caps are not retention candidates, S1/cpm fail clause (a) of the retention rule regardless, and the qualitative statement (the 180-kt cap's infinite-horizon kernel sits far above the LRP) is unchanged. But the printed number is wrong, the error is silently produced by a truncation bug, and the repo's verification battery cannot catch it because re-running the same code reproduces the same wrong value byte for byte.

**Suggested fix.** Raise the iteration cap (e.g. `max_iter = 5000 if T == "inf"`) or add a convergence assertion that raises on non-convergence; regenerate; print **2338.3**. (Also worth a one-line note in the manuscript that T=∞ boundaries are computed to a 1e-9 fixpoint tolerance.)

### W03 — Edwards intervention §4.2: BAU's physical kernel empties at T = 13, not "beyond T ≈ 14"

**Location:** `wave_e_edwards/manuscript/wave_E_edwards_intervention.md`, lines 94–96.

**Verbatim:**
> "Under the physical threshold (618 ft) and UC-min, BAU's kernel boundary climbs 618.8 (T=1) → 625.6 (T=5) → 658.4 (T=10) and the kernel is **empty** beyond $T \approx 14$ years — BAU is not robustly viable against a perpetual drought-of-record."

**Problem.** The three printed boundary values are exact (I reproduce 618.78 / 625.59 / 658.42 from a = 0.746094, H* = 615.72), but the horizon constant is wrong. The worst-case map is the affine contraction H ↦ 0.746094·H + 156.33 with attractor H* = 615.72, so the T-step kernel boundary is

  boundary(T) = H* + (618 − H*)/a^T,

which exceeds the domain top (710 ft) when (618−615.72)/a^T > 710−615.72, i.e. at **T > 12.71**. The kernel is nonempty through T = 12 (boundary 692.6) and empty from T = 13. The committed horizon grid {1,2,3,5,8,10,15,20,∞} contains no T between 10 and 15, so the committed artifacts show nonempty-at-10 / empty-at-15 and no direct evidence for "14".

The error is internal as well: two sentences later the paper states the zero-pumping institutional horizons by the **continuous-crossover** convention — "empty beyond T ≈ 6 (UC-min) / T ≈ 11 (UC-q10)" — whose crossovers are 5.45 and 10.96 (both round to the printed values). Applying the same convention to BAU at the physical threshold gives 12.71 → "≈ 13", not "≈ 14". `wave_e_edwards/SPECIFICATION.md` repeats the wrong constant ("BAU not robustly viable beyond ~14 yr under the perpetual-1956 floor").

**Suggested fix.** "…and the kernel is empty beyond T ≈ 13 years (the T=12 boundary is 692.6 ft; the T=13 kernel is empty)" — and correct the same constant in `SPECIFICATION.md`. Nothing else in either paper depends on the value.

### W04 — Cod forecast, Data & code availability: the byte-for-byte reproducibility claim is environment-fragile, and one Table 6 row is numerically unstable across environments

**Location:** `wave_e_cod/manuscript/wave_E_cod_forecast_ladder.md`, line 256.

**Verbatim:**
> "All computations are deterministic. The analysis was re-executed in a fresh environment: every archived result file was regenerated byte for byte, each file matching its recorded checksum, and all scored rows recompute from the per-observation forecast files and the committed series."

**Problem.** In this review environment (Python 3.12, numpy 2.1.3, scipy 1.14.1; no `requirements.txt`/environment pin exists anywhere in the repo), I executed all eight cod runners and all five Edwards runners:

- **Regenerated byte for byte:** both intervention runners (cod and Edwards), and all Edwards prediction runners (`run_ladder.py`, `run_recharge.py` — OLS-based, fully deterministic).
- **Regenerated with differences: 13 of the 19 cod result files** — everything produced by `run_ladder.py`, `run_xte.py`, `run_capelin_regime.py`, `run_capelin_index.py` (the L-BFGS-B-based scripts). Twelve of the thirteen differ only at the 4th–6th decimal, which does not touch any printed number.
- **One material instability:** `run_xte.py`'s **M1b** rolling row lands on a different local optimum: committed 151.63 / 445.48 kt (h=1 / h=5, printed in Table 6 as "152" and "446"), re-run here 153.19 / 462.53 kt (would print "153" and "463"; Brier 0.47 → 0.51). The script is deterministic *within* an environment (two consecutive runs here are byte-identical), so the committed values are pinned to the original environment's optimizer version, not to the algorithm.

The manuscript's unqualified claim therefore holds only in an environment whose SciPy matches the original one. The qualitative conclusion (persistence wins on Ω_xte at both horizons) is robust — M1b loses by 60+ kt either way — but Table 6's M1b entries are not, which is ironic given the paper's own (correct) emphasis on M1b's identification fragility. Note the Edwards forecast paper makes the same style of claim and it **holds** in this environment.

**Suggested fix.** (a) Commit an environment pin (e.g. `wave_e_cod/requirements.txt` with the exact numpy/scipy/pandas versions) and reference it in the data-availability section; (b) qualify the sentence ("re-executed in a fresh environment with the pinned library versions; every archived result file was regenerated byte for byte"); (c) optionally add one sentence noting that the M1b xte rolling scores are optimizer-sensitive at the ±17 kt level (a different local Allee optimum), consistent with the identification fragility already discussed.

### W12 — Edwards intervention runner: latent `lt` asymmetry (verified inert, but unpatched)

**Suggested fix.** Port the cod runner's `lt` (and its comment) into `wave_e_edwards/src/run_intervention.py`, re-run (outputs will regenerate byte-identically given the verified inertness), and keep a one-line note in the code.
