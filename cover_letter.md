I am pleased to submit the manuscript **"The Limits of Compensatory Aggregation: A Formal Separation of Weak and Strong Sustainability Assessment"** for consideration in [Journal Name].

**Summary.** The paper addresses a foundational question in sustainability assessment: when a weighted aggregate index over several "floors" — separately-binding constraints on distinct capital forms — certifies a transition as sustainable, does any single transition actually satisfy every floor? The manuscript proves a separation: there exist explicit, finite transition data on which every nonnegative weighting individually accepts some transition, while no single transition is accepted under all weightings at once. The acceptance gap is identified exactly with an "impossibility region," which a resource threshold splits into states rescuable by a resource-controlled action and states rescuable only through that action at a defined cost. Two bounding results complete the picture: convexifying the action menu (fractional allocation of control flows) collapses the gap exactly onto the compensatory region, whereas discrete time-sharing does not — so the separation is a property of the action space, not of the doctrines themselves.

**The core mechanism, in plain terms.** Consider two reservoirs that must each never run dry, together with a limited reserve. Inspectors differ: some check each reservoir separately, while others read only the combined level, each with their own weighting of the two. Suppose two operating modes are available, one that briefly drains the first reservoir mid-interval and then refills it, and another that drains the second in the same way. If neither reservoir carries enough headroom, then every inspector who uses a combined reading accepts *some* mode — but no single mode keeps both reservoirs individually above zero. The weighted aggregate is therefore lossless at any fixed instant, yet it over-certifies at the level of assessment, because certification is a statement about transitions, not about a number. The manuscript formalizes precisely this failure, locates it, and shows that running the two modes simultaneously in a fractional mix restores a single plan acceptable to every inspector, while merely alternating between the modes does not.

**Method and scope.** The results are stated for a typed exact-tube transition datum and proved analytically; the finite rational witness is independently checked by exact-integer computation (25 checks on a 31³ = 29,791-state grid). The paper makes no empirical claims; its consequences for composite-index design and for per-floor reporting requirements are developed in the policy implications (§5.4).
