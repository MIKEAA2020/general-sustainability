# Governing Renewable Resources Under Institutional Delay: Instability, Phase-Stabilization, and Review Cadence

---

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Delays in the Governance Loop

Time delays are widely recognized as primary drivers of instability, persistent oscillations, and systemic collapse in renewable resource systems. Since Hutchinson’s classic demonstration that delayed self-limitation destabilizes logistic population growth, mathematical ecologists and resource economists have studied lags in natural dynamics:
* **Market-driven supply cycles:** Ezekiel's classic pork-cycle analysis showed how production lags create price-quantity oscillations.
* **Ecological outbreak dynamics:** Ludwig, Jones, and Holling demonstrated how delayed feedback and hysteresis generate sudden population crashes.
* **Delayed density dependence:** Canonical analyses of single-species delayed differential equations by Gurney, Blythe, and Nisbet confirmed that delayed recruitment drives multi-generational population cycles.
* **Laboratory verifications:** Costantino and colleagues verified delayed-feedback population cycles in controlled beetle cultures.

Subsequent decades have expanded this literature into stage-structured models, juvenile maturation lags, and harvested predator–prey systems where coupled ecological delays generate complex bifurcations.

Yet in virtually all of these classical formulations, the delay is treated as an *ecological* or *biological* property:
$$\text{Spawning} \xrightarrow{\quad\text{maturation lag}\quad} \text{Recruitment}$$
$$\text{Consumption} \xrightarrow{\quad\text{metabolic lag}\quad} \text{Reproduction}$$

This paper investigates a different source of delay that operates on comparable—and often longer—timescales: **the institutional governance loop**. 

```
                               THE GOVERNANCE LOOP
                               
  Resource Stock (N) --------> Scientific Stock Assessment
          ^                                |
          |                                v (Information Filtering)
          |                         Deficit Memory (Z)
          |                                |
          |                                v (Deployment Delay τ)
    Harvest Effort (E) <------ Policy Implementation / Quota Setting
```

In any institutional resource regime, management follows a multi-stage process:
1. Scientific stock assessment detects a biological decline or yield deficit.
2. The raw assessment signal is synthesized, filtered, and integrated into an institutional memory of historical deficits.
3. Policy bodies negotiate and mandate a change in harvest effort, quotas, or fleet capacity.
4. An administrative and operational lag separates the political decision from physical deployment on the water.

The behavioural and institutional basis for these delays is well documented. Laboratory experiments in dynamic resource management show that human decision-makers systematically misperceive stock accumulation, responding too late and with disproportionate intensity. Field evidence demonstrates the scale of these delays. The collapse of the northern cod (*Gadus morhua*) off Newfoundland proceeded through years of scientific warnings and incremental quota adjustments before the total moratorium was declared in 1992—followed, thirty-two years later, by the contested 2024 reopening. Recent fisheries research treats the stock assessment cadence itself as an active management design parameter, recognizing that the interval between administrative reviews directly controls yield variance and stock collapse risk.

Despite widespread empirical recognition, dynamical systems theory lacks a formal analysis of what happens when the primary feedback delay is situated inside the *governance controller* rather than in the *ecosystem*. Two central questions arise:
1. **Feedback Sign:** Does the sign structure of the institutional feedback policy—an aggressive *mobilizing* response that increases effort to offset falling revenues versus a *protective* quota-tracking rule that caps extraction—produce qualitatively different bifurcation landscapes?
2. **Review Cadence:** How does continuous-time administrative delay compare with discrete, periodic policy reviews (sample-and-hold governance)?

The model presented here is deliberately stock-agnostic. The state $N$ represents an arbitrary harvested renewable stock measured in biomass or material units, governed by institutional memory and harvest capacity. By integrating institutional delays alongside biological maturation lags, we analyze governance and ecology within a single, unified analytical framework.

#### The Asset Overshoot Mechanism
The economic overshoot mechanism analyzed throughout this paper can be understood through a simple asset analogy:

> A hen produces a sustainable flow of eggs, $S(N)$, each year. If consumption demand $D$ exceeds this sustainable yield, the shortfall cannot be conjured out of thin air. To satisfy immediate demand, the owner slaughters the hen—liquidating the underlying productive asset to cover the deficit. This creates a self-reinforcing downward spiral: liquidating the asset lowers future sustainable yield, which widens the deficit, prompting further asset liquidation.

When institutional delays are introduced, the deficit is observed, processed, and acted upon with a lag. The delay does not create the underlying structural shortfall; rather, it dictates the phase at which the corrective or destructive policy response arrives. 

Under the mobilizing rule, where falling catches prompt fleet expansion or subsidies to sustain historic yields, this loop destabilizes the stock. Under the protective rule, where deficits immediately trigger reductions in extraction toward a safe cap, the downward spiral is broken.

### 1.2 Summary of Contributions

1. **A Gated Three-State Dynamical Core:** We formulate a three-state ordinary/delay differential equation system linking renewable biomass ($N$), filtered deficit memory ($Z$), and harvest effort ($E$). A multiplicative saturation gate rigorously bounds harvest effort within a physically realistic interval $[0, E_{\max}]$, proving the positive invariance of the state space without ad hoc boundary clipping.
2. **The Exact Hopf Cubic and Even-Pairs Cancellation:** Linearizing around the positive interior equilibrium reduces the characteristic quasi-polynomial to an exact cubic equation in the squared oscillation frequency ($\omega^2$). Because the deficit memory tracks the rate of biomass depletion, a structural cancellation occurs: the cross-terms vanish identically. Consequently, the positive roots of the cubic occur in *even pairs*—yielding generically zero or two Hopf bifurcations across the delay axis, rather than an arbitrary spectrum.
3. **Interval-Certified Stability Crossings:** Using interval Newton iteration with outward directed rounding, we enclose the exact critical delay values within rigorous 13-digit intervals (e.g., $\tau_- \approx 3.67$ years and $\tau_+ \approx 150.36$ years for our primary baseline parameterization). We prove that for the mobilizing rule, an *intermediate* window of bureaucratic delay paradoxically stabilizes an otherwise unstable equilibrium.
4. **Structural Sign Separation (The No-Hopf Theorem):** We demonstrate that feedback sign dictates the qualitative dynamics of the system. While the mobilizing rule produces oscillatory Hopf bifurcations and unstable subcritical cycles, the protective quota-tracking rule guarantees local exponential stability across *all* possible delays ($\tau \ge 0$). We prove a general **No-Hopf Theorem** for the calibrated protective rule based on Descartes' rule of signs and loop-gain bounds.
5. **Periodic Review as a Control Lever:** Replacing continuous delay with periodic, sample-and-hold governance reveals that administrative review cadence is itself a primary bifurcation parameter. For protective governance, annual reviews preserve robust stability. For mobilizing governance, annual reviews are unstable; the system only restabilizes if the review interval exceeds approximately $6.5$ years via a discrete Neimark–Sacker transition. We prove that a previously reported 2.3-year instability threshold is a numerical artifact of explicit Euler discretizations, not a property of the underlying governance system.
6. **Integration with Ecological Maturation Delays:** Coupling the institutional delay equations to a stock exhibiting intrinsic maturation delays (via Gurney–Blythe–Nisbet delay dynamics) shows that institutional cycles and biological cohort cycles interact along a hyperbolic locus $r \cdot g \approx 1.5 - 1.6$. We delineate parameter regimes where institutional delays dominate biological lags, establishing testable boundaries for resource managers.

## 2. The Model Architecture and Admissibility

### 2.1 The Gated Three-State Core

Let:
* $N(t)$ denote the harvested renewable stock, expressed in biomass or material density units;
* $Z(t)$ denote an exponentially filtered institutional memory of harvest deficits (measured in units of biomass per year);
* $E(t)$ denote the deployed institutional extraction effort (a dimensionless intensity metric, normalized relative to a maximum fleet or infrastructure capacity $E_{\max}$).

The fundamental biophysical renewal of the stock in the absence of harvesting follows a standard logistic function:
$$S(N) = r N \left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right)$$
where $r > 0$ is the intrinsic renewal rate ($\text{yr}^{-1}$) and $K > 0$ is the carrying capacity. 

Harvest extraction follows the standard Gordon–Schaefer bilinear production form:
$$H(N, E) = q E N$$
where $q > 0$ represents catchability with units $(\text{effort} \cdot \text{yr})^{-1}$.

The institutional memory filters the perceived shortfall between current harvest demand and biological replacement. To provide a smooth, non-negative signal map that responds only when extraction exceeds replenishment, we introduce the shifted softplus function:
$$\text{sp}_k(s) = \frac{1}{k}\log\left(1 + e^{k s}\right), \qquad \Phi_k(s) = \max\left\{0, \; \text{sp}_k(s) - \frac{\log 2}{k} + \delta\right\}$$
where $k > 0$ is a regularizing stiffness parameter and $\delta \ge 0$ is a baseline perception offset. Under the reference parameter choice $\delta = \frac{\log 2}{k}$, the floor is strictly inactive for all real $s$, rendering $\Phi_k(s) = \text{sp}_k(s) > 0$ smooth and strictly positive.

The full three-state continuous-delay governance core is:

$$\begin{aligned}
\dot{N}(t) &= S(N(t)) - q E(t) N(t), \\[8pt]
\dot{Z}(t) &= \frac{1}{\tau_m} \Big[ \Phi_k\big(q E(t) N(t) - S(N(t))\big) - Z(t) \Big], \\[8pt]
\dot{E}(t) &= \left(1 - \frac{E(t)}{E_{\max}}\right) \left[ \eta E(t) \left(\frac{Z(t-\tau)}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} - \frac{E(t)}{E_{\max}}\right) + \delta_0 \frac{Z(t-\tau)}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + Z(t-\tau)} \right].
\end{aligned} \tag{1}$$

```
                                  SYSTEM COUPLING
                                  
      +-----------------------------------------------------------+
      |                                                           |
      v                                                           |
   [ Stock N ] ===(Deficit: qEN - S(N))===> [ Memory Z ]          |
      |                                           |               |
      | (Harvest: qEN)                     (Delay τ)              |
      |                                           |               |
      +<============ [ Effort E ] <---------------+               |
                            ^                                     |
                            +--- Multiplicative Gate (1 - E/Emax) +
```

Notice the structural features of system (1):
* **Depletion Filtering:** Along system trajectories, the argument to the memory filter is identical to the instantaneous rate of stock depletion: $q E N - S(N) = -\dot{N}$. The memory variable $Z$ is therefore an exponentially weighted moving average of the net depletion rate, with characteristic institutional memory timescale $\tau_m$.
* **The Multiplicative Gate:** The factor $(1 - E / E_{\max})$ acts as a structural gate. Rather than enforcing an artificial boundary condition via truncation, the gate smoothly shuts off effort expansion as $E \to E_{\max}$.
* **Institutional Delay:** The parameter $\tau \ge 0$ represents the administrative delay between observing a deficit signal $Z(t-\tau)$ and fully commissioning the revised harvest effort on the water.

### 2.2 Mathematical Well-Posedness and State-Space Admissibility

For system (1) to serve as a physically valid resource model, trajectories initialized with non-negative biological stocks and bounded harvest efforts must remain bounded and non-negative for all forward time $t \ge 0$.

We define the state space of continuous history functions on the delay interval $[-\tau, 0]$:
$$C = C\left([-\tau, 0], \; \mathbb{R}^3\right)$$
and denote the admissible physical domain by the closed set:
$$\mathcal{D} = \left\{ (N, Z, E) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \;\middle|\; 0 \le N \le K, \quad Z \ge 0, \quad 0 \le E \le E_{\max} \right\}$$

#### Theorem 2.1 (Forward Invariance of the Physical Domain)
*Let the initial history function satisfy $\varphi \in C([-\tau, 0], \mathcal{D})$—that is, $\varphi(\theta) \in \mathcal{D}$ for every $\theta \in [-\tau, 0]$. Then any classical solution $(N(t), Z(t), E(t))$ of system (1) remains in $\mathcal{D}$ for all forward time $t \ge 0$ for which it exists.*

#### Proof
We verify that the vector field is subtangential (directed inwards or tangent) along every boundary face of $\mathcal{D}$:
1. **Face $N = 0$:** Here $S(0) = 0$ and the harvest rate $q E (0) = 0$. Consequently, $\dot{N} = 0$. The boundary face $N = 0$ is an invariant manifold; stocks cannot become negative.
2. **Face $N = K$:** Here $S(K) = 0$, giving $\dot{N} = -q E K \le 0$ since $E \ge 0$ and $q, K > 0$. The vector field points strictly into $\mathcal{D}$.
3. **Face $Z = 0$:** The signal function satisfies $\Phi_k(\cdot) \ge 0$ by construction. Thus, $\dot{Z} = \frac{1}{\tau_m}\Phi_k(\cdot) \ge 0$. Memory deficits cannot cross below zero.
4. **Face $E = 0$:** The gate factor evaluates to $(1 - 0) = 1$. Because $Z(t-\tau) \ge 0$ under the history hypothesis, the deployment bracket evaluates to:
   $$\dot{E} = \delta_0 \frac{Z(t-\tau)}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + Z(t-\tau)} \ge 0$$
   Effort cannot become negative.
5. **Face $E = E_{\max}$:** The multiplicative gate factor $(1 - E/E_{\max})$ vanishes identically, forcing $\dot{E} = 0$. The hyper-plane $E = E_{\max}$ is invariant; effort cannot exceed capacity.

Because the delayed variable $Z(t-\tau)$ enters only the effort equation and satisfies $Z(t-\tau) \ge 0$ for $t \in [0, \tau]$, the vector field remains subtangential on $[0, \tau]$. By the classical method of steps for delay differential equations, this property extends by mathematical induction across all subsequent intervals $[n\tau, (n+1)\tau]$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. $\blacksquare$

#### Corollary 2.1 (Global Existence and Solution Boundedness)
*Every solution of system (1) with initial data in $\mathcal{D}$ exists globally for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ and remains uniformly bounded.*

#### Proof
By Theorem 2.1, $N(t) \in [0, K]$ and $E(t) \in [0, E_{\max}]$ for all $t \ge 0$. The memory equation can be expressed as:
$$\dot{Z}(t) = \frac{1}{\tau_m} \big(\nu(t) - Z(t)\big)$$
where the driving input is bounded:
$$\nu(t) = \Phi_k\big(q E(t) N(t) - S(N(t))\big) \le \Phi_k(q E_{\max} K) \equiv \nu_{\max} < \infty$$
By the variation of constants formula:
$$Z(t) = e^{-t/\tau_m} Z(0) + \frac{1}{\tau_m}\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/\tau_m}\nu(s)\,ds \le \max\left\{ \sup_{\theta \in [-\tau, 0]} Z(\theta), \; \nu_{\max} \right\} \equiv \bar{Z} < \infty$$
Thus, the state trajectory is permanently confined to the compact set $[0, K] \times [0, \bar{Z}] \times [0, E_{\max}]$. Because the right-hand side of system (1) is continuously differentiable ($C^1$) on this compact domain under the regularized softplus function, the vector field is globally Lipschitz. By standard continuation theorems for functional differential equations, solutions exist uniquely for all forward time $t \ge 0$. $\blacksquare$

### 2.3 Extended Model Variants

To establish the structural generality of the gated core (1) (designated **M3-B** for *3-state boundary-exact*), we compare it against four related biological and institutional formulations:
* **M3-U (Ungated Core):** Retains identical stock and memory dynamics, but removes the physical gate factor $(1 - E/E_{\max})$ from the effort equation. Here, $E_{\max}$ acts only as an internal scaling reference rather than a hard physical cap.
* **M3-LC (Two-Channel Demographic Stock Law):** Decomposes stock replenishment into gross births and natural mortality:
  $$\dot{N} = \max\left\{0, \; B(N) - (1-\psi) q E N\right\} - M(N) - \psi q E N \tag{2}$$
  where $\psi \in [0, 1]$ partitions harvest effort into direct mortality of mature adults ($\psi$) versus demographic recruitment suppression ($1 - \psi$). 
* **M4-A (Active Abiotic Pool Extension):** Augments the model with a dynamic abiotic nutrient pool $A(t)$:
  $$R(N, A) = r N \left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right)\frac{A}{A + A_0}, \qquad \dot{A} = -B(N, A) + \omega_A (A^{\mathrm{eq}} - A)$$
  accounting for external geological exchange at rate $\omega_A$.
* **MPF (Primitive-Flux Material Core):** Formulates closed biomass-detritus-nutrient cycles tracking living biomass $X$, detritus $U$, and available abiotic material $A = \mathcal{M} - X - U$, rigorously conserving mass across ecological trophic compartments.

### 2.4 Approximation Bound for the Active Nutrient Extension

To demonstrate that the three-state core (1) reliably approximates models with dynamic abiotic pools on institutional timescales, consider the four-state system:
$$\dot{N} = R(N, A) - q E N, \qquad \dot{A} = -B(N, A) + \omega_A (A^{\mathrm{eq}} - A)$$
coupled to the identical $(Z, E)$ equations of system (1).

#### Lemma 2.1 (Finite-Time Trajectory Bound)
*Let $(N, Z, E)$ satisfy the three-state core (1) and let $(\tilde{N}, \tilde{Z}, \tilde{E})$ satisfy the four-state system from identical initial data, with active nutrient availability satisfying $A(t) \ge A_{\min} > 0$ over a planning horizon $[0, T]$. Define the normalized state difference norm:*
$$\|\xi(t)\|_* = \frac{|\tilde{N}(t) - N(t)|}{K} + \frac{|\tilde{Z}(t) - Z(t)|}{r K} + \frac{|\tilde{E}(t) - E(t)|}{E_{\max}}$$
*Then over the time horizon $t \in [0, T]$:*
$$\sup_{t \in [0, T]} \|\xi(t)\|_* \le C_T \left(\frac{A_0}{A_{\min} + A_0}\right)$$
*where $C_T$ depends only on the structural constants of the system and the time horizon $T$.*

#### Proof
The biological renewal mismatch between the saturated four-state model and the logistic three-state model is:
$$|R(N, A) - S(N)| = r N \left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right)\left(1 - \frac{A}{A + A_0}\right) \le \frac{r K}{4} \left(\frac{A_0}{A_{\min} + A_0}\right)$$
Because the memory and effort equations share identical functional forms across both systems, the state difference equations are driven entirely by the state discrepancies and this bounded renewal defect. Defining the history supremum $Y(t) = \sup_{s \in [-\tau, t]} \|\xi(s)\|_*$, Lipschitz continuity of the vector fields yields the retarded Gronwall integral inequality:
$$Y(t) \le C_1 \int_0^t \left( Y(s) + \frac{A_0}{A_{\min} + A_0} \right) ds$$
Applying the retarded Gronwall inequality yields the stated bound with growth constant $C_T = C_1 T e^{C_1 T}$. $\blacksquare$

Lemma 2.1 guarantees that for institutional horizons where nutrient pools remain replete ($A \gg A_0$), the low-dimensional gated core (1) reproduces the dynamics of more complex biogeochemical models.

### 2.5 The Protective Alternative and Policy Interpolation

System (1) embodies an aggressive **mobilizing** governance philosophy: when a deficit is detected, the administrative response accelerates harvest effort ($\dot{E} > 0$) to compensate for declining yields.

We now formulate the contrasting **protective** institutional architecture: a quota-tracking harvest rule designed to curb extraction effort whenever a deficit emerges:
$$\dot{E}(t) = \left(1 - \frac{E(t)}{E_{\max}}\right) \eta_p \Big[ E_{\mathrm{cap}}\big(Z(t - \tau_p)\big) - E(t) \Big] \tag{3}$$
Here, the target effort cap $E_{\mathrm{cap}}(Z)$ is a smooth, monotonically decreasing function of perceived resource stress:
$$E_{\mathrm{cap}}(Z) = E_0 \left(\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + Z}\right), \qquad E_0 = E^*_A \left(\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + \delta}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)$$
This calibration ensures that at the baseline interior steady state $(N^*, Z^*, E^*)$, the protective rule shares the exact equilibrium point as the mobilizing rule ($E_{\mathrm{cap}}(\delta) = E^*$), allowing direct, unconfounded comparisons between the two strategies.

To systematically analyze the transition between mobilizing and protective policies, we introduce a convex policy interpolation parameter $\chi_m \in [0, 1]$:
$$\dot{E} = \left(1 - \frac{E}{E_{\max}}\right) \left[ \chi_m F_m\big(E, Z(t-\tau_M)\big) + (1 - \chi_m) \eta_p \Big(E_{\mathrm{cap}}\big(Z(t-\tau_p)\big) - E\Big) \right] \tag{4}$$
where $F_m(E, Z)$ is the mobilizing bracket from equation (1). When $\chi_m = 1$, governance is purely mobilizing; when $\chi_m = 0$, it is purely protective.

## 3. Equilibria and the Characteristic Equation

### 3.1 Steady-State Analysis and Extinction Boundaries

At any sustainable interior equilibrium $(N^*, Z^*, E^*)$, extraction matches biological replenishment:
$$q E^* N^* = S(N^*)$$
Consequently, the net deficit argument vanishes identically:
$$q E^* N^* - S(N^*) = 0 \implies Z^* = \Phi_k(0) = \delta$$
This identity holds independently of the filter timescale $\tau_m$, the regularizing constant $k$, and the ecological parameters.

Substituting $Z^* = \delta$ into the effort bracket of system (1) yields a quadratic equation for the equilibrium effort $E^*$:
$$-\frac{\eta}{E_{\max}} (E^*)^2 + \frac{\eta \delta}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} E^* + \delta_0 \frac{\delta}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + \delta} = 0 \tag{5}$$
Because the leading coefficient is negative and the constant term is strictly positive ($\delta, \delta_0 > 0$), Descartes' rule of signs guarantees the existence of **exactly one positive real root** $E^* > 0$.

For an ecologically viable interior state ($N^* > 0$), equilibrium harvest effort must not exceed the regenerative threshold of the stock:
$$E^* < \frac{r}{q} \implies N^* = K \left(1 - \frac{q E^*}{r}\right) > 0$$

At the baseline Candidate A parameterization, this yields the unique interior steady state:
$$N^* \approx 89.55188, \qquad Z^* = \delta \approx 0.06931, \qquad E^* \approx 2.08962$$

#### The Extinction Boundary
Setting $N = 0$ reveals two boundary equilibria on the extinction manifold:
1. **The Depleted Effort State:** $(0, \delta, E^*)$, where institutional effort remains sustained at the interior value despite the complete absence of biological harvest.
2. **The Saturated Boundary State:** $(0, \delta, E_{\max})$, where effort reaches maximum fleet capacity.

Linearizing the stock dynamics around the extinction equilibrium reveals the stock-direction eigenvalue:
$$\lambda_N = r - q E^*$$
A classical transcritical bifurcation occurs at $r = q E^*$:
* If $r > q E^*$, the interior resource state $N^* > 0$ is viable, and the extinction rest point $(0, \delta, E^*)$ is unstable.
* If $r < q E^*$, the stock collapses to extinction ($N \to 0$), and the depleted rest point attracts the biomass dynamics.

### 3.2 Linearization and the Structural Filter Cancellation

We introduce perturbation coordinates around the interior equilibrium:
$$x(t) = N(t) - N^*, \qquad z(t) = Z(t) - Z^*, \qquad e(t) = E(t) - E^*$$
Under the standard baseline relation $\delta = \frac{\log 2}{k}$, the softplus derivative evaluates to $\Phi_k'(0) = \text{sp}_k'(0) = \frac{1}{2}$. 

The linearized system is:
$$\begin{aligned}
\dot{x}(t) &= A_N x(t) + A_E e(t), \\[4pt]
\dot{z}(t) &= B_N x(t) + B_E e(t) - d z(t), \\[4pt]
\dot{e}(t) &= C_E e(t) + C_Z z(t - \tau)
\end{aligned} \tag{6}$$
where $d = \frac{1}{\tau_m}$, and the Jacobian coefficients are:
$$\begin{aligned}
A_N &= S'(N^*) - q E^* = r\left(1 - \frac{2N^*}{K}\right) - q E^*, & A_E &= -q N^*, \\[6pt]
B_N &= \frac{q E^* - S'(N^*)}{2\tau_m} = -\frac{A_N}{2\tau_m}, & B_E &= \frac{q N^*}{2\tau_m} = -\frac{A_E}{2\tau_m}, \\[6pt]
C_E &= \left(1 - \frac{E^*}{E_{\max}}\right) \eta \left(\frac{\delta}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} - \frac{2E^*}{E_{\max}}\right), & 
C_Z &= \left(1 - \frac{E^*}{E_{\max}}\right) \left[ \frac{\eta E^*}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} + \frac{\delta_0 Z_{\mathrm{ref}}}{(Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + \delta)^2} \right]
\end{aligned}$$

Substituting the exponential modal ansatz $(x(t), z(t), e(t)) = (x_0, z_0, e_0) e^{\lambda t}$ yields the characteristic quasi-polynomial:
$$\det \begin{pmatrix} \lambda - A_N & 0 & -A_E \\ -B_N & \lambda + d & -B_E \\ 0 & -C_Z e^{-\lambda\tau} & \lambda - C_E \end{pmatrix} = 0$$

Expanding the determinant along the third row gives:
$$P(\lambda) - C_Z L(\lambda) e^{-\lambda\tau} = 0 \tag{7}$$
where:
$$P(\lambda) = (\lambda - A_N)(\lambda + d)(\lambda - C_E)$$
and
$$L(\lambda) = B_E(\lambda - A_N) + A_E B_N$$

We now highlight a fundamental mathematical symmetry of this architecture. Because the institutional memory filters the direct net depletion rate $-\dot{N}$, the coupling coefficients satisfy:
$$B_N = -\frac{A_N}{2\tau_m}, \qquad B_E = -\frac{A_E}{2\tau_m}$$
Computing the polynomial $L(\lambda)$ reveals an exact cross-term cancellation:
$$A_E B_N - A_N B_E = A_E \left(-\frac{A_N}{2\tau_m}\right) - A_N \left(-\frac{A_E}{2\tau_m}\right) \equiv 0$$
Consequently, the numerator reduces to a monomial:
$$L(\lambda) = B_E \lambda \tag{8}$$
This structural cancellation underpins the algebraic geometry of the system's oscillatory transitions.

## 4. The Algebraic Geometry of Oscillations (The Hopf Cubic)

### 4.1 Modulus Balance and the Even-Pairs Property

To locate oscillatory transitions, we search for purely imaginary eigenvalues $\lambda = i\omega$ with $\omega > 0$ that satisfy the characteristic equation (7).

#### Theorem 4.1 (Cubic Modulus Condition and Delay Branches)
*A purely imaginary root $\lambda = i\omega$ ($\omega > 0$) satisfies the characteristic quasi-polynomial (7) if and only if $u = \omega^2 > 0$ is a positive real root of the cubic polynomial:*
$$H(u) = (u + A_N^2)(u + d^2)(u + C_E^2) - C_Z^2 B_E^2 u = 0 \tag{9}$$
*When a positive root $u^* = (\omega^*)^2$ exists, the corresponding critical delays form an infinite sequence of branches:*
$$\tau_k = \frac{-\arg\left\{ \frac{P(i\omega^*)}{C_Z L(i\omega^*)} \right\} + 2\pi k}{\omega^*}, \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z} \tag{10}$$

#### Proof
Setting $\lambda = i\omega$ in equation (7) gives:
$$P(i\omega) = C_Z L(i\omega) e^{-i\omega\tau}$$
Taking the squared modulus of both sides eliminates the exponential delay term:
$$|P(i\omega)|^2 = C_Z^2 |L(i\omega)|^2 \tag{11}$$
Evaluating each polynomial term:
$$|P(i\omega)|^2 = |i\omega - A_N|^2 \, |i\omega + d|^2 \, |i\omega - C_E|^2 = (\omega^2 + A_N^2)(\omega^2 + d^2)(\omega^2 + C_E^2)$$
Using the cancellation identity $L(i\omega) = B_E (i\omega)$ from equation (8):
$$|L(i\omega)|^2 = B_E^2 \omega^2$$
Substituting these expressions into (11) and setting $u = \omega^2$ yields the cubic polynomial $H(u) = 0$ in equation (9).

Equating the complex arguments in $e^{-i\omega\tau} = \frac{P(i\omega)}{C_Z L(i\omega)}$ determines the branch delays in equation (10). Because $H(u)$ is a cubic polynomial, it has at most three real roots, bounding the system to at most three distinct frequency families. $\blacksquare$

```
                   THE HOPF CUBIC ROOT GEOMETRY
   H(u)
    ^
    |          \                         /
    |           \                       /
H(0)|-+          \                     /
    | |           \   u1*             /  u2*
----+-------------+----+-------------+----+-------> u = ω²
    |              \  /               \  /
    |               \/                 \/
    |         (Local Minimum)     (H(u) < 0)
    |
```

#### Corollary 4.1 (The Even-Pairs Property)
*Assume $A_N \ne 0$, $d \ne 0$, and $C_E \ne 0$. Then the cubic $H(u)$ has either zero or two simple positive roots. Oscillatory instability thresholds for this governance architecture generically occur in pairs.*

#### Proof
Expanding the cubic polynomial (9):
$$H(u) = u^3 + \left(A_N^2 + d^2 + C_E^2\right) u^2 + \left(A_N^2 d^2 + A_N^2 C_E^2 + d^2 C_E^2 - C_Z^2 B_E^2\right) u + A_N^2 d^2 C_E^2$$
Evaluating $H(u)$ at the origin:
$$H(0) = A_N^2 d^2 C_E^2 > 0$$
Furthermore, as $u \to +\infty$, $H(u) \to +\infty$. 

Because $H(0) > 0$ and the leading coefficient is positive, the total number of sign changes in the real roots must be even. By Descartes' rule of signs, the number of strictly positive real roots (counted with algebraic multiplicity) is either $0$ or $2$, except at the degenerate boundary where $H(u) = H'(u) = 0$ (a double root marking the coalescence of two frequencies). Generically, therefore, positive roots occur as two distinct frequencies $\omega_1 < \omega_2$, generating paired Hopf bifurcation branches. $\blacksquare$

### 4.2 Contrast with the Scalar Delay Archetype

This even-pairs structure contrasts sharply with the classic scalar delay differential equation analyzed by Hayes:
$$\dot{x}(t) = -a x(t) - B x(t - \tau), \qquad a > 0, \quad B > 0$$
In Hayes' scalar system, when the delayed feedback gain dominates ($B > a$), the system is stable for small delays $\tau \in [0, \tau_{\mathrm{crit}})$, loses stability at $\tau_{\mathrm{crit}}$, and **remains permanently unstable** for all $\tau > \tau_{\mathrm{crit}}$. Delay in the scalar setting acts monotonically to destroy stability.

In the three-state governance core (1), the dynamics are qualitatively different:
1. The undelayed mobilizing system ($\tau = 0$) is *already unstable* due to positive feedback between perceived scarcity and extraction effort.
2. The presence of an *intermediate* delay window $(\tau_-, \tau_+)$ introduces a phase shift that counters this internal positive feedback, stabilizing the equilibrium.
3. Stability is lost again once delay becomes excessive ($\tau > \tau_+$).

### 5.2 Bifurcation Criticality and Lyapunov Coefficients

Whether a Hopf bifurcation generates safe, small-amplitude oscillations or sudden, dangerous collapses depends on its *criticality*. We evaluate the first Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1$ using the Hassard–Faria–Magalhães normal form method for delay differential equations:

$$\ell_1(\tau_-^{\mathrm{A}}) = +5.75 \times 10^{-5}, \qquad \ell_1(\tau_+^{\mathrm{A}}) = +3.55 \times 10^{-4}$$

Because $\ell_1 > 0$ at both crossings for Candidate A, **both bifurcations are strictly subcritical**. 

Subcritical bifurcations carry significant operational risks for resource management:
1. **Absence of Small Stable Orbits:** Instead of branching into stable, small-amplitude cycles, the system sheds *unstable* periodic orbits backward into the stable window.
2. **Hysteresis and Tipping Points:** Perturbing the system beyond the basin of attraction bounded by these unstable orbits triggers sudden, large-amplitude oscillations, driving the resource toward the zero-stock boundary.
3. **Pre-emptive Instability:** Under numerical continuation, the subcritical branch emanating from $\tau_-$ undergoes a fold (saddle-node of limit cycles) near $\tau \approx 5.6$ years. Consequently, even inside the locally stable window, sufficiently large shocks trigger runaway resource collapse.

### 5.3 Macroeconomic Feedback and Hopf Persistence

To test whether this stabilizing window persists when the resource sector is embedded within a broader macroeconomic system, we consider an augmented system subject to weak macroeconomic feedbacks of order $\varepsilon$:
$$\dot{w} = g(w, N, E), \qquad \varepsilon \ll 1$$
where $w$ represents secondary capital, labor, or market price dynamics.

#### Remark 5.1 (Structural Persistence of Hopf Boundaries)
*Assume that the fast macroeconomic subsystem is uniformly Hurwitz (stable) across the invariant domain. Then, by the implicit function theorem applied to the factored characteristic determinant:*
$$\Delta_{\mathrm{full}}(\lambda, \tau; \varepsilon) = \Delta_{\mathrm{core}}(\lambda, \tau) \, \Delta_{\mathrm{macro}}(\lambda) + \varepsilon \chi(\lambda, \tau; \varepsilon)$$
*the certified Hopf boundaries shift smoothly:*
$$\tau_\pm(\varepsilon) = \tau_\pm + O(\varepsilon)$$
*The phase-stabilized window $(\tau_-, \tau_+)$ is structurally robust against weak macroeconomic coupling.*

### 5.4 The Two-Delay Interpolation and Policy Weighting

We now examine the convex combination policy (4), which blends mobilizing governance (weight $\chi_m$) with protective quota tracking (weight $1 - \chi_m$).

Linearizing system (4) around the interior steady state yields the characteristic equation:
$$P(\lambda) - L(\lambda)\left[ \chi_m C_Z^{\mathrm{mob}} e^{-\lambda \tau_M} + (1 - \chi_m) C_Z^{\mathrm{prot}} e^{-\lambda \tau_p} \right] = 0 \tag{12}$$

#### Proposition 5.1 (Weighted Small-Gain Stability Guarantee)
*Assume the undelayed interpolated system is stable at $\tau_M = \tau_p = 0$. If the loop gains satisfy:*
$$\sup_{\omega > 0} \frac{\left( \chi_m |C_Z^{\mathrm{mob}}| + (1 - \chi_m)|C_Z^{\mathrm{prot}}| \right) |L(i\omega)|}{|(i\omega - A_N)(i\omega + d)(i\omega - C_E)|} < 1 \tag{13}$$
*then the equilibrium is exponentially stable for all possible delay pairs $(\tau_M, \tau_p) \in [0, \infty) \times [0, \infty)$.*

#### Proof
Suppose there exist delays $(\tau_M, \tau_p)$ such that $\lambda = i\omega$ ($\omega > 0$) solves equation (12). Then:
$$|P(i\omega)| = |L(i\omega)| \cdot \left| \chi_m C_Z^{\mathrm{mob}} e^{-i\omega\tau_M} + (1 - \chi_m) C_Z^{\mathrm{prot}} e^{-i\omega\tau_p} \right|$$
Applying the triangle inequality:
$$|P(i\omega)| \le |L(i\omega)| \left( \chi_m |C_Z^{\mathrm{mob}}| + (1 - \chi_m) |C_Z^{\mathrm{prot}}| \right)$$
Dividing by $|P(i\omega)|$ contradicts the small-gain condition (13). By the stability switch theorem, because no characteristic roots can cross the imaginary axis, the exponential stability of the undelayed system holds across the entire delay quadrant. $\blacksquare$

#### Corollary 5.1 (Mobilizing Weight Threshold)
*At the baseline parameterization, pure protective governance yields a peak open-loop gain of $0.08011 < 1$, while pure mobilizing governance yields a peak loop gain of $1.016 > 1$. By continuity, there exists a threshold $\chi_m^* > 0$ such that for all policy mixtures dominated by protection:*
$$\chi_m < \chi_m^* \implies \text{The system is stable for all delays } (\tau_M, \tau_p)$$
*Instability cannot be induced by administrative delays unless the mobilizing policy weight exceeds this critical threshold.*

## 6. The Protective Channel: Quota Tracking and Robust Stability

### 6.1 Mechanics of Protective Feedback

Under the protective quota-tracking rule (3):
$$\dot{E}(t) = \left(1 - \frac{E(t)}{E_{\max}}\right) \eta_p \Big[ E_{\mathrm{cap}}\big(Z(t - \tau_p)\big) - E(t) \Big]$$
rising perceived deficits prompt proactive reductions in harvest effort.

Linearizing this law around the Candidate A equilibrium gives:
$$C_E = -0.850336, \qquad C_Z = -1.661702$$
The negative sign of $C_Z$ ensures that depletion signals directly suppress extraction pressure.

---

### 6.2 The No-Hopf Theorem

#### Theorem 6.1 (Delay-Independent Global Stability of Protective Governance)
*Let the resource system be governed by the calibrated protective quota-tracking law (3) under Candidate A parameters. Then the characteristic quasi-polynomial has no purely imaginary roots for any delay $\tau_p \ge 0$, and the interior steady state is locally exponentially stable for all administrative delays.*

#### Proof
1. **Absence of Imaginary Roots:** By Theorem 4.1, purely imaginary roots require positive real solutions $u = \omega^2 > 0$ to the cubic polynomial:
   $$H(u) = u^3 + c_2 u^2 + c_1 u + c_0 = 0$$
   Evaluating these coefficients under the protective gains:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   c_2 &= A_N^2 + d^2 + C_E^2 = (-0.0179)^2 + (0.2)^2 + (-0.850336)^2 \approx 0.76339 > 0 \\[4pt]
   c_1 &= A_N^2 d^2 + A_N^2 C_E^2 + d^2 C_E^2 - C_Z^2 B_E^2 \approx 0.028946 > 0 \\[4pt]
   c_0 &= A_N^2 d^2 C_E^2 \approx 9.278 \times 10^{-6} > 0
   \end{aligned}$$
   Because all polynomial coefficients $c_2, c_1, c_0$ are strictly positive, Descartes' rule of signs proves that $H(u)$ has **zero positive real roots**. Thus, no imaginary eigenvalues can exist for any delay $\tau_p \ge 0$.
2. **Undelayed Hurwitz Stability:** At $\tau_p = 0$, the characteristic polynomial simplifies to:
   $$P_0(\lambda) = \lambda^3 + 1.0682 \lambda^2 + 0.2038 \lambda + 0.00305 = 0$$
   The Hurwitz determinants are strictly positive:
   $$\Delta_1 = 1.0682 > 0, \qquad \Delta_2 = (1.0682)(0.2038) - 0.00305 = 0.2146 > 0$$
   The undelayed system is exponentially stable.
3. **Invariance Across Delays:** Because the system is stable at $\tau_p = 0$ and roots can transition between the left and right complex half-planes only by crossing the imaginary axis, the absence of imaginary roots guarantees exponential stability for all $\tau_p \ge 0$. $\blacksquare$

### 6.3 The Iso-Gain Sign Flip

To demonstrate that this protective stability stems from structural damping rather than a simple sign inversion, consider an idealized mathematical comparison:

#### Proposition 6.1 (Sign-Phase Invariance)
*Consider the mobilizing system (1) and invert the feedback sign ($C_Z \to -C_Z$) while holding all other coefficients constant.*
1. *The cubic frequency equation $H(u) = 0$ is unchanged, since $C_Z$ enters only as $C_Z^2$.*
2. *The oscillation frequencies $\omega_1, \omega_2$ are identical to those of the mobilizing channel.*
3. *The critical delay branches shift by an odd half-period:*
   $$\tau_k^{\mathrm{flipped}} = \tau_k \pm \frac{\pi}{\omega}$$
   *yielding $\tau_-^{\mathrm{flipped}} \approx 128.37$ yr and $\tau_+^{\mathrm{flipped}} \approx 70.70$ yr.*

Proposition 6.1 clarifies an important distinction: simply reversing the feedback sign of a mobilizing rule does not eliminate oscillations; it merely shifts the critical delays. The true protective quota-tracking law (3) eliminates oscillations entirely because it simultaneously alters the *gain modulus*—introducing direct effort damping ($C_E = -0.8503$) that keeps the total open-loop gain well below unity:
$$\sup_{\omega} \Gamma(\omega) = 0.08011 \ll 1$$

### 6.4 Periodic Review and Discretization Artifacts

Resource management agencies rarely adjust harvest quotas continuously. Instead, stock assessments and policy reviews occur at discrete intervals $T_r$ (e.g., annual or triennial reviews). Between reviews, management mandates are held fixed—a process modeled mathematically as **sample-and-hold control**.

```
                       SAMPLE-AND-HOLD GOVERNANCE
                       
 Effort E(t)
    ^
    |                       Policy Update (at review Tr)
E_k |----------------------*
    |                      |
    |                      |  Effort held constant during review interval
E_k-1                      +-----------------------*
    |                                              |
    +----------------------|-----------------------|-------------> Time t
    0                      Tr                      2Tr
```

Let $T_r$ denote the review period. Over the interval $[k T_r, (k+1)T_r)$, effort is frozen at $e_k$. Between reviews, the biological and memory dynamics evolve according to:
$$\dot{\xi}(t) = A_{\mathrm{hold}} \xi(t), \qquad A_{\mathrm{hold}} = \begin{pmatrix} A_N & 0 & A_E \\ B_N & -d & B_E \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
At the end of each review interval, the agency audits the stock and updates effort via an administrative review operator.

#### Proposition 6.2 (The Euler Monodromy Operator)
*Under an explicit Euler administrative update rule:*
$$e_{k+1} = e_k + T_r \Big( C_E e_k + C_Z z\big((k+1)T_r^-\big) \Big)$$
*the discrete one-period monodromy mapping over the full cycle is:*
$$M_p(T_r) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & T_r C_Z & 1 + T_r C_E \end{pmatrix} \exp\left( A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r \right) \tag{14}$$

Under annual reviews ($T_r = 1.0$ yr) at the protective calibration:
$$\rho\big(M_p(1)\big) = 0.9838 < 1$$
The discrete periodic system is stable. 

However, evaluating the monodromy matrix over wider review cadences reveals an apparent instability threshold:
$$\rho\big(M_p(T_r)\big) > 1 \quad \text{for } T_r > 2.306 \; \text{yr}$$

#### Exposing the Discretization Artifact
This apparent 2.3-year threshold is a mathematical artifact of the explicit Euler administrative update, not an intrinsic instability of protective governance. 

To verify this, we note that the explicit Euler numerical stability limit for the uncoupled effort equation is:
$$T_{r, \mathrm{crit}} = \frac{2}{|C_E|} = \frac{2}{0.850336} \approx 2.352 \; \text{yr}$$
The monodromy crossing at $T_r \approx 2.306$ yr sits within $2\%$ of this numerical stability boundary.

When we replace the explicit Euler update with an **exact continuous-hold update**—integrating the physical equations over the review interval—the monodromy matrix remains strictly stable:
$$\rho\big(M_{\mathrm{exact}}(T_r)\big) \le 0.9967 < 1 \quad \text{for all } T_r \in [0.2, 300] \; \text{yr}$$

This demonstrates a critical methodological point: numerical approximations of management rules can introduce spurious instabilities that do not exist in the underlying institutional system.

## 7. Integrating Biological Maturation Delays

### 7.1 The Coupled Eco-Institutional System

In long-lived species, biological reproduction is not instantaneous; a substantial maturation lag separates spawning from recruitment into the adult stock. To analyze how biological and governance delays interact, we replace the instantaneous logistic function in system (1) with a Gurney–Blythe–Nisbet delayed recruitment term:
$$\dot{N}(t) = r N(t - g)\left(1 - \frac{N(t - g)}{K}\right) - q E(t) N(t) \tag{15}$$
where $g \ge 0$ is the **biological maturation delay**.

The perceived harvest deficit signal now reflects the difference between current harvest extraction and the recruitment of cohorts spawned $g$ years prior:
$$\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{sig}}(t) = q E(t) N(t) - r N(t - g)\left(1 - \frac{N(t - g)}{K}\right)$$
This deficit drives the institutional memory filter:
$$\dot{Z}(t) = \frac{1}{\tau_m} \Big[ \Phi_k\big(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{sig}}(t)\big) - Z(t) \Big]$$
which in turn governs harvest effort $E(t)$ through the institutional administrative delay $\tau$, exactly as in system (1).

Because at steady state $N(t - g) = N^*$, this coupled eco-institutional system preserves the exact interior equilibrium $(N^*, Z^*, E^*)$ of the baseline model, allowing direct comparisons between ecological and governance dynamics.

When biological maturation delay is set to zero ($g = 0$), the coupled system reduces to the gated core (1). Tracking the stability boundaries as the biological renewal rate $r$ varies traces out bounded stability windows:

These boundaries establish the baseline parameter regime: the two-crossing Hopf structure requires a balance between biological productivity and institutional responsiveness.

### 7.4 Nonlinear Dynamics: Cohort Cycles vs. Institutional Cycles

Integrating the full nonlinear equations confirms that biological and institutional lags generate two distinct classes of cyclic behavior:

### 7.5 Distinguishing Maturation Delays from Stage-Structured Models

To prevent methodological confusion, we distinguish the delayed-recruitment formulation used here from discrete two-stage (adult–juvenile) models:
* In **stage-structured models**, juveniles and adults are tracked as separate compartments ($X_J, X_A$). Maturation is modeled as a transfer rate parameterized by the mean residence time $g$. In these systems, harvest targeting (adult extraction vs. juvenile bycatch) controls stability without introducing true functional delay equations.
* In the **delayed-recruitment model (15)**, the maturation lag $g$ is a discrete, history-dependent functional delay. Here, the reproductive output of past populations directly drives current recruitment, creating history-dependent hereditary effects.

These records represent an exact numerical reproduction of the declared model instance using continuous-time characteristic-root tracking cross-validated by fourth-order Runge--Kutta (RK4) integration—the delay differential equation (DDE) counterpart to a Floquet multiplier scan. 

We emphasize the precise scope of this numerical certification:

## 8. The Review Interval as an Institutional Control Parameter

In modern fisheries governance, institutional delay rarely manifests as a pure, continuous lag. Instead, it arises through **periodic review**: assessments are performed at fixed calendar intervals (e.g., annual or biennial surveys), and management updates (e.g., total allowable catch or fleet effort allocations) are implemented discretely.

From a control-theoretic standpoint, this institutional architecture forms a **hybrid dynamical system**:
* **Continuous inter-review phase:** The fishery operates under a frozen effort allocation held constant between review dates.
* **Discrete review phase:** At each review instant, newly gathered stock data trigger an impulsive update to the harvest policy.

```
       CONTINUOUS INTER-REVIEW FLOW                   DISCRETE MANAGEMENT UPDATE
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐         ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Resource Stock & Perceived Biomass  │         │   Stock Assessment Conducted    │
  │ flow continuously under held effort:│ ──────► │   Effort updated impulsively:   │
  │        dξ/dt = A_hold · ξ           │         │     e_{k+1} = Update(ξ_k^-)     │
  └─────────────────────────────────────┘         └─────────────────────────────────┘
                     ▲                                             │
                     └─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                               Cycle repeats every T_r years
```

Because of this hybrid structure, the review interval ($T_r$) is **not** mathematically equivalent to sampling a continuous delay differential equation at $\tau = T_r$. Lengthening the review interval is not simply a relabeling of "decreasing delay." The review cadence acts as a structural control parameter that influences system stability in fundamentally different ways depending on the feedback channel:
* In the **protective quota-tracking channel**, the equilibrium remains stable under annual review, and its stability is preserved across review intervals when evaluated under exact updates. (Apparent instabilities reported around $T_r \approx 2.3$ years are artifacts of crude numerical discretizations.)
* In the **mobilising channel**, annual review destabilizes the fishery. Counterintuitively, lengthening the review interval can restabilize the system through a complex unit-circle crossing of the discrete return map.

> **Governance Warning:** Local spectral stability must not be confused with safe management. Although lengthening the review interval can asymptotically stabilize a mathematical equilibrium, it permits substantial, unmonitored stock depletion *between* review dates. Guaranteeing that the stock never breaches a critical safety threshold throughout the entire inter-review window requires separate validation, which is not certified here.

### 8.1 Sampled-Data Monodromy of the Mobilising Channel

The stability of a periodic hybrid system is governed by its **monodromy matrix**—the linear return map over one full review cycle. If all eigenvalues (characteristic multipliers) of this matrix lie strictly inside the complex unit circle, the system returns exponentially to equilibrium after small shocks.

\begin{theorem}[Sampled-Data Monodromy of the Mobilising Channel]
\label{thm:monodromy-mobilising}
Linearize the gated three-state harvesting model about its interior equilibrium, and replace the continuous delay with a sample-and-hold mechanism of period $T_r$ paired with a forward Euler update step. 

Between reviews ($t_k \le t < t_{k+1}$), the state perturbations $\xi = (\delta N, \delta Z, \delta E)^\top$ evolve continuously under frozen effort:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\xi} = A_{\mathrm{hold}}\,\xi,
\end{equation}
where $A_{\mathrm{hold}}$ is the hold matrix from Proposition 6.2. At the review instant $t_{k+1}$, an impulsive management adjustment updates the state using the mobilising gains ($C_E = -0.0595$, $C_Z = +1.785$).

Adopting the **flow-then-update** timing convention—where the pre-review state $\xi_k^-$ flows for duration $T_r$ under held effort before receiving the end-of-period review update—the one-period monodromy operator is:
\begin{equation}
M(T_r) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & T_r C_Z & 1 + T_r C_E \end{pmatrix} \exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r).
\label{eq:monodromy_euler}
\end{equation}
Under this formulation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The sampled equilibrium is exponentially stable if and only if the spectral radius satisfies $\rho(M(T_r)) < 1$.
    \item A complex unit-circle crossing—the linear spectral precursor of a Neimark--Sacker bifurcation—occurs at values of $T_r$ where $M(T_r)$ possesses a simple conjugate pair of eigenvalues $e^{\pm i\theta}$ ($\theta \notin \{0, \pi\}$), with all other eigenvalues remaining strictly within the unit disc.
    \item As the review interval approaches zero, the normalized discrete map converges to the continuous-time undelayed Jacobian:
    \begin{equation}
    \lim_{T_r \to 0} \frac{M(T_r) - I}{T_r} = J_{\mathrm{cont}} = A_{\mathrm{hold}} + \mathbf{e}_3 (C_Z \mathbf{e}_2^\top + C_E \mathbf{e}_3^\top).
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The structure of $M(T_r)$ follows by composing the continuous matrix exponential $\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r)$ with the rank-one discrete effort update row. The stability and unit-circle crossing statements reflect standard spectral radius criteria for discrete linear mappings. 

For the consistency limit, expanding the matrix exponential yields $\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r) = I + A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r + O(T_r^2)$. The discrete update matrix can be factored as $I + T_r R_0$, where $R_0 = \mathbf{e}_3 (C_Z \mathbf{e}_2^\top + C_E \mathbf{e}_3^\top)$. Multiplying these terms gives:
\begin{equation}
M(T_r) = (I + T_r R_0)(I + A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r) + O(T_r^2) = I + T_r(A_{\mathrm{hold}} + R_0) + O(T_r^2).
\end{equation}
Subtracting the identity matrix and dividing by $T_r$ yields $A_{\mathrm{hold}} + R_0$ as $T_r \to 0$. Here, $R_0$ precisely restores the active effort feedback entries ($C_E, C_Z$) that vanish in the third row of $A_{\mathrm{hold}}$.

Regarding the ordering convention: reversing the sequence to $\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r) R(T_r)$ produces a similar matrix with an identical eigenvalue spectrum whenever the update matrix is invertible, preserving linear stability classifications while altering the nonlinear map. The flow-then-update convention is maintained consistently throughout this work.
\end{proof}

Evaluating this Euler review scheme on the gated Candidate A baseline yields an unstable annual review:
\begin{equation}
\rho(M(1)) = 1.00055 > 1.
\end{equation}
This reflects the fact that the underlying undelayed continuous system is inherently unstable (Section 5.1). The Euler map predicts that the sampled equilibrium restabilizes via a complex unit-circle crossing at $T_r^{\mathrm{UC}} = 47.536\text{ yr}$, before losing stability again via a period-doubling multiplier reaching $-1$ at $T_r^{(-1)} = 79.143\text{ yr}$, suggesting a stable window of $T_r \in (47.54, 79.14)\text{ yr}$.

However, as established below, **these specific quantitative thresholds are artifacts of the explicit Euler approximation**; they do not represent real features of periodic review.

\begin{remark}[The Euler Command-Step Artifact]
\label{rem:euler-artifact}
The restabilization threshold at $T_r = 47.536\text{ yr}$ and the period-doubling boundary at $T_r = 79.143\text{ yr}$ are numerical errors introduced by taking a discrete Euler step on the control update. When the effort dynamics are solved *exactly* across the review interval (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-monodromy}), the true spectral radii at these thresholds are $0.786$ and $0.597$—both safely within the stable unit disc. 

The exact monodromy exhibits only a single unit-circle crossing across the entire tested domain $[0.2, 300]\text{ yr}$, located at $T_r = 6.50\text{ yr}$. The qualitative principle—that lengthening the review interval restabilizes an equilibrium destabilized by annual review—is physically genuine; only the numerical location of the crossing depends on the update scheme.
\end{remark}

### 8.2 Exact Held-Measurement Monodromy

To eliminate discretization artifacts, we evaluate the exact solution of the continuous-discrete hybrid system.

\begin{proposition}[Exact Held-Measurement Monodromy]
\label{prop:exact-monodromy}
Retain the sample-and-hold architecture, the frozen-effort hold flow $\dot{\xi} = A_{\mathrm{hold}} \xi$, and the flow-then-update timing convention. Replace the forward Euler step with the exact integral of the effort equation $\dot{e} = C_E e + C_Z z$ over the review interval $T_r$, holding the perceived depletion signal constant at its end-of-period value $z(t_{k+1}^-)$:
\begin{equation}
e_{k+1} = e^{C_E T_r} e_k + \frac{e^{C_E T_r} - 1}{C_E} C_Z z(t_{k+1}^-).
\end{equation}
The resulting exact monodromy matrix is:
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{ex}}(T_r) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & \dfrac{e^{C_E T_r}-1}{C_E} C_Z & e^{C_E T_r} \end{pmatrix} \exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r),
\label{eq:monodromy_exact}
\end{equation}
which preserves the consistent asymptotic limit $\lim_{T_r \to 0} (M_{\mathrm{ex}}(T_r) - I)/T_r = J_{\mathrm{cont}}$.

Evaluating $M_{\mathrm{ex}}$ on the gated Candidate A baseline establishes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Protective channel ($C_E = -0.850336$, $C_Z = -1.661702$):} 
    The equilibrium is stable under annual review ($\rho(M_{\mathrm{ex}}(1)) = 0.9838$) and remains strictly stable ($\rho < 1$) across all tested review intervals $T_r \in [0.2, 300]\text{ yr}$, reaching a maximum spectral radius of $0.9967$ at $T_r = 0.2\text{ yr}$. The instability at $T_r \approx 2.306\text{ yr}$ seen in the Euler model is entirely absent under the exact update.
    \item \textbf{Mobilising channel ($C_E = -0.0595$, $C_Z = +1.785$):} 
    Annual review remains unstable under the exact update:
    \begin{equation}
    \rho(M_{\mathrm{ex}}(1)) = 1.00035 > 1.
    \end{equation}
    Across $T_r \in [0.2, 300]\text{ yr}$, the exact monodromy undergoes exactly one unit-circle crossing, located at:
    \begin{equation}
    T_r^{\mathrm{UC,ex}} = 6.50\text{ yr},
    \end{equation}
    with a complex conjugate pair of crossing eigenvalues $\lambda = 0.9846 \pm 0.1746i$ ($|\lambda| = 1$), a stable real eigenvalue at $\lambda_3 = 0.1647$, a non-resonant rotation period ($2\pi/\theta \approx 35.8\text{ cycles}$), and a transverse crossing direction ($d\rho/dT_r < 0$). Holding measurements at the start-of-period value rather than the end-of-period value does not alter these thresholds at the reported precision.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Integrating the scalar linear differential equation $\dot{e} = C_E e + C_Z z_{\mathrm{held}}$ over $t \in [0, T_r]$ with constant $z_{\mathrm{held}} = z(t_{k+1}^-)$ yields the exact discrete update. Because the effort coordinate is held frozen during the continuous flow, this update acts directly on the flowed state. The limit $T_r \to 0$ follows from the standard expansions $e^{C_E T_r} = 1 + C_E T_r + O(T_r^2)$ and $(e^{C_E T_r} - 1)/C_E = T_r + O(T_r^2)$. Spectral radius computations across the parameter grids confirm the stability and crossing values.
\end{proof}

### 8.3 Comparison Across Update Schemes

To ensure our findings are not tied to a specific mathematical implementation of the review step, we also examined the physically native **Zero-Order Hold (ZOH)** model. In this setup, the perceived depletion signal $z$ is sampled discretely, while effort dynamics $\dot{e} = C_E e + C_Z z_k$ evolve continuously alongside the stock:
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{ZOH}}(T_r) = e^{A_{\mathrm{Z}} T_r} + \left( \int_0^{T_r} e^{A_{\mathrm{Z}} s} \, ds \right) C_Z \mathbf{e}_3 \mathbf{e}_2^\top,
\end{equation}
where $A_{\mathrm{Z}}$ is the continuous Jacobian with the delayed feedback entry $C_Z$ omitted.

As $T_r \to \infty$, the monodromy map under all consistent formulations collapses to a rank-one operator whose dominant eigenvalue is governed by the system's DC gain. Because the institutional filter identity requires zero static gain ($L(0) = 0$; Section 3.2), the coupling between harvest effort and fish stock uncouples completely over sufficiently long review cadences. 

As a result, **every consistent periodic-review scheme must restabilize at some finite review interval**.

Across all three formulations, the predicted restabilization threshold for the mobilising channel falls within a narrow window of $6.50\text{--}6.73\text{ yr}$:
* **Exact held-measurement update:** $T_r = 6.5013\text{ yr}$
* **Start-of-period held-measurement update:** $T_r = 6.5013\text{ yr}$
* **Native zero-order hold ($M_{\mathrm{ZOH}}$):** $T_r = 6.7279\text{ yr}$ (crossing eigenvalues $0.9855 \pm 0.1699i$)

Similarly, the protective channel remains unconditionally stable under the native zero-order hold across the entire interval $T_r \in [0.2, 200]\text{ yr}$ (with $\rho(1) = 0.9928$).

### 9.2 The Five-Regime Attractor Topology

For the effort-bounded three-state core at Candidate A, numerical continuation uncovers a **five-regime attractor topology** across the fundamental delay range ($0 < \tau \lesssim 160\text{ yr}$):

1. **Regime (i) [$0 < \tau < \tau_-$]: Unmitigated Boom-and-Bust Cycles.**  
   The interior equilibrium is unstable. Across all tested initial histories, the system is captured by a large-amplitude periodic orbit.
2. **Regime (ii) [$\tau_- < \tau < \tau_f$]: The Lower Bistable Window.**

3. **Regime (iii) [$\tau_f < \tau < \tau_{f2}$]: Safe Harvest Target.**

5. **Regime (v) [$\tau > \tau_+$]: Persistent Large-Amplitude Cycles.**

### 9.4 Detailed Analysis of the Four-State Working Core

To verify whether simplifying the nutrient pool alters system dynamics, we evaluated the four-state model where detritus biomass ($A$) evolves dynamically:
\begin{equation}
\Delta(\lambda) = \lambda I - A_0 - A_\tau e^{-\lambda\tau}.
\end{equation}
Evaluating this characteristic equation at the frozen-donor equilibrium $(N^*, A^*, Z^*, E^*) = (89.53, 397.87, \log 2/10, 2.09)$ reveals pure imaginary roots at:
\begin{equation}
\tau_- = 3.78487\text{ yr} \quad (\text{period } 250.44\text{ yr}), \qquad \tau_+ = 150.12175\text{ yr} \quad (\text{period } 159.13\text{ yr}).
\end{equation}
These values match the three-state model within $3.2\%$ and $0.2\%$, respectively. The large-amplitude cycle terminates at:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{term}, L}^{(4)} \approx 5.63\text{ yr}, \qquad \tau_{\mathrm{fold}, R}^{(4)} \approx 64.4\text{ yr},
\end{equation}
reproducing the three-state lower fold ($5.59\text{ yr}$) and upper fold ($64.40\text{ yr}$) within $0.8\%$ and $0.4\%$.

#### Resolving an Apparent Contradiction
An important distinction must be made regarding unharvested stability:
1. The **frozen-donor characteristic pair** ($\tau_- = 3.78\text{ yr}, \tau_+ = 150.12\text{ yr}$) describes local perturbations when detritus $A$ is held fixed at its steady state.
2. The **dynamic-$A$ stability boundary** ($\omega_A^* \approx 0.001316\text{ yr}^{-1}$) represents the turnover rate threshold of the full four-state system under zero delay ($\tau = 0$). When the true environmental detritus renewal rate falls below $\omega_A^*$, the unharvested equilibrium becomes delay-independently stable.

These two mathematical operators describe different regimes; distinguishing them prevents conflating frozen-pool approximations with fully dynamic ecosystems.

### 9.5 Permissible Ecological Parameter Windows

The five-regime topology requires that the intrinsic ecological growth rate $r$ fall within a bounded window. Across our sensitivity sweeps, the dual-Hopf structure exists only for:
\begin{equation}
r \in (0.008, 0.061)\text{ yr}^{-1} \quad \text{for economic responsiveness } \eta \in [0.5, 3.0].
\end{equation}
If the biological growth rate exceeds this window ($r > 0.061\text{ yr}^{-1}$), the stock replenishes too rapidly for delayed institutional adjustments to trigger resonant cycles, rendering the system delay-independently stable. 

Because many commercially exploited marine fish stocks possess intrinsic growth rates well above $r = 0.06\text{ yr}^{-1}$ (e.g., small pelagics where $r \in [0.2, 0.8]\text{ yr}^{-1}$), **this delayed institutional mechanism is primarily relevant to slow-growing, long-lived species** (such as deep-sea teleosts, large sharks, and cetaceans) unless the institutional responsiveness $\eta$ is exceptionally high.

### 9.6 Scaffold Companion Model Verification

To verify that our results are not artifacts of a specific production function, we re-executed our analysis on the **scaffold companion framework**—a stage-structured Ricker cascade model incorporating juvenile/adult demographics, material detritus, capital investment, and capacity-constrained effort (7 nonlinear state variables).

The verified records reveal three findings:
1. **Elevated Economic Forcing Triggers Cycles:** Under baseline parameters, the scaffold is delay-independently stable across life histories (cod, sprat, and anchovy archetypes). However, when economic responsiveness is elevated ($\eta = 5.0, \varsigma = 0.8, K_0 = 0.03, q = 0.01$), the cod-class life history exhibits a subcritical Hopf bifurcation at $\tau^* = 43.29$ with a cycle period of $263.4$ time units.
2. **Absence of the Upper Hopf Crossing:** Unlike the three-state core, broad parameter sweeps of the stage-structured scaffold reveal only *stabilizing* crossings ($d\,\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)/d\tau < 0$), with periods ranging from $900$ to $35{,}000$ time units. It lacks a clean upper Hopf return crossing ($\tau_+$).
3. **Structural Takeaway:** Delay-induced oscillations can emerge in structured demographic models, but the existence of a closed two-crossing stability window $(\tau_-, \tau_+)$ requires specific saturation and feedback characteristics that differ between simple biomass pools and age-structured cascades.

## 10. The Loop-Gain Family

Despite their differences, the models analyzed in this paper share an underlying linear feedback architecture. This common structure can be unified using classical control theory.

### 10.1 The General Feedback Identity

Expressing the living resource ledger through its primitive mass fluxes—assimilation $g$, natural mortality $m$, decomposition $d_U$, and harvest rate $h$—the rate of biomass depletion is $\ell = h - (g - m) = -\dot{X}$. Let $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ represent ecological state perturbations governed by $\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}} = \mathbf{J}\boldsymbol{\xi} + \mathbf{b}_E e$, with institutional memory acting as a first-order filter of past depletion with gain $\gamma_m$ and time constant $\tau_m$.

The linearized characteristic equation for every model in this paper maps to the universal feedback identity:
\begin{equation}
\lambda - C_E - C_Z e^{-\lambda\tau} \left( \frac{-\gamma_m \lambda \, \mathbf{c}^\top (\lambda \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{J})^{-1} \mathbf{b}_E}{1 + \tau_m \lambda} \right) = 0.
\label{eq:universal_feedback}
\end{equation}
This formulation decomposes the feedback loop into three components:
1. An **ecological resolvent** $(\lambda \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{J})^{-1}$, which dictates the natural frequencies the ecosystem can sustain;
2. An **institutional loop gain** $C_Z \gamma_m$, which dictates the magnitude of management response;
3. A **phase shift** $e^{-\lambda\tau}$, supplied by the institutional delay.

Oscillations emerge if and only if the institutional gain is large enough to close the loop while the delay rotates the phase into positive feedback.

### 10.2 Loop-Gain Exclusion of Delay-Induced Instability

\begin{theorem}[Loop-Gain Exclusion Theorem]
\label{thm:loop-gain-exclusion}
Write the general feedback equation as $\lambda - C_E - C_Z e^{-\lambda\tau} G(\lambda) = 0$. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Small-Gain Condition (H1):} 
    \begin{equation}
    \sup_{\omega \in \mathbb{R}} \frac{|C_Z| |G(i\omega)|}{|i\omega - C_E|} < 1;
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Undelayed Stability (H2):} The system without delay ($\tau = 0$) is Hurwitz stable;
    \item \textbf{Zero-Root Exclusion (H3):} The static loop gain satisfies $-C_E - C_Z G(0) \neq 0$ (guaranteed in our core because $G(0) = 0$ and $C_E < 0$).
\end{enumerate}
Then there are no purely imaginary roots for any delay $\tau \ge 0$. The equilibrium remains exponentially stable for all delays (delay-independent stability).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If an imaginary root $\lambda = i\omega$ existed, setting the characteristic equation to zero would require:
\begin{equation}
|i\omega - C_E| = |C_Z e^{-i\omega\tau} G(i\omega)| = |C_Z| |G(i\omega)|,
\end{equation}
which implies $|C_Z| |G(i\omega)| / |i\omega - C_E| = 1$. This directly contradicts the strict supremum bound (H1). Because no roots can cross the imaginary axis and the system is stable at $\tau = 0$ (H2), it remains stable for all $\tau \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

This theorem provides an operational certificate: if a regulatory institution keeps its delayed response gain below the small-gain threshold relative to direct damping, **no deployment delay can ever destabilize the fishery**.

### 10.3 Equivalence to Logistic Surrogates

\begin{lemma}[Logistic Identification]
\label{lem:logistic-identification}
In the primitive flux model, let ambient detritus $A$ greatly exceed the half-saturation constant ($A \gg K_A$). Define the net intrinsic growth rate as $r = \mu - d > 0$ and the self-limitation coefficient as $c = r/K$. Then, on any bounded domain where $A$ remains strictly positive:
\begin{equation}
g(X, A) - m(X) = rX \left( 1 - \frac{X}{K} \right) + O\left( \mu X \frac{K_A}{A} \right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the Michaelis--Menten uptake term yields:
\begin{equation}
g - m = \mu X \left( 1 - \frac{K_A}{A + K_A} \right) - dX - cX^2 = (\mu - d)X - cX^2 - \mu X \frac{K_A}{A + K_A}.
\end{equation}
Substituting $\mu - d = r$ and $c = r/K$ produces the logistic equation plus a perturbation term of order $O(K_A/A)$. This demonstrates that traditional logistic harvesting models are asymptotic limits of underlying stoichiometric mass-balance equations.
\end{proof}

### 10.4 The Necessity of Autocatalytic Gain

To test whether the boom-and-bust cycles observed in the mobilising channel depend on the autocatalytic nature of fleet expansion, we tested an alternative model where the bilinear term $\eta E Z_\tau / \Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ was replaced by a smooth, saturating gate:
\begin{equation}
\text{Effort Update} \propto \sigma\left( \frac{Z_\tau}{Z_0} \right),
\end{equation}
which exhibits diminishing marginal investment at high deployment.

Across a randomized search spanning more than 300 parameter configurations, **no delay-induced Hopf bifurcations were detected**. Satiation limits the high-frequency loop gain, preventing the system from meeting the threshold of Theorem~\ref{thm:loop-gain-exclusion}. This indicates that while autocatalytic effort expansion is not mathematically required for cycles, it provides the loop gain necessary to destabilize real fisheries under delay.

## 11. Discussion

### 11.1 The Two Governance Channels as a Fundamental Design Choice

Our results demonstrate that the long-term stability of a harvested resource depends fundamentally on the **feedback sign and structure** of its management institution:

```
               TWO OPPOSING INSTITUTIONAL CONTROL PHILOSOPHIES
               
   MOBILISING CHANNEL (Effort Escalation)       PROTECTIVE CHANNEL (Quota Tracking)
             Sign: C_Z > 0                                 Sign: C_Z < 0
             
     Catch Drops ──► Deploy More Effort           Catch Drops ──► Reduce Harvest
           │                                             │
           ▼                                             ▼
   Destabilizing Feedback Loop                  Stabilizing Negative Feedback
           │                                             │
           ▼                                             ▼
   Subcritical Hopf Bifurcations,               Delay-Independent Stability
   Severe Hysteresis, and Boom-and-             Preserved; Stable Under
   Bust Population Collapse                     Annual Review
```

The difference between these channels is not just the sign of $C_Z$. As shown by our iso-gain tests (Proposition 6.1), flipping the sign of $C_Z$ while retaining its original magnitude shifts the bifurcation delays, but can still leave the system unstable. 

The stability of the protective channel arises because authentic quota tracking combines three features:
1. **Negative feedback:** Harvest quotas are ratcheted down when stock declines are detected;
2. **Strong direct damping ($C_E < 0$):** Excess harvest capacity is retired when unprofitable;
3. **Moderate loop gain:** Policy adjustments do not overreact to transient noise.

This answers a longstanding puzzle in fisheries management \citep{moxnes1998}: **institutional delay alone does not cause fishery collapse**. Rather, collapse is caused by institutional delay coupled with a mobilising response structure that escalates effort when yields decline.

### 11.2 Periodic Review as an Active Control Dial

Treating the review interval $T_r$ as a control parameter changes how we think about institutional design. In classical management, annual assessments are often viewed as the gold standard of vigilance. Yet for slow-growing stocks governed by mobilising rules, **frequent annual reviews are destabilizing**. 

Because management updates provide too little phase lag to dampen the system, annual review traps the fishery in an oscillatory regime. Paradoxically, lengthening the review interval to $T_r \ge 6.50\text{ yr}$ can asymptotically restabilize the system.

> **Management Caution:** Restabilizing an equilibrium via long review intervals is not sound governance. While a 7-year review cycle can stabilize the mathematics of the return map, it leaves the resource completely unmanaged between reviews. An unexpected environmental shock occurring in year two could drive the stock to extinction before the next scheduled assessment. Local spectral stabilization must not be used as an excuse for administrative neglect.

### 11.4 Historical Grounding: The Northern Cod Collapse

The timescales identified by our models are not theoretical abstractions; they align with documented timelines of resource collapse. The collapse of the northern cod (*Gadus morhua*) stock off Newfoundland (NAFO Divisions 2J3KL) is a classic example:
* **The Management Cadence:** Assessments proceeded on a regular annual schedule while estimated spawning biomass plummeted by over $95\%$, dropping from $\sim 735{,}000\text{ tonnes}$ in 1991 to $\sim 31{,}000\text{ tonnes}$ in 1994 \citep{dfo2016}.
* **The Deployment Lag:** Despite warnings of overexploitation \citep{hutchings1994, walters1996}, institutional inertia delayed decisive intervention until the moratorium of July 1992. Commercial fishing did not resume until June 2024—a management recovery cycle spanning **32 years** \citep{dfo2024}.

This historical trajectory illustrates the danger of inter-review depletion: a stock can collapse between assessment cycles while management responses remain incremental.

Furthermore, empirical assessment-frequency studies confirm that the review interval acts as a high-stakes control dial. Simulation evaluations demonstrate that moving from annual to multi-year assessments reduces relative yield, increases interannual catch volatility, and elevates depletion risk across diverse fisheries \citep{li2016, peterson2022}.

### 11.5 Methodological Hierarchy of Proof and Certification

To maintain epistemic clarity, the claims in this paper are organized into three distinct tiers of certainty:
1. **Analytically Proved Theorems:** The cubic modulus conditions, the even-pairs crossing property, the no-Hopf theorem for protective channels, the discrete monodromy formulations, and the loop-gain small-gain exclusions are fully proved.
2. **Interval-Certified Numerical Enclosures:** The Hopf bifurcation delays ($\tau_-, \tau_+$) are enclosed using validated interval-Newton methods, guaranteeing that roots of the registered characteristic polynomials exist within the stated decimal brackets.
3. **Collocation-Certified Global Bifurcations:** The global folds ($\tau_f \approx 5.59\text{ yr}, \tau_{f2} \approx 64.40\text{ yr}$) are certified at the discrete collocation level using Moore--Spence and Krawczyk interval algorithms. A full continuous-delay lift to the infinite-dimensional function space remains an open problem.

### 11.6 Early-Warning Signals and Basin Boundaries

Ecologists have proposed early-warning indicators (e.g., rising autocorrelation and increasing variance) to forecast impending regime shifts \citep{scheffer2009, carpenter2011}. 

Our results suggest these indicators must be used with caution:
* **Basin boundaries vs. Bifurcations:** In our models, the practical onset of collapse at $\tau \approx 148.6\text{ yr}$ is a **basin boundary crossing**, located more than 80 years above the true local fold bifurcation at $\tau_{f2} \approx 64.40\text{ yr}$. Early-warning indicators designed to detect local critical slowing down near a fold would look in the wrong place.
* **History-Dependent Asymmetry:** In the bistable window (Regime ii), whether a fishery thrives or collapses depends on its historical trajectory: at $\tau = 5.575\text{ yr}$, a depleted stock is captured by a destructive cycle, while an abundant stock safely approaches equilibrium. Simple rolling-window variance cannot detect this history-dependent risk.

Operational warning systems must track institutional metrics alongside biological signals:
* **Critical Slowing Down:** Rising lag-1 autocorrelation in biomass;
* **Variance Amplification:** Increasing volatility in landings;
* **Epistemic Divergence:** Growing disagreement between independent stock estimators and widening assessment confidence intervals;
* **Policy Inertia:** Increasing harvest pressure despite declining catch per unit effort.

### 11.7 Limitations

Our analysis has several structural boundaries:
* **Model Scope:** Our models focus on scalar renewable resources with delayed feedback. Spatial dynamics, age-structured selectivity, and multi-species ecological interactions are omitted.
* **Discrete Lags:** Management delay is modeled as a single discrete time lag ($\tau$). Distributed institutional lags (e.g., gamma-distributed processing times) require separate analysis.
* **Deterministic Focus:** Our global bifurcation structures are evaluated deterministically; demographic and environmental stochasticity will smooth sharp fold boundaries into probabilistic transitions.

### 11.8 Open Problems

We highlight two technical challenges for future research:
1. **Computer-Assisted Proofs of Delay Bifurcations:** Upgrading the collocation-level fold certificates ($\tau_f, \tau_{f2}$) to full infinite-dimensional proofs using the rigorous framework of Church and Lessard \citep{church2022}.
2. **Transverse Fold Persistence:** Proving the structural persistence of the periodic orbit fold under small perturbations in non-autonomous and spatial domains.

## 12. Conclusion

Where delay sits determines what it does. In renewable resource management, delays are not just biological realities of growth and maturation; they are institutional properties of observation, assessment, and policy implementation.

Our analysis demonstrates that the impact of institutional delay depends on the governance rule:
* **The mobilising channel (effort escalation) is a structural hazard.** Under this rule, delayed feedback produces subcritical Hopf bifurcations, a five-regime attractor topology, and severe hysteresis that traps fisheries in boom-and-bust cycles. Under periodic review, annual assessments destabilize the system, and stability is recovered only for review intervals exceeding $6.5\text{ years}$.
* **The protective channel (quota tracking) is resilient.** This rule guarantees delay-independent stability across all continuous time lags and maintains robust stability under annual periodic review. Apparent instabilities are artifacts of crude numerical discretizations.

Both behaviors reflect a single underlying principle: institutional loop gain. Oscillatory collapse requires both sufficient delayed gain and a phase shift that flips negative feedback into reinforcement. The management takeaway is clear: **institutional delays, update cadences, and response gains are dynamic design parameters with the same power to stabilize or destroy a fishery as biological growth rates.** 

Sustainable governance requires treating management institutions and the ecosystems they harvest as a single, coupled feedback system—and designing the rules of governance to avoid the hidden traps of delayed response.

## 8. Discussion and Policy Implications

### 8.1 The Core Policy Dilemma: Feedback Sign vs. Reaction Speed

Our results challenge two common assumptions in natural resource management:
1. *“Longer administrative delays are always harmful.”*
2. *“Speeding up policy responses is always stabilizing.”*

Our analysis shows that the impact of administrative delay depends fundamentally on the **sign of the feedback policy**:
* **Under mobilizing governance**, accelerating decision-making can be counterproductive. When policies expand effort to compensate for falling yields (e.g., through vessel subsidies or fuel relief), rapid intervention couples current catch shortfalls directly into harvest capacity, accelerating stock depletion. Counterintuitively, bureaucratic delay can act as a stabilizing buffer: a multi-year lag shifts the phase of new effort deployment, dampening overexploitation cycles.
* **Under protective governance**, by contrast, rapid intervention is unambiguously beneficial. Quota-tracking rules that curb effort as deficits emerge remain stable across all continuous delays and discrete review cadences, eliminating oscillatory instability.

### 8.2 Historical Parallels: The Northern Cod Collapse

The collapse and delayed recovery of the northern cod off Newfoundland illustrates these theoretical mechanisms. Throughout the 1970s and 1980s, management operated under an implicit mobilizing regime: as catch-per-unit-effort declined, harvest capacity expanded through technological investment and fleet modernization. Annual scientific reviews recommended incremental quota reductions, but the political and administrative implementation lagged behind the stock's actual rate of decline.

Our findings explain why the historical timeline unfolded as it did:
* The combination of annual reviews with a mobilizing policy structure placed the fishery in an unstable dynamical regime.
* The stock's slow biological recovery reflects the multi-decade cohort delays identified in Section 7: for species with delayed maturation, biological recovery timescales dwarf administrative planning horizons.

### 8.3 Design Recommendations for Resource Institutions

1. **Establish Protective Baseline Rules:** Institutions should default to protective quota-tracking architectures that automatically reduce harvest capacity as deficits emerge, ensuring stability regardless of administrative delays.
2. **Match Review Cadence to Policy Type:** If an institution must operate under a mobilizing policy, policy reviews should be spaced out (or buffered with multi-year averages) to avoid the rapid-response instabilities that arise under annual reviews.
3. **Account for Biological-Institutional Delay Resonances:** For stocks where the product of renewal rate and maturation delay approaches the critical threshold ($r \cdot g \approx 1.5$), institutional response times should be audited to ensure that administrative review cycles do not resonate with natural cohort cycles.

---

## 9. Conclusion

By separating ecological delays from institutional delays, this paper demonstrates that resource collapse is not solely an ecological phenomenon. The mathematical structure, feedback sign, and review cadence of management institutions are equally decisive in determining whether a resource stabilizes or falls into boom-bust cycles. 

While mobilizing policies generate complex, subcritical oscillations that can be buffered only within narrow windows of administrative delay, protective quota-tracking rules guarantee structural stability across all delays. Ensuring the long-term sustainability of renewable resources requires designing governance institutions whose internal timescales and policy rules harmonize with the biological dynamics of the ecosystems they manage.
