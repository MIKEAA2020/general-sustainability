**1. Introduction**

**1.1 Delays in the governance loop**

Delayed feedback has long been recognised as a source of instability in renewable-resource systems. Classic studies placed the delay inside the ecology itself: Hutchinson (1948) showed that a lag in self-limitation can destabilise logistic growth; Ezekiel (1938) documented the two-year lag between price signals and supply in the pork cycle; Ludwig, Jones and Holling (1978) put delays and hysteresis at the heart of insect-outbreak dynamics. Subsequent work analysed maturation lags, stage structure and harvested predator–prey systems whose ecological delays themselves produce oscillations.

The delay examined here sits in a different place—in the governance loop. Stock assessment produces a signal of decline. That signal is filtered into a running memory of the deficit. The memory then drives a change in deployed harvesting effort, but only after a deployment lag between decision and action. Such institutional lags are well documented. Decision makers in laboratory resource experiments systematically misperceive the systems they govern, responding too slowly and in the wrong functional form (Moxnes, 1998). Institutional design determines which signals reach which actors and when (Ostrom, 1990). The management record supplies the field-scale counterpart: the northern-cod collapse unfolded through years of assessment advice and incremental institutional response before the 1992 moratorium (Hutchings and Myers, 1994; Walters and Maguire, 1996). More recent literature treats assessment frequency itself as a managed design parameter with measurable consequences for yield and depletion risk (Li, Bence and Brenden, 2016; Peterson et al., 2022).

What the dynamical-systems literature has not yet supplied is an analysis of what this delay *does* to the stability of a harvested-stock system when the delay sits in the governance controller rather than in the ecology. Two questions follow. First, does the sign structure of the feedback law—whether managers mobilise extra effort or protect the stock by restoring harvest toward a cap—select qualitatively different bifurcation behaviour? Second, what happens when the continuous delay is replaced by periodic review (sample-and-hold), in which the stock is assessed once per review interval and the decision is held fixed until the next assessment?

The institutional loop is not fisheries-specific, and neither is the analysed class of models. Assessment on a fixed cadence, a rule that converts the assessed signal into a quota or an effort decision, and a deployment lag between decision and action recur across renewable-resource institutions, from quota-tracked harvests to capped withdrawals. It is the formal signature of a coupled human–natural system whose human component is a controller with memory. The model class developed below is deliberately stock-agnostic: the state \(N\) is a renewable stock in material or biomass units, not a named fishery. Later we register the same governance structure acting on a stock whose own ecology carries a maturation delay, so that institutional and ecological delays are analysed within one frame rather than opposed in caricature. Fisheries supply the motivating instance—the setting in which periodic review is most literally institutionalised—and the management language of the two rules (effort escalation versus quota tracking) is the language of harvest control.

The overshoot mechanism analysed throughout has a simple single-asset form. A hen produces a sustainable flow of eggs each year; consumption above that flow cannot be conjured, so the shortfall is covered by slaughtering the hen—the asset is destroyed to meet present demand. The loop is self-reinforcing. A deficit raises liquidation pressure, liquidation lowers the stock, the lower stock lowers sustainable yield, and the deficit grows. With the institutional deployment delay inserted into the loop, the deficit is observed, filtered and answered with a lag. The hen’s loop is then the delayed mobilising channel. The delay does not create the shortfall; it decides when the response to it arrives. The phase-stabilised window identified later is the interval of delays inside which the lag, not the feedback itself, holds the loop stable.

Not all liquidation channels are the same. Killing the hen removes a reproducing adult at once (culling the standing stock). Eating fertilised eggs suppresses next year’s recruits without touching a living adult (recruitment suppression). Eating an apple often leaves the seed unharmed, so consumption and reproduction can be substantially decoupled. These distinctions matter for large-amplitude behaviour even when they leave local spectra unchanged.

**1.2 Contributions**

The paper makes six principal contributions.

1. A gated three-state core. A stock–memory–effort system in which a multiplicative saturation gate enforces the effort boundary exactly, making the admissible region forward-invariant. The model is a mathematical parameterisation, not a calibration to a named fishery; two reference parameter sets serve as sensitivity anchors.

2. The complete Hopf cubic and the even-pairs algebra. The characteristic equation of the linearisation reduces to a cubic modulus condition together with a phase relation. At the interior equilibrium a filter identity makes one structural term vanish, so the total algebraic multiplicity of positive roots is even—generically zero or two distinct simple roots. The two-frequency structure of the architecture is therefore an algebraic fact, not a numerical accident.

3. Interval-certified crossings. The two Hopf delays of the mobilising channel are certified by interval Newton iteration with outward rounding—near 3.7 and 150 years at the first reference point. The certification applies to the local spectrum of the cubic; a stronger certificate of full Hopf bifurcation for retarded functional differential equations is not claimed.

4. The sign separation. The mobilising law (gain that grows with deployed effort) and the protective quota-tracking law (restoring gain) yield opposite local mathematics on the same stock–memory block. The mobilising channel carries a pair of Hopf crossings, both subcritical. The protective channel carries a no-Hopf theorem: all cubic coefficients are positive, Descartes’ rule and Routh–Hurwitz exclude every imaginary root, and the loop gain peaks well below one, so exponential stability holds for every delay. Interpolation of the two channels together with a weighted small-gain argument shows that delay-induced instability requires a sufficiently large mobilising weight.

5. The review interval as control. Under sample-and-hold review the monodromy (the one-period return map) is computed in closed form. The protective channel remains stable under annual review. The mobilising channel is unstable under annual review and restabilises only when the review interval exceeds about 6.5 years, through a Neimark–Sacker-type crossing. An apparent threshold near 2.3 years reported by a first-order numerical update is an artefact of the discretisation, not a property of the governance system. For a slow stock the review interval therefore acts as a local spectral design parameter, and the continuous-delay recommendation does not transfer to periodic review.

6. Global numerics at declared status. Continuation, Floquet-multiplier tracking and basin tests give a five-regime attractor topology whose certification tiers are stated precisely. Two folds are certified at the discrete-collocation level; the continuum off-grid residual stage and the continuous-delay lift remain open. A basin-boundary transition toward a large-amplitude attractor of unverified identity is reported as such.

**2. The Model Class**

Throughout, \(N\) is a renewable stock (material or biomass units), \(Z\) is a filtered deficit-memory state, and \(E\) is an extraction effort—a dimensionless institutional deployment intensity, not a conserved material stock. The delay \(\tau\) is a discrete action (deployment) delay. The filter timescale \(\tau_m\) is an ordinary state relaxation time, not a second delay. The two sign channels are the *mobilising channel*—the feedback law whose gain grows with deployment—and the *protective channel*—the quota-tracking law whose gain restores toward a cap. Periodic-review results use *sample-and-hold*: the delayed signal is measured once at each review instant and held fixed between reviews, so the review interval \(T_r\) is the cadence at which the institution re-decides.

The core three-state system is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot N &= S(N) - qEN, \\
\dot Z &= \frac{1}{\tau_m}\bigl[\Phi_k(qEN - S(N)) - Z\bigr], \\
\dot E &= \left(1 - \frac{E}{E_{\max}}\right)\left[\eta E\left(\frac{Z(t-\tau)}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} - \frac{E}{E_{\max}}\right) + \delta_0\frac{Z(t-\tau)}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + Z(t-\tau)}\right],
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(S(N)\) is logistic renewal and \(\Phi_k\) is a smoothed, non-negative signal map of the stock-decline rate. The multiplicative gate \((1 - E/E_{\max})\) is essential: it is a hard saturation architecture that enforces \(E \in [0, E_{\max}]\) by construction. Two reference parameter sets (Candidates A and B) serve as sensitivity anchors; they are mathematical parameterisations, not a joint calibration to a named resource or institution.

**Theorem 2.1 (Forward invariance of the admissible region).** For histories lying in the box \(\{0\le N\le K,\ Z\ge 0,\ 0\le E\le E_{\max}\}\), every classical solution remains in that box for as long as it exists. The five boundary faces are each either invariant or inward-pointing, and the argument proceeds by the method of steps.

Boundedness of the memory state then follows by variation of constants, the vector field is locally Lipschitz on the resulting compact set, and solutions continue for all future time.

Four further variants delimit robustness: an ungated effort law, a two-channel stock law that distinguishes culling of standing stock from recruitment suppression, an active-pool extension, and a primitive-flux core with detritus. No invariant set, equilibrium formula or local threshold transfers between them without a separate argument. A four-state working core that restores an active abiotic pool is related to the three-state system by a frozen-pool approximation valid on finite institutional timescales near equilibrium, but not for large-amplitude cycles or spectral quantities.

A protective institution replaces the effort law by a quota-tracking rule that restores effort toward a decreasing cap function of the delayed memory. The two channels can be interpolated by a convex combination of the mobilising and protective brackets, with independent delays if desired.

**3. Equilibria and the Characteristic Equation**

At any interior equilibrium the harvest equals renewal, the signal argument vanishes, and the memory state equals a positive constant independent of the delay and of the filter parameters. Substituting into the effort equation produces a quadratic with exactly one positive root. Admissibility then determines a unique positive stock. The equilibrium itself is independent of delay; its stability is not.

On the extinction face the same memory value appears, and the gated law supports both an interior-effort extinction rest and a boundary rest at maximum effort. The survival condition is identical to positivity of the interior stock; the two branches are never simultaneously stable.

Linearisation about the interior rest yields a characteristic quasi-polynomial
\[
P(\lambda) - C_Z L(\lambda) e^{-\lambda\tau} = 0.
\]
A filter identity special to the architecture collapses the cross term in \(L\), leaving \(L(\lambda) = B_E\lambda\). This cancellation is the structural identity that later produces the even-pairs algebra.

**4. The Complete Hopf Cubic**

A purely imaginary root \(\lambda = i\omega\) exists if and only if \(x = \omega^2\) is a positive root of a cubic modulus condition \(H(x) = 0\) and the delay satisfies a corresponding phase relation. A cubic has at most three positive roots, hence at most three Hopf-frequency families.

At the interior equilibrium the cross term vanishes identically, so
\[
H(x) = (x + A_N^2)(x + d^2)(x + C_E^2) - C_Z^2 B_E^2 x.
\]
Then \(H(0) > 0\) and \(H(x)\to +\infty\), so the total algebraic multiplicity of strictly positive roots is even. Generically one therefore observes zero or two distinct simple positive roots. The two-frequency structure is an algebraic fact of the architecture.

Two general statements frame the delay mathematics. The scalar Hayes result shows that a delayed negative-feedback term can destroy stability that undelayed feedback sustains, with no restabilisation as the delay grows. No delay conclusion is sign-free: the systems \(\dot x = -ax - bx(t-\tau)\) and \(\dot x = -ax + bx(t-\tau)\) have different stability properties. The named systems of this paper instantiate the two signs.

**5. The Mobilising Channel**

The mobilising channel is the effort law whose delayed gain on the deficit memory is positive. Its behavioural reading is direct: when an institution sees a deficit signal it deploys more extraction effort—a fleet expansion, a subsidy release, an open-access licence issuance—and the deployment is realised only after a delay.

The undelayed gated version of this law is already unstable. An interval of intermediate delays, rather than the short-delay regime familiar from the scalar Hayes example, is the window in which the loop is stabilised by phase. Institutional delay therefore acts as a phase filter that can either stabilise or destabilise depending on its value; it is not a one-directional hazard.

Complete cubic search together with verified simplicity and transversality yields a pair of crossings. At Candidate A they lie near 3.67 years (stabilising) and 150.36 years (destabilising). Interval Newton iteration with outward rounding certifies the enclosures
\[
\tau_- \in [3.6661490142739,\ 3.6661490142743],\qquad
\tau_+ \in [150.3584773101408,\ 150.3584773101421].
\]
Both crossings are subcritical. Enforcing the effort boundary relocates the local thresholds by tens of percent without changing the equilibrium; thresholds therefore do not transport between gated and ungated variants.

The first Lyapunov coefficients are positive at both gated Candidate-A crossings. Criticality is not invariant under regularisation of the signal map, and no uniform topology in the regularisation parameter is claimed.

A conditional persistence result shows that the local Hopf points survive a class of residual macroeconomic feedbacks and finite-reservoir perturbations, provided the fast block remains uniformly Hurwitz and non-feedback compartments stay outside the delay loop. Global fold events lie outside the hypotheses.

Interpolation of the two channels produces a two-delay characteristic equation. A weighted small-gain theorem then yields a conservative threshold: delay-induced cycles cannot be generated by accelerating the protective response alone; the mobilising weight must first exceed a sufficient-condition radius.

**6. The Protective Channel**

The protective channel is the quota-tracking law. When an institution observes a deficit signal, effort is restored toward a cap rather than mobilised further; landing pressure is reduced as the signal worsens. The channel acts on the same stock–memory block but with the sign of the delayed gain reversed and the gain modulus reorganised by the cap calibration.

**Theorem 6.1 (Delay-independent local stability).** At the calibrated Candidate-A point the modulus cubic has all coefficients positive and satisfies the Routh–Hurwitz conditions. The undelayed Jacobian is Hurwitz and the zero root is excluded independently of delay. Consequently the characteristic quasi-polynomial has no purely imaginary root for any delay, and the equilibrium is exponentially stable for every delay.

The same conclusion is visible in loop-gain form: the gain function peaks at 0.08011, strictly less than one. Destabilisation by short delay is therefore confined to the mobilising summand; the protective channel has no periodic branch born from the equilibrium at any delay.

A pure sign flip of the delayed gain leaves the frequency equation unchanged and merely shifts every admissible delay branch by an odd half-period. Feedback sign alone cannot create or eliminate Hopf-frequency families. The protective no-Hopf property is a property of the quota law’s modulus (its direct damping and delayed gain), not of its sign. A pure sign reversal is therefore not a protective institution.

Under Euler-reviewed sample-and-hold the protective monodromy has spectral radius 0.9838 at a one-year review interval, so annual review remains linearly stable. An apparent crossing near 2.3 years is carried entirely by the interaction of the explicit-Euler command step with the hold flow; it is not a Hopf point of the continuous delay equation. Under the exact held-measurement update the spectral radius stays below one at every tested review interval up to 300 years. The protective channel’s sampled stability is therefore not cadence-limited.

Three pacing statements follow. The mobilising equilibrium is linearly unstable for delays shorter than the lower crossing. The protective equilibrium is linearly stable for every delay. Wherever the weighted small-gain bound holds, stability of the interpolation is independent of both delays. Faster protective governance is not the hazard; the mobilising sign is.

**7. The Delayed-Recruitment (Maturation-Delay) System**

The two channels of the preceding sections act on a scalar gated core whose recruitment is instantaneous. The applied analogue replaces the stock equation by the Gurney–Blythe–Nisbet delayed-recruitment plant: recruitment at time \(t\) is generated by the cohort that spawned one maturation delay \(g\) earlier. The filter and effort law remain exactly those of the core. At steady state the equilibrium is identical to the base core, giving a clean comparison.

When the maturation delay is switched off the two-delay crossing criterion recovers the base windows exactly, confirming that the delayed-recruitment machinery is consistent. With the institutional channel on, the \(\tau=0\)-stable two-crossing structure occupies four recruitment-rate bands lying on the product locus \(rg\approx 1.5\)–\(1.6\). Longer maturation delays move the band to slower stocks. The associated \(\tau\)-windows and periods lie inside documented governance-lag distributions; the \(g=2\) window, with its decadal period, is the sharpest falsifiable empirical avenue (fast-maturing small pelagics under continuous harvest-control rules).

Nonlinear integration reproduces the recorded cycles. A slow-stock class carries a centuries-long biological cohort cycle, rendering the institutional-delay question moot. Faster classes carry institutional cycles whose windows are the stage analogue of the two families of the scalar core. A distinct two-stage (adult/juvenile) harvest model appearing in the same recovered code base is a separately provenanced object and is not the delayed-recruitment plant analysed here.

The remaining sections of the paper treat sample-and-hold review in full, report the five-regime global attractor topology at declared certification tiers, unify the two channels in a loop-gain family, discuss design consequences and the generality of the three institutional-loop coordinates (sign structure of the response rule, deployment delay, and review cadence), ground those coordinates in documented governance timelines such as the northern-cod record, and list open problems. The message remains the one stated in the abstract: the form and timing of institutional response, not ecological lag alone, determine whether governance stabilises or destabilises a harvested stock; and the review interval acts as a local spectral design parameter with opposite effects under the two rules.

# 8. The review interval as control

Periodic review is the form that institutional delay takes in modern fisheries governance: assessment is conducted on a fixed cadence (annual, biennial), and the result is acted on with a discrete update. On this architecture the review interval is a spectral design parameter that acts in opposite directions on the two channels.

One point is stated once, at the outset. Periodic review is a *hybrid* system—a frozen-effort continuous hold interleaved with a discrete effort update at the review instants—and is not the delay equation of §§2–6 sampled at \(\tau=T_r\). Its stability diagram is therefore not a re-plot of the continuous-delay \(\tau\)-axis, and “lengthen the interval” is not a relabelling of “decrease the delay.”

The protective channel is stable at annual review under both the Euler and the exact update; its apparent 2.3-year instability threshold is a discretisation artefact. The mobilising channel is unstable at annual review and restabilises as the review interval is lengthened—but its restabilising crossing is a complex unit-circle crossing of the sampled-data map, not a Hopf of the continuous-delay equation.

The implication for institutional design is that the review interval is a controller knob, not a governance recommendation. An interval that stabilises the equilibrium may still allow severe inter-review depletion, and inter-review tube safety is an additional criterion not certified here.

Throughout this section the *monodromy* is the one-period linear return map of the reviewed loop—the product of the held flow and the review update—whose spectral radius decides the linear stability of the sampled equilibrium (radius below one: stable; above one: unstable).

**Theorem 8.1** (Sampled-data monodromy of the mobilising channel). Linearise the gated three-state core about the interior equilibrium and replace the delayed feedback by sample-and-hold of period \(T_r\) with one Euler review step. Between reviews the variational system is \(\dot\xi=A_{\mathrm{hold}}\xi\) with \(A_{\mathrm{hold}}\) as in Proposition 6.2. The monodromy of one review interval is
\[
M(T_r)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&T_r C_Z&1+T_r C_E\end{pmatrix}\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}}T_r),
\tag{12}
\]
now with the mobilising gains \(C_E=-0.0595\), \(C_Z=+1.785\). Assume the flow-then-update timing convention (the pre-review state \(\xi_k^-=\xi(t_k^-)\) flows for one period under the held effort, and the review applies the Euler update to the flowed state, giving the post-review state \(\xi_{k+1}^-=M(T_r)\xi_k^-\) with \(M\) as displayed—the update factor acts on the end-of-period assessment; the reversed ordering \(\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}}T_r)R(T_r)\) is a different hybrid system, not used here; the two orderings have closely related linear stabilities, proved below; the nonlinear map differs, so the convention remains fixed throughout).

Then: (i) the sampled equilibrium is exponentially stable if and only if every eigenvalue of \(M(T_r)\) lies in the open unit disc; (ii) a complex unit-circle crossing—the linear spectral signature of a Neimark–Sacker bifurcation (transverse crossing, nonresonance, and the nonlinear first Lyapunov coefficient of the map are not verified here, and the bifurcation name is reserved for that verified case)—occurs at those \(T_r\) where \(M(T_r)\) has a simple pair \(e^{\pm i\theta}\), \(\theta\notin\{0,\pi\}\), with the remaining spectrum inside the disc; (iii) \((M(T_r)-I)/T_r\) converges to the continuous undelayed Jacobian as \(T_r\to0\). The statement concerns this sample-and-hold/Euler review scheme, not the continuous-delay equation.

*Proof.* The monodromy formula is the derivation of Proposition 6.2 with the mobilising gains; the stability criterion is the discrete-time spectral-radius condition; the unit-circle characterisation is the standard eigenvalue-crossing criterion for maps. The consistency limit follows from \(\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}}T_r)=I+A_{\mathrm{hold}}T_r+O(T_r^2)\) and the update factor \(I+T_r R_0\), where \(R_0=\mathbf{e}_3(C_Z\mathbf{e}_2^\top+C_E\mathbf{e}_3^\top)\) is the rank-one effort-update row, whose sum with \(A_{\mathrm{hold}}T_r\) is \(I+T_r(A_{\mathrm{hold}}+R_0)+O(T_r^2)\). Writing \(J_{\mathrm{cont}}=A_{\mathrm{hold}}+R_0\) for the full undelayed Jacobian of the continuous linearisation, the limit is \((M(T_r)-I)/T_r\to J_{\mathrm{cont}}\). The matrix \(R_0\) is precisely what restores the \(C_E,C_Z\) entries that vanish in the third row of \(A_{\mathrm{hold}}\), which is why the consistency limit is not read off from \(A_{\mathrm{hold}}\) alone. For the ordering comparison: \(R_0\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}}T_r)\) and \(\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}}T_r)R_0\) have identical spectra whenever \(R_0\) is invertible (they are then similar matrices), so the linear stability classification transfers between the two orderings in that case; the nonlinear map differs, and the convention stays fixed. \(\square\)

On the gated Candidate A hold map the Euler scheme reports: annual review unstable, \(\rho(M(1))=1.00055\) (the undelayed linearisation being already unstable, §5.1), with the sampled equilibrium’s restabilising complex unit-circle crossing at \(T_r^{\mathrm{UC}}=47.536\) yr and a multiplier reaching \(-1\) at \(T_r^{(-1)}=79.143\) yr, and the reported stable interval \((47.536,79.143)\) yr. These quantitative Euler values do **not** survive the exact held-measurement update; they are artefacts of the explicit-Euler command step and are recorded as such in Remark 8.1, with the exact record reported in Proposition 8.1 (unit-circle crossing at \(T_r=6.50\) yr; the Euler-reported crossings give exact spectral radius \(0.786\) at \(47.536\) yr and \(0.597\) at \(79.143\) yr). The load-bearing feature is therefore the *cadence* statement—lengthening the review interval restabilises a system that annual review destabilises—not the Euler map’s particular thresholds.

The computation is the zero set of \(\det(M(T_r)-e^{i\theta}I)=0\) on the declared map: for a slow stock under this review operator, **the review interval is a local spectral design parameter**. Local spectral stability is not governance: a long review interval may locally stabilise the equilibrium while allowing severe transient depletion between reviews, and inter-review tube safety—the stock floor held along the whole interval under the declared disturbance class—is the additional criterion a governance recommendation would require, which is not certified here.

The contrast with the protective channel—stable at annual review and at every tested interval under the exact update—is complete: the two sign channels respond to the review interval in opposite directions, and the protective channel’s only sampled instability is that of the Euler command step.

**Remark 8.1** (Euler command-step artefact). The Euler map’s reported restabilisation at \(T_r^{\mathrm{UC}}=47.536\) yr with stable interval \((47.536,79.143)\) yr is not a property of periodic review. Proposition 8.1 shows that those crossings do not survive under the exact held-measurement update (exact spectral radius \(0.786\) at \(47.536\) yr and \(0.597\) at \(79.143\) yr, both below one) and that the exact monodromy has a single unit-circle crossing on \([0.2,300]\) yr—the restabilising complex pair at \(T_r=6.50\) yr. The Euler thresholds are artefacts of the explicit command step, which is why they are labelled here and not reported as crossings of the review operator. The exact map and its single crossing are the quantities reported in Proposition 8.1.

The design conclusion is not, however, an artefact of the update. As \(T_r\to0\) every consistent scheme inherits the undelayed linearisation (Theorem 8.1(iii)), and as \(T_r\to\infty\) the exact hold tends to a rank-one operator whose eigenvalue is the DC gain, proportional to \(L(0)=0\) by the filter identity, so the stock–effort coupling is lost and the equilibrium is stable for long enough review intervals. Each consistent scheme therefore restabilises at some \(T_r\); only the location is scheme-dependent, quantified in the closing scheme-dependence remark of this section, which reports the tight \(6.50\)–\(6.73\) yr band across the three consistent schemes.

**Proposition 8.1** (Exact held-measurement monodromy). Keep the sample-and-hold architecture, the frozen-effort hold flow \(\dot\xi=A_{\mathrm{hold}}\xi\), and the flow-then-update timing convention of Proposition 6.2 and Theorem 8.1, and replace the explicit-Euler command step by the exact held-measurement update: the effort law \(\dot e=C_E e+C_Z z\) is integrated exactly over the review interval with the measurement held at the flowed end-of-period value, giving the update
\[
e_{k+1}=e^{C_E T_r}e_k+\frac{e^{C_E T_r}-1}{C_E}\,C_Z\,z(t_{k+1}^-).
\]
The monodromy of one review interval is therefore
\[
M_{\mathrm{ex}}(T_r)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&\dfrac{e^{C_E T_r}-1}{C_E}\,C_Z&e^{C_E T_r}\end{pmatrix}\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}}T_r),
\tag{13}
\]
which satisfies the same consistency limit as the Euler map, \((M_{\mathrm{ex}}(T_r)-I)/T_r\to J_{\mathrm{cont}}\) as \(T_r\to0\) (the update factor is the exact exponential of the effort gain, of which \(1+T_r C_E\) is the first-order Taylor). Then, at the gated Candidate A point:

(i) Protective channel (\(C_E=-0.850336\), \(C_Z=-1.661702\)). \(\rho(M_{\mathrm{ex}}(1))=0.9838\), and \(\rho<1\) at every tested review interval on \([0.2,300]\) yr, with maximum \(0.9967\) attained at the shortest interval tested. The protective hold map is stable at every review interval tested; the Euler crossing at \(2.306\) yr is carried entirely by the explicit-Euler command step and vanishes under the exact update.

(ii) Mobilising channel (\(C_E=-0.0595\), \(C_Z=+1.785\)). \(\rho(M_{\mathrm{ex}}(1))=1.00035\): the annual-review instability survives the exact update. The Euler-reported crossings do not: at \(47.536\) yr and \(79.143\) yr the exact spectral radius is \(0.786\) and \(0.597\). On \([0.2,300]\) yr the exact monodromy has exactly one unit-circle crossing—the restabilising complex pair at
\[
T_r^{\mathrm{UC,ex}}=6.50\ \mathrm{yr},
\]
with crossing eigenvalues \(0.9846\pm0.1746\,i\) (\(|\lambda|=1\)), third eigenvalue \(0.1647\) inside the disc, a non-resonant argument (\(2\pi/\theta\approx35.8\)), and transverse direction (\(\rho-1\) changes sign). The restabilisation is a property of review cadence, not of the command step. Holding the measurement at the start-of-period value instead of the flowed end-of-period value does not move the reported crossings at the reported precision. The Neimark–Sacker reading keeps the discipline of Theorem 8.1: the nonlinear conditions are not verified.

*Proof.* The update is the exact solution of the scalar effort equation with \(z\) held constant; the monodromy factor follows from the flow-then-update convention, under which the update acts on the flowed state, whose effort coordinate is unchanged by the frozen hold. The consistency limit follows exactly as in Theorem 8.1 with \(e^{C_E T_r}=1+C_E T_r+O(T_r^2)\) and \((e^{C_E T_r}-1)/C_E=T_r+O(T_r^2)\). The channel statements are the computed spectral-radius records of \(M_{\mathrm{ex}}\) on the declared grids, re-executable from the registered model constants; the same computation reproduces the registered Euler numbers of Proposition 6.2 and Theorem 8.1 before the exact map is evaluated, and both update conventions (start- and end-of-period measurement) agree at the reported precision. \(\square\)

**Remark** (Scheme-dependence of the sampled-data map). Neither \(M\) nor \(M_{\mathrm{ex}}\) is the physically natural zero-order hold of the delay equation, in which the delayed signal \(z\) is held at its sampled value \(z_k\) while the effort dynamics \(\dot e=C_E e+C_Z z_k\) runs continuously alongside the stock response. That scheme has monodromy, in closed form,
\[
M_{\mathrm{ZOH}}(T_r)=e^{A_{\mathrm{Z}}T_r}+\Bigl(\int_0^{T_r}e^{A_{\mathrm{Z}}s}\,ds\Bigr)C_Z\,\mathbf{e}_3\mathbf{e}_2^\top,
\]
with \(A_{\mathrm{Z}}\) the full undelayed Jacobian with the \(C_Z\) entry removed. The two maps analysed above freeze effort throughout the flow and integrate the effort law impulsively at the review instant; they are one choice among several consistent schemes, so the phrases “the Euler-reported crossings are artefacts of the command step” and “the restabilisation is a property of review cadence” are properly read as scheme-dependent claims, not as properties of periodic review per se.

The *qualitative* conclusion is not scheme-dependent, and it is provable without computing the location. As \(T_r\to0\) every consistent scheme inherits the undelayed linearisation (\((M-I)/T_r\to J_{\mathrm{cont}}\), Theorem 8.1(iii)); and as \(T_r\to\infty\) the exact hold tends to a rank-one operator whose eigenvalue is the DC gain, which is proportional to \(L(\lambda)\) evaluated at \(\lambda=0\)—and \(L(0)=B_E\cdot0=0\) by the filter identity of §3.2, so the map loses the stock–effort coupling and the equilibrium is stable for long enough review intervals. Hence every consistent periodic-review scheme restabilises at *some* \(T_r\); only the location is scheme-dependent.

The scheme-dependence is in fact small for this architecture. Re-executing the monodromy on the registered Candidate A constants: the exact held-measurement map has its mobilising complex crossing at \(T_r=6.5013\) yr; the same map with the measurement held at the *start*-of-period value gives \(T_r=6.5013\) yr (so the paper’s clause that start-of-period measurement does not move the reported crossings holds); and the physically native zero-order hold \(M_{\mathrm{ZOH}}\) gives \(T_r=6.7279\) yr (crossing eigenvalues \(0.9855\pm0.1699\,i\)), within about 4% of the exact value. All three consistent schemes therefore restabilise in a tight \(6.50\)–\(6.73\) yr band, and the reported \(6.50\) yr is a robust computed crossing of the declared map, not a certified invariant of periodic review. The protective channel is stable under \(M_{\mathrm{ZOH}}\) as well (no crossing on \([0.2,200]\) yr; \(\rho(1)=0.9928\)), consistent with the protective no-Hopf claim. The rank-one limit is verified numerically: \(\rho(M_{\mathrm{ZOH}})\) falls to \(0.242\), \(0.0165\), and below \(10^{-6}\) at \(T_r=200\), \(500\), \(2000\) yr, with the map’s numerical rank dropping to 2 at \(500\) yr and 1 at \(2000\) yr—confirming the DC-gain (\(L(0)=0\)) loss of coupling.

# 9. Global numerics at declared certification levels

All results in this section are numerical results at their declared status: computed outputs tied to registered equations, parameter sets, history classes, methods, tolerances and finite domains, with basins restricted to the histories actually tested. The fold-status discipline governs every global statement. The events below are numerical continuation, multiplier, basin and turning-region results. The lower fold of §9.2 and the second fold of the same section are both certified at the discrete collocation level (the rebuilt Moore–Spence/Krawczyk stage for the lower fold; the interval-Krawczyk certificate for the second fold—for each, the continuum off-grid residual stage remains unimplemented and the continuous-delay lift remains open); no continuous-delay fold proof is claimed. Rigorous saddle-node-of-periodic-orbit results for delay equations exist for specific classes (Beretka and Vas, 2020); none is claimed for the events below.

The implication for fisheries governance is that, on the declared architecture, the global attractor topology is a five-regime structure whose lower and upper boundaries are both fold-certified at the discrete collocation level, and whose basin boundary lies between the two folds rather than at either. The saddle-node classification remains provisional.

## 9.1 Methods

Hopf points are located by root tracking of the characteristic equation and independently by the cubic (Theorem 4.1); folds by Keller pseudo-arclength continuation of the periodic orbit with \(1.5\)–\(5\times10^6\) yr persistence tests per step and far-from-equilibrium cross-checks (collocation-continuation numerics in the DDE-bifurcation tradition: Engelborghs, Luzyanina and Roose, 2002; background: Guckenheimer and Holmes, 1983; Kuznetsov, 2004). Fixed-initial-condition bisection mislocates folds by more than 20 yr under the critical slowing down near each Hopf (linear rates \(10^{-4}\)–\(10^{-5}\) yr\(^{-1}\)) and is not used.

## 9.4 The four-state working core

The characteristic matrix \(\Delta(\lambda)=\lambda I-A_0-A_\tau e^{-\lambda\tau}\) of the gated working core at the frozen-donor equilibrium \((N,A,Z,E)=(89.52562,397.8665,\log2/10,2.08962)\) has simple imaginary roots at
\[
\tau_-=3.78487\ \mathrm{yr}\ (\text{period }250.44\ \mathrm{yr}),\qquad
\tau_+=150.12175\ \mathrm{yr}\ (\text{period }159.13\ \mathrm{yr}),
\]
with \(|\det\Delta(i\omega,\tau)|<10^{-18}\)—the characteristic-pinned pair, with \(\tau_-\) \(3.2\%\) higher and \(\tau_+\) \(0.2\%\) lower than the three-state values—the directly computed spectral sensitivity of the crossings to the frozen-\(A\) perturbation, \(12.7\) times the parameter ratio \(A_0/A^*\approx2.5\times10^{-3}\), and not a consequence of the trajectory bound of Lemma 2.1, which bounds state deviations on \([0,T]\), not eigenvalue locations. Fourier collocation produces a small periodic orbit of residual \(\sim10^{-13}\) immediately below \(\tau_+\) (amplitude \(0.090\) at \(\tau=150.082\)). Continuation of the large-amplitude cycle (converged orbit state and delay history carried between steps, \(10^4\)–\(10^5\) yr per step) locates the two global folds at
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{term},L}^{(4)}\approx5.63\ \mathrm{yr},\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{fold},R}^{(4)}\approx64.4\ \mathrm{yr},
\]
the lower bracketed by a persistent periodic orbit at \(\tau=5.62\) (peak-to-peak \(N\)-amplitude \(\approx23\)) and failure of continuation by \(5.64\), the upper by a persistent periodic orbit at \(\tau=64.5\) (amplitude \(\approx11\)) and failure of continuation by \(64.25\). The resulting topology is a narrow lower bistable window \((3.78,5.63)\) yr, a wide monostable interval \((5.63,64.4)\) yr, and a wide upper bistable window \((64.4,150.1)\) yr in which the large cycle coexists with the stable equilibrium but, from generic far-from-equilibrium histories, is reached only for \(\tau\) above roughly \(75\)–\(100\) yr (depleted-stock histories only near \(\tau\gtrsim135\)). Cycle periods run from \(\approx371\) yr at \(\tau=4.5\) to \(\approx320\) yr at the lower fold, and from \(\approx156\) yr near \(\tau_+\) to \(\approx73\) yr at the upper fold. Whether either fold is a saddle-node of periodic orbits is not established. A Neimark–Sacker bifurcation of a periodic orbit changes its stability and may create a torus but does not by itself terminate the branch, so branch termination, stability loss and basin-capture loss are reported separately, and a torus-mediated reading would explain simulation-based loss of attraction without explaining branch nonexistence.

A second bifurcation parameter invisible to the three-state core appears: at \(\tau=0\) the four-state equilibrium is unstable for \(\omega_A>\omega_A^*\approx0.001316\) and delay-independently stable below it; above it, on the ungated system, both Hopf points and both folds track smoothly, \(\tau_-\) falling from \(\approx17.5\) to \(\approx6.9\) yr with the monostable interval remaining \(\approx120\)–\(260\) yr; the gated \(\tau_-\approx3.78\) is the baseline \((\omega_A,\kappa_A)\) value, not a uniform constant. The coexistence of the characteristic-pinned pair with the sub-threshold delay-independent stability is not a contradiction, but the two objects must be labelled distinctly. The characteristic-pinned pair (\(\tau_-=3.78487\), \(\tau_+=150.12175\) yr) is the spectrum of the frozen-donor characteristic matrix \(\Delta(\lambda)=\lambda I-A_0-A_\tau e^{-\lambda\tau}\) with the donor pool \(A\) held fixed at its equilibrium value—the standard quasi-polynomial spectrum of the reduced \(4\times4\) system written with \(A\) pinned. The \(\omega_A^*\) boundary (\(\approx0.001316\)) is a different object: the dynamic-\(A\) turnover sweep at \(\tau=0\) of the full system (2), in which \(A\) is itself a state. Call the first the *frozen-donor characteristic pair* and the second the *dynamic-\(A\) stability boundary*; they are two different operators, and reporting both as “the four-state working core” invites exactly the confusion the distinction resolves. The frozen-donor pair is only a spectral statement about the quasi-polynomial with \(A\) frozen; it is not the spectrum of the dynamic-\(A\) system, whose \(\tau=0\) equilibrium is delay-independently stable below \(\omega_A^*\).

Oscillation periods throughout \(\tau<\tau_-\) are \(250\)–\(390\) yr, essentially independent of \(\tau\): the frequency is pinned by \(r\) and \(\tau_m\), not by institutional delay, and uniformly raising \(r\) toward fish-like values destroys the oscillatory regime rather than compressing the period into a decade. The frozen-donor quasi-equilibrium discipline governs all of these numbers: the working point requires continuing geological support (\(\approx4.652\) stock units per year—a companion-interface quantity, Remark of §2.4) and is not a rest point of the closed mass ledger; it is incompatible with the formal quasi-steady slaving target of §2.4; and the working-core thresholds are \(\sigma_{\mathrm{geo}}=1\) properties.

## 9.5 Parameter windows

Within the parameter subregion where the Candidate A two-Hopf pair exists, one-at-a-time variation across the reported ranges preserves the pair and the lower-boundary structure. Outside the bounded \(r\)-window the system is delay-independently stable or lacks a positive equilibrium. \(\tau_-\) lies in \(3.7\)–\(7\) yr across the two effort laws and both candidates, and in \(4\)–\(25\) yr across the full \((r,\eta)\) rectangle; \(\tau_+\) is primarily controlled by the biological time \(1/r\) but also depends on the effort-response chart—Candidates A and B share \(r=0.02\) and have \(\tau_+\approx132\)–\(150\) yr and \(\approx76\)–\(80\) yr respectively across the two effort laws (A: \(132.37\) ungated, \(150.36\) gated; B: \(76.29\) ungated, \(80.42\) gated). # 10. The loop-gain family

The two channels of §§5 and 6 look different on the stock–memory block, but they share a common linearised feedback identity. The unifying object is the loop-gain family: an ecological resolvent supplying admissible frequencies, an institutional gain that must close the loop, and a delay supplying phase. The gain condition is delay-independent; the phase condition rotates through match and past it. Non-monotone delay-stability is therefore possible and structurally natural in multi-frequency loop architectures. The implication for institutional design is that the two channels are two instances of one feedback identity, and the channel-separation theorem is a property of the institutional gain—its modulus and the phase of the direct damping—not of the sign alone.

## 10.1 The general feedback identity

The vector ledger and the reduced cores share a linearised feedback identity, not one nonlinear system. Writing the ledger in primitive fluxes—assimilation \(g\), mortality \(m\), decomposition \(d_U\), harvest \(h\)—the signed depletion signal \(\ell=h-(g-m)=-\dot X\) is an identity, and liquidation is \([\ell]_+\). Let \(\boldsymbol{\xi}\) be the ecological perturbation, \(\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}=\mathbf{J}\boldsymbol{\xi}+\mathbf{b}_E e\), with memory a gain-\(\gamma_m\) filter of \(-\mathbf{c}^\top\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\). The linearised loop of every core in this paper is
\[
\lambda-C_E-C_Z e^{-\lambda\tau}\,\frac{-\gamma_m\lambda\,\mathbf{c}^\top(\lambda\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{J})^{-1}\mathbf{b}_E}{1+\tau_m\lambda}=0.
\tag{14}
\]
The three-state core is the \(1\times1\) case (\(\mathbf{J}=A_N\), \(\mathbf{b}_E=A_E\), \(\gamma_m=1/2\)); the primitive-flux (stoichiometric) core is the \(2\times2\) stock–detritus block with \(\gamma_m=1\) (its signed memory \(\ell=-\dot X\) is unregularised, which places it outside the \(Z\ge0\) invariance statement of §2.2). Both reductions are symbolic identities: the three systems share this characteristic identity but differ in their nonlinear objects—the upper fold is version-robust at bracket resolution (\(\approx64.4\) yr: certified three-state second fold \(64.4023\); four-state bracket \([64.25,64.5]\), whose \(0.4\%\) width sets that resolution) while the Hopf pair stays within a few percent, the equilibrium memory differs (\(Z^*=\delta>0\) versus \(0\)), and the primitive-flux core has no baseline Hopf (\(\eta_{\mathrm{crit}}\approx2.34\)).

The three ingredients are an ecological resolvent supplying admissible frequencies, an institutional gain \(C_Z\gamma_m\) that must close the loop, and a delay supplying phase; the gain condition is \(\tau\)-independent, the phase condition rotates through match and past it, and non-monotone delay-stability is possible and structurally natural in multi-frequency loop architectures (an open-set transversality argument for genericity is not supplied, and no generic claim is made).

## 10.2 The loop-gain exclusion theorem

**Theorem 10.1** (Loop-gain exclusion of delay-induced Hopf). Write the general feedback equation as \(\lambda-C_E-C_Z e^{-\lambda\tau}G(\lambda)=0\). Assume

(H1) \(\displaystyle\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|C_Z|\,|G(i\omega)|}{|i\omega-C_E|}<1\),

(H2) the undelayed system is Hurwitz,

(H3) the zero root is excluded for every delay (\(-C_E-C_Z G(0)\neq0\); in the three-state architecture \(G(0)=0\) and \(C_E<0\) supplies this).

Then there is no purely imaginary characteristic root for any \(\tau\ge0\), hence no delay-induced Hopf, and the equilibrium is exponentially stable for each fixed \(\tau\ge0\).

*Proof.* An imaginary root \(\lambda=i\omega\) would give \(i\omega-C_E=C_Z e^{-i\omega\tau}G(i\omega)\), hence \(|i\omega-C_E|=|C_Z||G(i\omega)|\), contradicting the strict supremum (H1); the zero-root and reference-stability hypotheses (H2)–(H3) close the switch argument exactly as in Theorem 6.1. \(\square\)

The companion Nyquist reading: a Hopf root exists if and only if the loop transfer \(L(i\omega,\tau)=C_Z G(\lambda)e^{-\lambda\tau}/(\lambda-C_E)\) equals \(1\) for some \(\omega>0\), with the small-gain bound \(|L|<1\) as the uniform special case. A separate sufficient certificate in the same family is the Halanay-type delay-independent stability theorem, stated for systems of the form \(\dot x(t)=A_0 x(t)+A_1 x(t-\tau)\) in a declared norm: if for some \(\alpha_0>\beta_0\ge0\) the logarithmic matrix measure satisfies \(\mu_*(A_0)\le-\alpha_0\) and \(\|A_1\|_*\le\beta_0\), then the zero solution is exponentially stable for every fixed \(\tau\ge0\), with decay rate the unique \(\nu>0\) solving \(\nu=\alpha_0-\beta_0 e^{\nu\tau}\) (Halanay, 1966; the decay rate is denoted \(\nu\) here so that \(\eta\) remains the effort-response coefficient of §2 throughout). It is not a consequence of the scalar loop-gain formulation and is stated as its own theorem, not as “the” general certificate. Two qualifications travel with it: the decay rate depends on \(\tau\) and tends to zero as the delay grows (the stability is pointwise in \(\tau\), not delay-uniform), and the condition is sufficient, not necessary—its failure removes the certificate without proving instability; in nonlinear applications the theorem is local after a declared linearisation unless a global incremental bound is proved. The named-system theorems of §§5.4 and 6.2 are instances of this certificate family at declared linearisations.

## 10.3 The logistic identification lemma

**Lemma 10.1** (Logistic identification). In the primitive-flux core take \(A\gg K_A\) and set \(\mu-d=r>0\), \(c=r/K\). Then, uniformly on a declared domain where \(X\) is bounded and \(A\) is bounded away from zero,
\[
g-m=rX\Bigl(1-\frac{X}{K}\Bigr)+O\Bigl(\mu X\frac{K_A}{A}\Bigr),
\]
with the remainder carrying flux units. The identification requires the mortality identification: \(K_A\to0\) without \(\mu-d=r\) and \(c=r/K\) yields \(g\to\mu X\), which is not logistic.

*Proof.* Write \(g-m=\mu X\frac{A}{K_A+A}-dX-cX^2\); for \(A\gg K_A\), \(\frac{A}{K_A+A}=1-\frac{K_A}{A}+O((K_A/A)^2)\), so \(g-m=(\mu-d)X-cX^2+\mu X\,O(K_A/A)\). With \(\mu-d=r\) and \(c=r/K\) this is \(rX(1-X/K)+O(K_A/A)\). Taking \(K_A\to0\) alone gives \(\frac{A}{K_A+A}\to1\), hence \(g-m\to(\mu-d)X-cX^2\), which is \(\mu X\) only if \(d=c=0\); the logistic identification is a joint statement about mortality and saturation, not a limit of the assimilation flux alone. The claim concerns the linearised feedback identity, not the nonlinear objects, which also differ in the equilibrium memory level, the effort baseline, and the closed form of \(E^*\). \(\square\)

## 10.4 The saturating-gate negative screen

Replacing the autocatalytic factor \(\eta EZ_\tau/\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\) by a saturating gate \(\sigma(Z_\tau/Z_0)\) whose linearisation at equilibrium decreases in deployed effort and contains no factor \(\eta E^*/\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\), a search of more than 300 randomised parameterisations found no genuine imaginary-axis root of the general feedback equation (14)—the screen record, with the method stack of Newton eigenvalue tracking, joint modulus minimisation and nonlinear integration, and the nonexistence reading, is deposited in the supplement, S11, which also carries the relocated MPF sweep and intermittency records. This is a numerical nonexistence report on a compact searched set, not an analytic exclusion on the parameter space, and it does not establish that autocatalysis is necessary for a Hopf pair—other non-autocatalytic feedback laws could supply sufficient loop gain and phase. The gated law (1) is not of this class—its \(C_Z\) contains both the gate factor and the autocatalytic factor, and it admits the Hopf pair. In the searched parameter set, removing the autocatalytic factor reduced the gain enough that no imaginary-axis root was detected.

# 11. Discussion

## 11.1 The two channels as a design distinction

The sign separation is the paper’s structural finding. On the identical stock–memory block, the mobilising law (\(C_Z>0\)) carries a subcritical Hopf pair and a five-regime attractor topology, while the protective quota-tracking law (\(C_Z<0\)) carries the no-Hopf theorem—stability at every delay—and stability under annual review. The distinction is not an artefact of the calibration: the iso-gain sign flip (Proposition 6.1) shows that a sign flip alone, without the genuine quota law’s modulus change, retains the Hopf pair at shifted delays and a loop gain above one—so the sign alone does not decide, and the protective channel’s no-Hopf property is a property of the quota law’s combined direct damping and delayed gain. On this architecture, institutions whose calibrated effort law combines weak direct damping with sufficient delayed mobilising gain are exposed to delay-induced cycles; institutions tracking a quota with the calibrated damping are not. The channel-separation reading answers the question posed by the behavioural literature (Moxnes, 1998): the hazard is not slow governance as such, but slow governance of the calibrated mobilising law—weak direct damping with sufficient delayed gain, of which the sign is one ingredient among three (modulus, phase placement, and digital implementation).

## 11.2 The review interval as control

Under periodic review the two channels respond in opposite directions to the same instrument. The protective channel is stable at annual review and—under the exact held-measurement update (Proposition 8.1)—at every tested review interval. Its only sampled instability is the explicit-Euler command step’s near \(T_r\approx2.3\) yr—an artefact to be recognised, not a design threshold; for the calibrated local continuous-delay model, protective delay does not generate a Hopf instability, which is the precise form of the claim, not a general immunity of quota-tracking institutions. The mobilising channel is unstable at annual review and restabilises as the review interval is lengthened, through its complex unit-circle crossing at \(T_r=6.50\) yr under the exact held-measurement update.

The practical reading: for a slow stock governed by a mobilising effort law, frequent assessment and rapid response is destabilising, and within this operator the only computed spectral control is the review interval itself—a governance parameter, not an ecological one. Local spectral stabilisation is not governance: an interval that stabilises the equilibrium may still admit severe inter-review depletion, so the statement stops at the spectral level (§8). The result echoes, in a single-species institutional setting, the sampled-data principle that the sampling period is a controller design variable (Åström and Wittenmark, 1997), and it supplies the missing dynamical content to the observation that institutional timing structures resource governance (Ostrom, 1990).

## 11.3 Generality: what carries beyond the analysed class

Three coordinates of the institutional loop are independent of the ecology they govern: the sign structure and modulus of the response law, the deployment delay, and the review cadence. The theorems of this paper fix what those coordinates do within the declared stock–memory–effort class—the delay window and its two certified crossings, the no-Hopf property of the calibrated quota law, the opposite cadence responses of the two rules—and the class itself is stock-agnostic: a harvested renewable stock under institutional control, of which fisheries are the motivating instance, with the liquidation channels of §1.1 (culling the standing stock, suppressing recruitment, decoupled consumption) marking how far the same loop reaches beyond a single-sector reading. What transfers to other periodically reviewed renewable-resource regimes is therefore the mechanism’s form, not the numerical thresholds: the thresholds are theorems about the declared parameterisation, and any field application must re-identify the institutional coefficients—the gains, delays and cadences of the governance loop—with the same standing as the biological ones.

For management readers the paper’s technical vocabulary has a direct translation:

| Technical term | Management reading |
|---|---|
| Mobilising law (\(C_Z>0\)) | Effort-escalation rule: decline triggers more deployment |
| Protective quota-tracking law (\(C_Z<0\)) | Catch/quota rule: harvest restored toward a cap |
| Hopf crossing | Onset or loss of oscillatory stock dynamics |
| Neimark–Sacker-type crossing | Quasi-periodic instability emerging under periodic review |
| Monodromy spectral radius | Stability margin of one review cycle |
| Review interval \(T_r\) | Assessment/management review cycle length |

Two cautions travel with the translation. First, the discretisation artefacts are a general warning for management modelling, not a curiosity of this system: in both channels an explicit first-order update manufactured thresholds (near 2.3 yr for the protective channel, the half-century restabilisation for the mobilising channel) that the exact update of the same reviewed loop does not carry, so a discrete-time management model can report stability boundaries that belong to its discretisation rather than to the institution. Second, local spectral stabilisation is not governance: an interval that stabilises the equilibrium may still admit severe inter-review depletion (§8), and the capture boundaries of §9 are basin statements, not thresholds a manager can read off a spectrum. The design reading that survives both cautions is the paper’s central one: the response rule’s class is a design choice, the review interval is a control variable with opposite effects under the two rules, and the institutional coefficients are dynamical parameters to be identified, bounded and designed against. The documented institutional timelines collected in the next subsection ground the scales of those coordinates in the management record.

## 11.4 Documented institutional timelines

The two timing coordinates of the analysed loop—the deployment delay and the review cadence—have documented values at management scale, and the northern cod (NAFO 2J3KL) record is the canonical instance. Assessment of that stock proceeded on an annual cycle while the estimated spawning-stock biomass fell from about 735 kt in the 1991 assessment to about 31 kt in the 1994 assessment (DFO, 2016); the directed-fishery moratorium was announced on 2 July 1992, and the renewed commercial fishery on 26 June 2024—thirty-two years later (DFO, 2016, 2024). The collapse itself has been attributed to overexploitation under sustained harvest pressure (Hutchings and Myers, 1994), with the assessment-and-response chronology dissected as a management failure (Walters and Maguire, 1996).

For this paper the record does two things. It documents that institutional response delays of years, and management-cycle decisions spanning decades, are real objects at the scale the analysed windows occupy—the mobilising channel’s delay window between crossings near 3.7 and 150 yr, and the stage-analysis bands of §7.3, whose \(g=2\) \(\tau\)-window lies inside the documented \(2\)–\(13\) yr governance-lag distribution. And it supplies the canonical instance of the inter-review depletion that the local spectral statements of §8 explicitly do not certify: a stock falling by a factor of more than twenty between annual assessments while the institutional response remained incremental.

The review cadence, likewise, is an actively managed design parameter in operating fisheries rather than a theoretical construct. Annual assessment and annual catch-setting are the historical norm for major stocks, and management agencies are actively weighing moves to multi-year assessment cycles; the assessment-frequency literature finds that lengthening the interval changes fishery performance—less frequent assessment reduced relative yield and increased the risk of stock depletion and interannual variation in yield in simulation (Li, Bence and Brenden, 2016), with the same caution echoed for a large coastal shark fishery under multi-year assessment (Peterson et al., 2022). That literature treats the review interval as a management-design variable with risk consequences—the empirical counterpart of this paper’s reading of \(T_r\) as a local spectral design parameter—and its direction of caution matches the theory’s: the mobilising channel restabilises only for review intervals above about 6.5 yr, but local spectral stabilisation is not governance, and inter-review depletion is the uncertified cost that both the theorems and the assessment-frequency record place at the centre.

What the documented timelines ground is the scale of the two timing coordinates, not the coefficients of any theorem. No stock, effort law or delay value in this paper is calibrated to a named fishery, and the northern cod record is a timescale grounding and a caution, not a calibration: the institutional coefficients—the gains, delays and cadences of the governance loop—remain to be identified from field data before any threshold of the declared class is read against a fishery. A structured search across more than thirty resource systems for an unconfounded instance of the delayed institutional feedback mechanism—a responsive rule, an independently dateable lag, no dominant competing driver, and unit-level data—found no eligible case; that search and its null result are the subject of the companion sampled-governance paper, and they mark the empirical programme named in the conclusion as prospective. The documented record therefore supplies what a sustainability or management readership requires of a theory paper—real governance timelines at the analysed scales—without converting any theorem into a claim about a named institution.

## 11.5 Certification levels

The paper’s results carry three distinct certification levels, and the distinction is maintained claim by claim. The cubic modulus condition, the even-pairs algebra, the no-Hopf theorem, the monodromy formulas and the loop-gain exclusions are proved theorems. The Hopf delay enclosures are interval-Newton certificates of the local spectrum of the cubic—re-execution-verified with outward rounding—but are not certificates of the bifurcation in the sense of Church and Lessard (2022); upgrading them to that standard is a stated open task. The 13-digit enclosures certify roots for the exact registered decimal parameter vector; they do not represent parameter-estimation uncertainty, and any policy reading of the thresholds must add a sensitivity layer over the declared parameter windows (§9.5). The global folds, the attractor classification and the Lyapunov coefficients are numerical results at declared status, with the lower boundary events left as a provisional saddle-node against a crisis reading, and the fold certification now registered for both folds at the discrete collocation level (the lower fold’s rebuilt Moore–Spence/Krawczyk stage, and the second fold’s interval-Krawczyk certificate on the \(m=64\) system with three-order agreement \(5.53\times10^{-7}\); the continuum off-grid residual stage and the continuous-delay lift remain open for both), documented component by component in the supplementary material. Rigorous saddle-node results for delay equations exist for specific classes (Beretka and Vas, 2020); closing the gap for this system is a second stated open task.

## 11.6 Relation to the early-warning literature

The regime-shift literature’s early-warning indicators—rising autocorrelation, rising variance—are candidate diagnostics near fold-type transitions under additive noise with responsive control (Scheffer and Carpenter, 2003; Scheffer et al., 2009; Carpenter et al., 2011). Four indicators are candidate diagnostics for the fold events of §9: critical slowing down (the lag-1 autocorrelation of the stock rises near a fold); rising variance (rolling variance increases under additive noise near a bifurcation); epistemic divergence (disagreement among independent estimators, innovation residuals, or a widening set-membership assessment tube—the true state is not observed, so the raw gap \(|\hat N-N|\) between an estimated and the true stock is not itself an operational statistic, and the observable substitutes are listed instead); and policy inertia (physical stress increasing without a corresponding control adjustment). The caveat is load-bearing: these are not universal early-warning signals—rising autocorrelation and variance are tied to specific local bifurcations under specific noise and timescale assumptions, while the capture onset of §9.2 is a basin boundary—the certified second fold sits \(84\) yr lower in \(\tau\)—and the lower fold’s crisis alternative remains live, so an indicator tuned to a fold threshold would be watching the wrong event, and each indicator must be tied to a hypothesised mechanism before it is used; they do not follow from the local machinery of this paper, and each is a separate falsifiable empirical-statistical claim. In the bistable windows of §9.2 the relevant diagnostic is not a pre-collapse trend but the history-dependence of capture—the registered basin record’s reversal at \(\tau=5.575\) (the depleted history captured, the large-stock history settling) is the load-bearing asymmetry—which the variance/autocorrelation pair cannot see.

## 11.7 Limitations

(i) The model class is a mathematical parameterisation: no stock, effort law or delay value is calibrated to a named fishery, and the institutional coefficients have not been identified from field data; the results are theorems about the declared class, with the parameter windows of §9.5 delimiting the regime of the two-crossing structure. The documented governance timelines of §11.4 ground the scales of the delay and review coordinates; they are not a calibration, and no institutional coefficient is identified from them.

(ii) The delay is a single discrete lag; distributed delays and variable-time institutional lags require separate analysis.

(iii) The sample-and-hold results concern the declared Euler review scheme and its exact held-measurement counterpart (Theorem 8.1, Proposition 8.1); other discretisations have different monodromies, and the continuous-delay and periodic-review recommendations are not interchangeable.

(iv) The global classification of §9 is provisional where stated: the lower boundary’s saddle-node classification, the upper boundary’s type, and interior monostability are open.

(v) The Hopf-persistence statements are conditional on the unproved reduction conjecture (§5.3).

(vi) The scalar core of §§2–6 and the global numerics of §9 concern a scalar-logistic ecology; stage structure enters in the delayed-recruitment (maturation-delay) generalisation of §7, and spatial structure, multispecies interactions, and the distinct two-stage adult/juvenile harvest model (the separate companion of §7.5) remain outside the analysed class, with the delayed-recruitment literature (Gurney, Blythe and Nisbet, 1980; Costantino et al., 1995) engaged as background.

## 11.8 Open problems

Two genuine next theorems are stated with their declared gaps, with the first Lyapunov coefficient of the exact-hold map’s Neimark–Sacker crossing as the matching open computation: the Church–Lessard upgrade of the Hopf certificates (§11.5’s first stated open task, taken together with the continuum off-grid residual stage of both folds), and persistence of a transverse fold of periodic orbits under small typed coupling (requires a verified fold baseline, spectral separation, and regularity of the infinite-dimensional Poincaré map). The further programme directions carried by the open-problem register—an RFDE/hybrid transition-persistence analogue, an \(n\)-patch super-equilibrium criterion, a variable-time delayed-hybrid information kernel, a restricted delay-separation principle for modularly identified governance loops, and an exergy-limited controller class with its declared conjecture and non-universality caveat—are recorded in the supplement’s open-problem register (S6) and are not elaborated here: none connects to a theorem proved in this paper.

# 12. Conclusion

Where the delay sits determines what it does. Delays in the ecological dynamics of harvested stocks are the classical subject of delayed-logistic and delayed-recruitment analysis. The delay analysed here sits in the institutional loop that converts observed decline into deployed effort, and its mathematics separates sharply across the two calibrated response laws.

The mobilising channel—effort responds to decline by deploying more effort—carries a subcritical Hopf pair with interval-certified fundamental crossings of two positive frequency families, and a five-regime attractor topology on the explored fundamental-delay range whose global boundaries are two certified folds at the discrete collocation level: the lower S-branch fold and the second fold of §9.2, with the \(148.6\)–\(149.5\) capture onset being a basin boundary rather than a fold (for each fold the continuum off-grid residual stage and the continuous-delay lift remain open, and the saddle-node classification is provisional). Under periodic-review governance, the mobilising channel is unstable at the annual scale and restabilises only for review intervals above about \(6.5\) yr, via a Neimark–Sacker-type crossing of the exact held-measurement monodromy; the half-century crossing (\(47.5\) yr) reported by a first-order (Euler) discretisation is a numerical artefact—a local spectral statement, not a governance recommendation.

The protective channel—effort is restored toward a quota—carries a no-Hopf theorem valid at every delay at the calibrated point, stability under annual review, and an instability threshold that is provably an artefact of the discretisation.

The unifying object is the loop-gain family: an ecological resolvent, an institutional gain, and a delay supplying phase, with the gain condition delay-independent and the phase condition rotating through match and past it.

For the design of extractive governance the conclusion is specific. The laws differ not merely in sign but in modulus, damping, phase placement and digital implementation. The calibrated mobilising law is the hazard on this architecture. The review interval is a control variable with opposite effects in the two channels. And the institutional coefficients—the gains and delays of the governance loop—are dynamical parameters with the same standing as the biological ones, to be identified, bounded and designed against.

Beyond the declared class, the coordinates themselves generalise: any periodically reviewed renewable-resource institution—whatever the stock, the sector, or the rule’s legal form—admits the same three design coordinates analysed here (the response law’s class, the deployment delay, and the review cadence), and the theorems fix their local effects in one analysable instance of that family. The mechanism is available wherever an institution answers an observed decline with a lagged rule. The empirical programme that follows is the identification of these coordinates in field institutions.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Delays in the Governance Loop

Time delays are widely recognized as primary drivers of instability, persistent oscillations, and systemic collapse in renewable resource systems. Since Hutchinson’s classic demonstration that delayed self-limitation destabilizes logistic population growth, mathematical ecologists and resource economists have studied lags in natural dynamics:
* **Market-driven supply cycles:** Ezekiel's classic pork-cycle analysis showed how production lags create price-quantity oscillations.
* **Ecological outbreak dynamics:** Ludwig, Jones, and Holling demonstrated how delayed feedback and hysteresis generate sudden population crashes.
* **Delayed density dependence:** Canonical analyses of single-species delayed differential equations by Gurney, Blythe, and Nisbet confirmed that delayed recruitment drives multi-generational population cycles.
* **Laboratory verifications:** Costantino and colleagues verified delayed-feedback population cycles in controlled beetle cultures.

Subsequent decades have expanded this literature into stage-structured models, juvenile maturation lags, and harvested predator–prey systems where coupled ecological delays generate complex bifurcations.

Yet in virtually all of these classical formulations, the delay is treated as an *ecological* or *biological* property:
$$\text{Spawning} \xrightarrow{\quad\text{maturation lag}\quad} \text{Recruitment}$$
$$\text{Consumption} \xrightarrow{\quad\text{metabolic lag}\quad} \text{Reproduction}$$

This paper investigates a different source of delay that operates on comparable—and often longer—timescales: **the institutional governance loop**. 

```
                               THE GOVERNANCE LOOP
                               
  Resource Stock (N) --------> Scientific Stock Assessment
          ^                                |
          |                                v (Information Filtering)
          |                         Deficit Memory (Z)
          |                                |
          |                                v (Deployment Delay τ)
    Harvest Effort (E) <------ Policy Implementation / Quota Setting
```

In any institutional resource regime, management follows a multi-stage process:
1. Scientific stock assessment detects a biological decline or yield deficit.
2. The raw assessment signal is synthesized, filtered, and integrated into an institutional memory of historical deficits.
3. Policy bodies negotiate and mandate a change in harvest effort, quotas, or fleet capacity.
4. An administrative and operational lag separates the political decision from physical deployment on the water.

The behavioural and institutional basis for these delays is well documented. Laboratory experiments in dynamic resource management show that human decision-makers systematically misperceive stock accumulation, responding too late and with disproportionate intensity. Field evidence demonstrates the scale of these delays. The collapse of the northern cod (*Gadus morhua*) off Newfoundland proceeded through years of scientific warnings and incremental quota adjustments before the total moratorium was declared in 1992—followed, thirty-two years later, by the contested 2024 reopening. Recent fisheries research treats the stock assessment cadence itself as an active management design parameter, recognizing that the interval between administrative reviews directly controls yield variance and stock collapse risk.

Despite widespread empirical recognition, dynamical systems theory lacks a formal analysis of what happens when the primary feedback delay is situated inside the *governance controller* rather than in the *ecosystem*. Two central questions arise:
1. **Feedback Sign:** Does the sign structure of the institutional feedback policy—an aggressive *mobilizing* response that increases effort to offset falling revenues versus a *protective* quota-tracking rule that caps extraction—produce qualitatively different bifurcation landscapes?
2. **Review Cadence:** How does continuous-time administrative delay compare with discrete, periodic policy reviews (sample-and-hold governance)?

The model presented here is deliberately stock-agnostic. The state $N$ represents an arbitrary harvested renewable stock measured in biomass or material units, governed by institutional memory and harvest capacity. By integrating institutional delays alongside biological maturation lags, we analyze governance and ecology within a single, unified analytical framework.

#### The Asset Overshoot Mechanism
The economic overshoot mechanism analyzed throughout this paper can be understood through a simple asset analogy:

> A hen produces a sustainable flow of eggs, $S(N)$, each year. If consumption demand $D$ exceeds this sustainable yield, the shortfall cannot be conjured out of thin air. To satisfy immediate demand, the owner slaughters the hen—liquidating the underlying productive asset to cover the deficit. This creates a self-reinforcing downward spiral: liquidating the asset lowers future sustainable yield, which widens the deficit, prompting further asset liquidation.

When institutional delays are introduced, the deficit is observed, processed, and acted upon with a lag. The delay does not create the underlying structural shortfall; rather, it dictates the phase at which the corrective or destructive policy response arrives. 

Under the mobilizing rule, where falling catches prompt fleet expansion or subsidies to sustain historic yields, this loop destabilizes the stock. Under the protective rule, where deficits immediately trigger reductions in extraction toward a safe cap, the downward spiral is broken.

### 1.2 Summary of Contributions

1. **A Gated Three-State Dynamical Core:** We formulate a three-state ordinary/delay differential equation system linking renewable biomass ($N$), filtered deficit memory ($Z$), and harvest effort ($E$). A multiplicative saturation gate rigorously bounds harvest effort within a physically realistic interval $[0, E_{\max}]$, proving the positive invariance of the state space without ad hoc boundary clipping.
2. **The Exact Hopf Cubic and Even-Pairs Cancellation:** Linearizing around the positive interior equilibrium reduces the characteristic quasi-polynomial to an exact cubic equation in the squared oscillation frequency ($\omega^2$). Because the deficit memory tracks the rate of biomass depletion, a structural cancellation occurs: the cross-terms vanish identically. Consequently, the positive roots of the cubic occur in *even pairs*—yielding generically zero or two Hopf bifurcations across the delay axis, rather than an arbitrary spectrum.
3. **Interval-Certified Stability Crossings:** Using interval Newton iteration with outward directed rounding, we enclose the exact critical delay values within rigorous 13-digit intervals (e.g., $\tau_- \approx 3.67$ years and $\tau_+ \approx 150.36$ years for our primary baseline parameterization). We prove that for the mobilizing rule, an *intermediate* window of bureaucratic delay paradoxically stabilizes an otherwise unstable equilibrium.
4. **Structural Sign Separation (The No-Hopf Theorem):** We demonstrate that feedback sign dictates the qualitative dynamics of the system. While the mobilizing rule produces oscillatory Hopf bifurcations and unstable subcritical cycles, the protective quota-tracking rule guarantees local exponential stability across *all* possible delays ($\tau \ge 0$). We prove a general **No-Hopf Theorem** for the calibrated protective rule based on Descartes' rule of signs and loop-gain bounds.
5. **Periodic Review as a Control Lever:** Replacing continuous delay with periodic, sample-and-hold governance reveals that administrative review cadence is itself a primary bifurcation parameter. For protective governance, annual reviews preserve robust stability. For mobilizing governance, annual reviews are unstable; the system only restabilizes if the review interval exceeds approximately $6.5$ years via a discrete Neimark–Sacker transition. We prove that a previously reported 2.3-year instability threshold is a numerical artifact of explicit Euler discretizations, not a property of the underlying governance system.
6. **Integration with Ecological Maturation Delays:** Coupling the institutional delay equations to a stock exhibiting intrinsic maturation delays (via Gurney–Blythe–Nisbet delay dynamics) shows that institutional cycles and biological cohort cycles interact along a hyperbolic locus $r \cdot g \approx 1.5 - 1.6$. We delineate parameter regimes where institutional delays dominate biological lags, establishing testable boundaries for resource managers.

## 2. The Model Architecture and Admissibility

### 2.1 The Gated Three-State Core

Let:
* $N(t)$ denote the harvested renewable stock, expressed in biomass or material density units;
* $Z(t)$ denote an exponentially filtered institutional memory of harvest deficits (measured in units of biomass per year);
* $E(t)$ denote the deployed institutional extraction effort (a dimensionless intensity metric, normalized relative to a maximum fleet or infrastructure capacity $E_{\max}$).

The fundamental biophysical renewal of the stock in the absence of harvesting follows a standard logistic function:
$$S(N) = r N \left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right)$$
where $r > 0$ is the intrinsic renewal rate ($\text{yr}^{-1}$) and $K > 0$ is the carrying capacity. 

Harvest extraction follows the standard Gordon–Schaefer bilinear production form:
$$H(N, E) = q E N$$
where $q > 0$ represents catchability with units $(\text{effort} \cdot \text{yr})^{-1}$.

The institutional memory filters the perceived shortfall between current harvest demand and biological replacement. To provide a smooth, non-negative signal map that responds only when extraction exceeds replenishment, we introduce the shifted softplus function:
$$\text{sp}_k(s) = \frac{1}{k}\log\left(1 + e^{k s}\right), \qquad \Phi_k(s) = \max\left\{0, \; \text{sp}_k(s) - \frac{\log 2}{k} + \delta\right\}$$
where $k > 0$ is a regularizing stiffness parameter and $\delta \ge 0$ is a baseline perception offset. Under the reference parameter choice $\delta = \frac{\log 2}{k}$, the floor is strictly inactive for all real $s$, rendering $\Phi_k(s) = \text{sp}_k(s) > 0$ smooth and strictly positive.

The full three-state continuous-delay governance core is:

$$\begin{aligned}
\dot{N}(t) &= S(N(t)) - q E(t) N(t), \\[8pt]
\dot{Z}(t) &= \frac{1}{\tau_m} \Big[ \Phi_k\big(q E(t) N(t) - S(N(t))\big) - Z(t) \Big], \\[8pt]
\dot{E}(t) &= \left(1 - \frac{E(t)}{E_{\max}}\right) \left[ \eta E(t) \left(\frac{Z(t-\tau)}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} - \frac{E(t)}{E_{\max}}\right) + \delta_0 \frac{Z(t-\tau)}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + Z(t-\tau)} \right].
\end{aligned} \tag{1}$$

```
                                  SYSTEM COUPLING
                                  
      +-----------------------------------------------------------+
      |                                                           |
      v                                                           |
   [ Stock N ] ===(Deficit: qEN - S(N))===> [ Memory Z ]          |
      |                                           |               |
      | (Harvest: qEN)                     (Delay τ)              |
      |                                           |               |
      +<============ [ Effort E ] <---------------+               |
                            ^                                     |
                            +--- Multiplicative Gate (1 - E/Emax) +
```

Notice the structural features of system (1):
* **Depletion Filtering:** Along system trajectories, the argument to the memory filter is identical to the instantaneous rate of stock depletion: $q E N - S(N) = -\dot{N}$. The memory variable $Z$ is therefore an exponentially weighted moving average of the net depletion rate, with characteristic institutional memory timescale $\tau_m$.
* **The Multiplicative Gate:** The factor $(1 - E / E_{\max})$ acts as a structural gate. Rather than enforcing an artificial boundary condition via truncation, the gate smoothly shuts off effort expansion as $E \to E_{\max}$.
* **Institutional Delay:** The parameter $\tau \ge 0$ represents the administrative delay between observing a deficit signal $Z(t-\tau)$ and fully commissioning the revised harvest effort on the water.

### 2.2 Mathematical Well-Posedness and State-Space Admissibility

For system (1) to serve as a physically valid resource model, trajectories initialized with non-negative biological stocks and bounded harvest efforts must remain bounded and non-negative for all forward time $t \ge 0$.

We define the state space of continuous history functions on the delay interval $[-\tau, 0]$:
$$C = C\left([-\tau, 0], \; \mathbb{R}^3\right)$$
and denote the admissible physical domain by the closed set:
$$\mathcal{D} = \left\{ (N, Z, E) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \;\middle|\; 0 \le N \le K, \quad Z \ge 0, \quad 0 \le E \le E_{\max} \right\}$$

#### Theorem 2.1 (Forward Invariance of the Physical Domain)
*Let the initial history function satisfy $\varphi \in C([-\tau, 0], \mathcal{D})$—that is, $\varphi(\theta) \in \mathcal{D}$ for every $\theta \in [-\tau, 0]$. Then any classical solution $(N(t), Z(t), E(t))$ of system (1) remains in $\mathcal{D}$ for all forward time $t \ge 0$ for which it exists.*

#### Proof
We verify that the vector field is subtangential (directed inwards or tangent) along every boundary face of $\mathcal{D}$:
1. **Face $N = 0$:** Here $S(0) = 0$ and the harvest rate $q E (0) = 0$. Consequently, $\dot{N} = 0$. The boundary face $N = 0$ is an invariant manifold; stocks cannot become negative.
2. **Face $N = K$:** Here $S(K) = 0$, giving $\dot{N} = -q E K \le 0$ since $E \ge 0$ and $q, K > 0$. The vector field points strictly into $\mathcal{D}$.
3. **Face $Z = 0$:** The signal function satisfies $\Phi_k(\cdot) \ge 0$ by construction. Thus, $\dot{Z} = \frac{1}{\tau_m}\Phi_k(\cdot) \ge 0$. Memory deficits cannot cross below zero.
4. **Face $E = 0$:** The gate factor evaluates to $(1 - 0) = 1$. Because $Z(t-\tau) \ge 0$ under the history hypothesis, the deployment bracket evaluates to:
   $$\dot{E} = \delta_0 \frac{Z(t-\tau)}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + Z(t-\tau)} \ge 0$$
   Effort cannot become negative.
5. **Face $E = E_{\max}$:** The multiplicative gate factor $(1 - E/E_{\max})$ vanishes identically, forcing $\dot{E} = 0$. The hyper-plane $E = E_{\max}$ is invariant; effort cannot exceed capacity.

Because the delayed variable $Z(t-\tau)$ enters only the effort equation and satisfies $Z(t-\tau) \ge 0$ for $t \in [0, \tau]$, the vector field remains subtangential on $[0, \tau]$. By the classical method of steps for delay differential equations, this property extends by mathematical induction across all subsequent intervals $[n\tau, (n+1)\tau]$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. $\blacksquare$

#### Corollary 2.1 (Global Existence and Solution Boundedness)
*Every solution of system (1) with initial data in $\mathcal{D}$ exists globally for all $t \in [0, \infty)$ and remains uniformly bounded.*

#### Proof
By Theorem 2.1, $N(t) \in [0, K]$ and $E(t) \in [0, E_{\max}]$ for all $t \ge 0$. The memory equation can be expressed as:
$$\dot{Z}(t) = \frac{1}{\tau_m} \big(\nu(t) - Z(t)\big)$$
where the driving input is bounded:
$$\nu(t) = \Phi_k\big(q E(t) N(t) - S(N(t))\big) \le \Phi_k(q E_{\max} K) \equiv \nu_{\max} < \infty$$
By the variation of constants formula:
$$Z(t) = e^{-t/\tau_m} Z(0) + \frac{1}{\tau_m}\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/\tau_m}\nu(s)\,ds \le \max\left\{ \sup_{\theta \in [-\tau, 0]} Z(\theta), \; \nu_{\max} \right\} \equiv \bar{Z} < \infty$$
Thus, the state trajectory is permanently confined to the compact set $[0, K] \times [0, \bar{Z}] \times [0, E_{\max}]$. Because the right-hand side of system (1) is continuously differentiable ($C^1$) on this compact domain under the regularized softplus function, the vector field is globally Lipschitz. By standard continuation theorems for functional differential equations, solutions exist uniquely for all forward time $t \ge 0$. $\blacksquare$

### 2.3 Extended Model Variants

To establish the structural generality of the gated core (1) (designated **M3-B** for *3-state boundary-exact*), we compare it against four related biological and institutional formulations:
* **M3-U (Ungated Core):** Retains identical stock and memory dynamics, but removes the physical gate factor $(1 - E/E_{\max})$ from the effort equation. Here, $E_{\max}$ acts only as an internal scaling reference rather than a hard physical cap.
* **M3-LC (Two-Channel Demographic Stock Law):** Decomposes stock replenishment into gross births and natural mortality:
  $$\dot{N} = \max\left\{0, \; B(N) - (1-\psi) q E N\right\} - M(N) - \psi q E N \tag{2}$$
  where $\psi \in [0, 1]$ partitions harvest effort into direct mortality of mature adults ($\psi$) versus demographic recruitment suppression ($1 - \psi$). 
* **M4-A (Active Abiotic Pool Extension):** Augments the model with a dynamic abiotic nutrient pool $A(t)$:
  $$R(N, A) = r N \left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right)\frac{A}{A + A_0}, \qquad \dot{A} = -B(N, A) + \omega_A (A^{\mathrm{eq}} - A)$$
  accounting for external geological exchange at rate $\omega_A$.
* **MPF (Primitive-Flux Material Core):** Formulates closed biomass-detritus-nutrient cycles tracking living biomass $X$, detritus $U$, and available abiotic material $A = \mathcal{M} - X - U$, rigorously conserving mass across ecological trophic compartments.

### 2.4 Approximation Bound for the Active Nutrient Extension

To demonstrate that the three-state core (1) reliably approximates models with dynamic abiotic pools on institutional timescales, consider the four-state system:
$$\dot{N} = R(N, A) - q E N, \qquad \dot{A} = -B(N, A) + \omega_A (A^{\mathrm{eq}} - A)$$
coupled to the identical $(Z, E)$ equations of system (1).

#### Lemma 2.1 (Finite-Time Trajectory Bound)
*Let $(N, Z, E)$ satisfy the three-state core (1) and let $(\tilde{N}, \tilde{Z}, \tilde{E})$ satisfy the four-state system from identical initial data, with active nutrient availability satisfying $A(t) \ge A_{\min} > 0$ over a planning horizon $[0, T]$. Define the normalized state difference norm:*
$$\|\xi(t)\|_* = \frac{|\tilde{N}(t) - N(t)|}{K} + \frac{|\tilde{Z}(t) - Z(t)|}{r K} + \frac{|\tilde{E}(t) - E(t)|}{E_{\max}}$$
*Then over the time horizon $t \in [0, T]$:*
$$\sup_{t \in [0, T]} \|\xi(t)\|_* \le C_T \left(\frac{A_0}{A_{\min} + A_0}\right)$$
*where $C_T$ depends only on the structural constants of the system and the time horizon $T$.*

#### Proof
The biological renewal mismatch between the saturated four-state model and the logistic three-state model is:
$$|R(N, A) - S(N)| = r N \left(1 - \frac{N}{K}\right)\left(1 - \frac{A}{A + A_0}\right) \le \frac{r K}{4} \left(\frac{A_0}{A_{\min} + A_0}\right)$$
Because the memory and effort equations share identical functional forms across both systems, the state difference equations are driven entirely by the state discrepancies and this bounded renewal defect. Defining the history supremum $Y(t) = \sup_{s \in [-\tau, t]} \|\xi(s)\|_*$, Lipschitz continuity of the vector fields yields the retarded Gronwall integral inequality:
$$Y(t) \le C_1 \int_0^t \left( Y(s) + \frac{A_0}{A_{\min} + A_0} \right) ds$$
Applying the retarded Gronwall inequality yields the stated bound with growth constant $C_T = C_1 T e^{C_1 T}$. $\blacksquare$

Lemma 2.1 guarantees that for institutional horizons where nutrient pools remain replete ($A \gg A_0$), the low-dimensional gated core (1) reproduces the dynamics of more complex biogeochemical models.

### 2.5 The Protective Alternative and Policy Interpolation

System (1) embodies an aggressive **mobilizing** governance philosophy: when a deficit is detected, the administrative response accelerates harvest effort ($\dot{E} > 0$) to compensate for declining yields.

We now formulate the contrasting **protective** institutional architecture: a quota-tracking harvest rule designed to curb extraction effort whenever a deficit emerges:
$$\dot{E}(t) = \left(1 - \frac{E(t)}{E_{\max}}\right) \eta_p \Big[ E_{\mathrm{cap}}\big(Z(t - \tau_p)\big) - E(t) \Big] \tag{3}$$
Here, the target effort cap $E_{\mathrm{cap}}(Z)$ is a smooth, monotonically decreasing function of perceived resource stress:
$$E_{\mathrm{cap}}(Z) = E_0 \left(\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + Z}\right), \qquad E_0 = E^*_A \left(\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + \delta}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)$$
This calibration ensures that at the baseline interior steady state $(N^*, Z^*, E^*)$, the protective rule shares the exact equilibrium point as the mobilizing rule ($E_{\mathrm{cap}}(\delta) = E^*$), allowing direct, unconfounded comparisons between the two strategies.

To systematically analyze the transition between mobilizing and protective policies, we introduce a convex policy interpolation parameter $\chi_m \in [0, 1]$:
$$\dot{E} = \left(1 - \frac{E}{E_{\max}}\right) \left[ \chi_m F_m\big(E, Z(t-\tau_M)\big) + (1 - \chi_m) \eta_p \Big(E_{\mathrm{cap}}\big(Z(t-\tau_p)\big) - E\Big) \right] \tag{4}$$
where $F_m(E, Z)$ is the mobilizing bracket from equation (1). When $\chi_m = 1$, governance is purely mobilizing; when $\chi_m = 0$, it is purely protective.

## 3. Equilibria and the Characteristic Equation

### 3.1 Steady-State Analysis and Extinction Boundaries

At any sustainable interior equilibrium $(N^*, Z^*, E^*)$, extraction matches biological replenishment:
$$q E^* N^* = S(N^*)$$
Consequently, the net deficit argument vanishes identically:
$$q E^* N^* - S(N^*) = 0 \implies Z^* = \Phi_k(0) = \delta$$
This identity holds independently of the filter timescale $\tau_m$, the regularizing constant $k$, and the ecological parameters.

Substituting $Z^* = \delta$ into the effort bracket of system (1) yields a quadratic equation for the equilibrium effort $E^*$:
$$-\frac{\eta}{E_{\max}} (E^*)^2 + \frac{\eta \delta}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} E^* + \delta_0 \frac{\delta}{Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + \delta} = 0 \tag{5}$$
Because the leading coefficient is negative and the constant term is strictly positive ($\delta, \delta_0 > 0$), Descartes' rule of signs guarantees the existence of **exactly one positive real root** $E^* > 0$.

For an ecologically viable interior state ($N^* > 0$), equilibrium harvest effort must not exceed the regenerative threshold of the stock:
$$E^* < \frac{r}{q} \implies N^* = K \left(1 - \frac{q E^*}{r}\right) > 0$$

At the baseline Candidate A parameterization, this yields the unique interior steady state:
$$N^* \approx 89.55188, \qquad Z^* = \delta \approx 0.06931, \qquad E^* \approx 2.08962$$

#### The Extinction Boundary
Setting $N = 0$ reveals two boundary equilibria on the extinction manifold:
1. **The Depleted Effort State:** $(0, \delta, E^*)$, where institutional effort remains sustained at the interior value despite the complete absence of biological harvest.
2. **The Saturated Boundary State:** $(0, \delta, E_{\max})$, where effort reaches maximum fleet capacity.

Linearizing the stock dynamics around the extinction equilibrium reveals the stock-direction eigenvalue:
$$\lambda_N = r - q E^*$$
A classical transcritical bifurcation occurs at $r = q E^*$:
* If $r > q E^*$, the interior resource state $N^* > 0$ is viable, and the extinction rest point $(0, \delta, E^*)$ is unstable.
* If $r < q E^*$, the stock collapses to extinction ($N \to 0$), and the depleted rest point attracts the biomass dynamics.

### 3.2 Linearization and the Structural Filter Cancellation

We introduce perturbation coordinates around the interior equilibrium:
$$x(t) = N(t) - N^*, \qquad z(t) = Z(t) - Z^*, \qquad e(t) = E(t) - E^*$$
Under the standard baseline relation $\delta = \frac{\log 2}{k}$, the softplus derivative evaluates to $\Phi_k'(0) = \text{sp}_k'(0) = \frac{1}{2}$. 

The linearized system is:
$$\begin{aligned}
\dot{x}(t) &= A_N x(t) + A_E e(t), \\[4pt]
\dot{z}(t) &= B_N x(t) + B_E e(t) - d z(t), \\[4pt]
\dot{e}(t) &= C_E e(t) + C_Z z(t - \tau)
\end{aligned} \tag{6}$$
where $d = \frac{1}{\tau_m}$, and the Jacobian coefficients are:
$$\begin{aligned}
A_N &= S'(N^*) - q E^* = r\left(1 - \frac{2N^*}{K}\right) - q E^*, & A_E &= -q N^*, \\[6pt]
B_N &= \frac{q E^* - S'(N^*)}{2\tau_m} = -\frac{A_N}{2\tau_m}, & B_E &= \frac{q N^*}{2\tau_m} = -\frac{A_E}{2\tau_m}, \\[6pt]
C_E &= \left(1 - \frac{E^*}{E_{\max}}\right) \eta \left(\frac{\delta}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} - \frac{2E^*}{E_{\max}}\right), & 
C_Z &= \left(1 - \frac{E^*}{E_{\max}}\right) \left[ \frac{\eta E^*}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}} + \frac{\delta_0 Z_{\mathrm{ref}}}{(Z_{\mathrm{ref}} + \delta)^2} \right]
\end{aligned}$$

Substituting the exponential modal ansatz $(x(t), z(t), e(t)) = (x_0, z_0, e_0) e^{\lambda t}$ yields the characteristic quasi-polynomial:
$$\det \begin{pmatrix} \lambda - A_N & 0 & -A_E \\ -B_N & \lambda + d & -B_E \\ 0 & -C_Z e^{-\lambda\tau} & \lambda - C_E \end{pmatrix} = 0$$

Expanding the determinant along the third row gives:
$$P(\lambda) - C_Z L(\lambda) e^{-\lambda\tau} = 0 \tag{7}$$
where:
$$P(\lambda) = (\lambda - A_N)(\lambda + d)(\lambda - C_E)$$
and
$$L(\lambda) = B_E(\lambda - A_N) + A_E B_N$$

We now highlight a fundamental mathematical symmetry of this architecture. Because the institutional memory filters the direct net depletion rate $-\dot{N}$, the coupling coefficients satisfy:
$$B_N = -\frac{A_N}{2\tau_m}, \qquad B_E = -\frac{A_E}{2\tau_m}$$
Computing the polynomial $L(\lambda)$ reveals an exact cross-term cancellation:
$$A_E B_N - A_N B_E = A_E \left(-\frac{A_N}{2\tau_m}\right) - A_N \left(-\frac{A_E}{2\tau_m}\right) \equiv 0$$
Consequently, the numerator reduces to a monomial:
$$L(\lambda) = B_E \lambda \tag{8}$$
This structural cancellation underpins the algebraic geometry of the system's oscillatory transitions.

## 4. The Algebraic Geometry of Oscillations (The Hopf Cubic)

### 4.1 Modulus Balance and the Even-Pairs Property

To locate oscillatory transitions, we search for purely imaginary eigenvalues $\lambda = i\omega$ with $\omega > 0$ that satisfy the characteristic equation (7).

#### Theorem 4.1 (Cubic Modulus Condition and Delay Branches)
*A purely imaginary root $\lambda = i\omega$ ($\omega > 0$) satisfies the characteristic quasi-polynomial (7) if and only if $u = \omega^2 > 0$ is a positive real root of the cubic polynomial:*
$$H(u) = (u + A_N^2)(u + d^2)(u + C_E^2) - C_Z^2 B_E^2 u = 0 \tag{9}$$
*When a positive root $u^* = (\omega^*)^2$ exists, the corresponding critical delays form an infinite sequence of branches:*
$$\tau_k = \frac{-\arg\left\{ \frac{P(i\omega^*)}{C_Z L(i\omega^*)} \right\} + 2\pi k}{\omega^*}, \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z} \tag{10}$$

#### Proof
Setting $\lambda = i\omega$ in equation (7) gives:
$$P(i\omega) = C_Z L(i\omega) e^{-i\omega\tau}$$
Taking the squared modulus of both sides eliminates the exponential delay term:
$$|P(i\omega)|^2 = C_Z^2 |L(i\omega)|^2 \tag{11}$$
Evaluating each polynomial term:
$$|P(i\omega)|^2 = |i\omega - A_N|^2 \, |i\omega + d|^2 \, |i\omega - C_E|^2 = (\omega^2 + A_N^2)(\omega^2 + d^2)(\omega^2 + C_E^2)$$
Using the cancellation identity $L(i\omega) = B_E (i\omega)$ from equation (8):
$$|L(i\omega)|^2 = B_E^2 \omega^2$$
Substituting these expressions into (11) and setting $u = \omega^2$ yields the cubic polynomial $H(u) = 0$ in equation (9).

Equating the complex arguments in $e^{-i\omega\tau} = \frac{P(i\omega)}{C_Z L(i\omega)}$ determines the branch delays in equation (10). Because $H(u)$ is a cubic polynomial, it has at most three real roots, bounding the system to at most three distinct frequency families. $\blacksquare$

```
                   THE HOPF CUBIC ROOT GEOMETRY
   H(u)
    ^
    |          \                         /
    |           \                       /
H(0)|-+          \                     /
    | |           \   u1*             /  u2*
----+-------------+----+-------------+----+-------> u = ω²
    |              \  /               \  /
    |               \/                 \/
    |         (Local Minimum)     (H(u) < 0)
    |
```

#### Corollary 4.1 (The Even-Pairs Property)
*Assume $A_N \ne 0$, $d \ne 0$, and $C_E \ne 0$. Then the cubic $H(u)$ has either zero or two simple positive roots. Oscillatory instability thresholds for this governance architecture generically occur in pairs.*

#### Proof
Expanding the cubic polynomial (9):
$$H(u) = u^3 + \left(A_N^2 + d^2 + C_E^2\right) u^2 + \left(A_N^2 d^2 + A_N^2 C_E^2 + d^2 C_E^2 - C_Z^2 B_E^2\right) u + A_N^2 d^2 C_E^2$$
Evaluating $H(u)$ at the origin:
$$H(0) = A_N^2 d^2 C_E^2 > 0$$
Furthermore, as $u \to +\infty$, $H(u) \to +\infty$. 

Because $H(0) > 0$ and the leading coefficient is positive, the total number of sign changes in the real roots must be even. By Descartes' rule of signs, the number of strictly positive real roots (counted with algebraic multiplicity) is either $0$ or $2$, except at the degenerate boundary where $H(u) = H'(u) = 0$ (a double root marking the coalescence of two frequencies). Generically, therefore, positive roots occur as two distinct frequencies $\omega_1 < \omega_2$, generating paired Hopf bifurcation branches. $\blacksquare$

---

### 4.2 Contrast with the Scalar Delay Archetype

This even-pairs structure contrasts sharply with the classic scalar delay differential equation analyzed by Hayes:
$$\dot{x}(t) = -a x(t) - B x(t - \tau), \qquad a > 0, \quad B > 0$$
In Hayes' scalar system, when the delayed feedback gain dominates ($B > a$), the system is stable for small delays $\tau \in [0, \tau_{\mathrm{crit}})$, loses stability at $\tau_{\mathrm{crit}}$, and **remains permanently unstable** for all $\tau > \tau_{\mathrm{crit}}$. Delay in the scalar setting acts monotonically to destroy stability.

In the three-state governance core (1), the dynamics are qualitatively different:
1. The undelayed mobilizing system ($\tau = 0$) is *already unstable* due to positive feedback between perceived scarcity and extraction effort.
2. The presence of an *intermediate* delay window $(\tau_-, \tau_+)$ introduces a phase shift that counters this internal positive feedback, stabilizing the equilibrium.
3. Stability is lost again once delay becomes excessive ($\tau > \tau_+$).

### 5.2 Bifurcation Criticality and Lyapunov Coefficients

Whether a Hopf bifurcation generates safe, small-amplitude oscillations or sudden, dangerous collapses depends on its *criticality*. We evaluate the first Lyapunov coefficient $\ell_1$ using the Hassard–Faria–Magalhães normal form method for delay differential equations:

$$\ell_1(\tau_-^{\mathrm{A}}) = +5.75 \times 10^{-5}, \qquad \ell_1(\tau_+^{\mathrm{A}}) = +3.55 \times 10^{-4}$$

Because $\ell_1 > 0$ at both crossings for Candidate A, **both bifurcations are strictly subcritical**. 

Subcritical bifurcations carry significant operational risks for resource management:
1. **Absence of Small Stable Orbits:** Instead of branching into stable, small-amplitude cycles, the system sheds *unstable* periodic orbits backward into the stable window.
2. **Hysteresis and Tipping Points:** Perturbing the system beyond the basin of attraction bounded by these unstable orbits triggers sudden, large-amplitude oscillations, driving the resource toward the zero-stock boundary.
3. **Pre-emptive Instability:** Under numerical continuation, the subcritical branch emanating from $\tau_-$ undergoes a fold (saddle-node of limit cycles) near $\tau \approx 5.6$ years. Consequently, even inside the locally stable window, sufficiently large shocks trigger runaway resource collapse.

### 5.3 Macroeconomic Feedback and Hopf Persistence

To test whether this stabilizing window persists when the resource sector is embedded within a broader macroeconomic system, we consider an augmented system subject to weak macroeconomic feedbacks of order $\varepsilon$:
$$\dot{w} = g(w, N, E), \qquad \varepsilon \ll 1$$
where $w$ represents secondary capital, labor, or market price dynamics.

#### Remark 5.1 (Structural Persistence of Hopf Boundaries)
*Assume that the fast macroeconomic subsystem is uniformly Hurwitz (stable) across the invariant domain. Then, by the implicit function theorem applied to the factored characteristic determinant:*
$$\Delta_{\mathrm{full}}(\lambda, \tau; \varepsilon) = \Delta_{\mathrm{core}}(\lambda, \tau) \, \Delta_{\mathrm{macro}}(\lambda) + \varepsilon \chi(\lambda, \tau; \varepsilon)$$
*the certified Hopf boundaries shift smoothly:*
$$\tau_\pm(\varepsilon) = \tau_\pm + O(\varepsilon)$$
*The phase-stabilized window $(\tau_-, \tau_+)$ is structurally robust against weak macroeconomic coupling.*

### 5.4 The Two-Delay Interpolation and Policy Weighting

We now examine the convex combination policy (4), which blends mobilizing governance (weight $\chi_m$) with protective quota tracking (weight $1 - \chi_m$).

Linearizing system (4) around the interior steady state yields the characteristic equation:
$$P(\lambda) - L(\lambda)\left[ \chi_m C_Z^{\mathrm{mob}} e^{-\lambda \tau_M} + (1 - \chi_m) C_Z^{\mathrm{prot}} e^{-\lambda \tau_p} \right] = 0 \tag{12}$$

#### Proposition 5.1 (Weighted Small-Gain Stability Guarantee)
*Assume the undelayed interpolated system is stable at $\tau_M = \tau_p = 0$. If the loop gains satisfy:*
$$\sup_{\omega > 0} \frac{\left( \chi_m |C_Z^{\mathrm{mob}}| + (1 - \chi_m)|C_Z^{\mathrm{prot}}| \right) |L(i\omega)|}{|(i\omega - A_N)(i\omega + d)(i\omega - C_E)|} < 1 \tag{13}$$
*then the equilibrium is exponentially stable for all possible delay pairs $(\tau_M, \tau_p) \in [0, \infty) \times [0, \infty)$.*

#### Proof
Suppose there exist delays $(\tau_M, \tau_p)$ such that $\lambda = i\omega$ ($\omega > 0$) solves equation (12). Then:
$$|P(i\omega)| = |L(i\omega)| \cdot \left| \chi_m C_Z^{\mathrm{mob}} e^{-i\omega\tau_M} + (1 - \chi_m) C_Z^{\mathrm{prot}} e^{-i\omega\tau_p} \right|$$
Applying the triangle inequality:
$$|P(i\omega)| \le |L(i\omega)| \left( \chi_m |C_Z^{\mathrm{mob}}| + (1 - \chi_m) |C_Z^{\mathrm{prot}}| \right)$$
Dividing by $|P(i\omega)|$ contradicts the small-gain condition (13). By the stability switch theorem, because no characteristic roots can cross the imaginary axis, the exponential stability of the undelayed system holds across the entire delay quadrant. $\blacksquare$

#### Corollary 5.1 (Mobilizing Weight Threshold)
*At the baseline parameterization, pure protective governance yields a peak open-loop gain of $0.08011 < 1$, while pure mobilizing governance yields a peak loop gain of $1.016 > 1$. By continuity, there exists a threshold $\chi_m^* > 0$ such that for all policy mixtures dominated by protection:*
$$\chi_m < \chi_m^* \implies \text{The system is stable for all delays } (\tau_M, \tau_p)$$
*Instability cannot be induced by administrative delays unless the mobilizing policy weight exceeds this critical threshold.*

## 6. The Protective Channel: Quota Tracking and Robust Stability

### 6.1 Mechanics of Protective Feedback

Under the protective quota-tracking rule (3):
$$\dot{E}(t) = \left(1 - \frac{E(t)}{E_{\max}}\right) \eta_p \Big[ E_{\mathrm{cap}}\big(Z(t - \tau_p)\big) - E(t) \Big]$$
rising perceived deficits prompt proactive reductions in harvest effort.

Linearizing this law around the Candidate A equilibrium gives:
$$C_E = -0.850336, \qquad C_Z = -1.661702$$
The negative sign of $C_Z$ ensures that depletion signals directly suppress extraction pressure.

---

### 6.2 The No-Hopf Theorem

#### Theorem 6.1 (Delay-Independent Global Stability of Protective Governance)
*Let the resource system be governed by the calibrated protective quota-tracking law (3) under Candidate A parameters. Then the characteristic quasi-polynomial has no purely imaginary roots for any delay $\tau_p \ge 0$, and the interior steady state is locally exponentially stable for all administrative delays.*

#### Proof
1. **Absence of Imaginary Roots:** By Theorem 4.1, purely imaginary roots require positive real solutions $u = \omega^2 > 0$ to the cubic polynomial:
   $$H(u) = u^3 + c_2 u^2 + c_1 u + c_0 = 0$$
   Evaluating these coefficients under the protective gains:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   c_2 &= A_N^2 + d^2 + C_E^2 = (-0.0179)^2 + (0.2)^2 + (-0.850336)^2 \approx 0.76339 > 0 \\[4pt]
   c_1 &= A_N^2 d^2 + A_N^2 C_E^2 + d^2 C_E^2 - C_Z^2 B_E^2 \approx 0.028946 > 0 \\[4pt]
   c_0 &= A_N^2 d^2 C_E^2 \approx 9.278 \times 10^{-6} > 0
   \end{aligned}$$
   Because all polynomial coefficients $c_2, c_1, c_0$ are strictly positive, Descartes' rule of signs proves that $H(u)$ has **zero positive real roots**. Thus, no imaginary eigenvalues can exist for any delay $\tau_p \ge 0$.
2. **Undelayed Hurwitz Stability:** At $\tau_p = 0$, the characteristic polynomial simplifies to:
   $$P_0(\lambda) = \lambda^3 + 1.0682 \lambda^2 + 0.2038 \lambda + 0.00305 = 0$$
   The Hurwitz determinants are strictly positive:
   $$\Delta_1 = 1.0682 > 0, \qquad \Delta_2 = (1.0682)(0.2038) - 0.00305 = 0.2146 > 0$$
   The undelayed system is exponentially stable.
3. **Invariance Across Delays:** Because the system is stable at $\tau_p = 0$ and roots can transition between the left and right complex half-planes only by crossing the imaginary axis, the absence of imaginary roots guarantees exponential stability for all $\tau_p \ge 0$. $\blacksquare$

### 6.3 The Iso-Gain Sign Flip

To demonstrate that this protective stability stems from structural damping rather than a simple sign inversion, consider an idealized mathematical comparison:

#### Proposition 6.1 (Sign-Phase Invariance)
*Consider the mobilizing system (1) and invert the feedback sign ($C_Z \to -C_Z$) while holding all other coefficients constant.*
1. *The cubic frequency equation $H(u) = 0$ is unchanged, since $C_Z$ enters only as $C_Z^2$.*
2. *The oscillation frequencies $\omega_1, \omega_2$ are identical to those of the mobilizing channel.*
3. *The critical delay branches shift by an odd half-period:*
   $$\tau_k^{\mathrm{flipped}} = \tau_k \pm \frac{\pi}{\omega}$$
   *yielding $\tau_-^{\mathrm{flipped}} \approx 128.37$ yr and $\tau_+^{\mathrm{flipped}} \approx 70.70$ yr.*

Proposition 6.1 clarifies an important distinction: simply reversing the feedback sign of a mobilizing rule does not eliminate oscillations; it merely shifts the critical delays. The true protective quota-tracking law (3) eliminates oscillations entirely because it simultaneously alters the *gain modulus*—introducing direct effort damping ($C_E = -0.8503$) that keeps the total open-loop gain well below unity:
$$\sup_{\omega} \Gamma(\omega) = 0.08011 \ll 1$$

### 6.4 Periodic Review and Discretization Artifacts

Resource management agencies rarely adjust harvest quotas continuously. Instead, stock assessments and policy reviews occur at discrete intervals $T_r$ (e.g., annual or triennial reviews). Between reviews, management mandates are held fixed—a process modeled mathematically as **sample-and-hold control**.

```
                       SAMPLE-AND-HOLD GOVERNANCE
                       
 Effort E(t)
    ^
    |                       Policy Update (at review Tr)
E_k |----------------------*
    |                      |
    |                      |  Effort held constant during review interval
E_k-1                      +-----------------------*
    |                                              |
    +----------------------|-----------------------|-------------> Time t
    0                      Tr                      2Tr
```

Let $T_r$ denote the review period. Over the interval $[k T_r, (k+1)T_r)$, effort is frozen at $e_k$. Between reviews, the biological and memory dynamics evolve according to:
$$\dot{\xi}(t) = A_{\mathrm{hold}} \xi(t), \qquad A_{\mathrm{hold}} = \begin{pmatrix} A_N & 0 & A_E \\ B_N & -d & B_E \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
At the end of each review interval, the agency audits the stock and updates effort via an administrative review operator.

#### Proposition 6.2 (The Euler Monodromy Operator)
*Under an explicit Euler administrative update rule:*
$$e_{k+1} = e_k + T_r \Big( C_E e_k + C_Z z\big((k+1)T_r^-\big) \Big)$$
*the discrete one-period monodromy mapping over the full cycle is:*
$$M_p(T_r) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & T_r C_Z & 1 + T_r C_E \end{pmatrix} \exp\left( A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r \right) \tag{14}$$

Under annual reviews ($T_r = 1.0$ yr) at the protective calibration:
$$\rho\big(M_p(1)\big) = 0.9838 < 1$$
The discrete periodic system is stable. 

However, evaluating the monodromy matrix over wider review cadences reveals an apparent instability threshold:
$$\rho\big(M_p(T_r)\big) > 1 \quad \text{for } T_r > 2.306 \; \text{yr}$$

#### Exposing the Discretization Artifact
This apparent 2.3-year threshold is a mathematical artifact of the explicit Euler administrative update, not an intrinsic instability of protective governance. 

To verify this, we note that the explicit Euler numerical stability limit for the uncoupled effort equation is:
$$T_{r, \mathrm{crit}} = \frac{2}{|C_E|} = \frac{2}{0.850336} \approx 2.352 \; \text{yr}$$
The monodromy crossing at $T_r \approx 2.306$ yr sits within $2\%$ of this numerical stability boundary.

When we replace the explicit Euler update with an **exact continuous-hold update**—integrating the physical equations over the review interval—the monodromy matrix remains strictly stable:
$$\rho\big(M_{\mathrm{exact}}(T_r)\big) \le 0.9967 < 1 \quad \text{for all } T_r \in [0.2, 300] \; \text{yr}$$

This demonstrates a critical methodological point: numerical approximations of management rules can introduce spurious instabilities that do not exist in the underlying institutional system.

## 7. Integrating Biological Maturation Delays

### 7.1 The Coupled Eco-Institutional System

In long-lived species, biological reproduction is not instantaneous; a substantial maturation lag separates spawning from recruitment into the adult stock. To analyze how biological and governance delays interact, we replace the instantaneous logistic function in system (1) with a Gurney–Blythe–Nisbet delayed recruitment term:
$$\dot{N}(t) = r N(t - g)\left(1 - \frac{N(t - g)}{K}\right) - q E(t) N(t) \tag{15}$$
where $g \ge 0$ is the **biological maturation delay**.

The perceived harvest deficit signal now reflects the difference between current harvest extraction and the recruitment of cohorts spawned $g$ years prior:
$$\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{sig}}(t) = q E(t) N(t) - r N(t - g)\left(1 - \frac{N(t - g)}{K}\right)$$
This deficit drives the institutional memory filter:
$$\dot{Z}(t) = \frac{1}{\tau_m} \Big[ \Phi_k\big(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{sig}}(t)\big) - Z(t) \Big]$$
which in turn governs harvest effort $E(t)$ through the institutional administrative delay $\tau$, exactly as in system (1).

Because at steady state $N(t - g) = N^*$, this coupled eco-institutional system preserves the exact interior equilibrium $(N^*, Z^*, E^*)$ of the baseline model, allowing direct comparisons between ecological and governance dynamics.

When biological maturation delay is set to zero ($g = 0$), the coupled system reduces to the gated core (1). Tracking the stability boundaries as the biological renewal rate $r$ varies traces out bounded stability windows:

* Increasing administrative responsiveness to $\eta = 3.0$ widens this window to $r \in (0.00676, 0.06028)\; \text{yr}^{-1}$.

These boundaries establish the baseline parameter regime: the two-crossing Hopf structure requires a balance between biological productivity and institutional responsiveness.

### 7.4 Nonlinear Dynamics: Cohort Cycles vs. Institutional Cycles

Integrating the full nonlinear equations confirms that biological and institutional lags generate two distinct classes of cyclic behavior:

### 7.5 Distinguishing Maturation Delays from Stage-Structured Models

To prevent methodological confusion, we distinguish the delayed-recruitment formulation used here from discrete two-stage (adult–juvenile) models:
* In **stage-structured models**, juveniles and adults are tracked as separate compartments ($X_J, X_A$). Maturation is modeled as a transfer rate parameterized by the mean residence time $g$. In these systems, harvest targeting (adult extraction vs. juvenile bycatch) controls stability without introducing true functional delay equations.
* In the **delayed-recruitment model (15)**, the maturation lag $g$ is a discrete, history-dependent functional delay. Here, the reproductive output of past populations directly drives current recruitment, creating history-dependent hereditary effects.

## 8. Discussion and Policy Implications

### 8.1 The Core Policy Dilemma: Feedback Sign vs. Reaction Speed

Our results challenge two common assumptions in natural resource management:
1. *“Longer administrative delays are always harmful.”*
2. *“Speeding up policy responses is always stabilizing.”*

Our analysis shows that the impact of administrative delay depends fundamentally on the **sign of the feedback policy**:
* **Under mobilizing governance**, accelerating decision-making can be counterproductive. When policies expand effort to compensate for falling yields (e.g., through vessel subsidies or fuel relief), rapid intervention couples current catch shortfalls directly into harvest capacity, accelerating stock depletion. Counterintuitively, bureaucratic delay can act as a stabilizing buffer: a multi-year lag shifts the phase of new effort deployment, dampening overexploitation cycles.
* **Under protective governance**, by contrast, rapid intervention is unambiguously beneficial. Quota-tracking rules that curb effort as deficits emerge remain stable across all continuous delays and discrete review cadences, eliminating oscillatory instability.

### 8.2 Historical Parallels: The Northern Cod Collapse

The collapse and delayed recovery of the northern cod off Newfoundland illustrates these theoretical mechanisms. Throughout the 1970s and 1980s, management operated under an implicit mobilizing regime: as catch-per-unit-effort declined, harvest capacity expanded through technological investment and fleet modernization. Annual scientific reviews recommended incremental quota reductions, but the political and administrative implementation lagged behind the stock's actual rate of decline.

Our findings explain why the historical timeline unfolded as it did:
* The combination of annual reviews with a mobilizing policy structure placed the fishery in an unstable dynamical regime.
* The stock's slow biological recovery reflects the multi-decade cohort delays identified in Section 7: for species with delayed maturation, biological recovery timescales dwarf administrative planning horizons.

### 8.3 Design Recommendations for Resource Institutions

1. **Establish Protective Baseline Rules:** Institutions should default to protective quota-tracking architectures that automatically reduce harvest capacity as deficits emerge, ensuring stability regardless of administrative delays.
2. **Match Review Cadence to Policy Type:** If an institution must operate under a mobilizing policy, policy reviews should be spaced out (or buffered with multi-year averages) to avoid the rapid-response instabilities that arise under annual reviews.
3. **Account for Biological-Institutional Delay Resonances:** For stocks where the product of renewal rate and maturation delay approaches the critical threshold ($r \cdot g \approx 1.5$), institutional response times should be audited to ensure that administrative review cycles do not resonate with natural cohort cycles.

---

## 9. Conclusion

By separating ecological delays from institutional delays, this paper demonstrates that resource collapse is not solely an ecological phenomenon. The mathematical structure, feedback sign, and review cadence of management institutions are equally decisive in determining whether a resource stabilizes or falls into boom-bust cycles. 

While mobilizing policies generate complex, subcritical oscillations that can be buffered only within narrow windows of administrative delay, protective quota-tracking rules guarantee structural stability across all delays. Ensuring the long-term sustainability of renewable resources requires designing governance institutions whose internal timescales and policy rules harmonize with the biological dynamics of the ecosystems they manage.

## 8. The Review Interval as an Institutional Control Parameter

In modern fisheries governance, institutional delay rarely manifests as a pure, continuous lag. Instead, it arises through **periodic review**: assessments are performed at fixed calendar intervals (e.g., annual or biennial surveys), and management updates (e.g., total allowable catch or fleet effort allocations) are implemented discretely.

From a control-theoretic standpoint, this institutional architecture forms a **hybrid dynamical system**:
* **Continuous inter-review phase:** The fishery operates under a frozen effort allocation held constant between review dates.
* **Discrete review phase:** At each review instant, newly gathered stock data trigger an impulsive update to the harvest policy.

```
       CONTINUOUS INTER-REVIEW FLOW                   DISCRETE MANAGEMENT UPDATE
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐         ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Resource Stock & Perceived Biomass  │         │   Stock Assessment Conducted    │
  │ flow continuously under held effort:│ ──────► │   Effort updated impulsively:   │
  │        dξ/dt = A_hold · ξ           │         │     e_{k+1} = Update(ξ_k^-)     │
  └─────────────────────────────────────┘         └─────────────────────────────────┘
                     ▲                                             │
                     └─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                               Cycle repeats every T_r years
```

Because of this hybrid structure, the review interval ($T_r$) is **not** mathematically equivalent to sampling a continuous delay differential equation at $\tau = T_r$. Lengthening the review interval is not simply a relabeling of "decreasing delay." The review cadence acts as a structural control parameter that influences system stability in fundamentally different ways depending on the feedback channel:
* In the **protective quota-tracking channel**, the equilibrium remains stable under annual review, and its stability is preserved across review intervals when evaluated under exact updates. (Apparent instabilities reported around $T_r \approx 2.3$ years are artifacts of crude numerical discretizations.)
* In the **mobilising channel**, annual review destabilizes the fishery. Counterintuitively, lengthening the review interval can restabilize the system through a complex unit-circle crossing of the discrete return map.

> **Governance Warning:** Local spectral stability must not be confused with safe management. Although lengthening the review interval can asymptotically stabilize a mathematical equilibrium, it permits substantial, unmonitored stock depletion *between* review dates. Guaranteeing that the stock never breaches a critical safety threshold throughout the entire inter-review window requires separate validation, which is not certified here.

### 8.1 Sampled-Data Monodromy of the Mobilising Channel

The stability of a periodic hybrid system is governed by its **monodromy matrix**—the linear return map over one full review cycle. If all eigenvalues (characteristic multipliers) of this matrix lie strictly inside the complex unit circle, the system returns exponentially to equilibrium after small shocks.

\begin{theorem}[Sampled-Data Monodromy of the Mobilising Channel]
\label{thm:monodromy-mobilising}
Linearize the gated three-state harvesting model about its interior equilibrium, and replace the continuous delay with a sample-and-hold mechanism of period $T_r$ paired with a forward Euler update step. 

Between reviews ($t_k \le t < t_{k+1}$), the state perturbations $\xi = (\delta N, \delta Z, \delta E)^\top$ evolve continuously under frozen effort:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\xi} = A_{\mathrm{hold}}\,\xi,
\end{equation}
where $A_{\mathrm{hold}}$ is the hold matrix from Proposition 6.2. At the review instant $t_{k+1}$, an impulsive management adjustment updates the state using the mobilising gains ($C_E = -0.0595$, $C_Z = +1.785$).

Adopting the **flow-then-update** timing convention—where the pre-review state $\xi_k^-$ flows for duration $T_r$ under held effort before receiving the end-of-period review update—the one-period monodromy operator is:
\begin{equation}
M(T_r) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & T_r C_Z & 1 + T_r C_E \end{pmatrix} \exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r).
\label{eq:monodromy_euler}
\end{equation}
Under this formulation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The sampled equilibrium is exponentially stable if and only if the spectral radius satisfies $\rho(M(T_r)) < 1$.
    \item A complex unit-circle crossing—the linear spectral precursor of a Neimark--Sacker bifurcation—occurs at values of $T_r$ where $M(T_r)$ possesses a simple conjugate pair of eigenvalues $e^{\pm i\theta}$ ($\theta \notin \{0, \pi\}$), with all other eigenvalues remaining strictly within the unit disc.
    \item As the review interval approaches zero, the normalized discrete map converges to the continuous-time undelayed Jacobian:
    \begin{equation}
    \lim_{T_r \to 0} \frac{M(T_r) - I}{T_r} = J_{\mathrm{cont}} = A_{\mathrm{hold}} + \mathbf{e}_3 (C_Z \mathbf{e}_2^\top + C_E \mathbf{e}_3^\top).
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The structure of $M(T_r)$ follows by composing the continuous matrix exponential $\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r)$ with the rank-one discrete effort update row. The stability and unit-circle crossing statements reflect standard spectral radius criteria for discrete linear mappings. 

For the consistency limit, expanding the matrix exponential yields $\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r) = I + A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r + O(T_r^2)$. The discrete update matrix can be factored as $I + T_r R_0$, where $R_0 = \mathbf{e}_3 (C_Z \mathbf{e}_2^\top + C_E \mathbf{e}_3^\top)$. Multiplying these terms gives:
\begin{equation}
M(T_r) = (I + T_r R_0)(I + A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r) + O(T_r^2) = I + T_r(A_{\mathrm{hold}} + R_0) + O(T_r^2).
\end{equation}
Subtracting the identity matrix and dividing by $T_r$ yields $A_{\mathrm{hold}} + R_0$ as $T_r \to 0$. Here, $R_0$ precisely restores the active effort feedback entries ($C_E, C_Z$) that vanish in the third row of $A_{\mathrm{hold}}$.

Regarding the ordering convention: reversing the sequence to $\exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r) R(T_r)$ produces a similar matrix with an identical eigenvalue spectrum whenever the update matrix is invertible, preserving linear stability classifications while altering the nonlinear map. The flow-then-update convention is maintained consistently throughout this work.
\end{proof}

Evaluating this Euler review scheme on the gated Candidate A baseline yields an unstable annual review:
\begin{equation}
\rho(M(1)) = 1.00055 > 1.
\end{equation}
This reflects the fact that the underlying undelayed continuous system is inherently unstable (Section 5.1). The Euler map predicts that the sampled equilibrium restabilizes via a complex unit-circle crossing at $T_r^{\mathrm{UC}} = 47.536\text{ yr}$, before losing stability again via a period-doubling multiplier reaching $-1$ at $T_r^{(-1)} = 79.143\text{ yr}$, suggesting a stable window of $T_r \in (47.54, 79.14)\text{ yr}$.

However, as established below, **these specific quantitative thresholds are artifacts of the explicit Euler approximation**; they do not represent real features of periodic review.

\begin{remark}[The Euler Command-Step Artifact]
\label{rem:euler-artifact}
The restabilization threshold at $T_r = 47.536\text{ yr}$ and the period-doubling boundary at $T_r = 79.143\text{ yr}$ are numerical errors introduced by taking a discrete Euler step on the control update. When the effort dynamics are solved *exactly* across the review interval (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-monodromy}), the true spectral radii at these thresholds are $0.786$ and $0.597$—both safely within the stable unit disc. 

The exact monodromy exhibits only a single unit-circle crossing across the entire tested domain $[0.2, 300]\text{ yr}$, located at $T_r = 6.50\text{ yr}$. The qualitative principle—that lengthening the review interval restabilizes an equilibrium destabilized by annual review—is physically genuine; only the numerical location of the crossing depends on the update scheme.
\end{remark}

### 8.2 Exact Held-Measurement Monodromy

To eliminate discretization artifacts, we evaluate the exact solution of the continuous-discrete hybrid system.

\begin{proposition}[Exact Held-Measurement Monodromy]
\label{prop:exact-monodromy}
Retain the sample-and-hold architecture, the frozen-effort hold flow $\dot{\xi} = A_{\mathrm{hold}} \xi$, and the flow-then-update timing convention. Replace the forward Euler step with the exact integral of the effort equation $\dot{e} = C_E e + C_Z z$ over the review interval $T_r$, holding the perceived depletion signal constant at its end-of-period value $z(t_{k+1}^-)$:
\begin{equation}
e_{k+1} = e^{C_E T_r} e_k + \frac{e^{C_E T_r} - 1}{C_E} C_Z z(t_{k+1}^-).
\end{equation}
The resulting exact monodromy matrix is:
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{ex}}(T_r) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & \dfrac{e^{C_E T_r}-1}{C_E} C_Z & e^{C_E T_r} \end{pmatrix} \exp(A_{\mathrm{hold}} T_r),
\label{eq:monodromy_exact}
\end{equation}
which preserves the consistent asymptotic limit $\lim_{T_r \to 0} (M_{\mathrm{ex}}(T_r) - I)/T_r = J_{\mathrm{cont}}$.

Evaluating $M_{\mathrm{ex}}$ on the gated Candidate A baseline establishes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Protective channel ($C_E = -0.850336$, $C_Z = -1.661702$):} 
    The equilibrium is stable under annual review ($\rho(M_{\mathrm{ex}}(1)) = 0.9838$) and remains strictly stable ($\rho < 1$) across all tested review intervals $T_r \in [0.2, 300]\text{ yr}$, reaching a maximum spectral radius of $0.9967$ at $T_r = 0.2\text{ yr}$. The instability at $T_r \approx 2.306\text{ yr}$ seen in the Euler model is entirely absent under the exact update.
    \item \textbf{Mobilising channel ($C_E = -0.0595$, $C_Z = +1.785$):} 
    Annual review remains unstable under the exact update:
    \begin{equation}
    \rho(M_{\mathrm{ex}}(1)) = 1.00035 > 1.
    \end{equation}
    Across $T_r \in [0.2, 300]\text{ yr}$, the exact monodromy undergoes exactly one unit-circle crossing, located at:
    \begin{equation}
    T_r^{\mathrm{UC,ex}} = 6.50\text{ yr},
    \end{equation}
    with a complex conjugate pair of crossing eigenvalues $\lambda = 0.9846 \pm 0.1746i$ ($|\lambda| = 1$), a stable real eigenvalue at $\lambda_3 = 0.1647$, a non-resonant rotation period ($2\pi/\theta \approx 35.8\text{ cycles}$), and a transverse crossing direction ($d\rho/dT_r < 0$). Holding measurements at the start-of-period value rather than the end-of-period value does not alter these thresholds at the reported precision.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Integrating the scalar linear differential equation $\dot{e} = C_E e + C_Z z_{\mathrm{held}}$ over $t \in [0, T_r]$ with constant $z_{\mathrm{held}} = z(t_{k+1}^-)$ yields the exact discrete update. Because the effort coordinate is held frozen during the continuous flow, this update acts directly on the flowed state. The limit $T_r \to 0$ follows from the standard expansions $e^{C_E T_r} = 1 + C_E T_r + O(T_r^2)$ and $(e^{C_E T_r} - 1)/C_E = T_r + O(T_r^2)$. Spectral radius computations across the parameter grids confirm the stability and crossing values.
\end{proof}

### 8.3 Comparison Across Update Schemes

To ensure our findings are not tied to a specific mathematical implementation of the review step, we also examined the physically native **Zero-Order Hold (ZOH)** model. In this setup, the perceived depletion signal $z$ is sampled discretely, while effort dynamics $\dot{e} = C_E e + C_Z z_k$ evolve continuously alongside the stock:
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{ZOH}}(T_r) = e^{A_{\mathrm{Z}} T_r} + \left( \int_0^{T_r} e^{A_{\mathrm{Z}} s} \, ds \right) C_Z \mathbf{e}_3 \mathbf{e}_2^\top,
\end{equation}
where $A_{\mathrm{Z}}$ is the continuous Jacobian with the delayed feedback entry $C_Z$ omitted.

As $T_r \to \infty$, the monodromy map under all consistent formulations collapses to a rank-one operator whose dominant eigenvalue is governed by the system's DC gain. Because the institutional filter identity requires zero static gain ($L(0) = 0$; Section 3.2), the coupling between harvest effort and fish stock uncouples completely over sufficiently long review cadences. 

As a result, **every consistent periodic-review scheme must restabilize at some finite review interval**.

Across all three formulations, the predicted restabilization threshold for the mobilising channel falls within a narrow window of $6.50\text{--}6.73\text{ yr}$:
* **Exact held-measurement update:** $T_r = 6.5013\text{ yr}$
* **Start-of-period held-measurement update:** $T_r = 6.5013\text{ yr}$
* **Native zero-order hold ($M_{\mathrm{ZOH}}$):** $T_r = 6.7279\text{ yr}$ (crossing eigenvalues $0.9855 \pm 0.1699i$)

Similarly, the protective channel remains unconditionally stable under the native zero-order hold across the entire interval $T_r \in [0.2, 200]\text{ yr}$ (with $\rho(1) = 0.9928$).

### 9.1 Computational Continuation Methods

Local Hopf bifurcation points were identified through characteristic-root tracking and cross-checked against the cubic roots of Theorem 4.1. 

Global branches of periodic orbits were traced using Keller's pseudo-arclength continuation, using $1.5 \times 10^6$ to $5 \times 10^6\text{ yr}$ transient integration steps to verify orbital persistence and rule out numerical artifacts. Standard fixed-initial-condition bisection fails near these Hopf points: because the system experiences extreme critical slowing down (linear decay rates drop to $10^{-4}\text{--}10^{-5}\text{ yr}^{-1}$), bisection misplaces fold locations by more than 20 years.

### 9.2 The Five-Regime Attractor Topology

For the effort-bounded three-state core at Candidate A, numerical continuation uncovers a **five-regime attractor topology** across the fundamental delay range ($0 < \tau \lesssim 160\text{ yr}$):

1. **Regime (i) [$0 < \tau < \tau_-$]: Unmitigated Boom-and-Bust Cycles.**  
   The interior equilibrium is unstable. Across all tested initial histories, the system is captured by a large-amplitude periodic orbit.
2. **Regime (ii) [$\tau_- < \tau < \tau_f$]: The Lower Bistable Window.**  
   A subcritical Hopf bifurcation at $\tau_- = 3.666\text{ yr}$ gives birth to an unstable small-amplitude cycle and stabilizes the interior equilibrium. Over this window of width $\approx 1.92\text{ yr}$, a stable equilibrium focus coexists with the large-amplitude cycle. The boundary of this window is marked by a **single fold at $\tau_f = 5.5872362\text{ yr}$**, where the unstable small orbit and the stable large orbit collide and annihilate.
3. **Regime (iii) [$\tau_f < \tau < \tau_{f2}$]: Safe Harvest Target.**  
   Over this wide delay interval ($5.59\text{ yr} < \tau < 64.40\text{ yr}$), all tested initial histories converge to the stable interior equilibrium. The system exhibits robust monostability.
4. **Regime (iv) [$\tau_{f2} < \tau < \tau_+$]: Latent Mathematical Bistability.**  
   At the upper boundary, a second fold occurs at $\tau_{f2} = 64.4023272\text{ yr}$, creating a stable returning branch of periodic orbits that coexists with the stable equilibrium. However, near this fold, the basin of attraction for the stable periodic orbit is extremely narrow; across extensive numerical trials, almost all realistic histories continue to settle to the equilibrium. The practical onset of persistent oscillations is observed only much later, across the basin boundary at $\tau \in [148.6, 149.5]\text{ yr}$.
5. **Regime (v) [$\tau > \tau_+$]: Persistent Large-Amplitude Cycles.**  
   At $\tau_+ = 150.35848\text{ yr}$, the interior equilibrium loses stability through a second subcritical Hopf bifurcation, leaving a large-amplitude limit cycle riding the maximum effort boundary ($E \approx E_{\max}$) as the sole observed attractor.

```
          τ_- = 3.67 yr                                  τ_+ = 150.36 yr
```

The lower fold $\tau_f = 5.5872362\text{ yr}$ is verified via interval arithmetic at the collocation level:
* An interval-Krawczyk algorithm operating on a 64-mesh collocation discretization encloses the unique Moore--Spence zero within $[5.587236198689, 5.587236198691]\text{ yr}$.
* Independent solutions across three mesh refinements ($m = 64, 96, 128$) agree to within $2.69 \times 10^{-11}$.
* The dominant Floquet multipliers of both colliding branches cross $+1$ with imaginary components identically zero to within $10^{-6}$.

Similarly, the upper fold $\tau_{f2}$ is certified within $[64.402327203368, 64.402327203372]\text{ yr}$. Across 183 continuation steps from $\tau = 150.31\text{ yr}$ down to the turning point at $64.4023\text{ yr}$, numerical residuals remained below $3.7 \times 10^{-12}$, with 64/96/128-order mesh solutions agreeing to within $5.53 \times 10^{-7}$.

### 9.4 Detailed Analysis of the Four-State Working Core

To verify whether simplifying the nutrient pool alters system dynamics, we evaluated the four-state model where detritus biomass ($A$) evolves dynamically:
\begin{equation}
\Delta(\lambda) = \lambda I - A_0 - A_\tau e^{-\lambda\tau}.
\end{equation}
Evaluating this characteristic equation at the frozen-donor equilibrium $(N^*, A^*, Z^*, E^*) = (89.53, 397.87, \log 2/10, 2.09)$ reveals pure imaginary roots at:
\begin{equation}
\tau_- = 3.78487\text{ yr} \quad (\text{period } 250.44\text{ yr}), \qquad \tau_+ = 150.12175\text{ yr} \quad (\text{period } 159.13\text{ yr}).
\end{equation}
These values match the three-state model within $3.2\%$ and $0.2\%$, respectively. The large-amplitude cycle terminates at:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\mathrm{term}, L}^{(4)} \approx 5.63\text{ yr}, \qquad \tau_{\mathrm{fold}, R}^{(4)} \approx 64.4\text{ yr},
\end{equation}
reproducing the three-state lower fold ($5.59\text{ yr}$) and upper fold ($64.40\text{ yr}$) within $0.8\%$ and $0.4\%$.

#### Resolving an Apparent Contradiction
An important distinction must be made regarding unharvested stability:
1. The **frozen-donor characteristic pair** ($\tau_- = 3.78\text{ yr}, \tau_+ = 150.12\text{ yr}$) describes local perturbations when detritus $A$ is held fixed at its steady state.
2. The **dynamic-$A$ stability boundary** ($\omega_A^* \approx 0.001316\text{ yr}^{-1}$) represents the turnover rate threshold of the full four-state system under zero delay ($\tau = 0$). When the true environmental detritus renewal rate falls below $\omega_A^*$, the unharvested equilibrium becomes delay-independently stable.

These two mathematical operators describe different regimes; distinguishing them prevents conflating frozen-pool approximations with fully dynamic ecosystems.

### 9.5 Permissible Ecological Parameter Windows

The five-regime topology requires that the intrinsic ecological growth rate $r$ fall within a bounded window. Across our sensitivity sweeps, the dual-Hopf structure exists only for:
\begin{equation}
r \in (0.008, 0.061)\text{ yr}^{-1} \quad \text{for economic responsiveness } \eta \in [0.5, 3.0].
\end{equation}
If the biological growth rate exceeds this window ($r > 0.061\text{ yr}^{-1}$), the stock replenishes too rapidly for delayed institutional adjustments to trigger resonant cycles, rendering the system delay-independently stable. 

Because many commercially exploited marine fish stocks possess intrinsic growth rates well above $r = 0.06\text{ yr}^{-1}$ (e.g., small pelagics where $r \in [0.2, 0.8]\text{ yr}^{-1}$), **this delayed institutional mechanism is primarily relevant to slow-growing, long-lived species** (such as deep-sea teleosts, large sharks, and cetaceans) unless the institutional responsiveness $\eta$ is exceptionally high.

### 9.6 Scaffold Companion Model Verification

To verify that our results are not artifacts of a specific production function, we re-executed our analysis on the **scaffold companion framework**—a stage-structured Ricker cascade model incorporating juvenile/adult demographics, material detritus, capital investment, and capacity-constrained effort (7 nonlinear state variables).

The verified records reveal three findings:
1. **Elevated Economic Forcing Triggers Cycles:** Under baseline parameters, the scaffold is delay-independently stable across life histories (cod, sprat, and anchovy archetypes). However, when economic responsiveness is elevated ($\eta = 5.0, \varsigma = 0.8, K_0 = 0.03, q = 0.01$), the cod-class life history exhibits a subcritical Hopf bifurcation at $\tau^* = 43.29$ with a cycle period of $263.4$ time units.
2. **Absence of the Upper Hopf Crossing:** Unlike the three-state core, broad parameter sweeps of the stage-structured scaffold reveal only *stabilizing* crossings ($d\,\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)/d\tau < 0$), with periods ranging from $900$ to $35{,}000$ time units. It lacks a clean upper Hopf return crossing ($\tau_+$).
3. **Structural Takeaway:** Delay-induced oscillations can emerge in structured demographic models, but the existence of a closed two-crossing stability window $(\tau_-, \tau_+)$ requires specific saturation and feedback characteristics that differ between simple biomass pools and age-structured cascades.

## 10. The Loop-Gain Family

Despite their differences, the models analyzed in this paper share an underlying linear feedback architecture. This common structure can be unified using classical control theory.

### 10.1 The General Feedback Identity

Expressing the living resource ledger through its primitive mass fluxes—assimilation $g$, natural mortality $m$, decomposition $d_U$, and harvest rate $h$—the rate of biomass depletion is $\ell = h - (g - m) = -\dot{X}$. Let $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ represent ecological state perturbations governed by $\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}} = \mathbf{J}\boldsymbol{\xi} + \mathbf{b}_E e$, with institutional memory acting as a first-order filter of past depletion with gain $\gamma_m$ and time constant $\tau_m$.

The linearized characteristic equation for every model in this paper maps to the universal feedback identity:
\begin{equation}
\lambda - C_E - C_Z e^{-\lambda\tau} \left( \frac{-\gamma_m \lambda \, \mathbf{c}^\top (\lambda \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{J})^{-1} \mathbf{b}_E}{1 + \tau_m \lambda} \right) = 0.
\label{eq:universal_feedback}
\end{equation}
This formulation decomposes the feedback loop into three components:
1. An **ecological resolvent** $(\lambda \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{J})^{-1}$, which dictates the natural frequencies the ecosystem can sustain;
2. An **institutional loop gain** $C_Z \gamma_m$, which dictates the magnitude of management response;
3. A **phase shift** $e^{-\lambda\tau}$, supplied by the institutional delay.

Oscillations emerge if and only if the institutional gain is large enough to close the loop while the delay rotates the phase into positive feedback.

### 10.2 Loop-Gain Exclusion of Delay-Induced Instability

\begin{theorem}[Loop-Gain Exclusion Theorem]
\label{thm:loop-gain-exclusion}
Write the general feedback equation as $\lambda - C_E - C_Z e^{-\lambda\tau} G(\lambda) = 0$. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Small-Gain Condition (H1):} 
    \begin{equation}
    \sup_{\omega \in \mathbb{R}} \frac{|C_Z| |G(i\omega)|}{|i\omega - C_E|} < 1;
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Undelayed Stability (H2):} The system without delay ($\tau = 0$) is Hurwitz stable;
    \item \textbf{Zero-Root Exclusion (H3):} The static loop gain satisfies $-C_E - C_Z G(0) \neq 0$ (guaranteed in our core because $G(0) = 0$ and $C_E < 0$).
\end{enumerate}
Then there are no purely imaginary roots for any delay $\tau \ge 0$. The equilibrium remains exponentially stable for all delays (delay-independent stability).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If an imaginary root $\lambda = i\omega$ existed, setting the characteristic equation to zero would require:
\begin{equation}
|i\omega - C_E| = |C_Z e^{-i\omega\tau} G(i\omega)| = |C_Z| |G(i\omega)|,
\end{equation}
which implies $|C_Z| |G(i\omega)| / |i\omega - C_E| = 1$. This directly contradicts the strict supremum bound (H1). Because no roots can cross the imaginary axis and the system is stable at $\tau = 0$ (H2), it remains stable for all $\tau \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

This theorem provides an operational certificate: if a regulatory institution keeps its delayed response gain below the small-gain threshold relative to direct damping, **no deployment delay can ever destabilize the fishery**.

### 10.3 Equivalence to Logistic Surrogates

\begin{lemma}[Logistic Identification]
\label{lem:logistic-identification}
In the primitive flux model, let ambient detritus $A$ greatly exceed the half-saturation constant ($A \gg K_A$). Define the net intrinsic growth rate as $r = \mu - d > 0$ and the self-limitation coefficient as $c = r/K$. Then, on any bounded domain where $A$ remains strictly positive:
\begin{equation}
g(X, A) - m(X) = rX \left( 1 - \frac{X}{K} \right) + O\left( \mu X \frac{K_A}{A} \right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the Michaelis--Menten uptake term yields:
\begin{equation}
g - m = \mu X \left( 1 - \frac{K_A}{A + K_A} \right) - dX - cX^2 = (\mu - d)X - cX^2 - \mu X \frac{K_A}{A + K_A}.
\end{equation}
Substituting $\mu - d = r$ and $c = r/K$ produces the logistic equation plus a perturbation term of order $O(K_A/A)$. This demonstrates that traditional logistic harvesting models are asymptotic limits of underlying stoichiometric mass-balance equations.
\end{proof}

### 10.4 The Necessity of Autocatalytic Gain

To test whether the boom-and-bust cycles observed in the mobilising channel depend on the autocatalytic nature of fleet expansion, we tested an alternative model where the bilinear term $\eta E Z_\tau / \Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ was replaced by a smooth, saturating gate:
\begin{equation}
\text{Effort Update} \propto \sigma\left( \frac{Z_\tau}{Z_0} \right),
\end{equation}
which exhibits diminishing marginal investment at high deployment.

Across a randomized search spanning more than 300 parameter configurations, **no delay-induced Hopf bifurcations were detected**. Satiation limits the high-frequency loop gain, preventing the system from meeting the threshold of Theorem~\ref{thm:loop-gain-exclusion}. This indicates that while autocatalytic effort expansion is not mathematically required for cycles, it provides the loop gain necessary to destabilize real fisheries under delay.

## 11. Discussion

### 11.1 The Two Governance Channels as a Fundamental Design Choice

Our results demonstrate that the long-term stability of a harvested resource depends fundamentally on the **feedback sign and structure** of its management institution:

```
               TWO OPPOSING INSTITUTIONAL CONTROL PHILOSOPHIES
               
   MOBILISING CHANNEL (Effort Escalation)       PROTECTIVE CHANNEL (Quota Tracking)
             Sign: C_Z > 0                                 Sign: C_Z < 0
             
     Catch Drops ──► Deploy More Effort           Catch Drops ──► Reduce Harvest
           │                                             │
           ▼                                             ▼
   Destabilizing Feedback Loop                  Stabilizing Negative Feedback
           │                                             │
           ▼                                             ▼
   Subcritical Hopf Bifurcations,               Delay-Independent Stability
   Severe Hysteresis, and Boom-and-             Preserved; Stable Under
   Bust Population Collapse                     Annual Review
```

The difference between these channels is not just the sign of $C_Z$. As shown by our iso-gain tests (Proposition 6.1), flipping the sign of $C_Z$ while retaining its original magnitude shifts the bifurcation delays, but can still leave the system unstable. 

The stability of the protective channel arises because authentic quota tracking combines three features:
1. **Negative feedback:** Harvest quotas are ratcheted down when stock declines are detected;
2. **Strong direct damping ($C_E < 0$):** Excess harvest capacity is retired when unprofitable;
3. **Moderate loop gain:** Policy adjustments do not overreact to transient noise.

This answers a longstanding puzzle in fisheries management \citep{moxnes1998}: **institutional delay alone does not cause fishery collapse**. Rather, collapse is caused by institutional delay coupled with a mobilising response structure that escalates effort when yields decline.

### 11.2 Periodic Review as an Active Control Dial

Treating the review interval $T_r$ as a control parameter changes how we think about institutional design. In classical management, annual assessments are often viewed as the gold standard of vigilance. Yet for slow-growing stocks governed by mobilising rules, **frequent annual reviews are destabilizing**. 

Because management updates provide too little phase lag to dampen the system, annual review traps the fishery in an oscillatory regime. Paradoxically, lengthening the review interval to $T_r \ge 6.50\text{ yr}$ can asymptotically restabilize the system.

> **Management Caution:** Restabilizing an equilibrium via long review intervals is not sound governance. While a 7-year review cycle can stabilize the mathematics of the return map, it leaves the resource completely unmanaged between reviews. An unexpected environmental shock occurring in year two could drive the stock to extinction before the next scheduled assessment. Local spectral stabilization must not be used as an excuse for administrative neglect.

### 11.4 Historical Grounding: The Northern Cod Collapse

The timescales identified by our models are not theoretical abstractions; they align with documented timelines of resource collapse. The collapse of the northern cod (*Gadus morhua*) stock off Newfoundland (NAFO Divisions 2J3KL) is a classic example:
* **The Management Cadence:** Assessments proceeded on a regular annual schedule while estimated spawning biomass plummeted by over $95\%$, dropping from $\sim 735{,}000\text{ tonnes}$ in 1991 to $\sim 31{,}000\text{ tonnes}$ in 1994 \citep{dfo2016}.
* **The Deployment Lag:** Despite warnings of overexploitation \citep{hutchings1994, walters1996}, institutional inertia delayed decisive intervention until the moratorium of July 1992. Commercial fishing did not resume until June 2024—a management recovery cycle spanning **32 years** \citep{dfo2024}.

This historical trajectory illustrates the danger of inter-review depletion: a stock can collapse between assessment cycles while management responses remain incremental.

Furthermore, empirical assessment-frequency studies confirm that the review interval acts as a high-stakes control dial. Simulation evaluations demonstrate that moving from annual to multi-year assessments reduces relative yield, increases interannual catch volatility, and elevates depletion risk across diverse fisheries \citep{li2016, peterson2022}.

### 11.5 Methodological Hierarchy of Proof and Certification

To maintain epistemic clarity, the claims in this paper are organized into three distinct tiers of certainty:
1. **Analytically Proved Theorems:** The cubic modulus conditions, the even-pairs crossing property, the no-Hopf theorem for protective channels, the discrete monodromy formulations, and the loop-gain small-gain exclusions are fully proved.
2. **Interval-Certified Numerical Enclosures:** The Hopf bifurcation delays ($\tau_-, \tau_+$) are enclosed using validated interval-Newton methods, guaranteeing that roots of the registered characteristic polynomials exist within the stated decimal brackets.
3. **Collocation-Certified Global Bifurcations:** The global folds ($\tau_f \approx 5.59\text{ yr}, \tau_{f2} \approx 64.40\text{ yr}$) are certified at the discrete collocation level using Moore--Spence and Krawczyk interval algorithms. A full continuous-delay lift to the infinite-dimensional function space remains an open problem.

### 11.6 Early-Warning Signals and Basin Boundaries

Ecologists have proposed early-warning indicators (e.g., rising autocorrelation and increasing variance) to forecast impending regime shifts \citep{scheffer2009, carpenter2011}. 

Our results suggest these indicators must be used with caution:
* **Basin boundaries vs. Bifurcations:** In our models, the practical onset of collapse at $\tau \approx 148.6\text{ yr}$ is a **basin boundary crossing**, located more than 80 years above the true local fold bifurcation at $\tau_{f2} \approx 64.40\text{ yr}$. Early-warning indicators designed to detect local critical slowing down near a fold would look in the wrong place.
* **History-Dependent Asymmetry:** In the bistable window (Regime ii), whether a fishery thrives or collapses depends on its historical trajectory: at $\tau = 5.575\text{ yr}$, a depleted stock is captured by a destructive cycle, while an abundant stock safely approaches equilibrium. Simple rolling-window variance cannot detect this history-dependent risk.

Operational warning systems must track institutional metrics alongside biological signals:
* **Critical Slowing Down:** Rising lag-1 autocorrelation in biomass;
* **Variance Amplification:** Increasing volatility in landings;
* **Epistemic Divergence:** Growing disagreement between independent stock estimators and widening assessment confidence intervals;
* **Policy Inertia:** Increasing harvest pressure despite declining catch per unit effort.

### 11.7 Limitations

Our analysis has several structural boundaries:
* **Model Scope:** Our models focus on scalar renewable resources with delayed feedback. Spatial dynamics, age-structured selectivity, and multi-species ecological interactions are omitted.
* **Discrete Lags:** Management delay is modeled as a single discrete time lag ($\tau$). Distributed institutional lags (e.g., gamma-distributed processing times) require separate analysis.
* **Deterministic Focus:** Our global bifurcation structures are evaluated deterministically; demographic and environmental stochasticity will smooth sharp fold boundaries into probabilistic transitions.

### 11.8 Open Problems

We highlight two technical challenges for future research:
1. **Computer-Assisted Proofs of Delay Bifurcations:** Upgrading the collocation-level fold certificates ($\tau_f, \tau_{f2}$) to full infinite-dimensional proofs using the rigorous framework of Church and Lessard \citep{church2022}.
2. **Transverse Fold Persistence:** Proving the structural persistence of the periodic orbit fold under small perturbations in non-autonomous and spatial domains.

## 12. Conclusion

Where delay sits determines what it does. In renewable resource management, delays are not just biological realities of growth and maturation; they are institutional properties of observation, assessment, and policy implementation.

Our analysis demonstrates that the impact of institutional delay depends on the governance rule:
* **The mobilising channel (effort escalation) is a structural hazard.** Under this rule, delayed feedback produces subcritical Hopf bifurcations, a five-regime attractor topology, and severe hysteresis that traps fisheries in boom-and-bust cycles. Under periodic review, annual assessments destabilize the system, and stability is recovered only for review intervals exceeding $6.5\text{ years}$.
* **The protective channel (quota tracking) is resilient.** This rule guarantees delay-independent stability across all continuous time lags and maintains robust stability under annual periodic review. Apparent instabilities are artifacts of crude numerical discretizations.

Both behaviors reflect a single underlying principle: institutional loop gain. Oscillatory collapse requires both sufficient delayed gain and a phase shift that flips negative feedback into reinforcement. The management takeaway is clear: **institutional delays, update cadences, and response gains are dynamic design parameters with the same power to stabilize or destroy a fishery as biological growth rates.** 

Sustainable governance requires treating management institutions and the ecosystems they harvest as a single, coupled feedback system—and designing the rules of governance to avoid the hidden traps of delayed response.
