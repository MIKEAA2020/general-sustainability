                                                  Abstract
         Institutions observe renewable resources at discrete times, revise rules at scheduled reviews,
     and hold commands between them. We model this architecture as sample-and-hold governance
     and distinguish it from both continuous-delay feedback and an annual-step approximation. We
     prove forward invariance of the nonnegative state space and finite-horizon consistency as the
     review interval tends to zero; neither result implies stability at any fixed positive interval. On an
     illustrative logistic core, the exponential frozen-assessment update has one unit-circle crossing
     at 6.501 years, whereas a forward-Euler command update reports crossings at 47.536 and 79.143
     years. The exponential protective comparator is stable on the tested interval grid. The 6.501-
     year value is not a calibrated institutional threshold: across six-parameter joint perturbation
     boxes, a crossing is found at 60 of 64 corners at ±0.5% and 44 of 64 at ±1%; every no-crossing
     corner is stable on a 20,001-point scan. In a 42-stock screen, the Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted
     target-band count is zero. A recovered review-interval proxy test has only two proxy values,
     both below the modeled response windows, and its negative association is opposite the predicted
     approach gradient and confounded by region. A 32-system case inventory identifies no candidate
     meeting all four eligibility criteria. Northern cod contributes a descriptive two-window partition,
     not an identified governance mechanism. The results support operator-specific stability analysis,
     not a general claim that a particular review cadence stabilizes real fisheries.

1    Introduction
Renewable-resource institutions operate on schedules. Surveys generate observations; assessments
translate observations into advice; a rule sets a catch or effort command; and that command is
held, or updated only at the next authorized review. In fisheries, the review interval may be annual
or multi-year, while the ecological state evolves continuously between decisions (Butterworth and
Punt, 1999; Butterworth, 2007; Punt et al., 2016). The institutional clock is therefore part of the
dynamical system, not merely a reporting convention.
    Two common mathematical substitutes discard that architecture. A continuous-delay equation
lets feedback act continuously on a lagged signal. An annual difference equation compresses all
between-review dynamics into one step. Neither is generally the map implemented by an institution
that samples, decides, and holds. Sampled-data control has long treated the zero-order hold as an


                                                      1
operator with its own spectral properties (Chen and Francis, 1995; Åström and Wittenmark, 1997;
Nešić and Teel, 2004). Resource-governance models do not always preserve that distinction, even
though stability can change when the decision operator changes.
    A second problem is causal promotion: a spectral peak, cohort cycle, or crisis-driven monitoring
record does not by itself identify the sign, timing, or responsiveness of a governance rule. The
skeptical re-analysis of the Russell Cycle, for example, found that a long record treated as periodic
did not resolve a true periodicity (McManus, Licandro, and Coombs, 2016). Here the same
diagnostic discipline is applied to claims about institutional feedback. Periodicity is not treated as
a mechanism label.
    This paper makes four contributions. First, it specifies a sample-and-hold model in which
observation, assessment, review, decision, deployment, ecological-response, and memory times are
kept separate. Second, it proves forward invariance of the nonnegative state space and states precisely
what the rapid-review limit does establish: finite-horizon approximation under regularity conditions,
not finite-interval stability. Third, it compares an exponential frozen-assessment controller update
with forward Euler on a common illustrative ecological plant and records how crossings move with
the operator and parameter vector. Fourth, it re-examines the empirical layer: a multiplicity-
controlled 42-stock spectral screen, a documented but coarse review-interval proxy test, a curated
32-system case search, and the northern-cod record.
    The focal controller is deliberately diagnostic rather than normative. It raises extraction effort
in response to a perceived shortfall, representing a mobilising or extractive feedback. A protective
comparator reduces effort when the perceived deficit rises. The numerical coefficients are inherited
illustrative conventions, not field estimates; the exercise therefore identifies operator dependence
within a specified model class, not a calibrated review interval for a real fishery. This distinction
matters especially because the baseline crossing lies close to the unit circle and moves substantially
under small parameter perturbations.
    The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 defines the model and evidence procedures. Section 3
reports the two mathematical properties, the operator-specific crossing record, sensitivity, and
empirical results. Section 4 states what transfers to management design and what remains
unidentified. Detailed proofs, case records, data tables, and computational audits are in the
Supplementary Information.

2.2    Structural results and numerical protocol
The admissible state set is N ≥ 0, Z ≥ 0, and E ∈ [0, Emax ]. The signal map is nonnegative
and effort commands are projected onto the admissible interval. Supplementary Proposition S2.1
establishes forward invariance for held or convex-interpolated effort. Separately, if the continuous
vector field is C 1 with bounded derivative on a compact neighborhood of compared trajectories,
assessment is contemporaneous, projection is locally inactive, and there are no additional queues or
stochastic updates, the flow-then-update map has local one-step defect O(Tr2 ). Discrete Gronwall
then gives uniform O(Tr ) error on each fixed finite horizon as Tr → 0. This is a finite-horizon
approximation statement, not a finite-interval stability theorem.
    For the logistic core, the linearised held-flow matrix is combined with the exponential frozen-
assessment or Euler review update; its spectral radius ρ(Tr ) is scanned over [0.2, 200] years on a
200,001-point grid, with bisection refinement at sign changes. The crossing scan was re-executed
against the deposited campaign and agrees with the archived crossing table. A separate high-
resolution sensitivity implementation evaluates the six-parameter joint boxes on 20,001 review-
interval points. Numerical scans are not presented as analytic global stability theorems. A
finite-difference audit of the full nonlinear plant plus frozen-assessment controller flow reproduces
the local Jacobian and crossing; details and limitations are given in Supplementary S11. No
invariant-circle continuation was completed.
    A second plant, a delayed-recruitment two-stage map, is included only as a distinct structural
comparison. The archived trajectory-classified windows are provisional because their original solver
configuration and histories are not attached. The pre-registered stage-map reconstruction is a newly

specified object: its comparison with those windows is a consistency check, not a validation. The
state dimension, plant, and effort scale differ from the logistic core, so cross-plant response regions
do not isolate an operator effect; that contrast is made only on the common logistic plant.

2.3   Empirical and case procedures
Stock screen and power. The input is the RAM Legacy Stock Assessment Database v4.66. Of 58
forage-fish stock series, 42 have at least 20 non-missing annual spawning-stock-biomass (SSB) points
(range 20–77 years). The analysis linearly detrends each SSB series, computes a unit-total-power
Lomb–Scargle periodogram on 1,500 frequencies, and integrates power in bands A (2.5–5 years) and
B (5–9 years). For each stock and band, an AR(1) null is fitted to the detrended series and simulated
200 times with seed 7; empirical p = (k + 1)/201 values are adjusted by Benjamini–Hochberg over
the 84 stock-by-band tests. These are SSB periodicity diagnostics, not controller-sign tests. The
annual-review label and the review-interval proxies are analyst assignments, not database fields
or per-stock primary-verified classifications. Sensitivity variants, end-point trims, and alternative
bands are reported in Supplementary S9.
    The separate Tr -proxy test uses the group-documented coding in the recovered script: seven
Peru/Chile stocks receive Tr = 0.5 years (annual quota with in-season responsive closures), and 35
annual-TAC proxies receive Tr = 1 year. The 4-year anchovy and 8-year sprat response windows are
both above these two proxy values. Thus the cohort contains no documented multi-year responsive
review interval with which to estimate a window-approach gradient. The proxy assignments are
coarse and not primary-verified; the seven short-interval cases are geographically concentrated,
which limits any cross-sectional interpretation.
    A separate injected-signal exercise runs the committed sampled-governance effort model with
multiplicative lognormal observation noise. The canonical white-noise table comprises 17 cells,
50 trials per cell, trial seeds 0–49, and a 120-replicate seed-7 AR(1) detection null. It quantifies
conditional detection under the specified simulation grid; it is not a per-stock minimum-power
guarantee.

Structured case search. The 32-system inventory is curated rather than systematic. Each
candidate is assessed against four requirements: (i) responsive governance rather than a one-time cap
or ban; (ii) an independently dateable response or implementation lag; (iii) no competing ecological
or structural driver supported for the same outcome; and (iv) individual-resource or station-level
data. The evidential rule for (iii) is comparative: institutional feedback must outperform prespecified
alternatives on held-out prediction, phase order, or intervention response. The search log and full
row-level dispositions are in Supplementary S4.

Northern cod. The northern cod (NAFO 2J3KL) record is treated as an assessment-based
descriptive case. The strong-Allee surplus equation and its phase-line result are model-class
diagnostics, not a fitted explanation of the stock trajectory. Assessment-table values from DFO
CSAS SAR 2016/026 are used to distinguish the 1991–1995 crash window from the later non-recovery
record. Mortality allocation differs across assessment formulations, and the constrained-mortality
quantities are not reproduced here; they remain hypotheses and open reconstruction requirements
(Supplementary S13.2).


3     Results
3.1   Invariance and the scope of the rapid-review limit
Under the admissibility conditions in Section 2.2, the interval N ≥ 0, Z ≥ 0, 0 ≤ E ≤ Emax is
forward invariant. The logistic boundary has zero population growth at N = 0; the signal derivative
cannot point outward through Z = 0 because its forcing is nonnegative; and projection keeps effort
in its prescribed bounds. This is a state-space result, not a stability result.

                                                  5
    As Tr → 0, the held flow and contemporaneous update converge on fixed finite horizons to the
corresponding continuous-time system when the vector field is regular and no nonsmooth boundary
is crossed. For a fixed positive Tr , local stability is decided by the eigenvalues of the complete
review map, including the plant flow and update law. It is therefore possible for the rapid-review
approximation to be accurate at short horizons while a finite-interval map has a different stability
classification. The formal statement and proof are in Supplementary S2.

3.2   Response windows and operator-specific crossings
The archived trajectory-classified stage model reports persistent oscillation near a 3–4-year anchovy
review interval and across a 6–12-year sprat interval for its baseline parameterisation. These
are provisional classifications: the original solver and initial histories were not deposited. A
newly specified, preregistered stage-map reconstruction did not reproduce those anchovy or sprat
oscillation criteria; it did reproduce annual-review convergence for anchovy and convergence for the
cod and slow-stock criteria. This mismatch is not averaged away: the reconstruction and archived
classification are different evidence objects, and a mismatch does not adjudicate the unavailable
legacy generating object (Supplementary S9.2).
    On the common illustrative logistic plant, the exponential frozen-assessment mobilising update
has a single complex-pair unit-circle crossing at Tr = 6.50129495 years. The controller update
exactly integrates the effort-rate ODE with the endpoint assessment fixed; the multiplier calculation
uses its local linearization. The crossing angle is 0.175494 radians, or about 0.05586π. The sampled
multipliers are outside the unit circle immediately below the crossing and inside immediately above
it. The forward-Euler implementation, on the same plant and baseline vector, has a complex-pair
crossing at 47.535982 years and a second, real −1 crossing at 79.142688 years. Thus the transition
pattern is update-operator dependent; the Euler values are not estimates of an institutional threshold.
A continuous-delay comparator from the companion analysis gives crossings near 3.666 and 150.358
years (Abaee, 2026), further illustrating that a continuous lag, a frozen-assessment controller update,
and a forward-Euler command step are different models (Fig. 2; the common-plant scope is detailed
in Supplementary S11).
    The exponential frozen-assessment protective comparator has no crossing on the tested [0.2, 200]-
year grid, with maximum sampled spectral radius 0.996740. This numerical statement is specific to
the stated gain convention, equilibrium, projection-inactive local map, and grid. It is not a general
theorem that protective policies are stable.

3.4   Forage-fish screen: no multiplicity-adjusted target-band detections
Among 42 RAM Legacy v4.66 forage-fish stocks with at least 20 annual SSB points, no stock-band
cell is significant after Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across 84 tests in the baseline 2.5–5-year
and 5–9-year bands. The smallest raw empirical p-value is 0.0448, but its adjusted value is 1.00. A
wider sensitivity battery varying detrending, end-point trimming, red-noise simulation counts, seeds,
and band definitions also yields no adjusted target-band detections; one nominal baseline band-B
flag does not survive multiplicity control. The result is evidence about this particular diagnostic
and cohort, not evidence that governance feedback is absent.
    The screen’s null-excess distribution has many values below its AR(1) 95% thresholds, but an
AR(1) null controls only the fitted red-noise structure. It does not remove quasi-periodic external
forcing, regime shifts, observation error, or assessment changes. In particular, the stock screen does
not estimate the sign of a harvest-control rule or the causal effect of its review interval. A separate
17-cell synthetic power table detects sprat-class signals at 1.00 in the low-noise H = 100, 200
cells and at 0.24–0.70 in the corresponding high-noise cells; anchovy-class power is 0.00–0.10 and
the cod-labelled false-positive rate 0.02–0.08. These are conditional simulation proportions, not

3.5   Review-interval proxy test: no approach gradient is identifiable
The re-executed Tr -ranked test contains 42 stocks but only two assigned proxy values: seven
Peru/Chile cases at 0.5 years and 35 annual-TAC cases at 1 year. Both proxy values lie below both
sampled response windows (4 years for the anchovy-class window and 8 years for the sprat-class
window). There are no documented responsive multi-year cases in this cohort from which to
estimate how spectral excess changes as Tr approaches a modeled window. The correct inference
is therefore no identifiable window-approach gradient, not a supported gradient and not a direct
empirical confirmation of a particular threshold.

                                                   8
    The two-group contrasts do reproduce: the 0.5-year Peru/Chile group has greater median band-A
null-excess than the annual group (0.529 versus 0.257; two-sided Mann–Whitney p = 0.007), and
the rank association with assigned Tr is negative for band A (Spearman ρ = −0.408, p = 0.007) and
band B (ρ = −0.340, p = 0.028). The sign is opposite to a simple approach-to-window prediction:
the group assigned the shorter interval is farther from either modeled window. These reproduced
two-group associations must not be described as an approach gradient or as an institutional effect.
The 0.5-year assignments are group-level proxies for in-season responsive closures, not primary-
verified stock-specific review intervals; all seven are Peru/Chile cases. Regional ecology, including
ENSO-related quasi-periodic forcing, is a plausible confound, but is not established as the cause
by this analysis. The AR(1) null absorbs fitted red noise, not quasi-periodic forcing. At nominal
95% thresholds, the test records 0/42 band-A flags and 1/42 band-B flag; these are not additional
BH-adjusted discoveries. Per-stock intervals, thresholds, and coding notes are in Supplementary
S10.
    The companion F-placement audit locally reproduces the 42-stock cohort, matches fisheries-ADH
F to last-F for 42 of 43 intersected stocks, and matches all 13 broader-pool taxonomic-group F
quartiles. Mean stock-level F is associated with band-A null-excess before excluding Peru/Chile
(ρ = 0.3069, p = 0.0609) but not after exclusion (ρ = 0.0339, p = 0.8565); band-B results show the
same attenuation. This is consistent with regional confounding, not proof of its cause. The archived
ADH formula and cohort summaries also reproduce, but ADH remains a calculated buffer-years
proxy rather than a direct institutional-response measure (Supplementary S12).

3.6   Northern cod: a descriptive two-window record
The assessment record separates a 1991–1995 decline from the post-1995 low-stock period. SSB
falls from about 730 kt in 1991 to 11 kt in 1995; the later assessment values remain low through the
mid-2000s before increasing to about 300 kt in 2015. Natural mortality varies substantially across
assessment years and is not a monotone proxy for governance. The window split is descriptive: it
does not identify a switch in institutional timing, nor adjudicate environmental, predation, model,
and management explanations. The constrained-mortality formulation supplies useful hypotheses
but its underlying quantities are not reproduced in this edition. Fig. 5 and Supplementary S12
preserve the values and period definitions.


4     Discussion
4.1   The transferable result is an operator distinction, not a cadence threshold
The exponential frozen-assessment and forward-Euler controller updates, and the continuous-delay
comparator, yield different spectral transitions on the same illustrative ecological plant. This is
the central mathematical result. It cautions against transferring a stability boundary from one
representation to another without matching (i) what is observed and when, (ii) which state estimate
enters the rule, (iii) when the command changes, (iv) whether it is held between reviews, and (v)
how command gain scales with the interval. In a management-strategy evaluation, those items
should be explicit alongside operating-model uncertainty and implementation error (Punt et al.,
2016).
    The comparison does not show that real fisheries are unstable under annual reviews or that a
longer interval is generally safer. The baseline exponential-update crossing is parameter-sensitive,
the controller is uncalibrated, and the exponential protective result is only a scan over a finite
interval grid. A fitted institutional threshold would require independent estimates of ecological
response, observation and assessment error, decision and deployment lags, and the effective rule
gain. Without them, the reported years are reproducible properties of a stated model, not policy
prescriptions.

4.2   Empirical diagnostics constrain stronger claims
The baseline forage-fish screen contains no multiplicity-adjusted target-band detection, and the
sensitivity variants do not change that decision. This is a useful negative diagnostic but not a test
of a causal governance mechanism. Lomb–Scargle band power can be elevated by external forcing
and structural change; an AR(1) null does not model all such alternatives. Nor does a spectrum
alone reveal whether management responded to a signal before or after the ecological change.
    The corrected reading of the review-interval test is particularly important. There is no observed
interval spanning the modeled response windows, so there is no identifiable window-approach
gradient. What is reproduced is an opposite-direction contrast between two proxy groups: seven
regionally clustered 0.5-year assignments have larger null-excess than 35 annual assignments. Since
the shorter proxy is farther from both modeled windows, this contrast does not support the proposed
approach mechanism. The two-value coding, group-level rather than stock-level verification, and
regional concentration leave confounding unresolved. ENSO-related quasi-periodicity is a candidate
explanation, not a demonstrated cause. This distinction replaces any earlier shorthand suggesting
that the cross-sectional test found a predicted gradient.
    The 32-system search likewise has a narrow interpretation. No inventory candidate satisfies
all four evidential requirements, but the inventory is a curated search rather than an exhaustive
review, and missing station-level data could exclude otherwise relevant cases. The result is that a
qualifying demonstration was not found in this search, not that institutional feedback cannot occur.

4.3   A descriptive cod partition is not a causal decomposition
Northern cod provides an instructive record because the collapse and subsequent low-stock period
are well documented. However, a two-window plot cannot identify the causal contribution of
harvest rules, predation, climate, recruitment, observation, or assessment structure. The strong-
Allee surplus model is a local mathematical lens; unless fitted against alternatives and validated
out of sample, it does not establish an Allee mechanism. Likewise, the constrained-mortality
formulation discussed in prior notes cannot support numerical conclusions here until its source
table, uncertainty, and complete computation are recovered. The manuscript therefore retains

only the reproducible descriptive partition and lists the unreproduced mortality claims as open
hypotheses in Supplementary S13.2.

4.4   Requirements for a governance-feedback test
A stronger empirical design should treat institutions as measured mechanisms rather than inferred
labels. At minimum, it would require:
 1. dated observations, assessment releases, rule decisions, implementation, and ecological response
    at a resolution finer than the candidate response window;
 2. stock-specific evidence about the operative rule, compliance, command persistence, and the
    timing of responsive revisions;
 3. a state-space model that separates observation error, process noise, external forcing, regime
    shifts, and structural assessment changes;
 4. pre-specified comparisons among no-feedback, delayed-feedback, sample-and-hold, and ecological-
    only models, with held-out predictive scoring or a credible intervention design; and
 5. uncertainty propagation for the estimated review map, rather than reporting only a single point
    crossing.
These requirements are compatible with management-strategy evaluation and adaptive management
practice (Walters, 1986; Punt et al., 2016), but are more demanding than detecting a spectral
peak. Supplementary S7 sets out five prospective designs with estimands and decision rules; none
is reported as executed.

4.5   Limitations
This edition has six important limitations. First, the focal numerical parameters are illustrative,
with no joint field calibration. Second, local eigenvalue stability does not imply global nonlinear
stability or acceptable transient performance. Third, dense-grid non-crossing results are numerical
evidence only. Fourth, the RAM screen and Tr -proxy assignments inherit database coverage and
analyst coding; stock-specific primary verification is incomplete. Fifth, the stage-map reconstruction
does not reproduce all archived trajectory windows, which remain provisional. Sixth, the case
inventory and cod analysis do not identify a causal governance effect. Supplementary materials
retain complete outputs and provenance so that these limitations are not hidden by a concise main
text.


5     Conclusion
Review cadence is a dynamical design variable when decisions are sampled and commands are
held, but a cadence threshold is meaningful only for a fully specified and empirically supported
update map. In the illustrative system, exponential frozen-assessment, Euler, and continuous-delay
operators produce different crossings, while small joint parameter perturbations move or remove
them. The analytic invariance and rapid-review results do not supply a stability guarantee at finite
review intervals. The empirical screens do not reveal a window-approach gradient; the reproduced
two-group association points in the opposite direction and is confounded by coarse, regionally
clustered proxies. Northern cod remains a descriptive case. These findings motivate operator-aware,
data-rich evaluations rather than a general claim that any particular governance interval stabilizes
real resources.
