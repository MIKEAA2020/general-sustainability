5                                                     Abstract
6              Environmental governance runs on clocks: institutions observe resource systems at dis-
7          crete times and hold the resulting controls until the next review. Formal models routinely
8          substitute this sampled architecture with a continuous delay or a single annual step. We
9          show the substitution matters. Modelling periodic review as sample-and-hold governance —
10         a loop that observes and updates only at review times — we establish two exact properties,
11         forward invariance of the sampled state space and rapid-review consistency over finite hori-
12         zons only, and compute stability review-interval by review-interval, demonstrated on fish-
13         eries. Stability boundaries move or vanish when the operator is changed: at an illustrative
14         baseline the exact map crosses once near a 6.5-year interval while one-step approximations
15         report artefact crossings, and the protective channel is stable at every tested interval. That
16         crossing is reported as a band, not a threshold: under a half-percent joint perturbation of
17         the model parameters it ranges from under one year to nearly eleven, and under a one-
18         percent perturbation the instability verdict itself fails in a third of cases (Section 3.4.1), so
19         the robust content of the paper is the operator contrast and the protective-channel result
20         rather than the location of the crossing. A multiplicity-controlled screen of 42 fish stocks
21         finds no robust institutional cycles and a 32-system cross-sector case search no unconfounded
22         oscillator, showing periodicity alone cannot diagnose governance feedback; the northern cod
23         record splits into a formulation-dependent crash and an unresolved post-collapse identifi-
24         cation problem. What decides whether management stabilises or destabilises a renewable
25         resource is therefore not biology alone but the decision clock — the timing and form of
26         policy revision — a design variable with stability consequences for any periodically reviewed
27         regime, from fisheries assessment to climate stocktakes.

33   1 Introduction
34   Fisheries governance is periodic. Surveys are scheduled, assessments are produced at fixed ca-
35   dences, advice is delivered at review points, and the resulting catch or effort controls are held
36   in force until the next review. The review interval — the time between successive opportunities
37   to revise the command — is sometimes a single year, sometimes a multi-year plan, sometimes
38   a moratorium of indefinite length (Punt and Donovan, 2007; DFO, 2016). The formal litera-
39   ture treats this institutional clock in two ways, neither of which matches the operating object.


                                                          1
40   Population-dynamic theory models institutional response either as a continuous-time lag, that
41   is, a delayed signal entering an evolving control law, in the lineage of Gurney, Blythe, and
42   Nisbet (1980); or as an annual discrete-time decision appended to a surplus-production model,
43   in which the between-decision dynamics are compressed into a single annual step. Real insti-
44   tutions do neither. They observe and assess at discrete times, choose a rule-based command,
45   and then hold or interpolate that command until the next decision opportunity. This paper
46   calls that architecture sampled governance — a governance loop in which state observation and
47   control update occur at discrete review times, not continuously. It is the same architecture
48   the title and abstract call sample-and-hold governance: one object under two names — sampled
49   governance names the institutional loop, sample-and-hold its control-theoretic update law —
50   and the article body uses sample-and-hold (with sampled for its maps and loops) throughout.
51       Two failure modes follow from this mismatch. The first is architecture substitution. Replac-
52   ing the sample-and-hold architecture by a continuous delay, or by an annual difference equation,
53   can move or delete stability boundaries. Conclusions about a governance system then depend on
54   an operator that the institution does not implement. The second is causal promotion. Spectral
55   peaks, cohort cycles, and crisis-driven monitoring records circulate as evidence for institutional-
56   feedback mechanisms when they carry no information about the controller’s sign, cadence, or
57   timing at all. The marine-science literature already contains the disciplinary template for re-
58   sisting this promotion. The skeptical re-analysis of the Russell Cycle (McManus, Licandro, and
59   Coombs, 2016) showed that an 88-year zooplankton series long treated as a cycle failed to show
60   a resolvable true periodicity. This article extends that discipline from climate-forced cycles to
61   governance-feedback claims.
62       The contribution is fivefold. First, a sample-and-hold model of periodic review is specified,
63   in which the governance clock is decomposed into seven time objects (observation interval, as-
64   sessment interval, review interval, decision lag, deployment lag, ecological response lag, and
65   memory timescale) and the extractive controller is an explicit discretisation with specified com-
66   parator classes. Second, two properties of the sampled process are established exactly: forward
67   invariance of the sampled state space, and the precise scope of the rapid-review limit — a
68   finite-horizon numerical-consistency statement that implies nothing about stability at positive
69   review intervals. Third, the review-interval spectrum is computed under the discipline that the
70   sample-and-hold map and the continuous-delay equation are different operators. A stability
71   window located on one map does not transfer to the other, and the paper states which oper-
72   ator carries which statement. Fourth, an empirical layer is reported: a multiplicity-controlled
73   spectral screen of 42 annually assessed stocks, a power analysis of that screen, and a structured
74   case search across more than thirty resource systems. Fifth, the northern cod case (NAFO
75   2J3KL) is carried through the resulting falsification discipline. These five are retrospective; the
76   paper’s constructive content is the prospective programme — five prospective designs specified
77   as preregistration targets — that could convert or refute the mechanism.
78       The model class examined here is the extractive controller: a rule that raises extraction
79   effort in response to a perceived decline. This is the demand-side form of the overshoot loop.
80   The response to a perceived shortfall falls on the productive stock itself rather than on its
81   regeneration. Extraction is raised precisely because the base has declined, which accelerates the
82   decline it is reacting to. Its protective counterpart (effort reduced in response to decline), fixed
83   multi-year plans, and hybrid rules serve as comparators.
84       The extractive rule is not proposed as desirable management. It is a diagnostic repre-
85   sentation of perverse pressure: when a perceived shortfall threatens short-term employment
86   or revenue, the political-economic response — subsidies that sustain effort, pressure to hold
87   catches up — can push extraction upward as the base declines. Capacity-enhancing subsidies
88   are the documented form of this pressure (Sumaila et al., 2019). The protective controller is the
89   policy-relevant comparator; the pair exists to show how controller sign interacts with review
90   timing, not to recommend the extractive rule.

 91       Conclusions drawn inside the extractive class are not generalised to governance as a whole,
 92   and the screen below asks whether such a channel is empirically resolvable at all. The im-
 93   plication for institutional design is direct: an annual-review extractive rule and a multi-year
 94   protective plan are not interchangeable instruments, and a sampled-data analysis must state
 95   which operator a given stability claim is computed on.
 96       The architecture is not unique to fisheries. National climate pledges under the Paris Agree-
 97   ment are communicated every five years and informed by five-yearly global stocktakes (UN-
 98   FCCC, 2015, Articles 4.9 and 14): held commands under periodic review (Figure 1A). Failures
 99   then take a common form across sectors: the governance clock (the review interval), the eco-
100   logical clock (the recovery rate), and the environmental clock (exogenous cycles such as ENSO,
101   Section 3.7) fall out of phase. The timing and form of policy revision — the review interval
102   together with the revision rule — is the decision clock. This article develops the fisheries case
103   in full; Section 4.8 returns to the cross-sector reading.

104   Related work
105   Sampled-data control. That a controller which samples, computes and holds is a different
106   dynamical object from one that acts continuously is foundational in control engineering, not a
107   refinement of it: the sampled-data theory of Chen and Francis (1995) and the digital-control
108   treatment of Åström and Wittenmark (1997) are built on the distinction, and the framework of
109   Nešić and Teel (2004) exists precisely because stability of a continuous design does not transfer
110   automatically to its sampled implementation. The zero-order hold appears there as an operator
111   with spectral properties of its own. Little of that machinery is routinely imported into resource-
112   governance modelling. The two exact properties established below — forward invariance of the
113   sampled state space (Section 3.1) and rapid-review consistency over finite horizons only (Section
114   3.2) — are the governance analogues of facts that are standard in that literature and, to our
115   knowledge, are not stated in this one.

                                                        3
116   Periodic review as practice, and the interval as a design variable. Real institutions do
117   review periodically and hold. Management procedures are evaluated by closed-loop simulation
118   and then applied on multi-year cycles (Butterworth and Punt, 1999; Butterworth, 2007; Punt
119   et al., 2016), with review intervals of a few years the norm and the interval itself a choice the
120   institution makes rather than a constant of nature. Practice is therefore sampled by construc-
121   tion, while the modelling literature reaches for the continuous-time delay or the single annual
122   step. This paper treats the review interval as the design lever it already is in practice.
123   Delay, and the substitution this paper questions. Where lag is modelled in fisheries it
124   is usually continuous. Shertzer and Prager (2007) simulate a spectrum of fisheries and find
125   that management delay requires deeper catch reductions sustained for more years to recover
126   benchmark status, and causes collapse most often under depensatory recruitment or density-
127   dependent catchability. Brown et al. (2012) find that delays beyond about five years raise
128   collapse probability under environmental decline, and that the long-run cost of delay exceeds
129   the short-term benefit of overfishing. Adamson and Hilker (2020) model delayed knowledge
130   of the stock and show it can induce harvester–resource cycles. Karlsson and Gilek (2020)
131   treat delay as a governance pathology to be managed. Each of these is a statement about a
132   continuous lag. The question asked here is prior to theirs: whether substituting a continuous
133   lag for a sampled review is admissible at all. Sections 3.3 and 4.1 show that it is not — response
134   regions and stability boundaries move or vanish when the operator is changed — so the delay
135   literature and this paper concern different operators, and a result about one is not automatically
136   a result about the other.
137   Position. The contribution is therefore not the observation that institutions act periodically,
138   which is old, nor that delay is costly, which is well established. It is that the sampled operator
139   has properties the continuous surrogate does not; that two of those properties can be stated
140   exactly and proved; and that the review interval consequently carries a stability signature that
141   can be computed rather than negotiated.

142   2 Material and methods
143   2.1 The sample-and-hold model of periodic review
144   The model treats periodic review as a sample-and-hold control architecture: the command set
145   at a review instant is held fixed until the next review, and only the between-review ecological
146   dynamics are continuous in time. Seven time objects are kept distinct, and no two are col-
147   lapsed into a single “governance lag” unless the empirical record cannot resolve them and the
148   aggregation rule is stated (Table 1).
149       For fisheries readers, the control-theoretic vocabulary maps onto management-procedure
150   language: the controller is the harvest control rule; the command is the catch or effort limit
151   it sets; the review interval is the advice cycle; the hold is the plan period between revisions;
152   and the operator — the decision architecture on which a stability claim is computed — is
153   the management procedure itself. Assessment error and implementation error enter where
154   observation meets advice and where decisions meet deployment (Table 1).

273   2.3 The review-map operators
274   Three objects are in play: the continuous-delay equation, the logistic hold map, and the stage-
275   structured review map. The continuous delay equation and the logistic sample-and-hold map
276   are the same feedback loop under two delay operators. The stage-structured map is a different
277   ecological plant (delayed-recruitment stage structure against scalar logistic surplus production)
278   with a different state dimension; it is not an operator substitution on the logistic plant. A valid
279   architecture comparison holds the ecological plant, controller linearisation, equilibrium, and
280   parameter vector fixed while changing only the timing operator. The 3–4 yr windows and the
281   ≈ 6.5 yr exact-update crossing below are therefore reported as a comparison across plants and
282   operators, which does not isolate the operator effect; the one-plant operator contrast in Section
283   3.4 isolates the operator for the logistic plant. Changing the operator can move or delete a
284   crossing, and that relocation is a property of the review map’s spectrum, not a refutation of
285   the loop mechanism. The discipline throughout is that every stability statement names its
286   operator, and statements computed on the hold map, on the stage-structured review map, and
287   on the continuous-delay equation do not transfer to one another. The operative condition is
288   det(DPTr (X ∗ ) − eiθ I) = 0 on the map to which the statement refers, with DPTr (X ∗ ) the
289   Jacobian of the review map at its fixed point (the monodromy matrix of the sampled loop).
290       For a deterministic sampled system, the inter-review flow and the review update combine
291   into a Poincaré map Xn+1 = PTr (Xn ), where Xn includes the ecological state, memory, held
292   command, and any explicitly modelled queue. A fixed point is locally asymptotically stable if
293   and only if every eigenvalue of DPTr (X ∗ ) lies inside the unit disk. A verified crossing through
294   the unit circle as the fixed parameter Tr changes is a sampled-data parameter bifurcation. It is
295   not rate-induced tipping, which requires a nonautonomous parameter path and a loss of tracking
296   as the ramp speed changes (Ashwin, Wieczorek, Vitolo, and Cox, 2012).

297   2.4 The cross-sectional spectral screen
298   The screen’s input layer is a fixed 42-stock cohort of the RAM Legacy Stock Assessment
299   Database v4.66 (Ricard, Minto, Jensen, and Baum, 2012): the annual-review designation (ap-
300   plied from general regime knowledge, not per-stock primary verification) covers the full 58-stock
301   panel, and 42 stocks pass the n ≥ 20 valid-point filter (series lengths 20–77 yr). The release
302   records stock and assessment metadata and series such as biomass, fishing mortality or exploita-
303   tion rate, total allowable catch, catch advice, and effort. It does not encode controller sign,
304   the decision rule, or dated decision and deployment queues. The annual-review designation is
305   therefore a criterion applied in this analysis, not a database classification of a stock as extractive
306   or protective.
307       For each eligible biomass (spawning-stock biomass) series, the analysis removes a linear
308   trend, computes a Lomb–Scargle periodogram (Lomb, 1976; Scargle, 1982), normalises it to
309   unit total power, and integrates power in prespecified bands: target bands A (2.5–5 yr, an-
310   chovy class) and B (5–9 yr, sprat class), bracketing the observable-specific dominant biomass
311   peaks near 4 and 8 yr of the archived stage-map diagnostics (provisional records, Section 3.3),
312   with contextual bands C (9–14 yr, cohort/recruitment) and D (14–30 yr, trend/regime). No
313   multiplier-crossing verdict depends on the archived diagnostics. The tested statistic is band-
314   integrated power, compared with a per-series AR(1) red-noise null (coeﬀicient by lag-1 autocor-
315   relation of the linearly detrended series; 200 replicates, seed 7, detrending inside each replicate;
316   Lomb-Scargle on 1,500 frequencies from 1/(2 · span) to 0.5 yr−1 ). Empirical p-values over the 84
317   target-band cells (42 stocks by bands A and B) are adjusted by the Benjamini–Hochberg false-
318   discovery-rate procedure (Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995), which controls the false discovery
319   rate, not the familywise error rate. Dependence across stocks (shared climate forcing, shared
320   assessment methods) is acknowledged (Benjamini and Yekutieli, 2001). The reported zero count
321   is the BH-adjusted result. A peak is classified as robust only if it survives the null comparison

326   2.5 Power analysis
327   Power experiments inject the model-generated effort signal into white noise (multiplicative log-

334   2.6 The structured case search
335   The case search is a curated inventory, not a systematic review. It considered more than thirty
336   systems spanning fisheries, aquaculture, groundwater, surface water, rangeland, wildlife harvest,
337   forestry, and produced-capital markets, under four criteria:

338     (i) a responsive institutional feedback rather than a one-time cap or ban;
339    (ii) an independently dateable response or implementation lag;
340   (iii) no major environmental, biological, or structural driver empirically supported as gen-
341         erating the same outcome, where the comparative rule is that a candidate institutional
342         mechanism must outperform pre-registered alternatives on held-out prediction, phase or-
343         dering, or intervention response (since “capable of generating” is not a falsifiable exclusion
344         in open ecological systems);
345   (iv) individual-resource or individual-station data rather than an aggregate series.

346       Behind criterion (iii) is an explicit list of the alternative mechanisms that can mimic or
347   replace an institutional cycle: stage/maturation delays and cohort resonance; recruitment
348   suppression versus stock culling; support-pool/nutrient limitation; pollution/waste feedback;
349   predator–prey or climatic forcing; and direct abiotic mining and slow-store exhaustion. The
350   rule is that a candidate institutional mechanism must be compared with these alternatives
351   rather than identified from periodicity alone. No system in the searched set meets all four
352   criteria; the case-screening table (32 systems) and query log are recorded in Supplementary S4.

456   3.2 The rapid-review limit and what it does not establish
457   Define u = (N, Z, E) and the continuous no-delay system u̇ = G(u) assembled from Eqs. (1)–
458   (2) with the effort law evaluated continuously. Suppose G is continuously differentiable with
459   bounded derivative on a compact neighbourhood containing the compared trajectories on [0, T ],
460   assessments equal the contemporaneous state, no additional decision or deployment queue is
461   present, projection is inactive, and the sampled and continuous systems share the initial state.
462   The frozen-effort flow followed by the explicit effort step then has a one-step defect of order
463   O(Tr2 ) relative to the exact flow of G. The usual discrete Gronwall estimate yields an O(Tr )
464   review-time error and uniform convergence on every fixed finite horizon as Tr → 0 (the sampled-
465   data approximation framework of Nešić and Teel, 2004).
466        This is a numerical-approximation property, not a finite-review stability theorem. It gives
467   no uniform-in-time estimate, does not preserve stability automatically, and does not control
468   any trajectory-classified response region reported below. It excludes active projection, delayed
469   or erroneous assessment, a deployment queue, and stochastic updates. In particular, it nei-
470   ther identifies a finite Tr with a continuous discrete delay nor implies that suﬀiciently small
471   positive Tr is stable when the continuous target is unstable. The converse caution is equally
472   important. Under the additional assumptions of hyperbolicity of the continuous equilibrium
473   and C 1 -consistency of the sampled map with projection inactive locally, the multipliers sat-
474   isfy µj (Tr ) = 1 + Tr λj (A) + O(Tr2 ) (Stuart and Humphries, 1996), so local stability persists
475   for all suﬀiciently small positive review intervals provided every continuous eigenvalue satisfies
476   ℜλj < 0; if any ℜλj > 0, the sampled equilibrium inherits instability for suﬀiciently small
477   Tr . The finite-horizon result therefore neither proves nor refutes stability transfer; the transfer
478   hypotheses are what any such claim would have to supply. The institutional reading is that
479   “rapid review” is not a stability guarantee — a governance loop that reviews frequently approx-
480   imates a continuous controller only on finite horizons, and the question of whether short review
481   intervals stabilise or destabilise the equilibrium is decided by the spectrum of the map, not by
482   the rapid-review limit.

933   sult. The reliable contradiction is repeated direction reversal: convergence toward K can be
934   arbitrarily slow near degeneracy, so failure to reach an unspecified biomass by an unspecified
935   deadline is not a contradiction. And the obstruction must be separated from rejection under
936   measurement error, process noise, age structure, migration, time-varying mortality, and state-
937   space observation models — the incompatibility is a property of the exact trajectory class,
938   nothing broader.
939       The subsequent record does not close the second window. Rose (2026), comparing two
940   surplus-production reconstructions spanning 1983–2023 (Rose and Walters, 2019; DFO, 2024),
941   documents that surplus production and stock growth stalled after 2015, with some years nega-
942   tive, and that the stall-point biomass remains controversial. Structural-equation analyses in the
943   same review attribute the production deficit to capelin limitation and harp seal predation rather
944   than fishing alone. For the present analysis the key point is the persistence of the non-recovery:
945   a decade after the rebound reported by Rose and Rowe (2015), the post-2015 production stall
946   is inconsistent with the simplest fixed-parameter surplus-production interpretation considered
947   here, which is precisely what the split’s second window — a second identification problem not
948   resolved by the mortality-allocation comparison — states. Read through Lemma 2.2(ii), the
949   seal-predation attribution carries a directional consequence: to the extent predation acts as
950   additional mortality on the stock, the effective threshold facing recovery lies above s, so pre-
951   dation raises the threshold for re-establishment rather than merely slowing convergence. No
952   predation-mortality rate is estimated here; the lemma is used for the direction of the effect, not
953   its magnitude.

961   4 Discussion
962   4.1 Architecture substitution: what changes when the operator changes
963   The operator-spectra results of Section 3.4 are the paper’s core methodological finding. Stability
964   windows are operator-specific: they do not transfer across review-map operators. The same
965   feedback loop — surplus production, an institutional deficit signal, an effort law — carries an
966   archived, unreproduced instability record near 3–4 yr of review under the stage-structured map,
967   convergence over a 1–20 yr grid under that map’s cod-class parameterisation, and instability
968   at annual review with a complex crossing near 6.5 yr under the exact logistic hold map (full
969   records, the Euler command-step artefact, and the q-sensitivity caveat in Section 3.4 and its
970   Reading; the plant–operator confound is stated alongside and the operator effect is not claimed
971   isolated).
972       The complete crossing record sharpens the operator finding: on one plant the exact and
973   Euler updates disagree on the number and location of crossings (Section 3.4). The review
974   cadence is a local spectral design parameter, and the command step is a second, independent
975   one. None of these statements can be obtained from the continuous-delay equation, and none
976   transfers to it. Any empirical or theoretical claim about institutional feedback must therefore
977   state its operator: a stability window located under continuous delay is not a prediction about
978   an institution that reviews periodically, and an annual-review result does not generalise to
979   multi-year plans. The governance architecture itself is a testable component of the hypothesis,
980   not a re-parameterisation detail.

981   4.2 What the null does and does not establish
982   The 42-stock screen returns a spectral null, and its value lies in what it refutes: the claim, im-
983   plicit in treating periodicity as evidence, that robust target-band cycles are common in assessed-
984   stock records. It does not establish the contrary. With anchovy-class power between 0.02 and
985   0.14 across tested noise and record-length regimes, the screen cannot adjudicate the extractive
986   mechanism for that class; the sprat-class result is informative only in favourable regimes; and
987   no screen of retrospective series can compare controller signs, because the sign is not in the
988   data. This is the same null discipline that the skeptical re-analysis of the Russell Cycle applied
989   to climate-forced cycles (McManus, Licandro, and Coombs, 2016), transferred to governance-
990   feedback claims: a diagnostic is not a causal claim, and no accumulation of diagnostics converts
991   to causal evidence.

992   4.3 The cod case at its exact status
993   The cod case contributes a descriptive partition, not a mechanism: crash-window mortality
994   is formulation-dependent (Section 3.8), and the post-collapse record is not resolved by either



                                                      25
 995   mortality-allocation formulation, with the post-2015 production stall persisting under inde-
 996   pendent reconstructions (Rose, 2026). The obstruction mathematics applies within the scope
 997   stated in Sections 3.8 and 4.7(vii). What the case supplies is a falsification benchmark: a well-
 998   documented collapse-and-stall record against which prospective designs can be powered and
 999   scored.

1000   4.4 A falsification standard for institutional-feedback claims
1001   The model family gains empirical content by specifying the outcomes that count against its
1002   mechanism and parameterisation. Five outcomes:
1003     1. If independently measured implementation and deployment lags do not covary with the
1004        instability bands predicted under fitted resource parameters, the quantitative delay claim
1005        is weakened.
1006     2. If an observed oscillation remains after climate, cohort, and regime forcing are modelled,
1007        but its phase relation between decline signal, effort, and biomass contradicts the con-
1008        troller’s causal ordering, the mechanism is rejected for that case.
1009     3. If sampled-data analysis removes a continuous-delay band under realistic review rules, the
1010        continuous model cannot be used for that institution.
1011     4. If a protective controller dominates the extractive controller across uncertainty without
1012        inducing the claimed volatility, no anti-regulation conclusion may be drawn.
1013     5. If realistic record lengths have low power for the predicted signal, field spectral nulls cannot
1014        adjudicate the mechanism; prospective or experimental evidence is required. The Icelandic
1015        cod case illustrates the bind: its post-1995 variability (coeﬀicient of variation 0.37–0.39; no
1016        dominant spectral peak, Supplementary S11) overlaps the continuous-delay band (9.9–20.3
1017        yr) in timescale yet carries no institutional signature, because a competing environmental
1018        driver is empirically supported for the same outcome (criterion (iii); Supplementary S4).

1019   4.5 Prospective designs
1020   The retrospective evidence does not identify the institutional mechanism; the constructive con-
1021   tent of this paper is the programme that could. Retrospective evidence can still discipline the
1022   mechanism where a prespecified contrast exists: the Tr -ranked cross-sectional test across the
1023   42-stock cohort is executed in Supplementary S10 and returns a controlled null — no window-
1024   approach gradient, with Tr ≈ 1 dormancy holding at 0/42 and 1/42 flags in the two bands. Five
1025   designs are specified as preregistration targets; none has been executed, and each is intended
1026   for preregistration before outcome inspection — no registration identifier or archived protocol
1027   exists yet, and none is claimed.
1028       Governance-event panels. For each resource–jurisdiction unit, a source-linked event
1029   record dates each stage from raw observation to ecological response, with date uncertainty kept
1030   distinct rather than collapsed to one lag (full specification in Supplementary S13.3). The cod
1031   case supplies two dated decision events — the moratorium announcement of 2 July 1992 and the
1032   reopening of 26 June 2024 with an 18 kt total allowable catch — with two negative constraints:
1033   no governance lead is inferred from annual biomass data, and a fast response does not establish
1034   adequacy.
1035       Quasi-experimental timing. Event-study, difference-in-differences, synthetic-control,
1036   interrupted-time-series, or state-space intervention designs on schedule variation plausibly
1037   independent of the outcome innovation, with preregistered estimands (full specification in
1038   Supplementary S13.3). Two coding rules are binding: a change in implementation lag is not
1039   coded as a change in Tr , and a nominal schedule change is not a treatment unless it changes a
1040   responsive decision opportunity.
1041       Out-of-sample mechanism comparison and the displacement discipline. Held-
1042   out comparison across environmental-forcing, cohort-resonance, institutional-delay (specified

                                                       26
1043   controller sign), combined, and null time-series models, with frozen splits and declared outcomes
1044   (full specification in Supplementary S13.3). The displacement rule: a delay explanation is
1045   weakened when it cannot reproduce the pre-registered phase ordering, and displaced when a
1046   competitor predicts the held-out observations better.
1047       Controlled and randomized human-in-the-loop experiments. Randomised review
1048   cadence and feedback timing or sign in a common simulated renewable-resource environment,
1049   with the gain–phase signature as the diagnostic target (full specification in Supplementary
1050   S13.3). The randomisation described is a laboratory or advisory-interface design, not a field
1051   intervention on a live stock; mechanism-class precedent exists (Costantino, Cushing, Dennis,
1052   and Desharnais, 1995; Moxnes, 1998) but is not validation of the institutional equations.
1053       Closed-loop management strategy evaluation. Policy comparison in a closed loop
1054   keeping six uncertainty classes distinct (the management-procedure tradition: Punt and Dono-
1055   van, 2007), crossing review timing, delays, controller sign, observation error, parameter draws,
1056   operating-model class, and process-noise regime (full specification in Supplementary S13.3). At
1057   minimum, responsive extractive, responsive protective, and fixed-plan controllers are compared;
1058   a conclusion that compares only two review intervals inside the extractive class cannot be gener-
1059   alised to governance as a whole. Three wider empirical hypotheses of the programme’s general
1060   theory — observation aggregation, governance phase ordering, and substitution certificate —
1061   are adopted here only as motivation, and otherwise explicitly deferred: governance phase or-
1062   dering supplies the gain–phase diagnostic target the designs above inherit, while testing any
1063   of the three requires prospective, adequately powered designs this paper specifies but does not
1064   execute.

1075   4.7 Limitations
1076     (i) Diagnostics are not causal claims: the spectral null, the response regions, the power values,
1077         the case calculations, and the cod split carry their stated types and no more.
1078    (ii) The stage-structured response regions are exploratory finite-grid, trajectory-classified
1079         records pending complete stage registration and multiplier analysis; the logistic hold-map
1080         multiplier record is complete (Section 3.4), and the stage operator’s multiplier and tra-
1081         jectory record is supplied by the reconstruction of Section 3.4 — a newly specified object
1082         whose comparison with the archived windows is a consistency check, not a validation.
1083         No Poincaré-map multiplier classification is claimed for the archived stage-output values,
1084         and they are not reproducible numerical propositions until the original computational
1085         record is attached. The effort scale is uncalibrated: results are reported at imported
1086         catchability values, and reparameterising the controller in fishing mortality (F = qE) is
1087         the stated next step for the stage operator.
1088   (iii) The two review-map operators are distinct: statements computed on the hold map, on
1089         the stage-structured map, and on the continuous-delay equation do not transfer to one
1090         another.


                                                      27
1091     (iv) The screen is a selected-cohort consistency check whose power is high only in favourable
1092          noise and record-length regimes; the null is not proof of absence and adjudicates
1093          nothing about controller sign; the AR(1) null may understate low-frequency power. The
1094          eleven-variant battery of Supplementary S9.1 holds the zero count under ARMA(1,1),
1095          trend-stationary, block-bootstrap, detrending, and regime-shift nulls; the remaining
1096          masked-peak risk runs via overestimated null power (unmodelled observation-error or
1097          retrospective-bias variance raising the threshold), not the underestimation above.
1098      (v) The structured case search does not independently disconfirm the mechanism: finding no
1099          eligible cases under these criteria is not evidence against it.
1100     (vi) The cod case establishes a descriptive partition, and no mechanism is identified.
1101    (vii) The obstruction mathematics concerns exact trajectories of the fixed-parameter, fixed-

1103          age structure, migration, time-varying mortality, or state-space observation models.
1104   (viii) The social object is not operationalised: the human series is not assembled, the normative

1129       Beyond fisheries, the framework is reusable as a modelling discipline: it answers how periodic
1130   governance should be represented in ecological models, how the stages of the advice chain should
1131   be kept distinct, and how modellers can test whether a stability claim survives a change of
1132   decision operator.
1133       The logistic core is illustrative; the structural results are the operator-dependence of the
1134   stability verdict, the finite-horizon scope of the rapid-review limit, and the non-equivalence of
1135   one-step and exact updates as a caution within the studied class.
1136       Sample-and-hold architectures beyond fisheries face the same clock-mismatch failure mode:
1137   five-yearly climate pledges held between global stocktakes (UNFCCC, 2015) align a political
1138   review clock with ecological and environmental clocks only by design, not by default.

1139   5 Conclusion
1140   Periodic review is the operating architecture of fisheries governance, and it is not well represented
1141   by either of its standard formalisations. The sample-and-hold model specified here makes the
1142   governance clock an explicit object: seven time objects, a projected explicit controller, and a
1143   spectrum computed per operator rather than per loop. Its empirical layer contains one case
1144   whose positive content is a descriptive split — the northern cod two-window partition — and
1145   a falsification discipline for the rest: a selected-cohort screen with no target-band discoveries
1146   and stated power, a zero-count case search with its structural hypotheses, and a falsification
1147   standard with five outcomes that count against the mechanism. The mechanism is falsifiable,
1148   and the five prospective designs specify how it would be falsified, comparatively supported, or
1149   left unresolved. Until those designs are executed, the empirical layer stands as stated here: a
1150   well-posed mechanism, an exploratory computational record, limited screen power, a case search
1151   that does not disconfirm, and one descriptive split. The decision clock — the timing and form
1152   of policy revision — is therefore a design variable with stability consequences.

1197   table is not a licence-holder panel. A measured floor is a pair (Ik , ck ) — an instrument and a

1199   rule is that the arrangement fails as soon as any measured margin mk (G, t) = Ik (G, t)−ck is neg-
