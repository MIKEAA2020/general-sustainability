S2      Mathematical statements, proofs, and the review-map operator
S2.1     Forward invariance
Let the flow on a review interval be

                    Ṅ = rN (1 − N/K) − qE(t)N,                Ż = {Φ(qE(t)N − S(N )) − Z}/τm ,

where r, K, q, τm > 0, Φ ≥ 0, and E(t) ∈ [0, Emax ]. At each review, the effort command is projected onto
[0, Emax ]. Between reviews effort is either held at an admissible endpoint or interpolated convexly between
consecutive admissible commands.
Proposition S2.1 (Forward invariance of the sampled state space). If N (0) ≥ 0, Z(0) ≥ 0, and E0 ∈ [0, Emax ],
then every solution that exists remains in N ≥ 0, Z ≥ 0, and E ∈ [0, Emax ].
Proof. The effort statement follows from projection and convexity. For a fixed admissible effort path, the stock
equation is locally Lipschitz and has the factorised form Ṅ = N {r(1 − N/K) − qE(t)}. If N (t0 ) > 0, then

                                                     t0

if N (t0 ) = 0, uniqueness gives the zero solution. For the signal, variation of constants gives

These facts hold interval by interval. Projection places each next command in the same effort interval, completing
the induction. The logistic field also gives Ṅ ≤ 0 at N = K, so on a finite interval N (t) ≤ max{N (t0 ), K}. No
lower bound strictly above zero, persistence, viability, or stability follows from this proposition.

S2.2     Finite-horizon rapid-review consistency
Lemma S2.2 (Finite-horizon rapid-review consistency). Let u̇ = G(u), u = (N, Z, E), be the no-queue
continuous system, with the effort component evolving under the same local controller law. Suppose G is
continuously differentiable with bounded derivative on a compact neighbourhood of the trajectories over a
fixed horizon [0, T ]; assessments are contemporaneous and exact; projection is inactive on that neighbourhood;
and no deployment queue, stochastic jump, or unmodelled reset is added. Let ΨTr be either the exponential
frozen-assessment or forward-Euler flow-then-update map. Then, uniformly on the compact set,

                                         ΨTr (u) = u + Tr G(u) + O(Tr2 ),

and, for a common initial state, the sampled trajectory differs from the continuous trajectory by at most CT Tr at
review times with nTr ≤ T . This is a fixed finite-horizon statement; it does not imply that any fixed positive Tr
has the same stability classification as the continuous system.

Proof. Taylor expansion of the held ecological flow and endpoint effort update gives the displayed one-step
expansion. The exact flow of G has the same first-order expansion, so the local defect is O(Tr2 ). If L bounds the
Lipschitz constant of G, the global error ϵn = un − u(nTr ) satisfies

                                         ∥ϵn+1 ∥ ≤ (1 + LTr )∥ϵn ∥ + CTr2 .

Discrete Gronwall gives maxnTr ≤T ∥ϵn ∥ ≤ CT Tr , establishing the claimed uniform convergence at review times.
No infinite-horizon convergence or finite-interval stability result follows.

S2.3     Local stability and crossing conventions
                                                                               −
Let X ∗ = (N ∗ , Z ∗ , E ∗ ) be an interior fixed point of the pre-review map Xn+1 = PTr (Xn− ). The local multipliers
                                    ∗
are the eigenvalues of DPTr (X ). Local asymptotic stability requires all multipliers to have modulus below one.
A crossing is counted when a computed multiplier reaches the unit circle along the scanned interval branch. A
complex-pair crossing, a real +1 crossing, and a real −1 crossing are distinguished; the values in this paper are
numerical crossings refined by bisection and high-precision evaluation.
    The baseline values at Tr = 1 year are ρ = 1.0003506565 for exponential frozen-assessment mobilising,
1.0005452356 for forward-Euler mobilising, 0.9838095183 for exponential frozen-assessment protective, and
0.9837962340 for forward-Euler protective. The exponential-update mobilising crossing is a complex pair with
eigenvalue approximately 0.98464047 ± 0.17459425i, modulus one, and angle 0.175494 radians. Forward-Euler
mobilising crosses as a complex pair at 47.535982 years and at a real multiplier −1 at 79.142688 years. Forward-
Euler protective crosses at a real multiplier −1 at 2.306354 years. The exponential protective update has no
crossing on the tested [0.2, 200] interval and has maximum sampled radius 0.996740. The forward-Euler crossings
are properties of that distinct command update, not numerical estimates of the exponential-update boundary.

S4      Structured case-search record
S4.1     Eligibility criteria and adjudication rule
The inventory contains 32 candidate systems. It is a curated query-driven search, not a systematic review. A
candidate was eligible only if all four criteria were supported by evidence:
 1. an operative, repeatedly responsive governance rule (not a one-time cap, ban, fixed path, coordination
    deadlock, or nominal rule with no effective response);
 2. an independently dateable response or implementation lag at the scale relevant to the ecological outcome;
 3. no competing ecological or structural driver supported for that same outcome, or comparative evidence that
    institutional feedback predicts the outcome better than prespecified competing mechanisms; and
 4. individual-resource or station-level observations adequate to order the response signal, decision, implementa-
    tion, and ecological effect.
The third condition is not satisfied merely because an institutional mechanism is plausible. If ecology, recruitment,
climate, predation, cohort resonance, or assessment structure is supported for the same outcome and no
discriminating comparison exists, the case is coded as confounded or unresolved. “Yes” in the table means
that the available record supports the item; “no” means the criterion fails; a dot denotes no determination or
inapplicability; “partial” denotes incomplete evidence. The binding-fail column is the recorded failure, not a
composite score.
    Twenty cases are coded as having a responsive rule, with one additional case coded partial; 21 rows record
criterion (iii) as unmet. Two produced-capital examples are out of the regenerating-resource scope. No row
satisfies all four criteria simultaneously. The closest candidates—Bangkok groundwater and La Mancha Oriental
groundwater—remain confounded by subsidence/rainfall and crop/water substitution, respectively. These
decisions should be read as limits of this inventory, not as proof that no qualifying system exists.

S5      Ecosystem context, proxy risks, and case-level audits
S5.1     Peru and Chile: a plausible confound is not an identified cause
The seven stocks assigned Tr = 0.5 years in the ranked test are Peruvian or Chilean anchovy stocks with annual
quotas and group-described in-season responsive closures. The code does not verify each stock’s specific closure
trigger, decision date, or state-assessment cadence. The shorter proxy is therefore a regional group classification.
It is not a measured stock-specific treatment.
    A retained anchoveta catch series and prior analysis notes associate a roughly 3.7-year feature with ENSO-
related environmental variation. That association motivates a confound check, but the v53 ranked test does not
re-estimate a causal ENSO model on a fully verified primary-source governance panel. The per-stock AR(1)
surrogate reproduces fitted red-noise persistence but not quasi-periodic forcing. Consequently, the observed
negative Tr -rank association and the higher two-group null-excess in Peru/Chile are compatible with regional
forcing, cohort composition, assessment differences, or other omitted factors; they do not establish which factor
produced the contrast. The correct result remains: no identifiable approach gradient, plus an opposite-direction
proxy-group association with unresolved confounding.
    This matters because ENSO and longer regime shifts can structure anchoveta abundance, and oceanographic
change has been documented off Peru (Chávez et al., 2003; Gutiérrez et al., 2009). Those references provide
ecological context, not an instrumental variable or proof that ENSO caused the v53 contrast.

S5.2     Icelandic cod and haddock: numerical audit of an earlier forensics note
A recovered cod/haddock audit used current-vintage ICES standard-graph source-data tables, covering cod
1955–2026 and haddock 1979–2026. For 1996–2023, cod SSB has n = 28, CV 0.394, catch CV 0.150, fishing-
mortality CV 0.199, and recruitment CV 0.208. The previously archived cod CV of 0.387 is confirmed as the
same post-1995 concept under an earlier vintage; the current-vintage value is 0.394. For haddock over the same
years, SSB CV is 0.359 and recruitment CV is 0.894. The earlier value 0.143 for haddock is not reproduced
under standard SSB-CV definitions: detrended-residual CV is 0.345, log-difference volatility is 0.171, and the
observed SSB CV is 0.359. That number is therefore not used in v53.
    A post-1995 detrended cod spectrum has its strongest peak near 19.58 years; the 10–15-year band contains
14.9% of the power under the retained calculation. The peak is not a stable, narrow institutional signature
and the series is trend-dominated. The numerical audit confirms the earlier coding note’s broad environmen-
tal/recruitment confound, but neither this CV comparison nor the spectral summary identifies an HCR effect.
The haddock recruitment variability result is descriptive and does not adjudicate the governor’s review cycle.

S5.4     Model-class diagnostic for scalar autonomous surplus production
A simple scalar autonomous surplus-production equation with constant removals has the form
                                                                r
                               Ṡ = f (S) − C,      f (S) =        (K − S)(S − s),
                                                              K −s
where 0 < s < K, C ≥ 0, and S is the modeled stock. The phase-line shape of f limits the equilibrium
structure. If C > 0, the vector field at zero is −C, so the state requires an explicit nonnegativity constraint or a
different low-stock removal rule. For extra mortality Mx ≥ 0, write fM (S) = f (S) − Mx S = S(h(S) − Mx ) with
h(S) = f (S)/S. The zero equilibrium remains; positive equilibria solve h(S) = Mx . When 0 < Mx < max h,
the quadratic h can have two positive roots, including a lower threshold shifted above s; at the maximum the
roots coalesce and above it positive equilibria disappear.
    This is a model-class observation, not an empirical test of northern cod. The assessment series is not an
error-free trajectory and removals and mortality are not constant, so no contradiction between this scalar model
and the real stock is claimed. The lemma says what this class would imply if its assumptions held; it does not
establish those assumptions.

S5.5     Interpretive guardrails
The contexts above are used to motivate competing mechanisms and the need for discriminating data. They
do not turn the spectral screen, the review-interval proxy, a coefficient of variation, or a visual event sequence
into evidence of institutional causation. The classification of an environmental mechanism as “plausible” is
intentionally weaker than a causal finding.

S7      Prospective empirical programme and falsification rules
The designs below are proposals, not completed tests. No registration identifier, locked protocol, or field
intervention is claimed. Each should be registered before outcome inspection, with data provenance, estimand,
decision rule, and deviations recorded.

S7.1     Design 1: governance-event panel
Unit and data. Resource–jurisdiction–assessment cycle. Link dated raw observation, assessment data freeze,
advice release, committee decision, legal instrument, implementation, compliance, and ecological response.
Record date uncertainty and source document for each event. Separate Tobs , Tassess , Tr , τdec , τdep , and τeco ; do
not impute one “governance lag” from another.
   Estimand. Distribution of effective responsive-review intervals and the impulse/phase response of command,
extraction, and stock to a measured assessment innovation, conditional on operating-model class.
   Identification. The panel is descriptive until independent variation or a design-based assumption identifies
the effect. Use date ranges where records are incomplete; do not encode a scheduled annual TAC as a 0.5-year
response merely because in-season closures exist without trigger evidence.
   Falsification. A hypothesised sample-and-hold mechanism is weakened if decisions precede the information
they are said to respond to, if implementation does not follow the decision, or if the predicted command–stock
phase order fails on held-out cycles.

S7.2     Design 2: quasi-experimental schedule variation
Identify an external schedule change plausibly independent of current ecological innovations. Candidate
approaches include event studies, difference-in-differences, synthetic controls, interrupted time series, and
state-space interventions, selected according to the institutional change and comparison structure. Define
treatment as a change in responsive decision opportunities, not simply a change in implementation lag. A
nominal calendar reform is not a treatment if the same rule is still applied to the same information on the same
effective schedule.
    Estimand. Change in extraction and ecological outcomes attributable to a change in the review operator,
with pre-specified direct and lagged effects.
    Identification checks. Pre-trends, concurrent rule and data changes, ecological shocks, serial correlation,
spillovers, anticipatory behaviour, implementation/compliance, and measurement-vintage changes. Use placebo
dates and placebo stocks where feasible.
    Falsification. If estimated schedule variation is explained by an ecological shock or if outcomes move before
the schedule change, the causal interpretation is rejected or narrowed.

S7.3     Design 3: out-of-sample mechanism comparison
Fit competing models with distinct causal orderings: (i) ecological forcing only; (ii) cohort resonance or
recruitment structure; (iii) institutional feedback with a pre-specified rule sign and sample-and-hold operator;
(iv) combined ecology and governance; and (v) a non-periodic or regime-shift null. Freeze training and test
windows, transformations, priors, and scoring rules before fitting. Compare held-out log predictive density
or another proper score; report phase-order checks and posterior predictive discrepancies, not only in-sample
spectral fit.
    Estimand. Relative out-of-sample predictive performance of the institutional mechanism versus its alterna-
tives, and the estimated timing/gain parameters conditional on the winning model class.
    Falsification. The delay mechanism is weakened when it fails to predict held-out phase ordering or performs
worse than an ecological/cohort competitor. It is displaced for that case when the competitor predicts held-out
observations better under the pre-specified score. A significant spectrum by itself is not the decision rule.

S7.4     Design 4: randomized human-in-the-loop experiment
In a laboratory or advisory-interface setting, randomize review cadence and feedback sign or timing in a common
renewable-resource simulator. Participants receive a controlled stream of noisy observations and implement



                                                         12
decisions through the simulated institution. Randomize treatment assignment, mask the hypothesis where
possible, and measure state, command, decision latency, revisions, and errors. A live fishery is not randomized
by this proposal.
   Estimand. Intention-to-treat effects of assigned review cadence and rule architecture on stability, transient
stock loss, command volatility, and decision quality.
   Falsification. The proposed gain–phase signature is weakened if randomized cadence does not alter
outcomes in the predicted direction or if observed behavioural adaptation eliminates the modeled distinction.
Any laboratory result remains externally limited until field validation.

S7.5     Design 5: closed-loop management strategy evaluation
Construct an MSE with an operating-model ensemble that crosses review interval, decision lag, deployment
lag, observation error, rule gain and sign, stock productivity, age structure, environmental forcing, assessment
structure, compliance, and process-noise regime. Compare, at minimum, a responsive extractive rule, a
responsive protective rule, and a fixed-plan controller; keep catch/effort constraints and objectives identical
where meaningful. Use common random numbers across policies and report both local spectral margins and
long-run performance metrics.
    Estimands. Probability of breaching biological reference points, expected yield and variability, recovery
time, implementation burden, distributional indicators, and regret relative to an explicitly stated benchmark.
    Falsification. The result does not generalize beyond the MSE if rankings reverse across plausible operating
models or if the claimed stability signature does not predict the prespecified performance outcomes. Robustness
is reported across model classes, not just parameter draws from one operating model.

S7.6     Cross-cutting preregistration and displacement discipline
Every design should state whether it tests mechanism, prediction, or policy performance. It should lock data
vintage, response windows, rule coding, exclusions, the unit of analysis, multiplicity, uncertainty, and an
alternative-model set before outcomes are inspected. The cod case contributes two dated governance events
(moratorium announcement 2 July 1992; reopening 26 June 2024 with an 18 kt TAC), but these dates alone
do not supply a counterfactual or a governance lead. A fast ecological response does not establish adequacy;
annual SSB data do not establish a governance lag.
    Three broader hypotheses motivate, but are not tested by, the present work: (a) observation aggregation can
change the apparent feedback phase; (b) governance phase ordering can be more diagnostic than periodicity
alone; and (c) a substitution certificate can identify when continuous-delay and sampled-data models cease to
be interchangeable. Testing them requires the event-linked, high-frequency, independently varied data described
above.

S10       Recovered Tr -ranked test: procedure, output, and limits
S10.1        Procedure and estimands
The executable p7_empirical/tr_ranked_test.py implements a pre-specified cross-sectional test on RAM
v4.66 stocks. It uses the same series loader and spectral functions as the main stock screen: discard stocks with


                                                         18
fewer than 20 non-missing annual SSB observations; linearly detrend; compute a normalized Lomb–Scargle
spectrum on the declared frequency grid; integrate powers in bands A (2.5–5 years), B (5–9 years), C (9–14
years), and D (14–30 years); fit a per-stock AR(1) null; and estimate 95% thresholds from 200 simulations with
seed 7. The table retains A–D power but the ranked estimands below use bands A and B.
     For each stock, the code assigns Tr = 0.5 years to the seven Peru/Chile annual-quota/in-season-closure group
cases and Tr = 1.0 year to 35 annual-TAC proxy cases. The 4-year anchovy-class and 8-year sprat-class windows
are both above either assigned value. The South African OMP’s 4–5-year rule review is explicitly excluded from
Tr ; implementation lag is not Tr . The assignments are group-coded and not individually primary-verified.
     The prespecified null-excess estimand is band power divided by that stock’s AR(1) 95% threshold, A/A95 or
B/B95 . Raw power is reported only as a confound diagnostic. Test 1 is Spearman association of the assigned Tr
rank with each null-excess ratio; Test 2 is a two-sided Mann–Whitney comparison of the 0.5- and 1-year groups
in band A, on raw power and null-excess; Test 3 counts nominal 95% threshold flags by broad stock class. The
reported p-values are nominal and do not adjust for the several reported contrasts. Spearman has only two
distinct Tr values and extensive ties; it is not a continuous dose-response test.

S10.2     Results and the corrected interpretation
The output contains 42 stocks: 7 at the subannual 0.5-year proxy and 35 at the annual 1-year proxy. Both
groups are below the modeled 4- and 8-year windows. Therefore, this cohort has no observed proxy value near
or beyond either window, and it cannot identify how the response changes as Tr approaches a window. The
correct finding is no identifiable window-approach gradient.
    The script does reproduce opposite-direction two-group associations. Median band-A null-excess is 0.529 in
the 0.5-year group and 0.257 in the annual group (Mann–Whitney U = 200, p = 0.007). On raw band-A power
the medians are 0.193 and 0.069 (U = 219, nominal p < 0.001). Spearman association is negative for band-A
null-excess (ρ = −0.408, p = 0.007) and band-B null-excess (ρ = −0.340, p = 0.028); raw-power associations are
ρ = −0.509, p = 0.001 for band A and ρ = −0.393, p = 0.010 for band B. These signs are opposite a simple
prediction that excess rises as the review interval approaches the modeled response window: the shorter proxy
group is farther below both windows and has the larger observed ratios.
    These two-group associations are reproduced outputs, but they are not evidence of an institutional effect.
All seven shorter-interval observations are Peru/Chile cases; proxy intervals are group-level and unverified stock
by stock; other annual cases form the comparison group; regional ecology and data-generation differences are
not randomized. ENSO-related quasi-periodicity is a plausible candidate confound, not a cause established by
this test. AR(1) surrogates account for fitted red-noise persistence but do not remove quasi-periodic forcing. No
causal adjustment, region-stratified estimate, or multi-year responsive comparison is available here.
    At the nominal 95% threshold, there are 0/42 band-A flags and 1/42 band-B flag. By broad class, anchovy
has 0/18 A and 1/18 B flags; sprat 0/4 in both; herring 0/20 in both. These are not BH-adjusted discoveries from
a new test family. They agree with the main screen’s adjusted-zero result, but are not independent replications.

S14       Power record and unreproduced items
S14.1      Injected-signal power: 17 archived white-noise cells
The committed driver specifies a sampled-governance operating model, generates annual effort, adds multiplicative
observation error Etobs = Et exp(εt ), and tests target-band periodogram power against a per-series AR(1) red-
noise null. The null uses 120 simulations and seed 7. Each power cell has 50 trial seeds 0–49. White-noise
draws use εt ∼ N (0, σ 2 ), with σ ∈ {0.1, 0.3}. The analyzed record spans H years after burn-in; the cell-specific
integrator horizon is retained in the CSV.
    The model cells are: sprat class (r, g, Tr ) = (0.8, 2, 7) with target band 30–120 years; anchovy class (1.6, 1, 3)
with band 8–20 years; and a cod-labelled false-positive class (0.3, 5, 5) with band 8–20 years. The archived
17-cell design is not a complete Cartesian grid: sprat H = 400, σ = 0.1 is not among the specified cells. Fifteen
cell outputs are present in the recovered run log; the two cod H = 400 values were recovered from a follow-up
JSON. The complete driver previously stopped after 15/17 cells, so the consolidated table is an assembly of
archived outputs, not a claim of a successful full driver rerun.

S14.2     Northern-cod constrained-mortality formulation: not reproduced
The constrained-M formulation in earlier project notes proposes a mortality allocation that could distinguish
fishing from other losses. In v53, its underlying table, uncertainty structure, and complete executable calculation
were not recovered. The DFO assessment table used here reports SSB and natural mortality estimates, but
does not by itself identify the causes of mortality. Consequently, v53 does not report numerical constrained-M
estimates, attributed mortality shares, or a causal seal-versus-fishing decomposition. Those values must not be
reconstructed from the plotted series alone. The open item is to recover the original input table, document
the model definition and priors/constraints, and reproduce the calculation against alternative assessment
formulations before drawing a mortality-allocation conclusion.
