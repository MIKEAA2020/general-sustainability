several load-bearing positive bridge results are still **under-typed, over-stated, or proved with gaps**. If the goal is to elevate the mathematics toward a credible general theory of sustainability, the following flaws and internal inconsistencies should be addressed before the package is treated as closure.

---

## 1. Executive summary of the main problems

The most serious issues are:

1. **R02’s observation semantics are not fixed, and Prop. 3 is false as written.**  
   This is the flagship closed-loop bridge. The conservative-filter incompleteness witness uses an observation map that actually reveals the hidden mode, undermining the claimed obstruction.

2. **R02 proves existence only at the level of arbitrary selectors, while claiming computability and governance relevance.**  
   The record itself admits selector regularity is open, yet the theorem language says the policy is computable. That is an internal inconsistency.

3. **R01 contains a direct proof error: it uses an unbounded feedback while assuming compact controls.**  
   The endpoint-only false-positive theorem is likely true, but the proof as written violates its own control constraint.

4. **R09 overstates parts of the boundary theorem.**  
   In particular, the delay-instability witness proves only a local crossing as written, while the statement claims instability for all delay values beyond the critical threshold; and one of the local-to-global witnesses misstates the viability kernel.

5. **The packet claims compatibility with `TCS-1.0`, but several records already require proposed `TCS-1.1` types.**  
   R07’s specification path and the erosion-triple discipline are not first-class in `TCS-1.0`.

6. **Several “proved” bridges are really conditional certificate schemas, not constructive theories.**  
   In particular, R02 assumes a certificate family satisfying `(REG)`, but does not construct it; R03 classifies diagnostics but does not formalize the diagnostic functional `M`; R05 gives a restricted composition theorem but leaves the genuinely hard nonlinear/small-gain/shared-control problem open.

These problems do not destroy the programme. But they mean that the current claim of closure is premature. The packet is better described as **a strong partial closure with several critical repair obligations**.

# 2. Critical flaws in load-bearing results

## 2.1 R02: observation typing is unresolved, and Prop. 3 is internally inconsistent

### Location

`R02_T2_closed_loop_bridge.md`, especially:

- Field 3, Observation layer;
- Prop. 3, conservative incompleteness witness;
- Field 8, proof of Prop. 3.

### The issue

R02 defines a conservative observation map

\[
O^{co}_k(z,\theta)=z
\]

and then claims that a conservative, `z`-only filter is genuinely nonviable because the two hidden-mode branches decay according to

\[
z^+(k+1)+z^-(k+1)=z^+(k)+z^-(k)-2,
\]

so the sum becomes negative by \(t=3\).

But under the stated observation map, the numerical value of \(z\) can reveal the hidden mode.

From the prior

\[
C_0=\{(3,+1),(3,-1)\},
\]

if the first held command is \(u=+1\), then:

- for \(\theta=+1\): \(\dot z = (+1)(+1)-1=0\), so \(z\) remains \(3\);
- for \(\theta=-1\): \(\dot z = (-1)(+1)-1=-2\), so \(z\) falls to \(1\).

Thus at \(t_1\), observing \(z\) gives either \(3\) or \(1\), which distinguishes the two modes. The conservative compatible set then becomes a singleton, after which the mode-matched command is safe.

So the stated conservative filter is **not** forced to remain in the ambiguous pair state. The claimed common-action obstruction at

\[
C=\{(1,+1),(1,-1)\}
\]

is not reached under the stated observation semantics unless the observation deliberately suppresses the information contained in \(z\).

### Why this matters

This is not a minor arithmetic issue. Prop. 3 is supposed to establish that conservative assessment is sound but incomplete. It is one of the main conceptual pillars of R02 and is reused in the broader argument about observation, assessment, and implementation.

If the witness is invalid, then the claim

> conservative assessment is sound but incomplete

is not yet proved in the closed-loop setting.

### Deeper inconsistency

This exposes the same problem flagged in the master schema audit as `[GAP-4]`: observation typing is under-specified.

R02 says:

> deterministic set-valued observation map \(O:X\to \mathcal Y\) (closed values; the review-time observation is \(Y_k=O(x(t_k))\)).

But then the update uses

\[
O^{-1}(Y')
\]

as though \(Y'\) were a scalar observation value. If \(O\) is genuinely set-valued, then \(O(x)\) is a set, and the semantics of observing a set, a value, or a fibre need to be defined. If \(O\) is single-valued, “closed values” is misleading.

The record never defines what it means for one observation map to be a conservative coarsening of another. Without that, the comparison between exact and conservative filters is not well-typed.

### Repair direction

R02 should:

1. Define observation types precisely:
   - single-valued deterministic observations;
   - set-valued observations;
   - coarsened observations;
   - stochastic observation kernels, separately.

2. Define a conservative filter as an explicit over-approximation of the exact filter, e.g.

\[
C^{co}_k \supseteq C^{ex}_k
\]

along compatible observation histories.

3. Replace the Prop. 3 witness with one in which the conservative observation truly does not separate the hidden modes. For example, use a constant observation, or a coarse partition observation that maps both \((z,+1)\) and \((z,-1)\) to the same symbol for the relevant range of \(z\).

4. Remove the phrase “conservative processor trusts only \(z\)” unless the observation map actually preserves ambiguity.

Until this is fixed, R02 should not be treated as fully proved at the flagship level.

## 2.2 R02: theorem proves selector existence but claims computability

### Location

`R02`, Field 3, Theorem 1; Field 12.

### The issue

The theorem concludes:

> there is a causal, observation-history-based prescription policy \(\pi\) … and \(\pi\) is computable from \((C_k,c_k)\) alone.

But Field 12 says:

> Thm1’s policy is an arbitrary selector of the (REG)-witness correspondence (axiom-of-choice level).

These are not the same.

An arbitrary selector may exist set-theoretically, but it need not be:

- measurable;
- continuous;
- computable;
- implementable;
- robust to numerical approximation;
- stable under sampling.

For a governance theorem, this is a major gap.

### Why this matters

The whole point of the closed-loop bridge is to connect physical viability to institutional prescription and implementation. If the policy exists only by choice, then the bridge is mathematically valid in a weak sense but not operationally meaningful.

This is especially important because the master review presents R02 as a central interface theorem for governance.

### Internal inconsistency

The record simultaneously claims:

- a theorem-level causal policy exists;
- the policy is computable from the information state;
- selector regularity is an open obligation.

Those statements cannot all be true at the same strength.

### Repair direction

R02 should separate three results:

1. **Set-valued existence theorem**: there exists a selector of the `(REG)` witness correspondence.
2. **Measurable-policy theorem**: under measurable-selection hypotheses, there exists a measurable policy.
3. **Implementable-policy theorem**: under continuity/Lipschitz/computability hypotheses, there exists an implementable policy.

The current theorem should be demoted to the first unless the stronger regularity is proved.

## 2.3 R02: `(REG)` assumes the certificate rather than constructing it

### Location

`R02`, Field 3, regulation condition `(REG)`; Theorem 1.

### The issue

The theorem assumes a certificate family \(\mathcal V\) such that `(REG)` holds at every \((C,c)\in\mathcal V\).

But the hard part of a governance bridge is to show how such a \(\mathcal V\) is obtained from the system data.

As written, R02 says roughly:

> If there exists a certificate family satisfying the right recursive safety condition, then there is a safe closed loop.

That is true, but it is close to a restatement of the goal.

### Why this matters

For a general theory, one wants at least one of:

- a greatest certificate family;
- a fixed-point characterization;
- a backward recursion constructing \(\mathcal V\);
- an approximation scheme with certified error;
- a computability/selection theorem.

R02 gives none of these in full.

### Repair direction

Add a theorem defining the maximal certificate family, e.g. as a greatest fixed point of a monotone operator on information states. Then prove that `(REG)` holds on that family under suitable compactness/continuity assumptions.

Without that, the closed-loop bridge is conditional on an oracle-like certificate.

## 2.4 R02 Cor. 6: erosion bridge is not fully justified for the sampled institutional system

### Location

`R02`, Cor. 6.

### The issue

Cor. 6 imports the erosion lemma from corrected `02`:

\[
L_G r+\Delta_\epsilon\le \alpha
\]

and says the tube clause may be verified on an eroded set \(K_{-r}\).

But R02’s main theorem is a sampled, institutionally mediated, all-branches implementation theorem. The erosion lemma is a continuous-time strong-invariance result for a closed-loop envelope.

The missing bridge is:

- how the sampled held-action dynamics embed into the continuous envelope;
- how implementation branches are included in the envelope;
- how latency/deployment branches affect the margin;
- how the tube clause over an interval is verified by a boundary normal condition.

Cor. 6 asserts this by clause-level match, but the match is not fully established inside R02.

### Why this matters

Erosion is one of the main quantitative tools in the packet. If it is imported without a proper sampled-data bridge, then the quantitative safety margins used in diagnostics and composition may be unsound.

### Repair direction

State a separate sampled-data erosion theorem:

- held action set compact;
- disturbances bounded;
- flow Lipschitz;
- envelope over all implementation branches;
- proximal-normal margin on \(\partial K_{-r}\);
- maximum sampling interval constraint.

Then Cor. 6 can cite that theorem rather than relying on a continuous-time lemma alone.

## 2.5 R01: proof uses an unbounded control while assuming compact controls

### Location

`R01_T1_operator_II_false_positives.md`, Field 8, proof of R01.Thm1.

### The issue

The witness assumes

\[
U=[-1,1].
\]

In Step 1, the proof claims \(W_1=\mathbb R\) by choosing the rule

\[
u\equiv -x.
\]

But if \(x\notin[-1,1]\), then \(u=-x\notin U\). Therefore the argument violates the declared compact control set.

### Why this matters

The theorem explicitly assumes compact action sets. A proof that uses an unbounded feedback is invalid.

The endpoint-only false-positive result is still plausible, but the proof needs repair.

### Repair options

1. **Make the terminal set nonbinding**: set \(G=\mathbb R\). Then \(W_1=\mathbb R\) trivially, and no unbounded control is needed.

2. **Restrict the state space** to a bounded region where \(u=-x\) is admissible, and adjust the statement accordingly.

3. **Enlarge \(U\)** and drop compactness for that witness, but then the assumptions must be changed.

The cleanest fix is probably to take \(G=\mathbb R\) or otherwise make the successor clause void without using an inadmissible feedback.

### Consequence

R01.Thm1 should be marked “proved after proof repair” rather than accepted verbatim.

## 2.6 R09: the delay-instability witness proves less than the statement

### Location

`R09`, Field 3, Part M1; Field 8, proof of M1.

### The issue

Field 3 states:

\[
\dot x(t)=-x(t-\tau)
\]

is unstable for every \(\tau>\pi/2\).

Field 8 proves that at \(\tau=\pi/2\), a characteristic root crosses into the right half-plane with positive speed:

\[
\frac{d\lambda}{d\tau}
=
-\frac{\lambda^2}{1+\tau\lambda},
\]

and at \((\lambda,\tau)=(i,\pi/2)\),

\[
\operatorname{Re}\frac{d\lambda}{d\tau}>0.
\]

This proves instability on some interval \((\pi/2,\pi/2+\delta)\). It does not, by itself, prove instability for every \(\tau>\pi/2\).

To prove the global claim, one must also show there is no later restabilization, or cite a known theorem for the scalar delay equation.

### Why this matters

R09 is the boundary theorem. Its independence witnesses must be airtight. If one witness only proves local instability, then the corresponding independence claim is over-stated.

### Repair direction

Either:

1. weaken the statement to “unstable for \(\tau\in(\pi/2,\pi/2+\delta)\)”; or
2. add a complete argument showing that all crossings move roots into the right half-plane and no restabilization occurs; or
3. cite and verify the standard RFDE stability-switch theorem for \(\dot x(t)=-x(t-\tau)\).

A self-contained proof is possible by analyzing imaginary-axis crossings:

\[
\lambda+e^{-\lambda\tau}=0.
\]

If \(\lambda=i\omega\), then \(|\omega|=1\), and crossings occur at discrete delay values. One can then check the crossing direction at each crossing. But this is not currently in the record.

## 2.7 R09: M5 contains a misstated viability kernel

### Location

`R09`, Field 8, Part M5.

### The issue

The record gives as an opposite-direction example:

\[
\dot x=1
\]

on

\[
K=[0,1],
\]

and says the kernel collapses to \(\{1\}\), then adds “in fact empty of interior”.

But the robust viability kernel is not \(\{1\}\). It is empty.

At \(x=1\), the velocity \(+1\) points outward. At any \(x<1\), the trajectory reaches \(1\) in finite time and then exits. No point in \([0,1]\) supports an infinite-horizon safe trajectory.

### Why this matters

This is a small but real mathematical error in the boundary theorem’s independence register. The independence point can be preserved, but the example must be corrected.

### Repair direction

Use a cleaner no-fold/kernel-change example, for instance:

\[
\dot x=\mu,\qquad K=[0,\infty).
\]

Then:

- for \(\mu\ge 0\), the kernel is all of \(K\);
- for \(\mu<0\), the kernel is empty.

There is no fold bifurcation in the usual equilibrium sense, but the viability kernel changes drastically.

Alternatively, keep \(\dot x=1\) on \([0,1]\) but state correctly that the kernel is empty.

## 2.8 R09: Part U’s completeness claim is too strong

### Location

`R09`, Field 3, Part U; Field 8.

### The issue

Part U claims to give the exact universal consequences of the typed axioms. Field 8 says:

> No new mathematics; the theorem’s content is that this list is complete for the programme’s universal claims.

But completeness is only relative to the programme’s enumerated candidate claims. It is not a formal completeness theorem over all possible statements expressible in some language.

### Why this matters

The phrase “exactly universal” is too strong unless one specifies a formal language and proves that no other formula is axiomatically entailed. The record does not do that.

### Repair direction

Reword the claim as:

> Relative to the declared inventory of candidate universal sustainability claims, the universal consequences of the axioms are exactly U1–U5; every candidate predictive mechanism in the inventory is refuted by an independence witness in Part M.

That preserves the intended boundary function without overclaiming formal completeness.

## 2.9 R09: U4 mixes object-level and meta-level laws

### Location

`R09`, Part U4.

### The issue

U4 says:

> no integration step strengthens evidence status; no cross-module transfer without an admitted mapping + contract.

This is a rule about theorem records, proof status, and publication discipline. It is not a dynamical property of an instantiation \(\mathfrak A_q\).

But Part U is introduced as:

> Every instantiation satisfies U1–U5.

An instantiation of a sustainability system does not literally “satisfy” a theorem-record status rule.

### Why this matters

Mixing object-level system laws with meta-level proof-discipline laws weakens the boundary theorem’s logical clarity.

### Repair direction

Split Part U into:

1. **Object-level universal conditional laws**: conservation, viability monotonicity, noncompensation, kernel recursion exactness.
2. **Meta-theoretic discipline laws**: status monotonicity, interface explicitness, mapping contracts.

Both are important, but they belong to different logical levels.

# 3. Major mathematical gaps and internal tensions

---

## 3.1 Observation/filter calculus remains underdeveloped

The packet repeatedly relies on observation, information states, compatible sets, exact filters, and conservative filters. But the mathematical theory of these objects is not fully developed.

Missing items include:

1. A typed definition of observation maps:
   - single-valued;
   - set-valued;
   - quotient/coarsened;
   - stochastic kernels.

2. A definition of observation morphisms:
   - exact observation;
   - conservative observation;
   - lossy observation;
   - delayed observation.

3. A fibre criterion for when observation preserves safety information.

4. A theorem relating exact and conservative compatible sets:

\[
B^{ex}_k\subseteq B^{co}_k
\]

under explicit coarsening hypotheses.

5. A detectability or separability condition under which conservative filters become complete.

This gap directly affects:

- R02;
- R08;
- R04’s policy/information correspondence;
- R09 M4 and M6.

### Programme implication

A general sustainability theory needs an epistemic layer. The current packet has useful examples but not yet a general observation theory.

---

## 3.2 Selector and implementation regularity is a systemic weakness

The packet repeatedly allows arbitrary selectors:

- R02 policy selector;
- R03 Lem4 policy selector;
- R07 generation policy selector;
- corrected `04`-style predecessor witnesses.

This is acceptable for pure existence results, but not for governance.

A sustainability governance theory eventually needs:

- measurable selectors;
- continuous selectors;
- computable selectors;
- robust selectors;
- sample-and-hold implementable selectors;
- institutionally admissible selectors.

The packet flags this as open obligation D2, but many theorem statements still read as though implementation is automatic.

### Internal tension

The records say:

- “policy exists”;
- “policy is computable”;
- “selector regularity is open”.

These need to be separated.

### Programme implication

Without selector regularity, the theory can certify abstract viability but cannot guarantee implementable governance. That is a major gap for sustainability applications.

---

## 3.3 Strategic implementation is excluded by assumption, but sustainability institutions are strategic

The master audit’s `[CIRC-3]` requires the implementation map \(\mathsf I\) to be declared non-strategically.

This avoids circularity, which is good. But it also excludes a central feature of real institutions: strategic compliance.

If agents respond strategically to prescriptions, then the implementation map may depend on the policy. The current theory declares that out of scope.

### Why this matters

Many sustainability failures are not caused by lack of knowledge but by strategic noncompliance, regulatory capture, or incentive misalignment. A general sustainability theory will eventually need a game-theoretic implementation layer.

### Repair direction

Create a separate docket for strategic implementation:

- implementation as a game;
- equilibrium solution concept;
- mechanism design;
- compliance contracts;
- institutional viability under strategic response.

This should not be folded silently into the current non-strategic judgment family.

---

## 3.4 Erosion constants are often implicit

Several records use erosion conditions of the form

\[
L_G r+\Delta_\epsilon\le \alpha,
\]

or

\[
(L_G c+C)\epsilon+\mu\le \alpha,
\]

but do not always produce an explicit constant \(c\).

For a quantitative theorem, one should state:

\[
c = \frac{\alpha-\mu}{L_G\epsilon} - \frac{C}{L_G}
\]

when \(L_G>0\), or provide a uniform choice over an error range \(\epsilon\le \epsilon_*\), e.g.

\[
c
=
\frac{\alpha-\mu}{L_G\epsilon_*}-\frac{C}{L_G}.
\]

Otherwise the result is not fully quantitative.

### Additional issue

Erosion requires regularity:

- positive reach;
- \(C^{1,1}\) signed distance;
- tubular neighbourhoods;
- normal correspondence.

Many sustainability safe sets are:

- nonsmooth;
- union-of-regimes;
- hybrid;
- state-constrained;
- discontinuous at thresholds.

The packet correctly rejects arbitrary closed-set erosion, but that means the erosion machinery has limited applicability unless extended.

### Programme implication

The packet needs either:

1. a nonsmooth/prox-regular erosion theory; or
2. an explicit statement that quantitative erosion results apply only to regular safe sets.

---

## 3.5 Forward completeness and confinement certificates are still not first-class

The master audit identifies `[GAP-2]`:

> Forward-completeness / confinement certificate is not a typed field.

Several records assume linear growth, compactness, or confinement, but the packet does not consistently record the certificate.

Examples:

- R02 assumes linear growth and compact \(U,D\);
- R03 Lem4 requires compact state space or compact enclosure;
- R07 Thm5 requires compact confinement;
- R05 assumes linear growth of module envelopes.

This is better than nothing, but the programme needs a uniform field:

\[
\texttt{confinement\_certificate}
\in
\{\texttt{forward\_complete},\texttt{compact\_enclosure},\texttt{none}\}.
\]

Otherwise infinite-horizon claims may silently depend on unrecorded assumptions.

## 3.6 R05: composition theorem is useful but assumptions need tightening

R05 is one of the stronger mathematical contributions, but it needs several repairs.

### 3.6.1 Interface sensitivity must be Hausdorff-typed

The record states:

\[
\|F_i(x_i,z,d)-F_i(x_i,z',d)\|
\le
\Lambda_i\|z-z'\|.
\]

But \(F_i\) is set-valued. The norm must be defined as a Hausdorff distance, or the assumption must be rewritten elementwise:

\[
\forall v\in F_i(x_i,z,d)\ \exists w\in F_i(x_i,z',d):
\|v-w\|\le \Lambda_i\|z-z'\|.
\]

The proof uses this elementwise consequence, but the hypothesis does not state it clearly.

### 3.6.2 The gain operator assumes \(L_i>0\)

Cor. 3 defines

\[
\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(L_i)^{-1}M.
\]

If some \(L_i=0\), the inverse is undefined. This can happen if a module’s margin does not degrade with erosion depth.

The record should either assume \(L_i>0\) or handle zero entries separately.

### 3.6.3 Linear tightening bounds need caps

The assumption

\[
\delta_{ij}(r)\le \delta_{ij}(0)-\sigma_{ij}r
\]

cannot hold for large \(r\) if the right-hand side becomes negative, because \(\delta_{ij}(r)\ge 0\).

The correct form should be something like:

\[
\delta_{ij}(r)
\le
\max\{0,\delta_{ij}(0)-\sigma_{ij}r\},
\]

or the theorem should explicitly restrict \(r\) to the range where the linear bound is valid.

### 3.6.4 Shared controls remain open

R05 says it does not hide shared controls, but the theorem also does not handle them. Shared controls are left to the open problem R05.Open5.

That is honest, but the docket target asked for composition without hiding shared controls. Therefore T5 is not fully closed.

---

## 3.7 R06: aggregation obstruction is strong, but the safety bridge is under-specified

R06’s negative results are valuable:

- finite moment closure fails for quadratic field dynamics;
- exact coarse closure requires projectability;
- memory-bearing closure is necessary in rich fibre regimes.

However, there are gaps.

### 3.7.1 Thm2’s fibre-richness condition is vague

Thm2 says no finite augmentation exists if the fibres are rich enough that no continuous \(A:X\to\mathbb R^k\) separates points with distinct projected derivatives.

The phrase

> cardinality exceeding the separation capacity of \(\mathbb R^k\)-valued continuous maps

is not a standard formal condition. It needs a precise topological or measurable formulation.

Otherwise Thm2 is more a schema than a checkable theorem.

### 3.7.2 Cor. 4’s safety transfer needs a lifting map

Cor. 4 says approximate aggregate closure gives trajectory error bounds and safety erosion. But if the safe set is defined in the full state space, an aggregate error bound does not automatically give full-state safety.

One needs a lifting or admission map, e.g. from R04, to relate aggregate error to full-state distance from the unsafe set.

Without that, Cor. 4 risks contradicting R03.Thm3, which proves that aggregate margins are not kernels.

### Repair direction

State Cor. 4 conditionally:

> If the safety certificate factors through the aggregate and the admission map provides a Lipschitz lift, then aggregate defect \(\epsilon\) converts into erosion \(r\).

Otherwise aggregate approximation cannot certify componentwise safety.

## 3.8 R03: diagnostic trichotomy is useful but not formal enough

R03’s trichotomy is conceptually important:

1. adversarial-exit certificates are sound for nonviability;
2. margin-with-budget certificates are sound for viability;
3. other diagnostics are descriptive.

But the record does not fully formalize the diagnostic functional \(M\).

### Problems

1. What is the type of \(M\)?
2. How does \(M(x)<0\) imply the existence of an adversarial exit certificate?
3. How does \(M(x)\ge r\) imply membership in \(K_{-r}\)?
4. How are observation errors incorporated into \(M\)?
5. How does one verify that \(M\) belongs to class 1 or 2?

Without this, the trichotomy is more a classification slogan than a theorem schema.

### Repair direction

Define a diagnostic record:

\[
\mathsf{Diag}
=
(M,\ \text{certificate type},\ \text{error budget},\ \text{horizon},\ \text{regularity}).
\]

Then state precise introduction rules for when a diagnostic may be admitted as class 1 or class 2.

## 3.9 R03 Lem4 has a small but real proof gap

### Location

`R03`, Field 8, proof of Lem4.

### The issue

The proof writes:

\[
R_{n+1}=R_n\cap \operatorname{Pre}(R_n)=\operatorname{Pre}(R_n)
\]

and justifies this by saying \(\operatorname{Pre}(W)\subseteq K\) and \(R_n\subseteq K\).

That is insufficient. In general, \(\operatorname{Pre}(R_n)\subseteq K\) does not imply \(\operatorname{Pre}(R_n)\subseteq R_n\).

The equality can be proved by induction using monotonicity:

- \(R_1=\operatorname{Pre}(K)\subseteq K=R_0\);
- if \(R_n\subseteq R_{n-1}\), then \(\operatorname{Pre}(R_n)\subseteq \operatorname{Pre}(R_{n-1})=R_n\).

But the record does not present this induction at the point where the equality is asserted.

### Repair direction

Insert the induction argument explicitly. Also state the required uniform upper semicontinuity assumptions in the fixed-point step.

## 3.10 R04: admission certificate is strong, but necessity is over-broad

R04’s exact-admission certificate is one of the most useful structural results. It forces domain modules to declare:

- type/unit maps;
- phase-space maps;
- solution correspondence;
- safe-set correspondence;
- policy/information correspondence.

However, the necessity direction is stated too broadly.

### The issue

R04 says that if one of the maps fails, then no judgment transfer is possible.

But that is too strong for particular judgments. Some judgments may transfer even if the full five-map certificate fails. For example:

- a weak finite-horizon controlled viability claim may transfer under a non-injective state map;
- an aggregate conservation law may transfer without full policy correspondence;
- a diagnostic may transfer under partial observation if the fibre condition holds.

The certificate is necessary for **uniform transfer of the full judgment family**, not necessarily for every isolated judgment.

### Repair direction

Reword the necessity claim as:

> The five-map certificate is necessary for uniform transfer of all canonical judgments across all compatible horizons and policy classes. Individual judgments may admit weaker transfer conditions, but those must be proved separately.

## 3.12 R07: intergenerational theorem is solid but narrow

R07 is one of the cleaner records. The alternating-disjoint impossibility theorem is especially useful:

> Continuous evolution cannot cross disjoint specifications; typed resets are necessary.

However, the record remains narrow.

### Limitations

1. Generation epochs are fixed.
2. Endogenous generation boundaries are open.
3. Obligation translation variants `accumulate`, `cap`, `forgive` are declared but not fully analyzed.
4. Stochastic generation lengths are open.
5. Variable-event resets are open.

### Programme implication

Intergenerational sustainability often involves endogenous regime shifts, political transitions, and obligation renegotiation. The current theorem handles exogenous fixed-generation paths well, but not yet endogenous institutional change.

## 3.13 R08 is mostly sound, but depends on missing observation typing

R08’s typed-map necessity is correct and important:

The counterexamples are also sound, especially:

- controlled \(\not\Rightarrow\) robust;
- chance-\(p\) \(\not\Rightarrow\) robust;
- robust \(\not\Rightarrow\) chance-1 under support mismatch;
- physical nonempty \(\not\Rightarrow\) epistemic nonempty;
- implementation enlargement can shrink all-branches safety.

However, R08 inherits the observation-typing gap from R02 and `TCS-1.0` GAP-4. If observation maps are not typed, the exact-observation correspondence theorem cannot be fully integrated.

# 4. Cross-cutting internal inconsistencies

---

## 4.1 The packet claims `TCS-1.0` validity while using `TCS-1.1` types

The master review says:

> Every result record below is written so as to be valid under both `TCS-1.0` and the proposed `TCS-1.1`.

But several records use types that do not exist in `TCS-1.0`:

- R07’s `specification_path`;
- erosion triples proposed by GAP-3;
- tagged observation types proposed by GAP-4;
- harm-translation variants proposed by GAP-5.

This is an internal inconsistency.

### Repair

Either:

1. mark those records as `TCS-1.1`-dependent; or
2. provide an explicit encoding into `TCS-1.0`; or
3. state that the mathematical content is valid outside the frozen schema, but the record is not typable in `TCS-1.0`.

The current wording blurs this distinction.

## 4.4 The packet is strong on negative closure but weak on constructive closure

The packet excels at proving what cannot be universal:

- no universal delay law;
- no universal substitution law;
- no universal dynamic aggregation;
- no universal diagnostic causality;
- no universal local-to-global bifurcation law;
- no universal information/implementation monotonicity.

These are valuable.

But a general sustainability theory also needs constructive machinery:

- how to compute viability kernels;
- how to synthesize barriers;
- how to construct certificates;
- how to design observers;
- how to verify institutions;
- how to calibrate domain models;
- how to approximate safely;
- how to handle stochasticity and regime shifts.

The current packet mostly provides boundary conditions, not construction methods.

# 5. Conceptual risks for the broad research programme

---

## 5.1 The theory may be too deterministic for sustainability problems

The packet is overwhelmingly deterministic. Stochastic viability appears mainly through support alignment and chance constraints.

But many sustainability systems involve:

- environmental noise;
- climate stochasticity;
- ecological demographic noise;
- market shocks;
- institutional uncertainty;
- extreme events.

A deterministic boundary theorem is not enough. The programme needs a stochastic viability layer with:

- stochastic kernels;
- filtration-aligned observations;
- chance viability recursions;
- probabilistic erosion;
- stochastic barrier certificates;
- support and tail conditions.

This is currently open.

---

## 5.2 Hybridity and regime shifts are not yet closed

The packet handles review-synchronised events reasonably, but variable-event hybrid dynamics remain open.

Sustainability systems often have:

- tipping points;
- sudden regime shifts;
- disaster events;
- policy triggers;
- institutional collapses;
- technological transitions.

The current intergenerational theorem requires typed resets but assumes fixed epochs. That is a good start, but not enough.

A general theory needs:

- endogenous event surfaces;
- reset preservation theorems;
- non-Zeno conditions;
- event-time viability kernels;
- hybrid erosion margins.

---

## 5.3 Institutions are treated as implementation correspondences, not strategic actors

The non-strategic implementation assumption avoids circularity, but real institutions are strategic.

A sustainability theory that excludes strategic response can still be useful, but it cannot address:

- enforcement;
- compliance incentives;
- regulatory arbitrage;
- corruption;
- free-riding;
- polycentric bargaining.

The packet should explicitly state that the current institutional viability judgment is non-strategic, and that strategic institutional viability is a separate model class.

---

## 5.4 Aggregation obstructions are proved, but no replacement theory is provided

R06 proves that finite moment closure fails for quadratic field dynamics. This is important.

But then what?

The programme needs a constructive alternative, such as:

- certified approximate closures with explicit error budgets;
- memory-bearing closure approximations;
- projection methods with safety erosion;
- distributional closures under restricted classes;
- empirical moment validation protocols.

Otherwise the aggregation result becomes a prohibition without a usable path forward.

---

## 5.5 Diagnostics are classified but not operationalized

R03 tells us that many diagnostics are merely descriptive. That is valuable.

But the programme still needs:

- algorithms for constructing adversarial-exit certificates;
- algorithms for computing margin-with-budget certificates;
- numerical methods with certified error;
- observation-error-aware diagnostics;
- domain-specific diagnostic templates.

Without these, the diagnostic bridge remains theoretical.

---

# 6. Specific corrections that should be made before claiming closure

I would prioritize the following repairs.

---

## Priority 1: Fix R02 observation semantics and Prop. 3

Required actions:

1. Introduce a typed observation category.
2. Define exact, conservative, and coarsened observations formally.
3. Prove conservative soundness relative to an explicit coarsening map.
4. Replace the Prop. 3 witness with one where the conservative observation truly does not reveal the hidden mode.
5. Remove or qualify the word “computable” unless a selection theorem is proved.

Until this is done, R02 should be treated as repairable, not fully proved.

---

## Priority 2: Repair R01’s compact-control proof

Required actions:

1. Either set \(G=\mathbb R\) or restrict the state space so that the chosen feedback is admissible.
2. Re-verify that the endpoint-only predecessor remains nonempty while the exact-tube predecessor is empty.
3. Update any downstream dependencies, especially R09’s reuse of aggregate witnesses.

---

## Priority 3: Correct R09’s independence register

Required actions:

1. For M1, either prove global instability for all \(\tau>\pi/2\) or weaken the claim.
2. For M5, correct the kernel statement for the no-fold example.
3. Reword Part U completeness as relative to the declared candidate inventory.
4. Separate U4 as a meta-theoretic discipline law.

---

## Priority 4: Adopt the `TCS-1.1` migration explicitly

Required actions:

1. Add `specification_path`.
2. Add `confinement_certificate`.
3. Add `erosion_triple`.
4. Add tagged observation types.
5. Add harm-translation variants.
6. Add mandatory implementation-branch quantifier.
7. Add chance-support alignment clause.
8. Stop claiming that records depending on these types are valid under `TCS-1.0` without encoding.

---

## Priority 5: Tighten R05 assumptions

Required actions:

1. State Hausdorff or elementwise interface sensitivity precisely.
2. Assume \(L_i>0\) or handle zero Lipschitz constants separately.
3. Cap the linear tightening bounds.
4. Clarify that shared-control composition remains open.
5. State relaxation exactness or trajectory-level validity of convexification.

---

## Priority 6: Formalize R03 diagnostics and R06 aggregate safety transfer

Required actions:

1. Give a type for diagnostic functionals \(M\).
2. Define introduction rules for class 1 and class 2 certificates.
3. State when \(M(x)\ge r\) implies \(x\in K_{-r}\).
4. In R06 Cor. 4, require an explicit lifting/admission map before transferring aggregate error to full-state safety.

---

## Priority 7: Make erosion constants explicit

For every erosion claim, state:

\[
c = c(\alpha,L_G,C,\mu,\epsilon_*)
\]

or give a feasibility interval for \(c\). Avoid saying merely that an erosion depth exists.

For example, if

\[
(L_G c+C)\epsilon+\mu\le\alpha
\]

and \(0<c\epsilon<\rho\), then for all \(\epsilon\le\epsilon_*\), one valid choice is

\[
c
=
\min\left\{
\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_*},
\frac{\alpha-\mu-C\epsilon_*}{L_G\epsilon_*}
\right\},
\]

provided the numerator is positive. If not, no positive erosion certificate exists.

# 7. What remains genuinely valuable

Despite these flaws, the packet contains substantial mathematical value.

The strongest contributions are:

1. **The universal/model-class boundary idea**.  
   Separating conditional universal laws from model-class mechanisms is exactly the right move for a general theory.

2. **The independence witnesses**.  
   The refusal to promote delay, substitution, aggregation, diagnostics, bifurcation, or information monotonicity into universal laws is mathematically honest and important.

3. **The noncompensation discipline**.  
   The aggregate false-positive witness in R01 is especially useful for sustainability, where aggregate indicators can hide componentwise collapse.

4. **The typed admission certificate**.  
   R04 gives a real alternative to verbal analogy across domains.

5. **The intergenerational disjointness theorem**.  
   R07.Thm4 is simple but conceptually powerful: disjoint future specifications cannot be crossed by continuous evolution; typed transformation is required.

6. **The hierarchy completion in R08**.  
   The insistence on typed maps rather than literal inclusions across phase spaces is correct and should be enforced throughout.

---

# 8. Bottom-line judgment

The attempted documents are not flawed beyond repair. They represent a serious and often rigorous attempt to close a very difficult research programme. But the current closure is **partial**.

The most accurate description is:

> The packet proves a strong conditional boundary theory for sustainability viability, but several central bridges — especially observation, implementation, diagnostics, composition, and quantitative erosion — are not yet closed at the level required for a general theory.

To elevate the mathematics, the next revision should focus less on declaring closure and more on repairing the load-bearing bridges:

1. observation typing;
2. conservative-filter completeness/incompleteness;
3. selector regularity;
4. sampled-data erosion;
5. explicit erosion constants;
6. admission of hybrid/stochastic/strategic extensions as separate dockets.

If those repairs are made, the programme can legitimately claim a **conditional general theory of sustainability viability**. If they are not made, the phrase “general theory” will remain stronger than the mathematics supports.
