### R02 (T2) — verification lemma, not a constructed governance guarantee

**R02.Thm1 is true as a conditional verification theorem**, and only at the sampled, finite-horizon, exact/conservative-filter level written in Field 3.

What is proved: if a downward-closed family \(V\) of information states satisfies the regulation condition (REG) — one prescribed command whose **entire** realization set \(I(u^{\mathrm{cmd}},c,C)\) tube-keeps every \(x\in C\) in \(K\) and whose every nonempty conservative successor remains in \(V\) — then any selector of those witnesses, run against every implementation branch, every deployment branch, every disturbance, and every true initial state in \(B_0\subset C_0\), keeps \(x(t)\in K\) on \([0,T]\) and keeps \(B_k\subset C_k\in V\).

- true state lies in \(B_k\subset C_k\) (Lem2);
- (REG)(i) therefore tube-protects the true trajectory;
- the true endpoint witnesses nonemptiness of \(C_{k+1}=\Phi(C_k,\tilde U_k)\cap O^{-1}(Y_{k+1})\);

Quantifier order is the required one

\exists\pi\text{ causal, observation-based}\quad\forall x_0\in B_0\quad\forall\text{observations}\quad\forall u^{\mathrm{real}}\in I\quad\forall\text{DeP}\quad\forall d(\cdot).

Prescribed and realized controls are distinguished. \(I\) is declared non-strategic. That is the right institutional reading.

## 4. Controlling invariance theorem (replaces unverified B1)

This is the only strong-invariance engine that may be cited.

**Theorem (Lipschitz robust strong invariance).**  
Let \(U,D\) be compact, \(f\) continuous and locally Lipschitz in \(x\) uniformly on compact \((u,d)\)-sets, \(K\subset\mathbb{R}^n\) closed. Suppose there exists **one** locally Lipschitz feedback \(k:K\to U\) such that the envelope
\[
G_k(x)=\overline{\mathrm{co}}\{f(x,k(x),d):d\in D(x)\}
\]
is nonempty, compact, convex-valued, locally Hausdorff-Lipschitz, of linear growth, and
\[
\max_{v\in G_k(x)}\langle\zeta,v\rangle\le 0\qquad\forall x\in K,\;\forall\zeta\in N_K^P(x).
\]
If \(x\mapsto D(x)\) is used, it too must be locally Hausdorff-Lipschitz with compact values. Then, for every measurable \(d(\cdot)\) with \(d(t)\in D(x(t))\) and every Carathéodory (and, since \(k\) is locally Lipschitz, every Filippov) solution,
\[
x(0)\in K\quad\Rightarrow\quad x(t)\in K\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,+\infty).
\]
Quantifiers: \(\exists k\;\forall d(\cdot)\;\forall\) admitted solutions.

**Bibliographic clause.** Clarke–Ledyaev–Stern–Wolenski, *Nonsmooth Analysis and Control Theory*, Thm 4.3.8 (proximal Hamiltonian form). Proximal normals suffice because \(G_k\) is locally Lipschitz; the contingent, Clarke-tangent, proximal, and Clarke-normal forms then coincide.

**Why a viability argument is never enough.** Viability supplies one trajectory of one inclusion. Robust strong invariance demands every disturbance and every admitted solution. A measurable selection of a nonempty \(R_K\) does not make \(G_k\) Lipschitz and does not justify CLSW 4.3.8. Pointwise \(F_k(x)\subset T_K(x)\) plus Nagumo is the wrong theorem.

**Erosion (replaces unverified B1 Lemma 2).** False for arbitrary closed \(K\) (dumbbell of reach zero: corridor consumption creates new scar normals on which the field points out). True if \(K\) is prox-regular of reach \(r>0\), \(k\) and \(f\) Lipschitz, inward margin \(m>0\), total velocity error \(\le C\varepsilon\), and
\[
\varepsilon < \min\Bigl(r,\frac{m}{L+C}\Bigr).
\]
Then \(K_{-\varepsilon}\) is strongly invariant under the erroneous loop (\(c=1\)), and more generally any
\[
c\le\min\Bigl(\frac{r}{\varepsilon},\frac{m-C\varepsilon}{L\varepsilon}\Bigr)
\]
works. Metric erosion and barrier-superlevel erosion are different sets; comparison needs \(|\nabla b|\ge\mu>0\).

## 5. T5 — restricted composition (what the packet claimed to prove)

Two theorems only. No nonlinear gain matrix. No “choose all \(u_i\) at once” without a joint map.

### 5.1 Independent-control product theorem

Let
\[
\dot x_i=f_i(x_i,u_i,z_i,d_i),\qquad z_i=C_i(x_{-i}),\qquad i=1,\dots,n,
\]
\(U=\prod U_i\), \(D=\prod D_i\), each \(U_i,D_i\) compact, each \(f_i\) continuous and locally Lipschitz in \(x_i\) uniformly in the rest, each \(C_i\) continuous, each \(Q_i\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_i}\) closed, \(Q=\prod Q_i\). Solutions: Carathéodory, or Filippov if the selection below is discontinuous and the envelope is re-checked.

Assume:
1. **Interface bound on the candidate set:** \(\|C_i(x_{-i})\|\le\bar z_i\) for every \(x\in Q\).
2. **Local regulation maps nonempty:** for every \(i\), every \(x_i\in Q_i\), every \(\|z_i\|\le\bar z_i\),
   \[
   R_i(x_i,z_i)=\bigl\{u_i\in U_i:\forall d_i,\; f_i(x_i,u_i,z_i,d_i)\in T_{Q_i}(x_i)\bigr\}\ne\emptyset.
   \]
   If \(Q_i\) is a finite \(C^1\) intersection, constraint qualification is required so that membership in \(T_{Q_i}\) is the simultaneous system of active Lie-derivative inequalities — one scalar barrier is not enough.
3. **Selection:** each \(R_i(\cdot,C_i(\cdot))\) admits a locally Lipschitz selection \(\kappa_i\) on \(Q\).
4. **Envelope regularity:** \(G_\kappa(x)=\overline{\mathrm{co}}\{f(x,\kappa(x),C(x),d):d\in D\}\) is locally Hausdorff-Lipschitz, compact convex-valued, linear growth.
5. **Proximal inequality** for \(G_\kappa\) on \(Q\), which follows from (2)–(4) and the product formula \(T_Q(x)=\prod T_{Q_i}(x_i)\) once the envelope has been formed.

Then one causal memoryless policy \(u=\kappa(x)\) renders \(Q\) robustly strongly invariant: \(\exists\kappa\;\forall d(\cdot)\;\forall\) admitted solutions, \(x(t)\in Q\) on \([0,\infty)\).

**Proof.** Independence gives \(R(x)=\prod R_i(x_i,C_i(x_{-i}))\). Nonemptiness is the product of nonempty sets. A locally Lipschitz product selection exists by (3). Interface bound (1) keeps every \(z_i\) inside the region where (2) applies. The product of contingent cones is the contingent cone of the product, so \(G_\kappa(x)\subset T_Q(x)\) after convexification. CLSW 4.3.8 plus linear growth gives the claim.

### 5.2 Shared-control theorem (joint feasibility is an extra hypothesis)

Retain the data of §5.1 except \(U\subset\prod U_i\) need not be a product. Replace (2)–(3) by: the **joint** regulation map
\[
R(x)=\bigl\{u\in U:\forall d\in D,\; f_i(x_i,u_i,C_i(x_{-i}),d_i)\in T_{Q_i}(x_i)\ \forall i\bigr\}
\]
is nonempty on \(Q\) and admits a locally Lipschitz selection \(\kappa\). Then the same conclusion holds. Local nonemptiness of the projections of \(R\) is neither necessary nor sufficient.

**Minimal joint-infeasibility witness** (packet R01.Thm2 is a different geometry; this is the actuator-sharing witness required by the first audit). Two subsystems, \(C_i\equiv0\), shared \(u\in[-1,1]\),
\[
\dot x_1=u-\tfrac12,\quad Q_1=[0,\infty),\qquad \dot x_2=-u-\tfrac12,\quad Q_2=[0,\infty).
\]
Each local map is nonempty on its own boundary; at \((0,0)\) one needs \(u\ge1/2\) and \(u\le-1/2\). Every trajectory leaves \(Q\) instantly. Interface bounds hold. Separate feasibility does not imply joint invariance.

### 5.3 Tubular assume–guarantee with a linear gain operator

This is the narrow “Version B” that the packet named and did not prove.

Assume each \(Q_i\) is prox-regular of reach \(r_i>0\), each local closed-loop field (interfaces treated as bounded disturbances) is Lipschitz with constant \(L_i\) and has inward margin \(m_i>0\) whenever \(\|z_i\|\le \bar z_i+\delta_i\). Let the interface maps be Lipschitz,
\[
\|C_i(x_{-i})\|\le \bar z_i+\sum_{j\ne i}\gamma_{ij}\,\mathrm{dist}(x_j,\partial Q_j)^c
\]
on a tube about \(Q\), with the convention that one works with **erosions** \(Q_i^{-\rho_i}\) of depths \(\rho=( \rho_i)\). The first-order budget at the eroded boundary is of the form
\[
m_i \ge L_i\rho_i + \sum_{j\ne i}\Gamma_{ij}\rho_j + \Delta_i,
\]
i.e. \((m-\Delta)\ge (D_L+\Gamma)\rho\) componentwise, where \(D_L=\mathrm{diag}(L_i)\) and \(\Gamma=(\Gamma_{ij})\) collects interface gains (\(\Gamma_{ii}=0\)).

**Feasibility fixed point.** A depth vector \(\rho\) with \(0<\rho_i<r_i\) is admissible if and only if
\[
(D_L+\Gamma)\rho \le m-\Delta,\qquad \rho>0,\qquad \rho<r.
\]
If \(D_L+\Gamma\) is a nonsingular \(M\)-matrix (equivalently \(\rho(D_L^{-1}\Gamma)<1\) when \(L_i>0\)), the box is nonempty for all sufficiently small \(\Delta\), and the minimal feasible depth is
\[
\rho_\star=(D_L+\Gamma)^{-1}(m-\Delta).
\]
If a nonnegative \(\rho\) satisfying the inequality exists, the independent-control theorem applied to \(\prod Q_i^{-\rho_i}\) plus the repaired erosion theorem gives robust strong invariance of that product, hence of \(Q\).

**Collapse witness (gain loop kills the joint kernel).** Two scalar channels, \(m_1=m_2=1\), \(L_1=L_2=1\), \(\Gamma_{12}=\Gamma_{21}=2\), \(\Delta=0\):
\[
\rho_1+2\rho_2\le 1,\qquad 2\rho_1+\rho_2\le 1,\qquad \rho>0.
\]
Adding: \(3(\rho_1+\rho_2)\le 2\), but the two inequalities force \(\rho_1+\rho_2\le 2/3\) and also \(\rho_1\le 1-2\rho_2\), \(\rho_2\le 1-2\rho_1\), which together imply \(\rho_1+\rho_2\le 2-2(\rho_1+\rho_2)\), hence \(\rho_1+\rho_2\le 2/3\), compatible; increase the cross gains to \(\Gamma_{12}=\Gamma_{21}=3\):
\[
\rho_1+3\rho_2\le 1,\qquad 3\rho_1+\rho_2\le 1.
\]
Adding: \(4(\rho_1+\rho_2)\le 2\Rightarrow \rho_1+\rho_2\le 1/2\), while \(3\rho_1+\rho_2\le 1\) and \(\rho_1+3\rho_2\le 1\) with \(\rho\ge0\) force, e.g. from the first and \(\rho_2\ge0\), no obstruction yet. Take \(\Gamma_{12}=\Gamma_{21}=a>1\) large and \(m=L=1\): \((1-a)\rho_1+(1-a)\rho_2\le 2-2a\) after a linear combination; for \(a>1\) the matrix \(D_L+\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\a&1\end{pmatrix}\) has determinant \(1-a^2<0\) and is **not** an \(M\)-matrix. The inequalities \( \rho_1+a\rho_2\le 1\), \(a\rho_1+\rho_2\le 1\), \(\rho>0\) become inconsistent as soon as \(a>1\): if \(\rho_1>0\) then \(\rho_2\le(1-\rho_1)/a\) and \(a\rho_1+(1-\rho_1)/a\le 1\), i.e. \(a^2\rho_1+1-\rho_1\le a\), so \(\rho_1(a^2-1)\le a-1\), hence \(\rho_1\le 1/(a+1)\); symmetrically \(\rho_2\le 1/(a+1)\). Plug back: \(\rho_1+a\rho_2\le (1+a)/(a+1)=1\), tight. So a feasible \(\rho\) still exists for the **linear** budget.

A genuine collapse to \(\{0\}\) needs the margins themselves to shrink with the partner’s slack, e.g. \(m_i=\mu_i-\sum_j \gamma_{ij}(1-\rho_j)_+\) with \(\mu\) too small, or a **bilinear** interface \(\|z_i\|\ge c>0\) on every positive-depth product (strict coupling that cannot be made small by erosion). Concrete ODE witness:

\[
\dot x_1=u_1+x_2,\quad \dot x_2=u_2+x_1,\quad |u_i|\le 1,\quad Q_i=[-1,1].
\]
On \(Q\), \(\|z_1\|=\|x_2\|\le 1\), and the local inward margin on \(\{x_1=1\}\) is \(u_1+x_2\le 1+x_2\), which is \(\le 0\) for all \(x_2\in[-1,1]\) if and only if we can choose \(u_1=-1\) and need \(x_2\le 0\). No single \(u_1\) works for all \(x_2\in[-1,1]\). Eroding to \([-1+\rho,1-\rho]^2\) still leaves \(x_2\) of both signs unless one of the sets is reduced to \(\{0\}\). The joint kernel of \(Q\) is \(\{0\}\) (the linear system \(\dot x=u+Ax\) with \(\|u\|_\infty\le 1\) and \(A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) has unstable eigenvalue \(+1\), and the control budget cannot dominate the partner state throughout the square). ## 6. T6 — aggregation and memory (precise theorem, not the slogan)

The slogan “no finite family of moments closes for quadratic field dynamics” is **false as stated**. Two patches \(\dot x_i=x_i^2\) have exact autonomous closure in \((m,v)\):
\[
\dot m=m^2+v,\qquad \dot v=4mv,
\]
because a two-point empirical measure is determined by mean and variance up to order. Finite \(N\) polynomial systems often close in a finite jet.

**Theorem (non-closure for unknown or continuum ensembles).**  
Let \(\{\mu_t\}\) solve the Liouville equation for a quadratic field \(f(x)=Ax+Q(x,x)\) on \(\mathbb{R}^d\), \(d\ge 1\), \(Q\not\equiv 0\). Let \(\mathcal{P}_k\) be the vector of raw moments of order \(\le k\). Then \(\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{P}_k\) is a function of \(\mathcal{P}_k\) alone, for every compactly supported \(\mu\), if and only if \(Q=0\). In particular no finite \(k\) yields an exact autonomous moment ODE on the whole space of compactly supported measures.

**Proof.** The moment of a monomial \(x^\alpha\) with \(|\alpha|=k\) has derivative \(\mathbb{E}[\nabla(x^\alpha)\cdot(Ax+Q(x,x))]\), which contains a term of degree \(k+1\) whenever \(Q\not\equiv 0\). If this term were a function of moments of order \(\le k\) for every compactly supported \(\mu\), the polynomial \(x\mapsto \nabla(x^\alpha)\cdot Q(x,x)\) would be a linear combination of monomials of degree \(\le k\), contradiction. \(\square\)

**Exact coarse variables are memory-bearing** whenever the projectability criterion fails: if two states in a fibre \(P^{-1}(y)\) have different projected velocities, no Lipschitz vector field on the coarse space reproduces both. That is the packet’s B5 projectability criterion (classical) and is the correct exact-characterization. Approximate closure with an \(O(\kappa)\) or variance defect transfers to the coarse set only through the repaired erosion budget; there is no free “small variance \(\Rightarrow\) small safety loss” constant for arbitrary closed coarse sets.

**Identical-patch versus heterogeneous-patch pair** (M3, accepted as an independence witness once written): on the diagonal \(x_1=\cdots=x_N\), variance \(0\) is invariant and the mean ODE is exact; off-diagonal, the same mean has many mean-derivatives. Both instantiations satisfy the structural axioms.

**Disposition.** The projectability characterization is classical. The Liouville non-closure theorem above is classical kinetic-theory content. Memory-necessity is a corollary, not a new principle. Paper 2 remark / monograph aggregation chapter. External check against moment-closure literature is mandatory before any novelty sentence.

## 7. T7 — intergenerational continuation (minimal theorem)

**Theorem (fixed specification versus specification change).**  
Fix one \(\Omega\). Ordinary robust strong invariance of \(K_\Omega\) (the theorem of §4) is intergenerational continuation **only** in the trivial sense that the same specification is reused. If a locally finite path \((\Omega_g)_{g\in G}\) is declared, typed translation maps \(R_g\) on \((x,\mathrm{id},\mathrm{liability},h)\) must be part of the data (this is GAP-1 and GAP-5).

- **Necessity of resets.** If \(K_{\Omega_g}\) and \(K_{\Omega_{g+1}}\) have disjoint closures, no continuous trajectory of a locally bounded field can pass from one into the other. A typed reset is necessary.
- **Sufficiency (narrow).** If each epoch \(g\) has a Lipschitz envelope satisfying the proximal inequality on \(K_{\Omega_g}\), each reset satisfies \(R_g(K_{\Omega_g})\subset K_{\Omega_{g+1}}\), and the event set is non-Zeno / non-blocking, then every hybrid execution starting in \(K_{\Omega_0}\) remains in the time-dependent constraint. This is **not** a corollary of the continuous product argument; it is a separate hybrid theorem and must not be advertised as already proved.
- **Nested-compact existence.** If the admissible-set sequence is nested, compact, nonempty, and each is robustly invariant under the same envelope, the intersection is robustly invariant. Without compactness the intersection may be empty (standard).
- **Monotone obligation.** If a scalar obligation \(h\) only accumulates, \(\dot h\ge \eta>0\) off a harmless set, then \(h\) hits any finite cap in time \(\le (h_{\mathrm{cap}}-h_0)/\eta\). That is a finite-horizon obstruction, not an infinite-horizon kernel statement.

A001 Thm 14.2-style impossibility, if retained, must keep its dissipativity/compactness hypotheses explicit.

**Disposition.** TCS-1.1 type template + monograph paragraph. Not a Paper 2 main theorem. Hybrid reset/non-Zeno data remain an obligation.

## 8. T8 — hierarchy converses (classical; maps not supplied)

The five named converses are standard and may be recorded as such. They do not require the missing R08 text, but the **typed lift/projection maps** do, and those maps are not here.

## 9. T9 — universal / model-class boundary

## R05 — Tubular assume–guarantee (T5)

**Verdict: repairable \(\to\) proved, as a restricted independent-control, tubular, convexified-inclusion theorem. The general nonlinear / shared-control / nonconvex problem is still open, and the record correctly leaves it open.**

This is the first record in the docket that actually proves a composition theorem rather than assuming a certificate family.

### What is proved

Modules \(i=1,\dots,N\), closed \(Q_i\) of two-sided reach \(\rho_i>0\) with \(C^{1,1}\) signed distance in the tube, interfaces \(z_{ij}\) Lipschitz on that tube, nominal envelopes
\[
G_i(x_i)=\overline{\mathrm{co}}\{F_i(x_i,\hat z_i,d_i):d_i\in D_i\}
\]
locally Hausdorff-Lipschitz with constant \(L_i\), compact convex values, linear growth, and a uniform inward margin \(\alpha_i>0\) for \(G_i\) on \(\partial Q_i\). Interface sensitivity \(\Lambda_i\). Joint envelope \(G\) locally Hausdorff-Lipschitz (H4). Contract amplitudes
\[
\delta_{ij}(r)=\sup\bigl\{\|z_{ij}(x_j)-\hat z_{ij}\|:x_j\in Q_j^{-r}\bigr\}.
\]
Controls, if any, are already folded into \(F_i\). Shared \(U\) is not present.

**R05.Thm1.** If \(\Lambda_i\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{ij}(0)+\Delta_i\le\alpha_i\) for every \(i\), then \(Q=\prod Q_i\) is strongly invariant for the convexified joint inclusion.

**R05.Thm2.** If some \(0\le r_i<\rho_i\) satisfies
\[
L_ir_i+\Lambda_i\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{ij}(r_j)+\Delta_i\le\alpha_i\qquad\forall i,
\]
then \(Q^r=\prod Q_i^{-r_i}\) is strongly invariant for that inclusion.

**R05.Cor3.** Under the linear bound \(\delta_{ij}(r)\le\delta_{ij}(0)-\sigma_{ij}r\), write
\[
b_i=\alpha_i-\Delta_i-\Lambda_i\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{ij}(0),\quad
A=\mathrm{diag}(L)-M,\quad M_{ij}=\Lambda_i\sigma_{ij}\ (i\ne j),\quad
\Gamma=\mathrm{diag}(L)^{-1}M.
\]
If \(\rho(\Gamma)<1\), then \(A\) is a nonsingular \(M\)-matrix, \(A^{-1}\ge0\), and the uncapped linear system \(Ar\le b\), \(r\ge0\) is feasible iff \(A^{-1}b\ge0\). In that case \(r_\star=A^{-1}b\) is the componentwise-maximal feasible depth, the iteration \(r\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(L)^{-1}(b+Mr)\) converges to \(r_\star\) from \(0\) whenever \(b\ge0\), and certification is \(r_\star<\rho\) componentwise. For \(N=2\),
\[
\rho(\Gamma)<1\iff\Lambda_1\sigma_{12}\Lambda_2\sigma_{21}<L_1L_2.
\]
If both \(b_i<0\), no \(r\ge0\) is feasible.

Quantifiers: \(\forall x(0)\in Q^r\ \forall d(\cdot)\ \forall\) Filippov/envelope solutions, \(x(t)\in Q^r\) on \([0,\infty)\).

### Clause-level match to the controlling invariance theorem

H1+H4 give a locally Hausdorff-Lipschitz, compact convex, linearly growing envelope. The product formula \(N_Q^P(x)=\prod N_{Q_i}^P(x_i)\) holds for finite products. The Hamiltonian estimates in the two proofs are exactly the proximal inequality of Clarke–Ledyaev–Stern–Wolenski, Thm 4.3.8. Linear growth gives forward completeness. That is the all-solutions theorem. A viability argument is not used. Good.

Tubular normal correspondence (unique projection, common unit normal of \(\partial Q_i^{-r}\) and \(\partial Q_i\)) is the geometric fact that makes erosion quantitative. It holds on the declared \(C^{1,1}\)/positive-reach class and fails for arbitrary closed sets. The record says this. Good.

### Proofs, compressed and corrected

**Thm1.** Let \(x\in\partial Q\), \(\zeta\in N_Q^P(x)\), \(A(x)=\{i:x_i\in\partial Q_i\}\). Inactive blocks have \(\zeta_i=0\). For \(i\in A(x)\) every partner lies in \(Q_j\), so \(\|z_i(x)-\hat z_i\|\le\sum\delta_{ij}(0)\) in the declared product norm. H3 plus the exogenous ball give
\[
v_i\in G_i(x_i)+(\Lambda_i\sum\delta_{ij}(0)+\Delta_i)B.
\]
H2 yields \(\langle\zeta_i,v_i\rangle\le(-\alpha_i+\Lambda_i\sum\delta_{ij}(0)+\Delta_i)\|\zeta_i\|\le0\). Sum, pass to \(\overline{\mathrm{co}}\). CLSW 4.3.8 applies to \(G\).

**Thm2.** At \(x\in\partial Q^r\) let \(p_i=\pi_{Q_i}(x_i)\), \(\|x_i-p_i\|=r_i\), common unit outward normal \(n_i\). Then
\[
\sup_{G_i(x_i)}\langle n_i,\cdot\rangle\le\sup_{G_i(p_i)}\langle n_i,\cdot\rangle+L_ir_i\le-\alpha_i+L_ir_i,
\]
and the interface remainder is \(\Lambda_i\sum\delta_{ij}(r_j)+\Delta_i\). The budget is precisely the hypothesis. Same Hamiltonian criterion on \(Q^r\), which is again prox-regular of reach \(\rho_i-r_i>0\).

**Cor3.** The linear bound converts Thm2 into \(Ar\le b\). Off-diagonal entries of \(A\) are nonpositive. \(\rho(\Gamma)<1\) iff \(A\) is a nonsingular \(M\)-matrix, iff \(A^{-1}\ge0\). Then \(Ar\le b\), \(r\ge0\) implies \(r\le A^{-1}b\), so feasibility requires \(A^{-1}b\ge0\); conversely \(r_\star=A^{-1}b\) satisfies \(Ar_\star=b\). The Neumann series and the two-module formulae are correct. If \(b<0\) componentwise every entry of \(A^{-1}b\) is a positive combination of the \(b_i\), hence negative.

### Defects that must be written into the theorem, not the footnotes

1. **Convexification gap.** The conclusion is strong invariance of \(\dot x\in G(x)=\overline{\mathrm{co}}F\). If \(F_i\) is nonconvex, realized non-relaxed trajectories need not fill \(G\), but more importantly the theorem does **not** give strong invariance of the original nonconvex inclusion without a relaxation-exactness hypothesis. Field 9 says this; the theorem statement itself must say it.

2. **Independent, already-selected controls.** There is no joint regulation map and no shared \(U\). Separate local margins do not imply a jointly feasible \(u\). The shared-actuator witness (first audit; corrected-03 origin) remains outside the theorem. Open5 is the right residual, not a side remark.

3. **Cor3 is sufficient, not equivalent to Thm2.** The linear majorant \(\delta_{ij}(r)\le\delta_{ij}(0)-\sigma_{ij}r\) can be conservative. Feasibility of \(Ar\le b\) is equivalent to \(A^{-1}b\ge0\) only for that linear system. Rewrite Cor3.1 accordingly.

4. **Norm convention.** \(\|z_i-\hat z_i\|\le\sum\delta_{ij}\) must be fixed as a product/sum norm in the data. Euclidean stacking changes constants by at most \(\sqrt{N-1}\); record it.

5. **Selector silence is acceptable here** only because no selection is claimed. Any later claim that an implemented Lipschitz \(\kappa_i(x_i)\) sits inside \(F_i\) must re-verify H1–H4 for that closed loop.

6. **Replace every appeal to “corrected 02 Lemma 2”** by the repaired prox-regular erosion theorem (reach \(\rho_i\), margin \(\alpha_i\), Lipschitz constant \(L_i\), budget \(L_ir+\Lambda\delta+\Delta\le\alpha\)). The listed geometry already matches. Do not cite an unseen lemma.

### R05.Ex4

The Metzler system \(\dot x_1=-a_1x_1+c_1x_2\), \(\dot x_2=-a_2x_2+c_2x_1\), \(x\ge0\), boxes \([0,k_i]\), \(c_1c_2>a_1a_2\), has isolated-module kernels equal to the full boxes and coupled kernel \(\{(0,0)\}\). That is classical and correct.

Also \(Q_i=[0,k_i]\) has reach \(k_i/2\), so the \(C^{1,1}\) tube is a neighbourhood of the two endpoints, not of the midpoint. Harmless if \(\rho_i<k_i/2\).

### Open5

Accepted as the precise residual: nonlinear \(\Gamma_{ij}(r)\), nonconvex implementation, shared channels, non-tubular geometry. Missing hypotheses (i)–(iv) are the right list. Do not sketch a proof.

## R06 — Aggregation and memory (T6)

**Verdict: proved, after a mandatory scope lock. The slogan “no finite moment family closes for quadratic field dynamics” is false. The theorem that is actually proved is true.**

**R06.Lem1** is the necessity half of semiconjugacy: an exact autonomous closure of \(q=(P,A)\) forces \(dP_x F(x)\) constant on \(q\)-fibres. Classical. The “lifted” form is the same argument with extra coordinates.

**R06.Thm2** is Lem1 plus an assumption that every finite continuous augmentation leaves some pair in a \(P\)-fibre unseparated while \(dPF\) differs. Then no finite-dimensional Markovian closure exists. Logically correct, almost tautological. The phrase “separation capacity of \(\mathbb{R}^k\)-valued continuous maps” is not a precise cardinality statement (continuous maps to \(\mathbb{R}^k\) separate uncountable sets). Keep it as a hypothesis to be checked per class, which is what Thm3 then does.

**R06.Thm3 is correct for arbitrary bounded fields on a probability space.**

Pointwise logistic \(\partial_t X=X(\rho-X)\), \(m_k=\int X^k\,d\mu\), \(\rho\) constant:
\[
\dot m_k=k(\rho m_k-m_{k+1}).
\]
The mean identity \(\dot m_1=\rho m_1-m_1^2-\mathrm{Var}(X)\) is the usual second-moment defect. For each finite \(K\), two nonnegative bounded fields are built with equal moments \(1,\dots,K\) and unequal moment \(K+1\), hence unequal \(\dot m_K\). Lem1 obstructs any autonomous law on \((m_1,\dots,m_K)\).

The Chebyshev–Vandermonde construction is correct.

- Equioscillation: \(g=x^{K+1}-q\) attains \(\pm\|g\|_\infty\) at \(K+2\) nodes.
- Weights \(\lambda_i=1/\prod_{j\ne i}(x_j-x_i)\) span the kernel of the \((K+1)\times(K+2)\) Vandermonde block of degrees \(0,\dots,K\), because \(\sum_i x_i^j/\omega'(x_i)=0\) for \(j\le K\).
- \(\sum\lambda_i g(x_i)\ne0\) because \(\lambda\) and \(g\) both alternate.
- \(\nu=\mu+\varepsilon\sigma\) is a probability for small \(\varepsilon>0\), same moments \(0,\dots,K\), different moment \(K+1\).
- Step fields with those occupation measures are admissible bounded nonnegative states.

**Scope lock (non-negotiable).** Thm3 is a statement about *arbitrary* fields, i.e. an infinite-dimensional fibre. It does **not** say that every finite-patch quadratic system fails to close. Counter-example to the unrestricted slogan: two logistic patches, empirical mean and variance. The two-atom measure is determined (up to order) by \((m,v)\), the diagonal of identical patches is invariant, and \((m,v)\) closes. Finite \(N\) polynomial systems frequently close in a finite jet. Field 2’s headline must read: *no finite moment list closes the logistic moment hierarchy on the space of arbitrary bounded fields*. The parenthetical in Field 3 (“infinite-dimensional fibre regime”) is the theorem, not a comment.

**R06.Cor4** is Grönwall plus erosion. Correct. Approximate closure never yields an exact kernel transfer. Horizon \(T\) is part of the contract; the \(O(\kappa)\) support-saturation instance is the same conversion and is not re-proved here.

**R06.Ex5** is the correct identical-versus-heterogeneous pair for R09.M3. On \(\{x_1=x_2\}\) with \(g_1=g_2\) the mean closes. If \(g_1\ne g_2\), the swapped pair \((a,b)\), \((b,a)\) shares a mean and not a mean-derivative. Same axioms, opposite closure.

**Disposition.** Paper 2 / monograph aggregation chapter, next to projectability. Classical kinetic / spatial-ecology content with an explicit separator. External check against moment-closure literature is mandatory before any novelty sentence. Not a new principle of “memory.”

### Part M — item by item

**M1 (delay). Repairable \(\to\) proved after a root-locus paragraph.**

- Instantiation I. \(\dot x=-2x(t)-x(t-\tau)\). Characteristic \(\lambda+2+e^{-\lambda\tau}=0\). If \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda\ge0\) then \(|\lambda+2|\ge2>1\ge|e^{-\lambda\tau}|\), so no closed right-half-plane roots. Delay-independent exponential stability. (Halanay with \(\alpha=2>\beta=1\) is an alternative certificate on the positive cone; the characteristic argument does not need A002.)
- Instantiation II. \(\dot x=-x(t-\tau)\), \(F(\lambda,\tau)=\lambda+e^{-\lambda\tau}\). At \(\tau=\pi/2\), \(\lambda=i\) is a simple root. The crossing computation
  \[
  \frac{d\lambda}{d\tau}=\frac{1}{1+\tau\lambda},\qquad
  \operatorname{Re}\frac{d\lambda}{d\tau}\Big|_{(i,\pi/2)}=\frac{1}{1+\pi^2/4}>0
  \]
  is correct and gives instability on \((\pi/2,\pi/2+\delta)\).

  Field 3’s stronger claim — instability for **every** \(\tau>\pi/2\) — is true but **not proved in the writeup**. Completing it: purely imaginary roots occur only at \(\omega=\pm1\), \(\tau=\pi/2+2k\pi\); at each such crossing \(\operatorname{Re}(d\lambda/d\tau)=1/(1+\tau^2)>0\), so roots enter the right half-plane and never return. Hence unstable for all \(\tau>\pi/2\). Add that paragraph, or restrict Field 3 to an explicit interval.

**M2 (substitution). Proved, and thin.**

**M3 (aggregation). Proved only with the R06 scope lock.**

**M4 (diagnostics). Proved from records already accepted.**

\dot x_1=\mu+x_1^2,\qquad \dot x_2=-x_2+\tfrac12,\qquad K=\{x_2\le1\}.

Saddle-node in the uncoupled \(x_1\)-coordinate at \(\mu=0\). Every trajectory with \(x_2(0)\le1\) stays in \(K\) for every \(\mu\), so \(\operatorname{Viab}(K)=K\). Local bifurcation does not imply kernel change. (Parse “\(x_{12}\)” as \(x_1^2\).)
