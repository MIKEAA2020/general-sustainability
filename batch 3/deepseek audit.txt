## Overall assessment

The packet is unusually disciplined for a “general theory” document: it maintains quantifier discipline, separates universal axioms from model-class mechanisms, and records proofs at a useful level. But it is **not yet mathematically sealed**. I find several substantive flaws and internal inconsistencies that should be repaired before any paper treats these records as closed.

The most serious issue is **R06.Thm2**, whose stated obstruction criterion is not valid as written. There are also missing regularity hypotheses in **R03.Lem4**, an overclaim in **R05.Cor3**, and several smaller witness-level errors.

---

## 1. R06.Thm2 — the fibre-separation/cardinality argument is invalid

### Location

R06 Field 3, `R06.Thm2`, and the parenthetical:

> “because the set of distinct projected-derivative values along the fibre has cardinality exceeding the separation capacity of R^k-valued continuous maps on that fibre”

### Flaw

The projected derivative

\[
dP_{x}F(x)
\]

takes values in \(\mathbb R^m\). Therefore the set of distinct projected-derivative values has cardinality **at most continuum**, regardless of how infinite-dimensional the fibre is.

A continuous map \(A:X\to \mathbb R^k\) can be injective on a continuum-sized subset of a fibre in many standard spaces. For example, if the fibre contains a smooth curve parameterized by \(s\mapsto \gamma(s)\), then a single scalar observable \(A=s\) separates points on that curve. So the stated cardinality criterion does **not** imply that no finite continuous augmentation can separate the offending states.

Thus `R06.Thm2` is not a proved obstruction theorem as stated. The moment non-closure theorem `R06.Thm3` is fine, because it explicitly uses moment functionals, but `R06.Thm2` claims a much more general obstruction without a valid condition.

### Severity

High. This is one of the two central “aggregation is necessarily memory-bearing” results.

### Proposed upgrade

Replace `R06.Thm2` with a precise finite-rank obstruction lemma, for example:

> Let \(X\) be an infinite-dimensional Banach space, let \(P:X\to\mathbb R^m\) be a continuous linear projection, and let \(A_1,\dots,A_k:X\to\mathbb R\) be continuous. If \(dP_x F(x)\) contains a component that is a nonzero continuous linear functional not determined by \(P,A_1,\dots,A_k\), then no finite-dimensional augmented map \(q=(P,A_1,\dots,A_k)\) admits an exact autonomous closure.

Or state the result only for linear observables:

> If \(P\) is a finite-rank linear map and \(F\) is such that \(dP_xF(x)\) is a continuous linear functional of \(x\) with infinite rank relative to \(P\), then no finite set of continuous linear observables \(A_1,\dots,A_k\) closes the projected dynamics exactly.

This would be provable by a simple common-kernel argument: choose \(h\neq 0\) in the common kernel of the linear observables with nonzero projected derivative, then move along \(h\) inside the same \(q\)-fibre.

## 2. R03.Lem4 — missing joint upper semicontinuity in the state variable

### Location

R03 Field 3, `R03.Lem4`; Field 8 proof:

> “closedness of Pre follows from closed values of Succ and compactness of U,D — standard”

### Flaw

The predecessor operator is

\[
\mathrm{Pre}(W)
=
\{x\in K:\exists u\in U\ \forall d\in D:\ \mathrm{Succ}(x,u,d)\subseteq W\}.
\]

To prove that \(\mathrm{Pre}(W)\) is closed when \(W\) is closed, one takes a sequence

\[
x_n\to x,\qquad
u_n\in U,\qquad
\mathrm{Succ}(x_n,u_n,d)\subseteq W\quad\forall d.
\]

Passing to a subsequence \(u_n\to u^*\), the proof needs

\[
\mathrm{Succ}(x,u^*,d)\subseteq W.
\]

This requires **joint upper semicontinuity of \(\mathrm{Succ}\) in \((x,u)\)**, or at least a closed-graph condition. The stated hypothesis only says:

> “Hausdorff-upper-semicontinuous in \(u\) uniformly in \((x,d)\)”

That is not enough, because \(x_n\) is also moving. A sequence of sets \(\mathrm{Succ}(x_n,u_n,d)\) can remain inside \(W\) while their limit jumps outside \(W\) if \(\mathrm{Succ}\) is only upper semicontinuous in \(u\).

### Severity

High. The compactness/finite-intersection proof of `R03.Lem4` depends on closedness of each \(R_n\). `R07.Thm5` inherits this.

### Proposed upgrade

Add to the hypotheses of `R03.Lem4`:

> \(\mathrm{Succ}\) is Hausdorff-upper-semicontinuous jointly in \((x,u)\), uniformly in \(d\), with closed values.

Or assume the stronger packet condition already present in corrected 08:

> \(\mathrm{Succ}\) is Hausdorff-continuous in \((x,u)\).

Then the proof becomes valid.

## 3. R05.Cor3 — the “iff \(A^{-1}b\ge 0\)” feasibility statement is only for the linearized sufficient condition

### Location

R05 Field 3, `R05.Cor3`, part 1:

> “the feasibility condition of Thm2 (without the tubular cap) holds iff \(A^{-1}b\ge 0\)”

### Flaw

This is true only under the linear tightening bound

\[
\delta_{ij}(r)\le \delta_{ij}(0)-\sigma_{ij}r.
\]

The actual feasibility condition in `R05.Thm2` is

\[
L_i r_i+\Lambda_i\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{ij}(r_j)+\Delta_i\le \alpha_i.
\]

If the true interface bounds \(\delta_{ij}\) are nonlinear and decrease more slowly than the linear bound, the linearized inequality may be infeasible while the true `Thm2` condition is still feasible. Conversely, since the linear bound is an upper bound, linearized feasibility implies true feasibility, but **not conversely**.

So the word “iff” is too strong.

### Severity

Medium. The theorem’s positive direction remains useful, but the record overclaims exactness.

### Proposed upgrade

Restate as:

> Under the linear tightening bounds \(\delta_{ij}(r)\le \delta_{ij}(0)-\sigma_{ij}r\), the linearized sufficient condition \(Ar\le b\) is feasible iff \(A^{-1}b\ge 0\). Hence `Thm2` is certified under those bounds. If the bounds are nonlinear, one must solve the exact monotone feasibility problem
>
> \[
> \exists r\ge 0:\quad
> L_i r_i+\Lambda_i\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{ij}(r_j)+\Delta_i\le \alpha_i
> \quad\forall i.
> \]

## 4. R09.M1 — the proof only establishes instability near \(\tau=\pi/2\), not for every \(\tau>\pi/2\)

### Location

R09 Field 3, Part M1:

> “Instantiation II: \(\dot x=-x(t-\tau)\) — instability for every \(\tau>\pi/2\)”

Field 8 proof:

> “for every \(\tau\in(\pi/2,\pi/2+\delta)\) there is a root \(\lambda\) with \(\mathrm{Re}\lambda>0\)”

### Flaw

The proof computes the eigenvalue crossing at \(\tau=\pi/2\) and shows that a root enters the right half-plane. That proves instability **for some** \(\tau>\pi/2\), not for all \(\tau>\pi/2\).

The stronger claim is true for this scalar equation, but it is not proved here. A referee cannot accept a derivative computation at one point as a proof of global instability for all later delay values.

### Severity

Medium. The independence result only needs one unstable delay value, but the record states a stronger universal-instantiation claim.

### Proposed upgrade

Either:

1. prove the global claim, e.g. via the characteristic equation or Nyquist criterion, or  
2. weaken the statement to:

> “there exists \(\tau>\pi/2\) such that \(\dot x=-x(t-\tau)\) is unstable.”

That is sufficient for the M1 independence witness.

## 5. R09.M5 — incorrect kernel statement in the “opposite” witness

### Location

R09 Field 8, Part M5:

> “no-fold-with-kernel-collapse (e.g., \(\dot x=1\) on \(K=[0,1]\): no bifurcation, kernel collapses to \(\{1\}\)... in fact empty of interior; either way trivially opposite)”

### Flaw

The viability kernel of

\[
\dot x=1,\qquad K=[0,1]
\]

is **empty**, not \(\{1\}\). At \(x=1\), the velocity is \(1>0\), so the trajectory exits immediately. Every state in \([0,1]\) exits in finite time. The statement “kernel collapses to \(\{1\}\)” is false.

The phrase “either way trivially opposite” is also not a proof.

### Severity

Medium. It weakens the Part M register, which is supposed to be a permanent counterexample record.

### Proposed upgrade

Use a cleaner parameterized witness with no local bifurcation:

\[
\dot x=\mu-x,\qquad K=[0,1].
\]

Then:

- for \(0<\mu<1\), every trajectory entering \([\mu-\varepsilon,\mu+\varepsilon]\subseteq K\) stays in \(K\);
- for \(\mu>1\), the upper boundary is immediately violated;
- the vector field has a unique hyperbolic equilibrium \(x=\mu\) for every \(\mu\), so there is no local bifurcation.

Thus the viability kernel changes with \(\mu\) without any local bifurcation, giving the required opposite witness.

## 6. R02.Thm1 — “computable” policy is not proved

### Location

R02 Field 3, `R02.Thm1`:

> “π is computable from \((C_k,c_k)\) alone”

### Flaw

The proof chooses an arbitrary witness of the regulation condition:

\[
\pi_k := u^{cmd}\quad\text{any witness of }(REG)\text{ at }(C_k,c_k).
\]

That is an axiom-of-choice level selector, not a computable function. The record later acknowledges selector regularity as an open obligation, but the theorem statement uses the stronger word **“computable”**.

### Severity

Low-to-medium. The mathematical invariance claim is unaffected, but the implementation claim is stronger than proved.

### Proposed upgrade

Change “computable” to:

> “causally determined by \((C_k,c_k)\)”

or add an explicit computable-selection hypothesis.

## 7. R07.Thm5 — compactness across architecture changes is not well-typed

### Location

R07 Field 3, `R07.Thm5`, and Field 8 proof.

### Flaw

`R07.Def1` allows generation-dependent architectures \(q(g)\), so the safe sets \(K_g\) live in different phase spaces \(Z_{q(g)}\). But `R07.Thm5` assumes:

\[
K_{g+1}\subseteq K_g
\]

as nested compact sets. That inclusion is not well-typed unless the phase spaces are the same or a common embedding has been declared.

The proof then applies `R03.Lem4` to a generation-indexed predecessor chain, but the predecessor operator is defined per generation in a possibly different space.

### Severity

Medium. The theorem is formally ambiguous.

### Proposed upgrade

Add an explicit common compact enclosure:

> There exists a compact metric space \(\mathcal C\) and typed embeddings \(\iota_g:Z_{q(g)}\to\mathcal C\) such that \(\iota_g(K_g)\supseteq \iota_{g+1}(K_{g+1})\).

Then formulate the nestedness and finite-intersection argument in \(\mathcal C\). Alternatively, restrict `R07.Thm5` to the fixed-architecture case and state the architecture-changing compactness closure as a separate conditional result.

## 8. R06.Thm3 is sound, but its relation to R06.Thm2 must be clarified

### Issue

`R06.Thm3` proves that no finite family of **moments** closes the quadratic field dynamics. That proof is solid.

`R06.Thm2` claims a much broader obstruction for arbitrary finite-dimensional augmentations. Because `R06.Thm2` is flawed as stated, the record currently gives the impression that `Thm3` follows as an instance of a general theorem, when in fact `Thm3` is the rigorous part and `Thm2` is conditional/ill-posed.

### Proposed upgrade

Downgrade `R06.Thm2` to a **conditional programme** or restate it with a precise linear-observable assumption as proposed above. Make the dependency explicit:

\[
\text{R06.Thm3 is proved.}
\]
\[
\text{R06.Thm2 is open/conditional in full generality.}
\]

---

## 9. Minor consistency issues

### 9.1 `R03.Lem4` proof of \(R_\infty\subseteq \mathrm{Pre}(R_\infty)\)

The subsequence argument is basically correct, but it implicitly uses sequential compactness of \(U\) and joint upper semicontinuity. Once the missing hypothesis of issue 2 is added, this proof is fine.

### 9.2 `R05.Cor3` and nonlinear gains

The record rightly refuses to put nonlinear gains into a numerical matrix. The linear gain operator \(\Gamma\) is legitimate. But the feasibility statement should not be called exact for nonlinear interface bounds.

### 9.3 `R09.M5` and “trivially opposite”

Several Part M witnesses are rigorous, but the M5 opposite direction is not. The whole Part M register should meet the same standard as the other five items.

## Final line

The packet is **repairable**, but not yet closure-ready. The three high-priority repairs are:

1. re-prove or restate `R06.Thm2`;
2. add joint upper semicontinuity to `R03.Lem4`; and
3. correct the overclaim in `R05.Cor3`.

Without those, the corresponding records should not carry the verdict **proved** in a publication.
