### Internal inconsistencies

**1. Abstract / §1.2 vs Theorem 5(4) on what the gap is.**  
Abstract and §1.2 say the gap between per-weight and common-plan acceptance is a region *partitioned* by STAGED into a rescue set \(R\) and an impossibility set \(I\). Theorem 5(4) states \(\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}} = \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} = I\). Because \(\{x\ge 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}\), one has \(R\subset\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}\). \(R\) is **not** in the gap. What STAGED partitions is \(\mathrm{FP}_0\) (sum-vs-floors), not \(\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}}\). That is a direct contradiction between the paper’s lead claim and its theorem.

**2. Title / keywords vs §5.1.**  
Title: “Formal Separation of Weak and Strong Sustainability.” §5.1: the operators are *not* weak/strong sustainability simpliciter. Those two cannot both stand at full strength.

**3. Canonical tuple \(C\) vs weight cone \(C\).**  
§2.2: \(C\) = command architecture. §3.1: \(C=\mathbb{R}^n_+\setminus\{0\}\). Same letter, different objects, both in force for the rest of the paper. \(A\) is also overloaded (assessment operator vs action set in §5.5).

**4. “Closed cone” that is not closed.**  
Lemma 2 / Theorem 3(ii) use \(C=\mathbb{R}^n_+\setminus\{0\}\), which is neither closed nor open. The zero vector is correctly excluded; the name is still wrong. (The identity also holds for the open positive cone, so “closed-cone choice isolates a purely dynamic separation” is slightly overstated.)

**5. Theorem 5 lists “three sets” and then four** (\(\mathrm{FP}_0,R,I,\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}}\)). Harmless, but of a piece with the \(\mathrm{FP}_0\)/gap conflation.

**6. Own modelling discipline is not applied to the witness.**  
§2.2: a model *is* a specified 13-tuple. §4.5 never instantiates \(T,B_{\mathrm{out}},V,\Gamma,O,A,C,R,P,K\). §2.5’s “no silent transfer” is violated by the paper’s own main example. §2.6’s failure taxonomy is unused; the “impossibility region” is not classified as action-menu infeasibility vs physical infeasibility.

**7. Disturbance object \(\{\beta,\alpha\}\) vs the table.**  
If \(\forall d\in\{\alpha,\beta\}\) and the two disturbances are *coordinate-wise* hits (α dips \(s_1\), β dips \(s_2\)) on every action, then FAST must survive an \(s_2\) dip as well, typed FAST becomes \(s_1\ge 2\) *and* \(s_2\ge 2\), and the separation collapses. The table only works if dips are **action-indexed** (FAST is vulnerable only on \(s_1\)). The text never says that. This is the load-bearing modelling ambiguity.

### Flaws (substance, not typos)

**8. \(I\) is not genuine impossibility — it is exhaustion of four named actions.**  
Theorem 5(7) and the abstract (“admit no typed-safe transition”) read as “no path exists.” The certificate is: NO-SWITCH, FAST, SLOW, STAGED each violate something. A mixed action (dip \(s_1\) by \(\delta\in[0,2]\), \(s_2\) by \(2-\delta\), or smaller simultaneous dips) can be typed-safe on a large subset of \(I\) whenever \(s_1,s_2\ge 0\) and dips do not cross zero. §5.5(1) later admits this; the theorem name, abstract, and “genuine impossibility” do not. Under the paper’s own §2.6 rule, the obstruction is **action-set incompleteness**, not physical impossibility.

That does not kill the *existence* separation (a sparse menu is a legal datum). It does kill the rhetoric that \(I\) is a structural void in compensatory aggregation rather than a hole in \(A\).

**9. Category mismatch with the cited weak-sustainability core.**  
Genuine savings / inclusive wealth: \(\frac{d}{dt}(p\cdot K)\ge 0\) (or nondeclining wealth), prices endogenous. Here: *level* floors \(s_i\ge 0\) vs \(w\cdot s\ge 0\), \(w\) any nonnegative weight, \(x\) *excluded* from the aggregate. So:
- this is compensatory **constraint scalarization**, not Hartwick/GS;
- the bridge stock that “rescues” is omitted from inclusive wealth, which is the opposite of the literature being positioned against.

§5.1’s disclaimer does not repair the title, abstract, or §1.1 framing.

**10. \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}=\bigcap_w\mathcal{V}_w\) is an unusual “weak” reading.**  
Standard weak: *some* accounting prices, or the *correct* shadow prices. Here: **every** \(w\) has its own robust action. That is the *strictest* compensatory reading (Theorem 4), which is mathematically the right one for a separation, but it is not how weak sustainability is used in World Bank / Neumayer / genuine-savings practice. A practitioner who fixes one \(w\) lives in \(\mathcal{V}_w\), which is larger, and the common-plan comparison is then different.

**11. Proposition 1’s equality condition is not a theorem.**  
“FIP + compactness/closedness” is a gesture. Action space is not topologized. The paper does not need it: Theorem 3(ii) goes through Lemma 2, and the witness has \(|A|=4\). Prop 1 is the elementary quantifier fact they elsewhere decline priority on.

**12. Theorem 6 does not support the introduction’s “during a transition.”**  
Holds prepend identity maps. Strictness persists because nothing happens. §6.1(iii) honestly says a later stage can erase the gap. The dynamical claim of the paper is therefore **one review interval** (plus trivial padding). That is a real gap relative to viability / multi-stage sustainability.

**13. Exact-tube witness is underspecified as a trajectory.**  
FAST: mid-interval \(s_1-2\), successor \((1,x,s+e)\) with original \(s\). So the dip is fully reversed by \(t=1\) before \(e\) is applied — a V-shaped tube that is never written as a function of \(t\). STAGED “floors grow” and also get \(+e\). For constraint checking the min/max suffice; for a paper that advertises *exact* tubes and machine-checked breakpoints \(\{0,\tfrac12,1\}\), the paths should be one line each.

**14. Machine layer cannot support what it is stacked under.**  
29,791 \(=31^3\): a truncated box, unspecified range, 25 unnamed checks, artifact not in the manuscript. Correctly labelled as not a continuum proof. Then it should not appear in the abstract as a co-equal verification of “the finite rational instance” of the *theorem*.

**15. §2 is ornamental.**  
Types, four uncertainty levels, four model maps, ten failure classes, 13-tuple: none are premises of Theorems 3–6. They inflate the claim of a “typed framework” while the math uses a finite-horizon robust reach-avoid instance.

**16. Lemma 2 is applied to \(s\) only; \(x\) is kept outside the index.**  
If \(x\) is capital, STAGED’s spend should hit \(w\cdot(x,s)\). Leaving \(x\) unweighted is what makes STAGED universally \(w\)-admissible for \(x\ge 1\). That is a design choice that *creates* \(R\), not a consequence of aggregation.

### What is actually correct (so upgrades do not “fix” non-bugs)

- Lemma 2 and Theorem 3(i)–(ii) are right: at a **fixed** tube, full-cone scalarization is lossless, so \(E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)=\bigcap_w E_w(z)\). The gap can only be quantifier order on **which** action is chosen.

The paper’s real theorem, stripped: *there exists a finite-horizon exact-tube datum and a 4-element menu such that \(\{z:\bigcap_w E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}\subsetneq\bigcap_w\{z:E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}\), with the difference equal to \(I\), and on \(\mathrm{int}\,I\) FAST/SLOW licensing splits at \(\rho_1,\rho_2\).* That is true.

## 1. Flaws

### 1.1 Abstract and §1.2 misidentify the gap (load-bearing)

Theorem 5(4) is unambiguous:

\[
\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}} = \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} = I.
\]

\(R = \mathrm{FP}_0\cap\{x\ge 1\}\) is a subset of \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}\) (STAGED is typed-admissible there). It is **not** in the acceptance gap.

The abstract says the gap “contains a relatively open region, which a resource-controlled action partitions into a rescue set and a genuine impossibility set.” §1.2 says the same: “the gap between the two acceptance criteria is a region with nonempty interior — split … into a rescue set … and a genuine impossibility region.”

That is false of \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}\). What is partitioned is \(\mathrm{FP}_0\) (aggregate budget met, neither floor survives its own dip, \(x\) free). The genuine acceptance-criteria gap is only \(I\). \(R\) is the part of \(\mathrm{FP}_0\) that is **not** a false positive.

The body already draws this distinction carefully. The front matter undoes it.

### 1.2 Witness datum is not a fully specified transition datum

Section 4.5 never defines \(\mathrm{Tube}(a,d)\) on the full product (4 actions \(\times\) \(\{\beta,\alpha\}\)). It gives a “worst case” column, a disturbance set that “scales the depth of a worst-case dip of size 2,” and piecewise-linear breakpoints. That is not enough to instantiate the operators of §3.1.

Missing, all of which the proofs silently assume:

- The actual path of \(s_1\) under FAST: V-shaped return to \(s_1\) at \(t=1\), then reset \(+e\), or dip-and-stay? The successor formula \((1,x,s+e)\) and the tube interval \([s_1-2,s_1]\) jointly require a return. Not written.
- What \(\alpha\) does to SLOW, what \(\beta\) does to FAST, what either does to STAGED/NO-SWITCH. If \(D\) is shared, \(\forall d\) is not “the worst-case column.”
- STAGED: “floors grow” vs successor \(s+e\) vs FAST/SLOW which also get \(+e\) without mid-interval growth. Either the growth **is** the reset, or the successor should be larger than \(s+e\).
- \(e=(1/4,1/4)\) is never used in any inequality of Theorem 5. Vestigial. If it is a destination reset, say so and drop it from the geometry; if it is load-bearing, exhibit the inequality it binds.
- Destination maintainability is “a declared datum.” For a one-interval problem it does no work. Either construct the hold policy or delete the claim.

Theorem 5 is an existence separation on an explicit witness. The witness must be a function, not a table of binding slices.

### 1.3 \(E_{\mathrm{end}}\) is not “constraint structure only”

§3.1: “they share the same disturbance quantifier and differ only in constraint structure.” False of \(E_{\mathrm{end}}\), which also changes the evaluation map (\(\mathrm{End}\) vs \(\mathrm{Tube}\)). The chain

\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}\subseteq E_w\subseteq E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}\subseteq E_{\mathrm{end}}
\]

mixes two axes (typed/aggregate/phys **and** tube/endpoint). The interesting missing operators are typed-endpoint and aggregate-endpoint. The introduction’s own motivation — “aggregated accounting in practice is frequently endpoint accounting” — is exactly that missing cell, and it is not proved.

### 1.4 Proposition 1’s “equivalently” is not equivalent

The equality condition “common selector on the RHS” is tautological. FIP + compactness is a **sufficient** condition in some topology on the action set, not an equivalent one. On the witness \(|A|=4\), the topology is discrete, compactness is free, and distinct values of \(E_w\) are finite in number (\(\le 16\)). The compactness clause is dead text. Either delete it or state a real lemma (finite \(A\) \(\Rightarrow\) only finitely many distinct \(E_w\) \(\Rightarrow\) FIP \(\Leftrightarrow\) nonempty total intersection).

### 1.5 §1.2 cites the wrong theorem for the always-valid inclusion

“The paper proves the always-valid inclusion (Theorem 3).” That inclusion is Proposition 1. Theorem 3 is the operator identity and the nested-constraint chain. Small, but this paper’s stated discipline is status-and-citation hygiene.

### 1.6 \(\mathrm{FP}_0\) vertex description is wrong

Figure 1: “upper-right triangle with vertices \((0,2),\ (2,0),\ (2,2)\), subject to the strict boundaries \(s_i<2\).”

\(\mathrm{FP}_0\) requires \(s_1<2,\ s_2<2,\ s_1+s_2\ge 2\). So \((0,2),\ (2,0),\ (2,2)\) are all **outside** \(\mathrm{FP}_0\). The set is the open-sided triangle including the open hypotenuse and excluding the two legs \(s_1=2\) and \(s_2=2\).

### 1.7 Interior of \(I\) is written ambiguously and omits \(x>0\)

“\(\{0<s_1,\ s_2<2,\ s_1+s_2>2,\ x<1\}\)” does not parse as \(0<s_1<2,\ 0<s_2<2\). And \(I\subset\{x\ge 0\}\), so the interior in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) (or the relative interior in \(X_0\)) needs \(x>0\). The open set you actually want is

## 2. Internal inconsistencies

### 2.1 “Rescue set” vs rescue-as-augmentation (naming vs §5.5)

Theorem 5: \(R\) is where STAGED already works — no extra resource needed.

§5.5: \(r^*=1-x\) **converts \(I\)-states into typed-admissible states**. Rescue is an operation on \(I\), with image in \(\{x\ge 1\}\).

### 2.2 Closed-cone rhetoric vs what the cone is actually doing

Theorem 3(ii) does **not** need the full cone. The extreme rays \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) already give

\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)=\bigcap_{i=1}^n E_{e_i}(z),
\]

because \(e_i\cdot s=s_i\). The full cone is needed for a different fact: it minimises \(\mathcal{V}_W\) among compensatory readings (Theorem 4). On the witness this split is sharp and unused:

- \(\bigcap_i E_{e_i}=E_{\mathrm{typ}}\) (identity already at pure-floor weights);
- \(\bigcap_i \mathcal{V}_{e_i}=X_0\supsetneq\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}\) (SLOW is \(e_1\)-admissible everywhere on \(X_0\); FAST is \(e_2\)-admissible everywhere).

So “closed-cone choice isolating the separation as purely dynamic” is true of **accepted sets**, not of the action-set identity. The paper attributes both jobs to Theorem 3(ii). Split them.

### 2.3 Weak-sustainability naming vs the operator

\(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}=\bigcap_w\mathcal{V}_w\) is **the most demanding compensatory reading** (every weight has a plan). Literature “weak sustainability” is often \(\exists w\) (some prices make inclusive wealth work), which is \(\bigcup_w\mathcal{V}_w\), strictly larger. §5.1 owns this, then the rest of the paper uses “compensatory / weak / per-weight” interchangeably. The abstract does not own it.

This matters: the result is stronger than a casual reading thinks (even the strictest full-cone compensatory viability over-certifies), and weaker than a genuine-savings reading thinks (you never include \(x\) in the aggregate; you never take market prices).

### 2.4 Status-labelling promise vs delivery

§1.1 promises every claim labelled definition / theorem / conditional / open. Section 2’s taxonomies, the four operators, \(\mathcal{V}[\cdot]\), \(\mathrm{FP}_0,R,I,\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}}\), and \(\mathsf{Aug}_r,r^*\) are not labelled. The 10-item “minimum report” in §5.4 is presented as implication of the theorem; items (3)–(10) are framework wishlist. The discipline the paper demands of others is not applied uniformly to itself.

### 2.5 Tube-vs-endpoint collapse, claimed and unclaimed

§3.1: last inclusion “strict in general, though it collapses on the witness.” Theorem 5(3) only equates the **accepted sets** \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{end}}=X_0\). On this witness the **action sets** also coincide (STAGED with \(x<1\) fails both tube and endpoint on \(x\); FAST/SLOW pass both). So the collapse is stronger than stated, and the endpoint operator does no separating work at all. Either change the witness so \(\mathrm{End}\subsetneq\mathrm{Tube}\) binds, or drop \(E_{\mathrm{end}}\) from the hierarchy display.

### 2.6 “No single action satisfies all typed floors simultaneously” (abstract)

Parsed as a property of a state, that is “the current point is already infeasible.” You mean: no single action **keeps** all floors nonnegative along its tube. The whole paper is about trajectories; the abstract sentence is static.

### 2.7 Quantifier that actually fails

§2.3: “Section 3 studies what happens when a second quantifier — over scalarization weights — interleaves with this one,” where “this one” is \(\exists\pi\,\forall d\). In §3 the disturbance quantifier never moves: it stays innermost, \(\forall d\), inside every \(E\). The noncommutation is \(\exists a\,\forall w\) vs \(\forall w\,\exists a_w\). Say that. Do not suggest a three-way interleaving you do not study.

## 4. Profound upgrades

### 4.1 State the two gaps as two theorems

**Gap A (static, in the \(s\)-plane).** Budget vs floors: \(\{s_1+s_2\ge 2\}\) vs \(\{s_1\ge 2\}\cup\{s_2\ge 2\}\). This is \(\mathrm{FP}_0\), independent of actions, and is **not** the assessment gap.

**Gap B (dynamic, in accepted states).** \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}=I=\mathrm{FP}_0\cap\{x<1\}\).

**Resource split.** \(x\) does not partition Gap B; it partitions Gap A into “typed-admissible via STAGED” and “compensatory-only.” That one sentence repairs abstract, §1.2, Figure 1 caption, and §5.5 at once.

### 4.2 Make the forcing destination a named necessary condition

If NO-SWITCH were typed-safe, \(E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)\ne\varnothing\) on all of \(X_0\) and Gap B vanishes. Separation **requires** an obligation that rules out stay-put (here \(G\) demands \(q=1\)). That is the content of “transition assessment.” Elevate it: a lemma that if a typed-safe inaction exists at \(z\), then \(z\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}\) and the quantifiers commute at \(z\). Theorem 6 then reads as: hold-prefixes do not create a stay-put escape because the terminal obligation is preserved.

### 4.3 Extreme-ray vs full-cone as an explicit lemma

Add: for any \(z\),

\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)=\bigcap_{i}E_{e_i}(z)=\bigcap_{w\in C}E_w(z),
\]

but on the witness

\[
\bigcap_{i}\mathcal{V}_{e_i}=X_0\supsetneq\bigcap_{w\in C}\mathcal{V}_w=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}.
\]

This is the cleanest explanation of why “more prices” **shrinks** compensatory acceptance and why the remaining gap after the full cone is purely \(\exists a\forall w\) vs \(\forall w\exists a\). It also blocks the mistaken thought that checking the \(n\) pure-floor indices already captures \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}\).

### 4.4 Fill the 2×2 of evaluation maps

|  | tube | endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| typed floors | \(E_{\mathrm{typ}}\) | \(E_{\mathrm{typ,end}}\) (missing) |
| aggregate \(w\) | \(E_w\) | \(E_{w,\mathrm{end}}\) (missing) |
| physical only | \(E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}\) | \(E_{\mathrm{end}}\) |

On **this** witness \(E_{\mathrm{typ,end}}\) would accept FAST/SLOW on all of \(X_0\) (endpoints return to \(s\ge 0\)), so the practice-relevant comparison the introduction advertises — snapshot indices vs trajectory floors — is actually \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ,end}}\) vs \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}\), not \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}\) vs \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}\). You can keep Theorem 5 as the quantifier result and add one proposition: endpoint-typed collapses to \(X_0\), hence snapshot accounting cannot detect even the typed dips that Theorem 5 uses. That closes the loop with “the index is evaluated on audited snapshots.”

To make \(E_{\mathrm{end}}\) vs \(E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}\) strict, put a physical dip that recovers by the endpoint (non-monotone \(x\)). Currently \(x\) is monotone, so the inclusion cannot be strict on action sets.

### 4.5 Write the common-selector certificate as the composite-index bridge

Conclusion already says this in prose. Formalise:

> A weight family \(W\) certifies typed transition safety at \(z\) iff \(\bigcap_{w\in W}E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\). Checking \(\bigcap_{w\in W}\mathcal{V}_w\) is not sufficient.

That **is** Theorem 3(ii) + Proposition 1, and it is the only implication for composite indices that the math supports. The 10-item report then collapses to: (1) floors and provenance, (2) \(W\), (3) a common-selector witness or a negative certificate exhausting \(A\), (4) tube status, (5) completeness status of \(A\). The rest is not a theorem.

### 4.6 \(n\)-floor witness and the covering geometry

For \(n>2\), one dip-action per coordinate, same depth \(\delta\), the condition “every \(w\in\Delta^{n-1}\) licenses at least one dip-action” is a covering of the simplex by \(n\) regions \(\{w:w_i\le (w\cdot s)/\delta\}\). The L1-budget \( \sum s_i\ge\delta \) is necessary (same algebra) but the covering may fail on parts of \(\{\sum s_i\ge\delta\}\cap\{s<\delta\mathbf{1}\}\) when \(n\ge 3\) if you only have those \(n\) actions. Either the gap **grows** with \(n\), or you need more actions. This is the first genuine extension that is still exact-tube and still rational. It also answers “is \(s_1+s_2\ge 2\) an artefact of \(n=2\)?”

### 4.7 Connect \(r^*\) to genuine savings without claiming identity

On \(I\), compensatory assessment says “the budget \(s_1+s_2\) is enough”; typed assessment says “buy \(1-x\) units of reserve and take STAGED.” That is the precise sense in which **investment in a named stock** substitutes for **reweighting**, and reweighting cannot. It is also the precise sense in which this is not genuine savings: \(x\) is not in the aggregate, the increment is not a shadow-price NNP, and the criterion is set-viability not welfare. One paragraph in §5.1 closing that citation loop would earn the World Bank / Neumayer / Asheim references, which currently decorate.

### 4.8 Complete the datum, then keep the proof

Specify, for example:

- Time \(\{0,1/2,1\}\), affine on each piece.
- FAST: \(s_1(t)=s_1-4t\) on \([0,1/2]\), \(s_1-4(1-t)\) on \([1/2,1]\); \(s_2,x\) constant; at \(t=1^+\) apply \(q\leftarrow 1,\ s\leftarrow s+e\). One disturbance realises this path; the other is dominated (no dip or shallower).
- SLOW: symmetric.
- STAGED: \(x(t)=x-t\), \(s(t)=s\) on \([0,1]\), then same reset; or \(s(t)\) affine to \(s+e\) with no extra reset. Pick one.
- NO-SWITCH: constant, no reset.
- \(S^{\mathrm{phys}}=\{x\ge 0\}\), \(G^{\mathrm{phys}}=\{q=1,x\ge 0\}\).
- Delete \(e\) or use it.

Then Theorem 5’s proof is unchanged but the object it talks about exists.
