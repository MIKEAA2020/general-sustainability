Here is a bedrock-level critique of the intervention-selection manuscript. This pass bypasses surface methodology and directly targets the algebraic topology of the viability kernels, the mathematical mismatch between robust control theory and population biology, and the downstream consequences of boundary-pinned optimization. 

### 1. The Deepest Axiomatic Clashes

**The Growth vs. Control Contradiction (The Lipschitz/Biology Paradox)**
*   **Location:** Section 3.4 (Certified layer: the expansion obstruction).
*   **The Flaw:** You state the governed map is expansive at the LRP ($F' = 1.153 > 1$), making the contraction form of the safety margin inapplicable and emptying the kernel beyond $T=5$. In robust control theory, an expansion rate $> 1$ is indeed an obstruction because uncertainty explodes. But in population biology, $F' > 1$ defines the exponential growth phase of a logistic curve. A stock *must* be in an expansive phase (where $S < K/2$) to generate high surplus production (yield). 
*   **The Bedrock Issue:** Your certification mathematics require a Lipschitz constant $a < 1$ (a contracting map). For the logistic model, this only occurs when $S > K/2$ (when the population is near carrying capacity and density dependence violently suppresses growth). By demanding a contracting closed loop, the viability framework inherently outlaws certifying any stock operating near $B_{\mathrm{MSY}}$ or attempting to recover from collapse. 
*   **The Upgrade:** Do not present this merely as an "obstruction" to Northern cod. Present it as a profound epistemological clash between viability theory and fisheries science. State explicitly: *"The framework's requirement for a contracting closed loop ($F' < 1$) is mathematically antagonistic to the biological requirement for surplus production ($F' > 1$). The certification layer structurally rejects populations in their recovery or high-yield phases."*

**The Epistemology of Perpetual Shocks vs. Transient Collapse**
*   **Location:** Section 2 (Methods) and Section 3.1.
*   **The Flaw:** The disturbance classes apply the worst one-step residual ($-460.0$ kt yr$^{-1}$) as a *persistent additive floor*. 
*   **The Bedrock Issue:** This is algebraically doomed. The maximum theoretical surplus production of your fitted model is $g_{\max} = rK/4 = 296$ kt yr$^{-1}$. If you apply a perpetual annual mortality shock of $-460$ kt to a system that can biologically only generate $+296$ kt, the mathematical fixed point requires $S_{t+1} - S_t = -164$ kt yr$^{-1}$. Extinction is an algebraic certainty regardless of catch. 
*   **The Upgrade:** In Section 3.2, you frame this as "no catch policy — zero catch included — holds the LRP." This implies a failure of governance. It is not. It is a mathematical proof that the magnitude of the 1992 transient anomaly exceeded the maximum theoretical productivity ($g_{\max}$) of the stationary model. Revise to clarify: *"Because the worst-case observed transient shock exceeds the theoretical $g_{\max}$ of the fitted model, simulating it as a perpetual floor structurally guarantees extinction. This constitutes a mathematical proof of scale, not a failure of demand management."*

### 2. Internal Inconsistencies & Metric Flaws

**The Reactive-Rule Penalty (Comparing Step-Functions to Moratoria)**
*   **Location:** Section 3.2 (Selection).
*   **The Flaw:** The retention rule rejects the reactive S1 and cascade policies because they are "strictly less protective than the moratorium at the boundary". 
*   **The Bedrock Issue:** This is a category error in policy evaluation. BAU is a permanent, open-loop near-zero cap (5 kt). S1 is a state-dependent step-function allowing 60 kt strictly *above* the LRP. Right at the boundary ($884.6 + \epsilon$), S1 permits 60 kt; BAU permits 5 kt. Therefore, S1's viability kernel is mathematically smaller. But penalizing S1 for this is absurd—S1's explicit design purpose is to allow harvest when the stock is safe. A permanent ban will always mathematically yield a larger safe set than a reactive harvest rule. 
*   **The Fix:** Your preregistered retention rule (Clause a: "at least as protective as BAU's at every reading") structurally prevents the retention of *any* reactive harvest control rule compared to a moratorium. You must explicitly confess this in the Discussion. State: *"The retention criteria structurally bias against state-dependent harvest control rules, as any rule permitting harvest above the LRP will mathematically shrink the viability kernel compared to a permanent moratorium."*

**The Artificiality of the $K$ Bound dictating the Theorem**
*   **Location:** Section 2 ($K = 5000$ kt pinned at optimization bound) and Section 3.4.
*   **The Flaw:** The entire "expansion obstruction" finding in Section 3.4 rests on the calculation that $F'(K^*) = 1.153 > 1$. This derivative depends entirely on $K$. 
*   **The Bedrock Issue:** Because $K$ hit your artificial optimization bound of 5000 kt, the inflection point of the logistic curve ($K/2$) is artificially placed at 2500 kt. This forces the LRP (884.6 kt) deep into the expansive zone. If the optimizer had converged at $K = 1500$ kt, the LRP would be in the contracting zone ($> 750$ kt), and the certified layer might hold. The most profound theoretical finding of this paper is entirely downstream of a failed optimization parameter.
*   **The Fix:** This is a critical vulnerability. In Section 4 (Discussion), you must declare: *"The classification of the LRP as an expansive boundary ($F' > 1$) is entirely contingent on the carrying capacity $K$ hitting its upper optimization bound (5000 kt). Had the data supported a lower identified $K$, the boundary could have been mathematically contracting. The certified-layer obstruction is therefore highly sensitive to the parameterization bounds."*

### 3. Gap Closures & Conceptual Framing

**Defining the "Constructive Boundary" Topography**
*   **Location:** Section 3.3.
*   **The Gap:** You identify 57.6 kt as the maximal robust flat catch under the 10th-percentile class. But you do not explain the topography of *why* this number exists. 
*   **The Closure:** Add one sentence to anchor this algebraically. It exists because $57.6$ is exactly the residual between the model's natural surplus production at the LRP ($172.4$ kt) and the 10th-percentile disturbance floor ($114.8$ kt). Stating this explicitly demystifies the viability kernel output, showing readers that the "robust boundary" is simply the algebraic remainder of baseline surplus minus expected persistent anomaly. 

**The Replay Misdirection**
*   **Location:** Section 3.5 (Stress replay).
*   **The Gap:** The stress replay from 1990 uses observed residuals. You note that zero catch holds 1991 above the limit, but falls below by 1992. 
*   **The Closure:** You note in Section 2 that the 1990 stock was already at 861.9 kt (below the 884.6 LRP). Simulating a viability kernel's defence of a boundary when the initial condition is *already outside the safe set* is mathematically undefined in standard viability theory (you are in the capture basin, not the viability kernel). Clarify that this replay tests *recovery* dynamics under observed shocks, not *maintenance* viability.

### 1. The Epistemological Paradox of the Falsified Plant
*   **Location:** Section 1 (Introduction) and Section 2 (Methods).
*   **The Flaw:** You state explicitly in the Introduction that the underlying governed object (the 1D surplus production model) received a "negative certificate" in the companion study because it failed to beat naive persistence out-of-sample. You then use this *exact falsified model* as the deterministic core (the plant) to calculate viability kernels.
*   **The Bedrock Issue:** This is an epistemological paradox. A viability kernel defines the exact boundary of survival for a specific dynamic system. If you have already mathematically proven that Northern cod *does not follow this dynamic system*, then the viability kernel you have calculated has zero physical mapping to the real ocean. You are calculating, to multiple decimal places, the precise survival coordinates of a ghost ship.
*   **The Upgrade:** You must justify why a falsified predictive model is a valid governance testbed. Frame it strictly as a *methodological stress-test of the policy architecture*, not a statement about the actual cod stock. State: *"While the companion study falsified this model class for empirical prediction, it is retained here exclusively as a synthetic mathematical object to demonstrate how viability kernels process catastrophic defects. The resulting boundaries evaluate the logic of the governance architecture, not the physical limits of Northern cod."*

### 2. The Erroneous Compounding of Additive Shock
*   **Location:** Section 2 (Methods) and Section 3.4 (Certified layer).
*   **The Flaw:** The certified layer calculates the safety erosion margin using the formula $r_T = \varepsilon(a_{\max}^T-1)/(a_{\max}-1)$. 
*   **The Bedrock Issue:** Look closely at the physics of this formula. It assumes that the model defect ($\varepsilon = 460$ kt) compounds geometrically at the system's maximum internal expansion rate ($a_{\max} = F'$). But the 1992 collapse was an *additive* mortality shock (likely exogenous environmental factors, capelin collapse, or unreported catch). Additive exogenous mortality does not geometrically self-replicate at the intrinsic biological growth rate of cod. By feeding an additive ecological shock into a geometric control-theory error bound, you have mathematically engineered an explosion of uncertainty ($r_8 = 6385.9$ kt) that violates the thermodynamics of the ecosystem.
*   **The Upgrade:** Acknowledge the physical mismatch of the error-propagation topology. State: *"The expansive certification formula assumes the model defect compounds geometrically with the system state. Because the observed 1992 defect was primarily an exogenous additive mortality event rather than an endogenous geometric divergence, the certification layer structurally over-penalizes the future state, leading to the rapid emptying of the kernel."*

### 3. The Omniscience Fallacy in "Governance"
*   **Location:** Section 2 (Methods - The governed object).
*   **The Flaw:** The paper claims to test "intervention selection" and "catch governance." The control equation assumes $C_t$ can be selected with perfect knowledge of $S_t$ at time $t$. 
*   **The Bedrock Issue:** Real fisheries governance operates with a minimum 1-to-2-year observation and assessment lag. In your companion paper, you explicitly modeled this exact lag as module `M4`. By stripping the information delay out of the governance equation here, you are not testing the viability of human governance; you are computing the viability of an omniscient deity. A true robust viability kernel for a system with a 1-year delayed observation manifold is vastly smaller—and often immediately empty—compared to a perfect-state-feedback kernel. 
*   **The Upgrade:** You must declare the absence of the observation delay as a critical limit of the governance simulation. Add to Section 4 (Discussion): *"The control loop assumes perfect, instantaneous observation of the stock to set $C_t$. Because true fisheries governance operates under assessment lags (the M4 module in the prediction ladder), the computed viability kernels represent a theoretical upper bound of control. Factoring in real-world information delays would further shrink or empty the robust safe sets."*

### 4. The Half-Space Absurdity at the Upper Boundary
*   **Location:** Section 2 (Safe set definition) and Section 3.1 (Table 1).
*   **The Flaw:** The safe set is defined as $K^* = \mathrm{LRP} = 884.6$ kt. Because viability theory looks for states that stay inside the safe set, it treats the safe set as a half-space: $[884.6, \infty)$. 
*   **The Bedrock Issue:** Look at Table 1. The nominal kernels for BAU and flat-0 kt extend to $10^4$ (10,000 kt). But your carrying capacity $K$ is pinned at 5,000 kt. The mathematics of the viability kernel are formally classifying states at *double the biological carrying capacity of the ecosystem* as "viable and safe." Biologically, a population massively overshooting $K$ would trigger catastrophic density-dependent starvation and crash below the LRP. 
*   **The Upgrade:** Viability in population biology requires an upper boundary. The safe set should not be $[LRP, \infty)$; it must be $[LRP, K]$. Acknowledge this boundary failure in the discussion: *"The nominal kernels extend to $10^4$ kt because the safe set was defined as an open half-space ($S \ge \mathrm{LRP}$). In reality, states vastly exceeding carrying capacity would trigger density-dependent collapse. The upper reaches of the computed kernels are therefore artifacts of the open boundary condition rather than biologically viable states."*
