**4. Brier explanation on Spec A is logically wrong.**  
§3.3: persist Brier = 0.00 “because every target year 1991–2015 is below the LRP, so the persistence indicator is always correct.” Brier uses \(f=\mathbf{1}\{\hat S_{t+h}<\mathrm{LRP}\}=\mathbf{1}\{S_t<\mathrm{LRP}\}\), not the target indicator. Either the 0.00 is a bug, origins were silently restricted, or \(S_t\) was already below LRP at every origin (then the parenthetical is still the wrong reason). Recompute and rewrite the sentence.

**5. Stated retention rule ≠ executed rule.**  
§1: retain structure if it improves **early warning, OOS prediction, or intervention selection**. Execution: primary RMSE vs next-simpler **and** vs persist. Intervention selection is never tested. Early warning (Brier) **improves** on Spec B at \(h=1\) (M3 0.02, M1 0.05 vs persist 0.06) and is discarded. Either narrow the rule in §1 to “primary RMSE” or apply the other two legs. As written this is an internal contradiction, not a wording quibble.

**6. No uncertainty on the score that decides retention.**  
Rolling \(n\approx 21\)–\(63\), no Diebold–Mariano, Clark–West, or bootstrap RMSE intervals. §4 says \(n\) “do not suffice to certify a small skill difference,” then issues a “negative certificate” / “certified non-existence.” Those cannot coexist. Spec A \(h=5\): 265 vs 289 is not a certificate.

**7. Predictand stacks the deck for persistence.**  
Primary series is NCAM **smoothed state**, jointly estimated with time-varying \(M\). Last-value persistence is the default forecast of a random-walk-like state. Constant-\(r\) surplus is then asked to track an assessment built around \(M(t)\). “Crash is not a catch-drop event” largely restates Cadigan/Regular (\(M\) pulse, peak \(\approx 2.5\)). That can still be a useful scored demonstration, but “negative certificate for the autonomous surplus-production **class**” is too large for “constant-\(r\) lost to persist on an \(M\)-shift smoother.” §4 already says the test is not of NCAM-as-filter; the certificate language ignores that limit.

## 1. Flaws

### 1.1 “Preregistered” is false as written

Abstract, §1, and §2.3 call the retention rule **preregistered**. §4 then states there is **no dated pre-score protocol file** and that passes evolved (primary → annual landings → survey-start → capelin → Spec B) in the manuscript.

Those cannot both stand. Script-order is not preregistration. For *Fisheries Research* this is the first thing a reviewer will flag. Either:

- drop “preregistered” everywhere and keep the freeze-discipline caveat, or
- lock a dated protocol now and label everything after it as exploratory.

The Edwards contrast makes this worse, not better: it documents that the authors know what a lock looks like and did not do it here.

### 1.2 Retention rule is stated as three criteria, implemented as one

Abstract / §1: retain structure if it improves **early warning, out-of-sample prediction, or intervention selection** (OR).

§2.3: retain only if it reduces **primary RMSE** vs the nested parent **and** vs persistence (AND).

Intervention selection is never operationalized (§1 explicitly declines to evaluate the moratorium). Early warning exists only as secondary Brier and is forbidden from changing the verdict (Spec B: M3 Brier 0.02 vs persist 0.06 at \(h=1\), still not retained). The OR in the abstract is not the rule that was run.

### 1.3 Predictand is a smoothed assessment state — persistence is then the default, not a tough bar

The predictand is NCAM / xteNCAM SSB, i.e. a state-space *filter/smoother output*. Such series are autocorrelated by construction. Last-value persistence on a smoother is the forecast to beat only in a weak sense; it is often unbeatable even when the assessment is wrong in real time.

The paper cites Hutchings & Myers (1994) and Walters & Maguire (1996) — papers about **real-time overestimation** — then never uses a vintage assessment. Every origin uses 2016/2025 hindsight \(S_t\). That is pseudo out-of-sample on a final smoother, not early warning. The historical failure mode is not in the experiment.

This does not invalidate “can this ladder track the hindsight trajectory?” It does invalidate any sentence that reads as a test of assessment architecture, early warning, or 1992 decision quality. Several sentences still read that way (highlight 3, abstract “early warning”, §4 “leading-indicator claim”).

### 1.4 Origin sets are not matched — Spec B rolling and Table 8

Spec B: ladder min 12 training years (\(n=59/55\)), naive min 8 (\(n=63/59\)). Table 6 therefore compares persist and M1 on **different origins**. That is not a scored ablation.

Table 8: index model \(n=24/20\) (A) and \(n=36/32\) (B) vs persist \(n=25/21\) and \(n=59/55\). On B the origin sets are not even close. You already know how to do this: post-break Table 7 reports “M_cap 107 versus persistence 75 on the **identical** origin set.” Table 6 and Table 8 do not.

Until persist is recomputed on the exact origin set of each competitor, Spec B “second negative certificate” and the index-module non-retention are not machine-verified.

### 1.5 Scope of the “negative certificate” overruns the design

What was shown: on two SSB columns, one-step LS Schaefer/Allee, this catch/residual/delay/capelin ladder, persist has lower RMSE.

What is written: “negative certificate for the **autonomous surplus-production model class**”; “certified non-existence”; “incompatible with the observed path.”

That class, in this journal, includes Pella–Tomlinson, SPiCT, JABBA, time-varying \(r\), process-error state-space SPMs. None of those were fitted. One-step LS with \(r\) hitting 2 is not a sufficient statistic for the class.

Separately, “an exact fixed autonomous scalar trajectory cannot reproduce a non-monotone path” is a tautology, not a certificate. The empirical content is mean-reversion toward a mis-identified \(K\) under regime shifts — M2–M4 and prey, not M1.

“Machine-verified” also overruns the checksums: 29/29 verifies arithmetic, not incompatibility of a model class.

### 1.6 M4 is information-disadvantaged relative to persist; no delayed-persistence control

M4 starts from \(S_{t-1}\); persist uses \(S_t\). The gap (196 vs 98 on A, \(h=1\)) mixes delay cost with information loss. The control is \(\hat S_{t+h}=S_{t-1}\). Without it, “information cost of the one-year delay” is not identified, and “M4 is the worst structural model” is partly by construction.

### 1.7 Estimation method is a straw opponent for this journal

One-step LS, \(r\in(0.001,2]\), \(K\) above training max, no process/observation error split, no SPiCT/JABBA/BSP. Surplus-production estimation bias is a 30-year literature. A reviewer will say: persist beating a boundary-hitting OLS Schaefer is not news; persist beating a state-space SPM on the same score would be.

The ablation logic (add one module at a time) can still justify OLS — but then the certificate must be restricted to **this estimator and this ladder**, not the class.

### 1.8 Table 3 has no persistence row on the collapse window

Abstract: collapse missed by every model, RMSE 694–819. Table 3: structural only. Persist frozen at \(S_{1990}\) for 1991–95 is the number that tells whether M1’s 694 is “everyone failed” or “high frozen \(C\) accidentally beats persist.” Without it, highlight 3 and the collapse paragraph are incomplete.

Same table: recovery M1b 90 vs later “frozen-persistence error of 104 kt” (annual, §3.2). On that **single origin** M1b beats persist. You correctly keep retention on rolling RMSE; then Table 3 cannot be presented as if the structural RMSEs are self-interpreting.

### 1.9 Log-RMSE at the floor is unspecified

M1/M1b log-RMSE 8.02 / 8.70 because trajectories hit \([\,\cdot\,]_+\). \(\log 0\) is undefined. The 8.02 implies an undeclared \(\varepsilon\)-floor. Secondary scores that depend on an undeclared floor cannot be reproduced from the text.

### 1.10 Sample-size / “certificate” tension

§4: \(n=25/21\) “suffice to rank … do not suffice to certify a small skill difference.” Adjacent: “negative certificate.” No Diebold–Mariano, no block bootstrap, no interval on RMSE. Ranking with large gaps (98 vs 115–196) is fine; “certificate” is not a substitute for a test. Drop the word or add a test on matched origins.

### 3.1 Model-free catch accounting bound (stronger than the whole ladder)

The decisive fact is already in prose: apparent net production \(S_{t+1}-S_t+C_t\) is “strongly negative in 1991–93” and “observed \(\Delta S\) is far larger than \(C_t\).”

\[
\Delta S_t,\quad C_t,\quad \Delta S_t+C_t,\quad \text{implied }M\text{ or extra removals}.
\]

A one-line bound: even with \(r=0\) (no production), required catch to match \(\Delta S\) is \(\sim -\Delta S\); even with \(r=2\) at low \(S\), extra removals still dwarf Schijns/STATLANT. Regular et al.’s \(M\approx 2.5\) is the same residual.
