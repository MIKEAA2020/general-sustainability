# Overall assessment

The manuscript has a potentially strong paper inside it, but the present version combines four different levels of claim without keeping them consistently separated:

1. **proved structural results** for a three-state RFDE;
2. **high-precision numerical spectral calculations**;
3. **uncertified nonlinear bifurcation computations**;
4. **institutional and policy interpretations** that are substantially broader than the mathematics.

The central mathematical architecture is useful:

\[
\text{ecological block}
\longrightarrow
\text{filtered decline signal}
\longrightarrow
\text{delayed effort response}
\longrightarrow
\text{stock}.
\]

The exact cancellation

\[
L(\lambda)=B_E\lambda
\]

is especially valuable. The protective-channel modulus calculation is also clean at the declared Candidate A point.

However, several headline claims are presently false or materially overstated. The most consequential problems are:

- there are **two positive frequency roots**, not only two delay crossings;
- each frequency generates infinitely many delay branches, contradicting the claimed five-regime topology for all \(\tau>\tau_+\);
- the paper’s own iso-gain sign-flip result shows that the presence or absence of Hopf frequencies is **not determined by the sign of \(C_Z\)**;
- the “two-fold lower boundary” is not established and is contradicted by the reported stable collocation orbit beyond the alleged disappearance boundary;
- the frozen-pool proposition does not justify the claimed Hopf-threshold transfer;
- “sample-and-hold” and “explicit Euler discretisation” are conflated;
- unit-circle multiplier crossings are called Neimark–Sacker and period-doubling bifurcations without the required nonlinear conditions;
- local equilibrium stability is repeatedly translated into governance recommendations without checking safety, transient loss, or viability between reviews.

The paper needs a substantial mathematical restructuring. It should become a paper about:

> **phase-induced stability switches in two specified institutional response laws, with a separate Euler-reviewed hybrid approximation and explicitly local policy implications.**

It does not yet establish that “the mobilising sign is the hazard” or that “the review interval is the control” in the broad form claimed.

# I. Highest-priority mathematical contradictions

## 1. “Two crossings” is not the same as “two Hopf-frequency families”

Theorem 2 correctly states that every positive root \(x_n=\omega_n^2\) generates delay branches

\[
\tau_{n,k}
=
\tau_{n,0}+\frac{2\pi k}{\omega_n}.
\]

But the abstract, Sections 5 and 8, and the conclusion repeatedly treat the two fundamental delays \(\tau_-\) and \(\tau_+\) as if they were the only crossings.

That is inconsistent with the manuscript’s own theorem.

For Candidate A, the reported frequencies are approximately

\[
\omega_1\approx 0.02518,
\qquad
\omega_2\approx0.03944.
\]

Therefore the recurrence intervals are approximately

\[
\frac{2\pi}{\omega_1}\approx249.5\ \mathrm{yr},
\qquad
\frac{2\pi}{\omega_2}\approx159.3\ \mathrm{yr}.
\]

Thus, after the fundamental crossings

\[
\tau_{1,0}\approx3.666,
\qquad
\tau_{2,0}\approx150.358,
\]

the next crossings should occur near

\[
\tau_{1,1}\approx253.2,
\qquad
\tau_{2,1}\approx309.7,
\]

followed by further recurrences.

This directly conflicts with statements such as:

> “\(\tau>\tau_+\), the equilibrium unstable…”

and with the global five-regime classification as a classification of the entire positive delay axis.

If the lower family is stabilising at its fundamental branch and the crossing orientation is branch-independent—as is standard for a simple frequency family under the usual derivative formula—then its recurrence near \(253.2\) yr may remove an unstable pair. That creates the possibility of a further stability window before the next upper-family recurrence near \(309.7\) yr.

### Required correction

Replace “two Hopf crossings” by:

> two simple positive Hopf-frequency roots and their fundamental positive delay branches.

Restrict all five-regime claims to a declared finite interval, for example:

\[
0\le\tau<\tau_{1,1},
\]

or to the actual numerically searched range.

Add a complete crossing-count table:

\[
\{\tau_{n,k}:0\le\tau_{n,k}\le\tau_{\max}\},
\]

including crossing orientations and unstable-root counts on every interval.

### Stronger upgrade

Prove a root-count theorem of the form

\[
\nu(\tau^+)-\nu(\tau^-)
=
2\,\operatorname{sgn}
\left(
\frac{d\,\operatorname{Re}\lambda}{d\tau}
\right),
\]

where \(\nu(\tau)\) is the number of characteristic roots in the open right half-plane, counted with multiplicity. Then construct the full stability-switch diagram over the stated delay domain.

Without this, the phrase “complete Hopf cubic” is acceptable only for the frequency equation, not for the full delay-stability diagram.

---

## 2. The central “sign separation” interpretation is contradicted by the modulus equation

The manuscript repeatedly claims:

> “the sign structure of the feedback law … selects between qualitatively different bifurcation behaviour”

and concludes:

> “the mobilising sign is the hazard.”

But the modulus condition is

\[
|P(i\omega)|^2
=
C_Z^2|L(i\omega)|^2.
\]

The existence of candidate Hopf frequencies is independent of the sign of \(C_Z\). The sign changes only the phase condition, hence the delay branches at which a given frequency is realised.

Proposition 5 explicitly confirms this:

> replacing \(C_Z\) by \(-C_Z\) leaves \(H\) identical and retains both Hopfs.

Therefore the no-Hopf property of the quota-tracking law cannot be attributed to \(C_Z<0\). It results from the **joint change in the effort-row coefficients**, especially the much stronger direct damping:

\[
C_E^{\mathrm{mob}}\approx-0.0595,
\qquad
C_E^{\mathrm{prot}}\approx-0.8503,
\]

together with the changed delayed gain.

This is not a minor interpretive nuance. It overturns the headline causal claim.

### Correct structural statement

The mathematically supported result is:

> A sign reversal changes phase placement but does not change the modulus frequencies. The calibrated quota-tracking law is delay-independently stable because its combined direct damping and delayed gain keep the loop modulus below unity at every frequency.

Or more compactly:

\[
\boxed{
\text{sign controls phase placement;}
\quad
\text{gain–damping structure controls frequency feasibility.}
}
\]

### Required title-level correction

“The Mobilising and Protective Channels” remains defensible as a comparison between two complete laws. But the paper should not imply that their difference is exhausted by feedback sign.

A more accurate subtitle would be:

> **Phase Placement, Direct Damping, and Review Discretisation in Institutional Feedback**

---

## 3. The “two-fold lower boundary” is not established

The abstract says:

> “Global numerics give a five-regime attractor topology with a two-fold lower boundary…”

Section 8.2 says the stable large cycle disappears around

\[
\tau\in[5.574,5.576],
\]

but then reports that collocation continues what is described as the large-cycle branch through

\[
\tau=5.5815
\]

with dominant multiplier

\[
0.964<1.
\]

If all nontrivial Floquet multipliers are strictly inside the unit circle, that periodic orbit is locally asymptotically stable and has an open, nonempty basin. It therefore has not disappeared, and its basin has not collapsed to the empty set.

Failure of selected far-from-equilibrium histories to converge to it proves only:

\[
\text{those tested histories are not in its basin}.
\]

It does not prove:

- disappearance of the orbit;
- disappearance of its basin;
- a fold;
- a crisis;
- a global basin collapse.

The manuscript then reports a \(+1\)-multiplier crossing on a small branch near \(5.587\). That may be evidence for a fold of that branch, but it does not establish a second fold of the large branch.

### Internal contradiction

The following claims cannot all be maintained simultaneously:

1. the large stable cycle disappears near \(5.575\);
2. the same large branch exists and is stable at \(5.5815\);
3. the lower boundary consists of two folds;
4. the large-branch event may instead be a crisis.

A crisis can change or sharply shrink a basin, but a hyperbolically stable periodic orbit still has a local basin. A “basin collapse” to zero while the orbit remains hyperbolically stable is impossible.

### Required correction

Separate four quantities:

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{capture}},
\quad
\tau_{\mathrm{stab}},
\quad
\tau_{\mathrm{turn}},
\quad
\tau_{\mathrm{exist}}.
\]

They mean:

- \(\tau_{\mathrm{capture}}\): selected test histories cease to reach the orbit;
- \(\tau_{\mathrm{stab}}\): a Floquet multiplier crosses the unit circle;
- \(\tau_{\mathrm{turn}}\): continuation detects a turning point in \(\tau\);
- \(\tau_{\mathrm{exist}}\): the branch ceases to exist.

The data presently support only a change in tested global capture near \(5.575\) and a possible small-branch fold near \(5.587\).

### Correct wording

Replace:

> “the lower boundary is a pair of nearby folds”

with:

> “Two nearby numerical events are observed: loss of capture from the tested far-field histories near \(5.575\), and a continuation-supported \(+1\)-multiplier event on the small branch near \(5.587\). Whether the former is associated with a separate branch fold, a basin-boundary reorganisation, or numerical under-resolution remains unresolved.”

The abstract and conclusion must use the same cautious status.

# II. Abstract and contributions: line-level claim corrections

## 4. “Positive roots occur in even pairs” needs qualification

The abstract says:

> “positive roots occur in even pairs.”

Corollary 2 argues this from

\[
H(0)>0,
\qquad
H(x)\to+\infty.
\]

What follows is that positive roots occur with even **total multiplicity**, provided

\[
H(0)>0
\]

and roots are counted algebraically. It does not imply that there must be zero or two distinct positive roots. A single positive double root is possible.

Moreover, the corollary’s parenthetical treatment of degenerate cases is wrong:

> “in the degenerate coincidence \(A_N=0\) or \(C_E=0\) the count is unchanged since \(H(0)\ge0\)…”

If \(H(0)=0\), the endpoint sign argument no longer gives the claimed parity statement for strictly positive roots. For example, after factoring \(x\), the residual quadratic can have one positive root.

Also:

> “the gate factor makes \(C_E\ne0\)”

is false. The gate factor can be nonzero while the inner derivative vanishes:

\[
\frac{\delta}{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}}
-
\frac{2E^*}{E_{\max}}
=0.
\]

### Correct corollary

> Assume \(A_NdC_E\neq0\). Then \(H(0)>0\), and the total algebraic multiplicity of strictly positive roots of \(H\) is even. Since \(H\) is cubic, it is either zero or two. Generically, away from a double-root discriminant, this means zero or two distinct simple positive roots.

Then identify the double-root boundary by

\[
H(x)=0,
\qquad
H'(x)=0.
\]

That boundary is scientifically useful: it marks birth or annihilation of the pair of Hopf-frequency families.

---

## 5. “Complete cubic” is too broad unless completeness is carefully scoped

Equation (6) gives a complete modulus condition for imaginary roots, but not by itself:

- the complete RFDE spectrum;
- the number of unstable roots;
- simplicity;
- transversality;
- absence of simultaneous resonances;
- nonlinear Hopf existence;
- Hopf criticality;
- global continuation.

The manuscript partly acknowledges this, but later repeatedly says the interval enclosures certify “the local spectrum.”

They do not certify the local spectrum in full. They certify roots of the modulus polynomial and corresponding phase-evaluated delay candidates, assuming branch handling is rigorous.

### Better terminology

Use:

> interval-certified imaginary-root candidates and delay branches

or, after additionally proving simplicity, transversality, and exclusion of all other imaginary roots:

> interval-certified simple spectral crossings.

Do not call an enclosure of a cubic root a certificate of the DDE’s “local spectrum.”

---

## 6. The abstract overstates the Lyapunov-coefficient status

The abstract combines:

- interval-certified spectral crossings;
- numerical first Lyapunov coefficients;
- a global five-regime topology.

This presentation makes the whole sentence sound equally certified.

The \(\ell_1\) values are later described as numerical evaluations. They are not interval-certified, and no error bounds are provided.

### Required abstract revision

Say:

> Numerical normal-form evaluation gives positive first Lyapunov coefficients at both fundamental Candidate A crossings, consistent with subcritical Hopf bifurcations.

If the authors want the categorical phrase “both are subcritical,” they should provide:

- the full normal-form convention;
- reproducible derivative tensors;
- left and right eigenvectors;
- normalization formula;
- numerical conditioning;
- interval or validated error bounds proving \(\ell_1>0\).

Because the reported values are small,

\[
5.75\times10^{-5},
\qquad
3.55\times10^{-4},
\]

sign certification matters.

## 7. Theorem numbering is inconsistent with the contribution list

Contribution 5 says:

> “the monodromy is computed in closed form (Theorem 4).”

In the body:

- Proposition 6 gives the protective monodromy;
- Theorem 4 is “Channel-specific pacing”;
- Theorem 5 gives the mobilising monodromy.

Renumber or revise the contribution statement.

# III. Section 2: model and invariance

## 8. \(\mathcal D\) is not a box

The manuscript defines

\[
\mathcal D
=
\{0\le N\le K,\; Z\ge0,\;0\le E\le E_{\max}\}
\]

and calls it an “admissible box.” It is unbounded in \(Z\).

Use “admissible region” or define a bounded invariant box after establishing the \(Z\)-bound.

## 9. The signal map is not generally \(C^1\)

The model class defines

\[
\Phi_k(s)
=
\max\left\{
0,\,
\mathrm{sp}_k(s)-\frac{\log2}{k}+\delta
\right\}.
\]

For general \(\delta\), this map has a kink where the inner expression is zero. Yet Corollary 1 says:

> “the smooth signal map … [is] \(C^1\) there.”

That is false for the declared model class.

For the registered relation

\[
\delta=\frac{\log2}{k},
\]

the floor cancels and

\[
\Phi_k=\mathrm{sp}_k,
\]

so the reported numerical model is smooth. But the theorem is stated for the larger class.

### Repair

State one of:

1. \(\Phi_k\) is locally Lipschitz, which suffices for standard RFDE existence and uniqueness; or
2. restrict the smooth bifurcation analysis to parameter sets where the floor is inactive on a neighborhood of the equilibrium and periodic orbit.

The latter is already conceptually intended, but it must be applied consistently.

---

## 10. The \(Z\)-bound is valid but inelegantly written

The bound

\[
qEN-S(N)\le qE_{\max}K
\]

holds on \(0\le N\le K\), because \(S(N)\ge0\). The use of monotonicity of \(\Phi_k\) is therefore appropriate.

But the proof defines

\[
\bar Z
=
\max\left\{
\sup_{[-\tau,0]}Z,\,
\Phi_k(qE_{\max}K)
\right\}
\]

and then concludes

\[
Z(t)\le\max\{Z(0),\bar Z\}\le\bar Z.
\]

The first maximum is redundant.

More importantly, if the model family includes nonsmooth \(\Phi_k\), global continuation should be based on local Lipschitz continuity, not \(C^1\).

# IV. Proposition 1: frozen-active-pool approximation is not valid as used

## 13. The bound has dimensional and analytical problems

The proposition states

\[
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}
\left(
|\tilde N-N|+|\tilde Z-Z|+|\tilde E-E|
\right)
\le
C_T
\left(
\frac{A_0}{A_{\min}}+V_AT
\right).
\]

But:

- \(A_0/A_{\min}\) is dimensionless;
- \(V_AT\) has units of stock;
- the left side adds quantities with different units: stock, stock/time, and effort.

The norm is dimensionally incoherent unless the states are nondimensionalized.

### Repair

Introduce scales \(N_s,Z_s,E_s,A_s\) and define

\[
\|\xi\|_*
=
\frac{|\Delta N|}{N_s}
+
\frac{|\Delta Z|}{Z_s}
+
\frac{|\Delta E|}{E_s}.
\]

Then a dimensionless estimate can be stated.

---

## 14. The \(V_AT\) term is not derived correctly

The renewal mismatch is exactly

\[
R(N,A)-S(N)
=
-S(N)\frac{A_0}{A+A_0}.
\]

If \(A(t)\ge A_{\min}\), this gives the direct uniform bound

\[
|R(N,A)-S(N)|
\le
\max_{0\le N\le K}S(N)
\frac{A_0}{A_{\min}+A_0}
=
\frac{rK}{4}
\frac{A_0}{A_{\min}+A_0}.
\]

No \(V_AT\) term is needed to compare \(R(N,A(t))\) with \(S(N)\). The proof invents an additional term:

> “the last term from \(|\dot A|\le V_A\) entering through the \(A\)-dependence of \(R\)”

but does not define a frozen reference value of \(A\) against which such drift is measured. The three-state renewal is already the \(A\to\infty\) saturation \(S(N)\), not \(R(N,A(0))\).

---

## 15. Ordinary Grönwall is applied as though the system had no delay

The \(E\)-difference equation contains

\[
\Delta Z(t-\tau).
\]

Therefore the estimate must be written in a history norm, for example

\[
Y(t)
=
\sup_{s\in[-\tau,t]}
\|\xi(s)\|_*,
\]

and then handled by a retarded Grönwall inequality or method-of-steps estimate.

The displayed inequality

\[
|\dot\xi|
\le
L|\xi|+\cdots
\]

omits the delayed difference.

### Correct schematic estimate

\[
\|\xi(t)\|_*
\le
C
\int_0^t
\left[
\sup_{u\in[-\tau,s]}\|\xi(u)\|_*
+
\varepsilon_A
\right]ds,
\]

which yields

\[
\sup_{s\in[-\tau,T]}\|\xi(s)\|_*
\le
C_T\varepsilon_A.
\]

---

## 16. A finite-time trajectory bound does not bound a Hopf threshold shift

The manuscript says the proposition justifies the three-state core for local questions:

> “in particular the location of \(\tau_-\), whose 3.2% shift … lies inside the bound.”

This inference is invalid.

A finite-time solution difference estimate does not imply a bound on:

- characteristic roots;
- imaginary-root frequencies;
- Hopf delays;
- spectral projectors;
- transversality.

A Hopf-threshold perturbation requires a spectral perturbation theorem for the characteristic function, such as:

\[
\|\Delta_\varepsilon-\Delta_0\|_{\partial\Omega}
<
\inf_{\partial\Omega}|\Delta_0|
\]

followed by Rouché’s theorem, or an implicit-function estimate at a simple root:

\[
|\tau_*(\varepsilon)-\tau_*(0)|
\le
\frac{
|\partial_\varepsilon F|
}{
|\partial_\tau F|
}
|\varepsilon|
+o(|\varepsilon|).
\]

### Required deletion

Delete the claim that the observed \(3.2\%\) Hopf shift “lies inside” Proposition 1’s bound.

### Strong upgrade

Add a separate proposition on characteristic-function perturbation between the three- and four-state systems.

# VI. Equilibria and linearisation

## 20. “The equilibrium is independent of \(k\)” needs its domain stated

At the reported interior equilibrium,

\[
Z^*=\Phi_k(0)=\delta
\]

for \(\delta>0\), and

\[
\Phi_k'(0)=\frac12
\]

while the positive branch of the max is active. Thus the equilibrium and linearisation are independent of \(k\) if \(\delta\) is held fixed.

But the parameter table sets

\[
\delta=\frac{\log2}{k}.
\]

Under a sweep in \(k\), it is unclear whether:

- \(\delta\) remains numerically fixed; or
- \(\delta\) changes with \(k\).

Section 5.2 says:

> “Hopf points are invariant under \(k\in\{5,10,20,40\}\) at fixed \(\delta\).”

That is consistent only with a fixed-\(\delta\) sweep, not with maintaining the table relation \(\delta=\log2/k\).

State this explicitly.

---

## 21. The characteristic equation is sound, but Theorem 2 needs nonzero-factor assumptions

The phase relation divides by

\[
C_ZL(i\omega).
\]

The theorem should assume

\[
C_ZL(i\omega)\ne0.
\]

For the interior architecture,

\[
L(i\omega)=iB_E\omega
\]

and this is nonzero for \(\omega>0\) provided \(B_E\ne0\). But Theorem 2 is stated before specializing fully to that case.

A degenerate imaginary root with both sides zero would need separate treatment.

---

## 22. Simultaneous-frequency collisions are not excluded

Even after certifying the two positive roots of \(H\), a given delay branch from one frequency could coincide with a branch from the other:

\[
\tau_{1,k}=\tau_{2,\ell}.
\]

That would produce a double-Hopf point rather than a simple Hopf point.

The manuscript says simplicity and transversality are checked, but it should also certify:

\[
\operatorname{dist}
\left(
\tau_{1,k},\tau_{2,\ell}
\right)>0
\]

over the declared search range.

This matters especially if the paper later extends beyond the two fundamental branches.

# VII. Mobilising-channel analysis

## 23. “The interval enclosures certify the local spectrum” is inaccurate

An interval Newton proof for roots of \(H(x)\), followed by interval phase evaluation, establishes:

- existence and uniqueness of the relevant positive \(x\)-roots in stated intervals;
- corresponding delay enclosures for chosen phase branches.

It does not by itself certify:

- all RFDE roots near the imaginary axis;
- no additional imaginary roots from other phase branches at the same delay;
- the count of unstable roots;
- nonlinear Hopf bifurcation.

The manuscript partly acknowledges this, but “local spectrum” remains too broad.

Use:

> certified simple imaginary-root frequencies and corresponding delay branches.

If direct quasi-polynomial root tracking is not interval validated, describe it as an independent numerical check, not part of the certificate.

---

## 24. The undelayed Routh–Hurwitz calculation is numerically inconsistent in presentation

The cubic is stated as

\[
\lambda^3
+
0.2774\lambda^2
+
0.00056\lambda
+
0.000213.
\]

The criterion is

\[
a_2a_1>a_0.
\]

But

\[
0.2774(0.00056)\approx0.000155,
\]

which is indeed less than \(0.000213\). The conclusion is correct.

However, with such small differences, coefficients should be reported at enough precision to make the unstable-root count reproducible. Rounded coefficients may materially alter a near-boundary Routh test.

Provide the exact coefficient expressions and higher-precision values.

---

## 25. The phrase “phase-stabilised window” is useful, but it is only the first such window unless all recurrences are analysed

The manuscript says delay opens

\[
(\tau_-,\tau_+).
\]

That is the first observed stability window. Given recurrent branches, call it:

> the fundamental or first phase-stabilised window.

Do not imply uniqueness over all \(\tau\).

---

## 26. The first Lyapunov coefficient needs a reproducibility block

The reported values are convention-dependent in magnitude. The sign is the main invariant, but even that needs numerical validation.

Include:

- the exact RFDE bilinear pairing;
- right eigenvector \(p\);
- adjoint eigenvector \(q\);
- normalization;
- derivative tensors \(D^2F,D^3F\);
- formula used for \(g_{20},g_{11},g_{02},g_{21}\);
- definition of \(\ell_1\);
- precision and conditioning;
- independent implementation comparison.

The phrase

> “Hassard–Faria–Magalhães cubic”

is not enough to reproduce the calculation.

---

## 27. “Roundoff alone” is not a scientifically stable branch test

The statement that the orbit

> “escapes onto the large cycle by roundoff alone”

depends on:

- numerical scheme;
- precision;
- collocation projection;
- unstable multiplier;
- integration tolerances.

It can support instability qualitatively, but should not be used as a primary classification. The Floquet multiplier is the relevant object.

# VIII. Two-channel interpolation and small gain

## 28. Proposition 4 excludes nonzero imaginary roots, not instability

The condition

\[
\sup_{\omega>0}
\frac{(|C_m|+|C_p|)|L(i\omega)|}{|P(i\omega)|}<1
\]

excludes \(\lambda=i\omega\) for \(\omega>0\).

It does not by itself prove stability. The system may already be unstable at the reference delays and remain unstable without crossing.

To conclude delay-independent stability, add:

1. stability at one reference point, typically \((\tau_M,\tau_P)=(0,0)\);
2. exclusion of \(\lambda=0\);
3. a characteristic-root continuation theorem over the two-delay parameter domain.

---

## 29. Corollary 3 does not prove that a Hopf “requires a sufficiently large mobilising weight” globally

Continuity plus a strict protective small-gain margin proves that an open neighborhood of the protective endpoint satisfies the sufficient small-gain bound.

It does not prove a unique threshold \(\chi_m^*\) separating all safe and unsafe weights unless monotonicity is established.

The current statement:

> “there exists \(\chi_m^*\) such that every interpolation with \(\chi_m<\chi_m^*\) satisfies (10)”

# Final diagnosis

The paper’s strongest rigorous results are:

1. forward invariance of the gated three-state core, with minor regularity corrections;
2. the characteristic reduction;
3. the exact cancellation \(L(\lambda)=B_E\lambda\);
4. the cubic frequency condition;
5. the calibrated protective no-imaginary-root calculation;
6. the closed-form monodromy of a precisely defined Euler-reviewed hybrid map.

Its weakest current claims are:

1. the unrestricted two-crossing/five-regime narrative;
2. attribution of no-Hopf behavior to feedback sign;
3. the asserted two-fold lower boundary;
4. transfer from finite-time state approximation to Hopf-threshold accuracy;
5. calling linear multiplier crossings nonlinear bifurcations;
6. treating local spectral stabilization as a governance recommendation.

The paper can become substantially stronger by making one central correction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The laws differ not merely in sign, but in modulus, damping, phase,
and digital implementation.}
}
\]

After that correction, the unifying claim should be:

> Institutional delay does not have a sign-free or monotone effect. Gain magnitude and direct damping determine whether oscillatory frequencies are feasible; feedback sign and delay determine where those frequencies satisfy phase closure; recurrent phase branches produce potentially repeated stability switches; and review design is meaningful only after local stability is intersected with inter-review safety and institutional feasibility.

That version is mathematically coherent, closes the largest internal contradictions, and provides a much stronger bridge from delay bifurcation analysis to a predictive theory of sustainable governance.
