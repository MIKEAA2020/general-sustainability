# Overall assessment

The manuscript has a valuable methodological premise: review cadence should be represented by the operator institutions actually implement, rather than automatically replaced by a continuous delay. It also shows commendable caution about causal interpretation, numerical bifurcation labels, controller sign, and retrospective spectral evidence.

At present, however, the paper is not mathematically or empirically closed. Its central difficulties are deeper than missing computational appendices:

1. **The review-event timing is internally inconsistent.**
2. **The stage-structured map, continuous-delay comparator, parameter sets, and linearised review operators are not defined.**
3. **The claimed architecture comparison changes ecology, state dimension, controller implementation, and parameterisation simultaneously, so it does not isolate “operator substitution.”**
4. **The rapid-review claim is overstated: finite-horizon consistency alone proves no stability, but consistent sampled-data approximations of a hyperbolically stable continuous equilibrium generally are stable for sufficiently small sampling periods under additional hypotheses.**
5. **The \(47.54\)-yr result is called a Neimark–Sacker threshold without the required spectral and nonlinear calculations, and may largely be an explicit-Euler controller artifact.**
6. **The spectral analysis conflates FDR and familywise control, does not fully define its statistic or null calibration, and is not reproducible from the manuscript.**
7. **The northern cod phase-line obstruction does not “apply directly” because the assessment series is not an exact scalar state trajectory and removals were not fixed.**
8. **The “two-window split” is framed as a positive result, but one of the two formulations is explicitly unreproduced and neither formulation establishes the claimed non-recovery conclusion.**
9. **Many reported numerical results are described as exploratory and unavailable, yet are still used to support substantive conclusions in the abstract, Results, Discussion, and title.**

The paper can become strong, but it needs to be reorganised around a fully specified operator comparison and a more modest empirical claim.

---

# I. Critical internal inconsistencies

## 1. The review timing is not well defined

The model states:

\[
E_n \text{ is held on }[t_n,t_{n+1}),
\]

and

\[
E^{\rm cmd}_{n+1}
=
\Pi_{[0,E_{\max}]}
\left\{
E_n+T_rF_B(E_n,\widehat Z_n)
\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Section 2.2 then says:

> “The deterministic record fixes a one-step flow-then-update convention: \(E_n\) is held on \([t_n,t_{n+1})\) and the assessment indexed by \(n\) defines the next command.”

This creates at least three incompatible interpretations.

### Interpretation A: assessment and update at \(t_n\)

At \(t_n\), \(\widehat Z_n\) is available and equation (4) computes the command for the interval immediately beginning at \(t_n\). Then the held control should be called \(E_n\), and the update should be

\[
E_n
=
\Pi\{E_{n-1}+T_rF_B(E_{n-1},\widehat Z_n)\}.
\]

### Interpretation B: assessment at \(t_n\), deployment at \(t_{n+1}\)

Equation (4) computes \(E_{n+1}\), which is not deployed until the next review. This introduces a full review-period deployment delay even though Section 2.2 declares

\[
\tau_{\rm dec}=\tau_{\rm dep}=0.
\]

### Interpretation C: flow first, update at \(t_{n+1}\)

The state first evolves under \(E_n\), then the endpoint assessment is used to compute \(E_{n+1}\). In that case the update should use \(\widehat Z_{n+1}\), not \(\widehat Z_n\).

This is not merely an indexing issue. The different conventions produce different Poincaré maps and different spectra.

### Required repair

Define pre- and post-review states explicitly. For example:

\[
X_n^-=(N(t_n^-),Z(t_n^-),E_{n-1}),
\]

\[
E_n
=
\Pi\left\{
E_{n-1}
+
T_rF_B(E_{n-1},\widehat Z_n)
\right\},
\]

\[
X_n^+=(N(t_n^-),Z(t_n^-),E_n),
\]

followed by

\[
(N(t_{n+1}^-),Z(t_{n+1}^-))
=
\varphi_{T_r}^{E_n}(N(t_n^-),Z(t_n^-)).
\]

The resulting pre-review map is then

\[
X_{n+1}^-
=
\mathcal P_{T_r}(X_n^-).
\]

Alternatively, use an endpoint update, but derive that map consistently.

Until this is resolved, the claimed review-map spectrum is not uniquely defined.

---

## 2. “Exact contemporaneous assessment” is not connected to the latent signal

Equation (2) defines a latent filtered signal \(Z(t)\), but equation (3) defines

\[
\widehat Z_n
=
\mathcal A_n(\{Y_j:t_j\le t_n-\tau_{\rm dec}\}).
\]

Section 2.2 says assessments are “contemporaneous and exact,” but does not say whether that means

\[
\widehat Z_n=Z(t_n),
\]

\[
\widehat Z_n=qE_nN(t_n)-S(N(t_n)),
\]

or an exact estimate of \(N(t_n)\) subsequently transformed into a deficit.

The distinction matters because the controller is driven by \(\widehat Z_n\), while the memory equation evolves \(Z\). Unless

\[
\widehat Z_n=Z(t_n)
\]

is imposed, the variable \(Z\) may not affect the effort update at all.

### Required repair

Specify the deterministic assessment operator, for example:

\[
\widehat Z_n=Z(t_n^-)
\]

in the baseline model.

For noisy assessment, specify:

\[
\widehat Z_n
=
\Psi\bigl(Z(t_n^-),\epsilon_n\bigr)
\]

or derive \(\widehat Z_n\) from observations and the assessment model. Do not leave \(\mathcal A_n\) arbitrary while claiming a definite sampled operator.

---

## 3. The assessment model can make the effort law undefined

The manuscript allows assessment errors that:

> “need not be independent or Gaussian.”

No support restriction is imposed on \(\widehat Z_n\). But the controller contains

\[
\frac{\widehat Z_n}{Z_{\rm ref}+\widehat Z_n}.
\]

If

\[
\widehat Z_n=-Z_{\rm ref},
\]

the update is singular. If \(\widehat Z_n<-Z_{\rm ref}\), the term changes sign in a way not discussed. Projection of the final effort command does not repair an undefined update.

### Required repair

Either impose

\[
\widehat Z_n\ge0,
\]

for example through

\[
\widehat Z_n=\Pi_{[0,\infty)}(\widetilde Z_n),
\]

or replace the rational term by a globally defined bounded response function. State the admissible observation-error support.

---

## 4. The paper refers to three operators, not two

Section 2.3 is titled “The two review-map operators,” but the paper distinguishes:

1. the logistic sample-and-hold map;
2. the stage-structured review map;
3. the continuous-delay equation.

The Discussion also refers to all three.

More importantly, the first two are not merely two delay operators applied to the same loop. They appear to use different ecological plants:

- logistic stock dynamics;
- delayed-recruitment or stage-structured dynamics.

They may also have different state dimensions, equilibria, gains, and parameterisations.

### Consequence

The contrast

\[
3\text{–}4\ \mathrm{yr}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
47.5\ \mathrm{yr}
\]

cannot presently be attributed to sample-and-hold architecture. It confounds:

- ecological-model substitution;
- state-dimension substitution;
- parameter substitution;
- controller discretisation;
- review-event timing;
- possibly assessment timing.

### Required conceptual correction

The paper must distinguish:

\[
\text{review-operator effect}
\]

from

\[
\text{ecological-plant effect}.
\]

A valid operator comparison holds the ecological plant, equilibrium, controller linearisation, and parameters fixed while changing only the timing operator.

A factorial comparison would be preferable:

| Ecology | Controller update | Timing operator |
|---|---|---|
| Logistic | continuous controller | no delay |
| Logistic | continuous controller | fixed delay |
| Logistic | Euler-reviewed controller | sample-and-hold |
| Logistic | exact reviewed controller | sample-and-hold |
| Stage structured | same controller variants | same timing variants |

Only matched rows can identify architecture substitution.

# II. Abstract and headline claims

## 5. “Forward invariant—stock, signal, and effort constraints hold” overstates the proposition

The proposition proves:

\[
N(t)\ge0,\qquad Z(t)\ge0,\qquad E(t)\in[0,E_{\max}].
\]

It does not state or prove a stock upper constraint such as

\[
N(t)\le K.
\]

If “stock constraints” means only non-negativity, say so. If the intended admissible stock interval is \([0,K]\), add the assumption

\[
N(0)\in[0,K]
\]

and prove invariance of the upper face:

\[
\dot N\big|_{N=K}=-qE(t)K\le0.
\]

More generally,

\[
0\le N(t)\le\max\{N(0),K\}.
\]

The abstract should say:

> Non-negativity of stock and signal and admissibility of projected effort are preserved over review intervals.

---

## 6. “The rapid-review limit establishes nothing about stability at any positive interval” is too categorical

The finite-horizon consistency theorem alone does not imply stability. That part is correct.

But the statement as written suggests that no positive-\(T_r\) stability conclusion can follow from rapid-review analysis. Under standard additional assumptions, that is false. If:

- the continuous equilibrium is locally exponentially stable;
- it is hyperbolic;
- the sampled map is a \(C^1\)-consistent approximation;
- projection remains inactive locally;
- the event timing is fixed consistently;

then the sampled fixed point generally remains locally stable for all sufficiently small \(T_r>0\).

At the linear level, if the continuous Jacobian \(A\) is Hurwitz and

\[
D\mathcal P_h(x^*)
=
I+hA+O(h^2),
\]

then its multipliers satisfy

\[
\mu_j(h)
=
1+h\lambda_j(A)+O(h^2),
\]

so

\[
|\mu_j(h)|<1
\]

for sufficiently small \(h>0\).

### Correct statement

> Finite-horizon consistency by itself does not establish stability at any fixed positive review interval. Stability transfer requires additional hyperbolicity and \(C^1\)-consistency assumptions.

The same correction is needed in the abstract, Section 3.2, and the conclusion.

---

## 7. The abstract gives numerical findings a stronger status than the manuscript allows

The abstract reports:

- stage-structured response windows near \(3\)–\(4\) and \(6\)–\(12\) yr;
- cod convergence over \(1\)–\(20\) yr;
- restabilisation near \(47.5\) yr;
- a 42-stock null;
- a zero-count search;
- a cod two-window result.

But Sections 2.2, 4.7, and Data availability say that:

- the stage model is not fully registered;
- solver configurations and histories are pending;
- multiplier analysis has not been done;
- computational artifacts are not attached;
- some cod quantities are unreproduced;
- the stage-output values are exploratory.

The abstract must distinguish completed results from pending or exploratory records.

### Better wording

> Exploratory trajectory classifications suggest response regions near \(3\)–\(4\) and \(6\)–\(12\) yr in an incompletely archived stage-structured implementation. A separately defined logistic Euler-review map has a reported linear unit-circle crossing near \(47.5\) yr.

If the artifacts are unavailable, exact-looking numerical claims should not be presented as established results.

---

## 8. “A 42-stock spectral null” is too strong for the title

The screen is:

- selected rather than population-representative;
- based on an incompletely supplied eligibility table;
- low-powered for at least one target class;
- unable to classify controller sign;
- based on assessment-derived series;
- sensitive to detrending, record length, and red-noise calibration.

A more defensible title phrase is:

> a selected 42-stock spectral screen

rather than:

> a 42-stock spectral null.

“Null” can mean either no rejection or evidence for a null model. Here it is only a no-discovery result.

---

# III. Model definition and forward invariance

## 9. The explicit controller is not the same as a generic sample-and-hold controller

Equation (4),

\[
E_{n+1}
=
E_n+T_rF_B(E_n,\widehat Z_n),
\]

is a forward-Euler discretisation of a continuous effort law. It is not intrinsic to sample-and-hold governance.

A reviewed institution could instead use:

- a direct command rule \(E_{n+1}=H(\widehat Z_n)\);
- exact integration of the controller under held assessment;
- an implicit update;
- a bounded incremental rule independent of \(T_r\);
- a policy table or optimisation;
- a fixed proportional change limit.

The manuscript acknowledges that it is an explicit discretisation, but later interprets the \(47.5\)-yr result as an effect of periodic review generally.

### Required terminology

Use consistently:

> projected forward-Euler reviewed controller

or:

> Euler-reviewed sample-and-hold implementation.

Then compare it with at least one non-Euler reviewed controller.

---

## 10. Multiplication by \(T_r\) changes the institutional rule as cadence changes

The update is

\[
\Delta E_n=T_rF_B(E_n,\widehat Z_n).
\]

Thus increasing \(T_r\) increases both:

1. the duration of the hold;
2. the size of the command update.

A five-year review does not merely hold an annual decision for five years. It applies a five-times larger Euler increment.

Consequently, a sweep over \(T_r\) changes two mechanisms simultaneously:

\[
\text{hold duration}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{controller gain per review}.
\]

This is a major identification problem.

### Needed comparison

Analyze at least two conventions:

#### Rate-preserving Euler rule

\[
E_{n+1}
=
E_n+T_rF_B(E_n,\widehat Z_n).
\]

#### Decision-increment-preserving rule

\[
E_{n+1}
=
E_n+\kappa F_B(E_n,\widehat Z_n),
\]

with fixed \(\kappa\).

Or calibrate a continuous controller and compare its exact held-measurement update with forward Euler. This will show whether the crossing is due to cadence, accumulated gain, or Euler stability restrictions.

---

## 11. The signal map is not generally smooth

The map

\[
\Phi_k(s)
=
\max\left\{
0,\,
\frac1k\log(1+e^{ks})-\frac{\log2}{k}+\delta
\right\}
\]

is locally Lipschitz but not \(C^1\) at an active flooring point.

Section 3.2 assumes that the assembled continuous vector field \(G\) is continuously differentiable. That assumption is not automatic for the declared model.

### Required repair

State either:

- the floor is inactive on a neighborhood containing the compared trajectories; or
- the rapid-review result is proved under local Lipschitz assumptions with an appropriate nonsmooth consistency argument.

Also declare the numerical relation among \(k\) and \(\delta\). If \(\delta>0\), the floor may be locally inactive around \(s=0\), but that needs to be stated.

---

## 12. Global existence should be proved rather than qualified away

The invariance proposition applies:

> “for every time at which the sampled solution exists.”

For this system, global existence should be straightforward under a globally defined assessment update.

Since \(E(t)\in[0,E_{\max}]\),

\[
\dot N
\le
rN\left(1-\frac NK\right),
\]

so

\[
0\le N(t)\le\max\{N(0),K\}.
\]

The forcing of \(Z\) is then bounded on the resulting compact stock-effort region if \(\Phi\) is continuous. Hence \(Z\) cannot blow up in finite time. Projection bounds \(E\).

### Upgrade

Prove:

\[
0\le N(t)\le N_{\max},
\]

\[
0\le Z(t)
\le
\max\left\{
Z(0),\,
\sup_{0\le N\le N_{\max},\,0\le E\le E_{\max}}
\Phi(qEN-S(N))
\right\},
\]

and global existence follows.

---

## 13. The proof uses an interpolation not included in the baseline model

The baseline says effort is held. The proposition additionally covers:

> “any between-review deployment interpolation…”

That is a useful extension, but it belongs in a separately defined deployment model. With positive \(\tau_{\rm dep}\), the interpolation timing and queue are part of the state.

The proof should distinguish:

- zero-order hold;
- linear deployment interpolation;
- queued delayed deployment.

These do not have the same review map.

---

# IV. Rapid-review analysis

## 14. The one-step consistency argument depends on the unresolved timing convention

The manuscript claims:

> “The frozen-effort flow followed by the explicit effort step then has a one-step defect of order \(O(T_r^2)\).”

That is plausible for a Lie splitting or Euler-type method, but only after the map is written exactly.

If the endpoint signal is used, the step differs from one using the beginning-of-period signal. If the command computed at \(t_n\) is not deployed until \(t_{n+1}\), the method includes a one-step delay and may not approximate the intended no-delay controller in the claimed way.

### Required derivation

Display

\[
\mathcal P_h(u)
=
u+hG(u)+O(h^2)
\]

component by component, including the signal at which \(F_B\) is evaluated.

Then establish a derivative estimate:

\[
D\mathcal P_h(u)
=
I+hDG(u)+O(h^2)
\]

if any local stability comparison is intended.

---

## 15. The citation to sampled-data stabilisation does not substitute for a theorem matched to this hybrid model

Nešić and Teel concern sampled-data approximations under explicit hypotheses. The manuscript should state:

- the exact compact set;
- uniform local truncation-error constants;
- regularity of the projected map;
- whether projection is inactive;
- whether the held flow and review reset satisfy the required consistency notion.

The present paragraph is a correct sketch, not an exact established property.

---

# V. Missing mathematical core of the spectral claims

## 16. The stage-structured review map is absent

Section 3.3 reports detailed results for:

- anchovy class;
- sprat class;
- cod class;
- slow-stock class;
- delayed recruitment;
- parameter \(g\);
- parameter \(\eta\);
- biomass and effort peaks.

But the manuscript never displays:

- the stage equations;
- the recruitment law;
- state variables;
- parameter values;
- equilibrium equations;
- review update;
- assessment rule;
- initial histories;
- numerical integrator;
- convergence criterion.

The stage-structured results are therefore not independently interpretable, much less reproducible.

### Required minimum

Add a complete model block:

\[
X_{n+1}
=
\mathcal P^{\rm stage}_{T_r,\theta}(X_n),
\]

with every state and parameter defined. Supply a class table giving the exact anchovy, sprat, cod, and slow-stock vectors.

Without this, Sections 3.3, 3.4, and parts of the abstract and Discussion should be removed.

---

## 17. The continuous-delay comparator is also absent

The manuscript reports continuous-delay windows near:

\[
rg\approx1.5\text{–}1.6,
\]

with delay ranges such as:

\[
2.6\text{–}7.8\ \mathrm{yr}.
\]

But no continuous-delay equation or characteristic equation is given. The parameter \(g\) is undefined in the main model.

The phrase

> “the continuous delay equation and the sample-and-hold map are the same feedback loop under two delay operators”

cannot be assessed until the continuous-delay equation is shown and matched coefficient by coefficient to the sampled model.

---

## 18. The logistic hold-map Jacobian and monodromy are not given

The paper repeatedly invokes

\[
\det(M-e^{i\theta}I)=0,
\]

but \(M\) is never defined for either map.

For the logistic hold model, the authors should derive the flow. With held \(E\), let

\[
a(E)=r-qE.
\]

Then, for \(a(E)\ne0\),

\[
N(t+h)
=
\frac{
a(E)N(t)e^{a(E)h}
}{
a(E)+\frac rK N(t)(e^{a(E)h}-1)
}.
\]

When \(a(E)=0\),

\[
N(t+h)
=
\frac{N(t)}
{1+\frac rK N(t)h}.
\]

The signal flow may require quadrature because of \(\Phi\), but its variational equations can still be integrated exactly or numerically. The complete map derivative should be assembled from:

- the held-flow state-transition matrix;
- the assessment derivative;
- the review-reset derivative;
- the projection derivative where inactive.

Only then can multiplier claims be checked.

---

## 19. The \(47.54\)-yr label \(T_r^{\rm NS}\) is unjustified

Section 3.4 states:

\[
T_r^{\rm NS}=47.54\ \mathrm{yr}.
\]

A pair of unit-modulus multipliers is not by itself a Neimark–Sacker bifurcation. The following are required:

- a simple conjugate pair on the unit circle;
- all other multipliers strictly inside;
- transverse modulus crossing;
- exclusion of low-order resonances;
- nonlinear nondegeneracy;
- nonzero first Lyapunov coefficient for the map.

Section 2.2 explicitly says no multiplier or monodromy calculations support the response regions. It is unclear whether the \(47.54\)-yr result is exempt from that caveat.

### Correct wording

Unless all conditions are computed, use:

> a reported complex unit-circle crossing near \(47.54\) yr

and denote it

\[
T_r^{\rm UC},
\]

not \(T_r^{\rm NS}\).

---

## 20. “Restabilises only near 47.5 yr” requires a complete crossing count

To claim “only” requires showing that:

- the equilibrium is unstable for every \(0<T_r<47.54\);
- no earlier real or complex crossing restores stability;
- the crossing direction at \(47.54\) is stabilising;
- all multipliers lie inside afterward;
- the later stability interval is stated;
- no immediate second crossing occurs.

None of that is displayed.

A table is needed:

| \(T_r\) interval | multiplier count outside unit disk | dominant multiplier | status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| \(0<T_r<T_1\) |  |  |  |
| \(T_1<T_r<T_2\) |  |  |  |

## 21. The \(47.5\)-yr result may be an explicit-Euler artifact

For a scalar controller mode with linear coefficient \(C_E<0\), forward Euler contributes a factor approximately

\[
1+T_rC_E.
\]

Large review intervals can therefore cross the Euler stability boundary even when the corresponding exact continuous controller remains stable.

The manuscript should compare equation (4) with an exact held-assessment controller update. If

\[
\dot e=C_Ee+C_Zz_n
\]

and \(z_n\) is held during the review interval, then

\[
e_{n+1}
=
e^{C_ET_r}e_n
+
\frac{e^{C_ET_r}-1}{C_E}C_Zz_n,
\qquad C_E\ne0.
\]

Comparing this exact update with forward Euler would identify whether \(47.54\) yr is caused by:

- review cadence;
- zero-order holding;
- ecological feedback;
- Euler discretisation.

Without that comparison, the result should not be interpreted as a general governance-cadence phenomenon.

---

## 22. Local spectral stability at 47.5 yr says nothing about safe governance

Even if the equilibrium is locally stable, a 47.5-year hold may allow severe depletion before the next review.

A meaningful governance criterion should intersect local stability with inter-review safety:

\[
\rho(D\mathcal P_{T_r}(X^*))<1
\]

and

\[
N(t)\ge N_{\min}
\quad
\text{for every }t\in[t_n,t_{n+1}]
\]

over an uncertainty set.

Define a governable review set:

\[
\mathcal T_{\rm gov}
=
\mathcal T_{\rm stable}
\cap
\mathcal T_{\rm viable}
\cap
\mathcal T_{\rm institutionally\ feasible}.
\]

The \(47.5\)-yr threshold should be presented as a local spectral curiosity unless viability is checked.

# VI. Response-region interpretation

## 23. Long-horizon trajectory classification cannot establish stability or bifurcation windows

The manuscript correctly calls the regions exploratory, but later uses terms such as:

- “window relocates”;
- “annual-review stability”;
- “restabilises”;
- “two-operator spectrum.”

Trajectory convergence from a finite set of starts does not establish local stability for all perturbations, and persistent oscillation does not identify a bifurcation.

Use consistently:

- “tested trajectories converged”;
- “persistent oscillations were observed”;
- “finite-grid response region.”

Reserve “stability window” for multiplier-supported intervals.

---

## 24. “Annual review is stable at every tested response value” is internally imprecise

Stability is not something tested at finitely many initial histories and parameter values. The supported statement is:

> At every tested response value, all declared annual-review trajectories converged to the equilibrium within the declared numerical criteria.

The distinction matters because the manuscript itself emphasizes this methodological discipline.

---

## 25. Different dominant component periods need a clearer interpretation

The manuscript correctly notes that components of a stationary periodic orbit share a fundamental period. Reported peaks of approximately:

- 4 yr in biomass;
- 12 yr in effort;

could reflect a third harmonic, subharmonic response, modulation, transient dynamics, or different spectral leakage.

But the target bands in the empirical screen are then set differently for biomass and effort:

\[
4\text{–}8\ \mathrm{yr}
\quad\text{and}\quad
12\text{–}60\ \mathrm{yr}.
\]

If the model mechanism has not established which frequency relation is structural, those bands may be analysis-dependent rather than genuine predictions.

### Required upgrade

Report the full cross-spectrum:

- coherence;
- phase;
- fundamental frequency;
- harmonic ratios;
- transient versus asymptotic spectra.

The more identifiable prediction is not separate univariate peaks but a joint gain–phase signature.

---

## 26. The “institutional signal is carried by effort and quota utilisation” does not follow from the presented analysis

The model contains effort, but not quota utilisation as a distinct state or observable. An effort excursion of

\[
80\%\text{–}240\%
\]

of equilibrium is not the same as quota utilisation.

The claim also appears in the “two-operator spectrum” section without a derivation.

Replace it with:

> In the reported simulations, relative effort excursions were larger than relative biomass excursions.

Any quota-utilisation conclusion requires a quota, realised catch, and compliance model.

---

## 27. “No real small-pelagic system currently operates a responsive 3–4 yr review” needs evidence and a defined universe

This is a universal institutional claim. It requires:

- jurisdictional scope;
- date;
- definition of “responsive review”;
- search protocol;
- treatment of multiannual plans with annual adjustment clauses.

Otherwise use:

> No qualifying example was found in the declared case search.

# VII. The 42-stock spectral screen

## 28. FDR is not familywise-error control

The manuscript says:

> “familywise interpretation … under false-discovery-rate control in the Benjamini–Hochberg sense.”

Benjamini–Hochberg controls the false discovery rate under independence or certain positive-dependence conditions. It does not generally control the familywise error rate.

Under a global null and ideal independence, FDR and the probability of any rejection are related, but this does not justify calling BH a familywise procedure.

### Correct wording

> P-values were adjusted across the declared family using the Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate procedure.

If familywise control is desired, use Holm, Bonferroni, max-statistic permutation, or an explicitly justified alternative.

---

## 29. The multiplicity family is underdefined

The paper mentions multiplicity across:

- stocks;
- observables;
- bands.

But it also requires robustness across:

- detrending methods;
- endpoint choices;
- possibly several effort proxies;
- several periodogram peaks.

Are these sensitivity analyses filters, separate hypotheses, or researcher degrees of freedom? The multiplicity family must be specified before seeing results.

Provide the exact number of tests:

\[
m
=
\sum_{\text{stocks}}
\sum_{\text{series}}
\sum_{\text{bands}}
\sum_{\text{predeclared variants}}1.
\]

---

## 30. The statistic is described inconsistently as band power and peak power

Section 2.4 says the analysis:

> “integrates power in the predeclared bands”

but then classifies:

> “A peak … as robust…”

These are different statistics:

\[
T_{\rm band}
=
\int_{\omega_1}^{\omega_2}I(\omega)\,d\omega
\]

versus

\[
T_{\max}
=
\max_{\omega\in[\omega_1,\omega_2]}I(\omega).
\]

Their null distributions and power differ.

The manuscript must state exactly which statistic is tested.

---

## 31. The AR(1) red-noise null needs a complete calibration procedure

A valid per-series null requires specifying:

- how the AR coefficient is estimated;
- whether uncertainty in that estimate is propagated;
- whether detrending occurs before or within each null simulation;
- how missing observations are handled;
- whether the fitted variance is conditioned on;
- the number of Monte Carlo replicates;
- whether the full selection pipeline is rerun on every null replicate.

Analytical Lomb–Scargle false-alarm formulas generally do not remain valid after estimated AR(1) noise, detrending, band search, and endpoint sensitivity.

A parametric bootstrap of the entire pipeline is the natural solution.

---

## 32. Dependence across stocks and observables is ignored

Fish stocks share:

- climate forcing;
- assessment methods;
- survey systems;
- regional management;
- database revisions.

Biomass and fishing-mortality series from the same assessment are also strongly dependent.

Standard BH is valid under independence and some positive-dependence structures, but not under arbitrary dependence. The manuscript should either justify positive regression dependence, use Benjamini–Yekutieli, or employ a hierarchical/max-statistic resampling design.

---

## 33. “Annually assessed” does not establish annual review

RAM contains assessment series. It does not necessarily establish that:

- decisions were made annually;
- decisions were responsive;
- commands were implemented annually;
- the controller was extractive;
- the same rule persisted throughout the record.

The manuscript acknowledges part of this, but the title and comparison with annual-review simulations still encourage an unsupported mapping from annual assessment records to annual sample-and-hold governance.

Call the cohort:

> stocks with annual assessment-series resolution satisfying the declared data criterion.

Do not call it an annual-review cohort unless review dates are independently established.

---

## 34. Lomb–Scargle is not automatically the best choice for annual assessment series

Lomb–Scargle is useful for irregular observations, but many RAM series are annual and regularly spaced, possibly with missing years. More important issues are:

- assessment smoothing;
- retrospective revisions;
- model-induced autocorrelation;
- time-varying uncertainty;
- nonstationarity;
- regime shifts.

Assessment outputs are not raw observations. Their spectra partly reflect assessment-model regularisation.

At minimum, include sensitivity to:

- ordinary Fourier or multitaper estimates on complete annual segments;
- state-space uncertainty;
- assessment revision vintages;
- change-point or regime models;
- local spectral methods.

---

## 35. A 12–60 yr effort band is poorly resolved in many records

A 60-year period requires substantially more than one cycle for meaningful frequency discrimination. On 50–80-year records, power near that endpoint is dominated by:

- detrending;
- endpoints;
- low-frequency leakage;
- regime shifts.

Predeclare a minimum cycles criterion, such as requiring at least three complete candidate periods:

\[
L_{\rm record}\ge3P.
\]

Otherwise “12–60 yr” is partly a trend test, not a cycle test.

---

## 36. “No robust peak” is not a spectral null model result

The reported result is:

\[
\text{zero discoveries under one testing pipeline}.
\]

It does not establish that an AR(1) null adequately describes the series, nor that no periodic component exists. The manuscript often states this correctly, but “spectral null” is still potentially misleading.

Use:

> no target-band discoveries after the declared robustness and multiplicity filters.

---

# VIII. Power analysis

## 37. The power experiment is not sufficiently specified

Power depends critically on:

- signal amplitude;
- phase;
- transient alignment;
- record length;
- sampling pattern;
- AR coefficient;
- innovation variance;
- detrending;
- multiplicity adjustment;
- number of Monte Carlo replicates;
- whether all 42 stocks are simulated jointly.

“Noise scale \(\sigma=0.1\)” is uninterpretable without saying relative to what scale.

### Required definition

For example:

\[
Y_t
=
a\,s_t+\epsilon_t,
\qquad
\epsilon_t=\rho\epsilon_{t-1}+\sigma u_t,
\]

with \(s_t\) standardised in a declared way.

Report Monte Carlo confidence intervals for power. A value of \(1.0\) should be reported as, for example,

\[
1000/1000
\]

with a binomial interval, not as exact power one.

---

## 38. The power design should reproduce the empirical record-length distribution

The empirical screen includes records shorter than \(100\)–\(200\) yr, while the power analysis focuses on \(100\)–\(200\) yr synthetic records.

That power analysis does not characterize the actual screen.

For every empirical series \(i\), power should be simulated using:

\[
\{t_{ij}\},
\quad
L_i,
\quad
\widehat\rho_i,
\quad
\widehat\sigma_i,
\]

and its exact missingness pattern. Report the distribution of stock-specific power.

---

## 39. Family-level power is different from single-series power

If BH is applied across 42 stocks and multiple observables, the power experiment must simulate the entire multiple-testing family. Injecting one signal into one AR(1) series and applying a per-series threshold does not estimate discovery power under the actual screen.

Specify whether the reported power is:

- unadjusted per-series power;
- BH-adjusted marginal power;
- probability of at least one discovery;
- probability of discovering a particular injected stock.

---

# IX. Structured case search

## 40. The search is not systematic enough to support a meaningful zero count

“More than thirty systems” is too vague. The manuscript needs:

- the exact number;
- a predefined search universe;
- databases and search strings;
- dates searched;
- inclusion and exclusion decisions;
- source hierarchy;
- duplicate handling;
- reviewer count;
- inter-rater agreement;
- adjudication procedure.

Without these, the zero count is an author-curated case inventory.

---

## 41. Criterion (iii) is effectively impossible and structurally guarantees exclusion

The criterion requires:

> “no major environmental, biological, or structural driver capable of generating the same outcome.”

In open ecological systems, some alternative driver is almost always capable of generating a similar pattern. “Capable of” is much stronger than “empirically supported” and is generally not falsifiable.

This criterion selects only unrealistically isolated systems and makes the zero count unsurprising.

### Better criterion

Require that the institutional model outperform preregistered alternatives on:

- held-out prediction;
- phase ordering;
- intervention response;
- likelihood or predictive score;
- causal timing.

Do not require proof that no competing mechanism is capable of generating the pattern.

---

## 42. The search mixes mechanism eligibility with evidence quality

A case can contain institutional feedback while also containing climate or cohort effects. Confounding makes identification difficult; it does not make the mechanism absent.

Separate:

1. mechanism-present eligibility;
2. timing-data adequacy;
3. confounding risk;
4. identification strength.

Then report a graded evidence table rather than a binary eligible/ineligible count.

---

## 43. The “two structural reasons explain the null” are post hoc hypotheses

The manuscript says:

> “Two structural reasons explain the null.”

The proposed reasons—crisis-driven record selection and predicted periods longer than institutional persistence—are plausible, but they are not established by the search.

Use:

> Two hypotheses may help explain the zero count.

The claim that:

> “at any regeneration rate inside the instability window the predicted period is centuries”

also conflicts with the paper’s own reported 3–12 yr response regions unless it refers only to a particular slow-stock parameter regime. State the exact model and parameter subset.

---

## 44. Some case comparisons use mismatched timescales

The Icelandic cod discussion compares an implementation lag of \(0.2\)–\(0.3\) yr with a predicted period and review interval. These are different time objects, as Table 1 itself emphasizes.

Likewise, the anchoveta comparison invokes subannual review versus a \(3\)–\(4\)-yr response region without establishing:

- controller sign;
- controller gain;
- ecological parameter match;
- whether the model’s \(T_r\) corresponds to actual decision opportunities.

These examples illustrate screening logic, but do not test the model quantitatively.

---

# X. Northern cod model and phase-line obstruction

## 45. The phase-line proposition is mathematically sound but scientifically nearly tautological

For a locally Lipschitz scalar autonomous ODE,

\[
\dot x=f(x),
\]

non-equilibrium trajectories are monotone. Therefore any observed reversal excludes that model class.

This is correct, but it excludes virtually every fluctuating ecological time series from a one-dimensional deterministic autonomous model. It does not identify:

- an Allee effect;
- governance feedback;
- changing mortality;
- ecological regime change;
- predation;
- age structure.

Its scientific contribution is therefore a model-class adequacy check, not a mechanism result.

---

## 46. The proof should use Rolle’s theorem rather than claiming an interior extremum

If

\[
x(t_1)=x(t_2),
\]

Rolle’s theorem gives some \(\tau\in(t_1,t_2)\) with

\[
\dot x(\tau)=0.
\]

That is sufficient. The statement that the trajectory “attains an interior extremum” is not the cleanest argument.

A concise proof is:

\[
x(t_1)=x(t_2)
\implies
\exists\tau:\dot x(\tau)=0
\implies
f(x(\tau))=0.
\]

By uniqueness, the trajectory through \(x(\tau)\) is the equilibrium trajectory, contradicting nonconstancy.

---

## 47. The obstruction does not “apply directly” to the cod assessment series

The manuscript says:

> “The phase-line obstruction … applies directly.”

It does not, for two independent reasons.

### First: the data are estimated observations, not an exact trajectory

The SSB values are outputs of NCAM and carry:

- observation error;
- process assumptions;
- smoothing;
- retrospective uncertainty;
- age structure;
- model dependence.

The proposition concerns an exact scalar trajectory \(S(t)\).

### Second: removals were not fixed

Equation (5) is

\[
\dot S=f(S)-C(t).
\]

It is autonomous only when \(C(t)\) is constant. The post-moratorium period still had time-varying:

- reported removals;
- bycatch;
- food and recreational fisheries;
- unreported removals;
- changing natural mortality.

No evidence is shown that \(C(t)\) was fixed.

### Required correction

Replace:

> “applies directly”

with:

> “The reversals are incompatible with an exact fixed-parameter scalar autonomous representation if removals are constant and the reported SSB sequence is treated as an error-free observation of the state. Those assumptions are not satisfied by the assessment record, so the proposition is a diagnostic of model inadequacy rather than a direct empirical rejection.”

---

## 48. The claim that the stock crossed an Allee threshold in both directions is unsupported

The manuscript says:

> “the series cannot remain in one basin of the threshold and cannot cross the threshold in both directions.”

No numerical value or fitted interval for \(\mathfrak s\) is supplied. Repeated direction reversals do not establish threshold crossing. They merely exclude monotone motion under a scalar autonomous vector field.

Delete the threshold-crossing language unless \(\mathfrak s\) is estimated with uncertainty and the state trajectory is shown to cross it.

---

## 49. The strong-Allee model with constant catch does not preserve nonnegative biomass

With

\[
\dot S=f(S)-C,
\qquad C>0,
\]

one has

\[
\dot S\big|_{S=0}=-C<0.
\]

Thus the model crosses into negative biomass unless catch is constrained near zero or the state equation is modified.

Because the manuscript emphasizes forward invariance elsewhere, it should acknowledge this inconsistency.

A biologically admissible formulation could use:

\[
C(t,S)\le cS
\]

or impose a state-constrained harvest rule.

---

## 50. Constant loss and extra mortality are not the same perturbation

The lemma groups:

- constant loss \(C>0\);
- extra mortality \(M_x>0\).

But these modify production differently:

\[
f_C(S)=f(S)-C,
\]

\[
f_M(S)=f(S)-M_xS.
\]

For constant loss, \(S=0\) is no longer an equilibrium. For proportional mortality, it remains an equilibrium. The relevant production maxima and saddle-node conditions differ.

The lemma should be split into two statements and proved separately.

For constant catch, positive equilibria solve

\[
f(S)=C.
\]

For proportional mortality, positive equilibria solve

\[
\frac{f(S)}S=M_x.
\]

---

## 51. “The crash interpretation is formulation-dependent” is defensible only as assessment attribution

The manuscript later clarifies that this is an attribution conditional on model structure. That qualification should appear immediately and consistently.

The two formulations are not exhaustive causal explanations. Formulation dependence shows non-identifiability between modeled mortality components, not that the true crash mechanism is one of exactly two alternatives.

Better wording:

> The allocation of estimated deaths between natural mortality and unreported fishing is assessment-formulation dependent.

---

## 52. The constrained-\(M\) quantities cannot support Results or conclusions if they are unreproduced

The manuscript gives specific values:

\[
M=0.46,\quad F=1.37,\quad 257.8\ {\rm kt\,yr^{-1}},
\]

then says they are:

> “unreproduced … hypotheses, not results.”

These values should not appear as substantive Results until the equations, data, code, windows, and uncertainty are supplied.

Also,

> “102.5% of mean spawning biomass”

compares a yearly flow with a stock. The dimensionally correct ratio is approximately:

\[
1.025\ {\rm yr}^{-1}
\]

relative to mean SSB, not simply “102.5%.”

---

## 53. “Non-recovery unexplained in both formulations” is not demonstrated

One formulation is unreproduced. More importantly, mortality-attribution formulations are not necessarily full post-collapse growth models. Failure of a scalar Allee equation to reproduce reversals does not show that both NCAM formulations fail to explain non-recovery.

The manuscript must define “explain” operationally:

- fit the trajectory?
- reproduce reversals?
- predict the post-2015 stall?
- identify causal mortality components?
- forecast recovery?

At present, “unexplained” shifts among these meanings.

---

## 54. The “two-window split” is not a positive result in the strong sense claimed

The proposed split is:

1. crash attribution is model dependent;
2. later dynamics are not captured by a fixed scalar autonomous model.

Both are useful cautions, but neither is a novel empirical mechanism. Calling the split the paper’s “one defensible positive result” gives it more inferential weight than warranted.

A more accurate statement is:

> The cod case illustrates two distinct identification problems: mortality-component attribution during collapse and structural nonstationarity during the post-collapse period.

That is a strong methodological contribution without overstating the evidence.

---

## 55. “Production has not returned to levels implied by any single-regime growth law” is unsupported

No family of single-regime growth laws is fitted or excluded. A sufficiently flexible density-dependent model with environmental covariates or time-varying observation error might reproduce the record. Even among autonomous laws, sparse noisy data do not permit the universal statement “any.”

Use:

> The post-2015 production stall is inconsistent with the simplest fixed-parameter surplus-production interpretation considered here.

---

## 61. “Confirmed” is too strong for the prospective programme

The conclusion says the designs specify how the mechanism would be:

No finite empirical design confirms a mechanistic model in the strong sense, especially under structural alternatives.

Use:

## 62. The most powerful prospective target is the gain–phase relation, not periodicity

This is one of the manuscript’s strongest ideas and should be made central.

For a local linear system, identify the closed-loop transfer relationships among:

\[
\text{assessment}
\longrightarrow
\text{command}
\longrightarrow
\text{realised effort}
\longrightarrow
\text{stock}.

The empirical targets should include:

- gain;
- phase;
- coherence;
- delay decomposition where identifiable;
- controller sign;
- saturation and nonlinearities.

A peak alone cannot identify the loop direction. A preregistered gain–phase curve is much more diagnostic.

## 63. Randomising “feedback sign” raises ethical and interpretive problems

A human-in-the-loop experiment may randomise simulated signs safely. A field pilot that randomises extractive response to decline could be ecologically dangerous.

Field designs require viability constraints and stopping rules before controller-sign randomisation is considered.

# XV. Recommended profound upgrade: separate four inferential layers

The paper will be much clearer if it is rebuilt around four layers.

## Layer 1: fully specified sampled-data mathematics

Define one baseline ecological plant and one review convention. Derive:

\[
X_{n+1}=\mathcal P_{T_r}(X_n),
\]

\[
D\mathcal P_{T_r}(X^*),
\]

and the fixed-point and multiplier conditions.

Prove:

- global existence;
- stock and effort invariance;
- rapid-review state consistency;
- \(C^1\) consistency;
- conditional small-\(T_r\) stability preservation.

## Layer 2: matched architecture comparison

For the same ecological plant and coefficients, compare:

1. continuous controller;
2. continuous fixed-delay controller;
3. exact reviewed controller;
4. Euler-reviewed controller.

This isolates:

\[
\text{delay operator},
\quad
\text{holding},
\quad
\text{Euler discretisation}.
\]

Only after that should the stage-structured plant be introduced as a separate ecological sensitivity analysis.

## Layer 3: empirical identification

Replace univariate periodicity as the main empirical signature with:

\[
\text{gain–phase–coherence across assessment, command, effort, and stock}.
\]

The spectral screen can remain as a weak descriptive check, but it should not occupy the title unless fully archived and calibrated.

## Layer 4: governance feasibility

Intersect local spectral stability with ecological safety and institutional feasibility:

\[
\mathcal T_{\rm gov}
=
\left\{
T_r:
\rho(D\mathcal P_{T_r}(X^*))<1
\right\}
\cap
\left\{
T_r:
\operatorname{Reach}_{[0,T_r]}(B,a)
\subseteq\mathcal V
\right\}
\cap
\mathcal T_{\rm inst}.
\]

This prevents the paper from treating a 47.5-year local restabilisation as a plausible management recommendation.

# XVII. Recommended title and paper scope

The current title combines three studies:

1. sampled governance theory;
2. a 42-stock spectral screen;
3. a northern cod case.

The mathematical operator comparison is the strongest potential contribution, but it is presently the least completely specified. The empirical components are cautious but do not identify the mechanism.

A more accurate title would be:

> **Periodic Review as Sampled Governance: Operator-Matched Effort Control, a Selected-Stock Spectral Screen, and an Identification Audit for Northern Cod**

Or, if the paper is narrowed:

> **Periodic Review as Sampled Governance: Exact Review Maps, Euler Artifacts, and Gain–Phase Identification in Fisheries Control**

The latter would produce a substantially more coherent methodological article.

# Final diagnosis

The manuscript’s most defensible current results are:

1. a useful seven-part governance-time ontology;
2. a basic positivity and projected-effort invariance argument;
3. the correct warning that finite-horizon consistency alone is not a fixed-review stability theorem;
4. the insistence that sampled maps and continuous-delay equations are different operators;
5. a no-discovery result in a selected spectral screen, subject to full calibration and release;
6. a valid scalar phase-line proposition, if kept at its exact mathematical scope;
7. a strong prospective emphasis on gain–phase identification and closed-loop MSE.

Its least defensible claims are:

1. that the reported map comparison isolates architecture substitution;
2. that the review timing is fully specified;
3. that a Neimark–Sacker threshold occurs at \(47.54\) yr;
4. that “restabilises only” has been established;
5. that annual-review stability follows from trajectory convergence;
6. that the cod phase-line obstruction applies directly to the assessment record;
7. that non-recovery is shown to be unexplained in two formulations;
8. that unavailable exploratory computations can support title- and abstract-level numerical conclusions.

The most important upgrade is to replace the present “same loop, different operator” narrative with a controlled decomposition:

\[
\boxed{
\text{ecological plant}
\times
\text{controller law}
\times
\text{review timing}
\times
\text{numerical implementation}.
}
\]

Only a comparison that varies one factor at a time can identify architecture substitution. Once that is done, the empirical target should be changed from “does a spectral peak exist?” to:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does the observed assessment}\to\text{command}\to
\text{effort}\to\text{stock gain–phase relation match the registered loop?}
}
\]

That shift would close the paper’s largest gap: the gap between a plausible sampled-data mechanism and evidence capable of identifying it.
