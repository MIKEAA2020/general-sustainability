**Theorem 8 (local threshold-horizon bracket).**  
Assume \(A\) is absolutely continuous, starts above the threshold, and while it remains above the threshold its decline rate stays inside the relative band \((1\pm\varepsilon)v_0\). Then a crossing time \(H\) exists and satisfies
\[

\]
where \(H_0=(A(0)-A_{\min})/v_0\).  

The proof is a direct integration/comparison argument: if no crossing has occurred by \(t^*=H_0/(1-\varepsilon)\) the lower rate bound already forces \(A(t^*)\le A_{\min}\), a contradiction; integrating the two rate bounds over \([0,H]\) then yields the two-sided estimate. ## 1. Theorem 12 is false as literally stated for m = 1 (the main flaw)

The theorem quantifies over "every weight vector \(w\in\mathbb{R}_+^m\), \(w\neq0\)" with no lower bound on \(m\). But check the case \(m=1\): a single component, weight \(w>0\). The hypothesis is \(w(s_1-d_1)\ge0\); since \(w>0\), this divides through to give \(s_1-d_1\ge0\) directly — i.e., componentwise nonnegativity **is** certified, trivially, by the unique positive weighting. So for \(m=1\), "no nonnegative weighting certifies componentwise nonnegativity" is simply false.

This isn't a pedantic corner case — it's exposed by the proof's own structure. The proof says: "let \(j\) be an index with \(w_j>0\); choose the deficit in the **other** component \(m\neq j\)." This step is impossible when the ledger has only one component. The construction silently assumes at least two components, but the theorem's stated hypotheses never say so. Every other theorem in this paper (8, 9, the earlier orthant-invariance and rest-point results) states its preconditions explicitly and tightly; this is the one place a load-bearing dimensional assumption (\(m\ge2\)) is missing from the statement, even though the whole point of Section 11 is to license exactly this kind of universal negative claim. Given that this theorem is the one explicitly advertised in the abstract and Section 1.1 as the paper's signature negative result, the gap is more consequential than most of the smaller issues found earlier.

A fix is easy (add "\(m\ge2\)"), but as stated, the theorem overclaims.

## 2. A genuine type collision: ρ means two different things

Section 4.2 fixes \(\rho\in[0,1]\) as the **retirement-routing fraction** (fraction of retired product returned to detritus \(U\) vs. the absorbing sink \(W\)) — a dimensionless split fraction, reused consistently in Section 10.1 for the phosphorus recovery fraction.

Section 10.3 then writes the modified golden rule as \(g'(S_\rho)=\rho\), where \(\rho\) is now an **economic discount rate**. These are conceptually unrelated quantities (a material-routing fraction vs. a time-preference parameter) sharing one symbol. This is a real internal inconsistency, and it's a pointed one: Section 2.2 explicitly makes strict typing a foundational discipline ("entries are added within a row only when their types and units agree; a conversion between types is an explicit stoichiometric coefficient, never an implicit sum"). A paper built around not conflating types under shared notation reuses \(\rho\) for two different types of quantity elsewhere in the same document.

## 3. "The modified golden rule" is stated in its restricted form without saying so

Clark's actual fundamental equation of renewable resource economics is
\[
g'(S^*) - \frac{c'(S^*)\,g(S^*)}{p-c(S^*)} = \delta,
\]
which includes a marginal-stock-effect-on-cost term. The simple form \(g'(S_\rho)=\rho\) given in the text is the special case that holds only when harvesting cost is stock-independent. That assumption is at least *consistent* with the paper's own \(S_{OA}=c/(pq)\) model (constant unit cost \(c\)), so it isn't an error relative to the model actually being used — but presenting it as "the modified golden rule" without flagging that the general version carries an extra term is a minor mislabeling relative to the standard bioeconomics literature (Clark 1990) that the section cites.

## 4. The attainability claim in Theorem 12's proof sits in tension with the paper's own conservation machinery

The witness ledger in the proof is a two-compartment system with a single donor-limited transfer \(f=kx_1\) from compartment 1 to 2, no boundary term. But this is *exactly* an instance of the moiety-conservation structure the paper spends Sections 2–5 establishing: \(\ell=(1,1)\) satisfies \(\ell^\top N=0\) for this transfer (column \((-1,1)\)), so by the paper's own Proposition 1, \(x_1+x_2\) is invariant along any trajectory of this closed system. The proof nonetheless asserts "every \(x\in\mathbb{R}_+^2\) is admissible with the declared flux," which is true only if "admissible" means "a legitimate instantaneous/initial state," not "jointly reachable from a common initial condition over time" — a distinction the proof never makes explicit, even though the entire rest of the paper is about trajectories and conserved quantities, not free-standing points. A reader applying the paper's own Theorem 3/Proposition 1 machinery to this witness system would initially expect the two coordinates to be constrained, not free. The proof survives once you read "attainable" as "valid at \(t=0\) for some choice of initial mass," but it doesn't say so, leaving a real ambiguity given the paper's own emphasis elsewhere.

## 5. Minor items

- **Future-dated citation.** "U.S. Geological Survey, 2026" appears twice (Sections 9.3 and 10.1). Not necessarily wrong, but worth confirming against the manuscript's actual date — a 2026 USGS Mineral Commodity Summary is only available if the paper itself postdates early 2026.
- **Unspecified generalization.** Theorem 12's proof is carried out explicitly only for a 2-compartment ledger. For general \(m>2\) the natural fix (hold all other components exactly at their demand, \(s_i=d_i\), and run the same two-index argument on \(j,m\)) is obvious but is never stated, leaving the general-\(m\) case technically unproven as written, not just the \(m=1\) case.

**Net assessment:** the load-bearing content of Section 11 — the noncompensation result — is directionally correct and philosophically important (this is the honest formal cash-out of Martinez-Alier et al.'s weak-comparability thesis flagged back in Section 1.1), but the theorem as literally stated has an unguarded degenerate case that makes it false at \(m=1\), and the surrounding notation has a real type collision on \(\rho\) that the paper's own stated methodology would flag as a violation if it appeared in someone else's ledger. These are exactly the kind of narrow, locatable defects (like the Theorem 6 boundary gap found earlier) that are typical of genuine, mostly-correct mathematical writing with an unpolished edge — not evidence of wholesale fabrication, but real flaws worth fixing before this could be called fully rigorous.

**The manuscript is internally coherent on its core accounting and dynamical claims, but it has a cluster of notational/proof errors, numbering gaps, suspicious or anachronistic data, blurred object types, and a large gap between the “full ontology + theorems” framing and what is actually executed.** The mathematics that is written down is mostly elementary and correct in substance; the problems are in execution, cross-references, data, and over-complete packaging.

**Mathematical and notational flaws**
- Theorem numbering is broken: Theorem 1 appears in 4.5 (support-saturated logistic), then the next numbered result is Theorem 3 (mass identity). Theorem 2 is missing.
- Theorem 12 (no weighted certification) has a symbol mismatch. The proof defines the balance as \(s = x - d\), then writes the claim as \(w^\top(s - d) \ge 0\) while the displayed algebra is \(w^\top(x - d)\). The unused donor-limited flux \(f = kx_1\) is introduced and never used. The witness still proves the intended linear-algebra fact, but the write-up does not match.
- Inverse-Gaussian CDF in Theorem 10 / Corollary 2 is written without the square roots that belong in the standard formula (\(\Phi(\sqrt{\lambda/t}(t/\nu-1))\) etc.). The qualitative claims (median \(<\) mean, concentration as \(\sigma\to 0\)) are true for the IG, so this is a formula error rather than a conceptual one.
- Theorem 6 (extinction–geochemical rest set) is incomplete on the \(A^{\rm act}=A^{\rm geo}=0\) face. Because regeneration and uptake are multiplied by the support factor \(s\), there is no independent basal mortality; biomass \(N\) can freeze at any nonnegative value when nutrients hit zero. The paper excludes this corner by the geo-balance argument and by treating \(A_{g0}=0\) as a discontinuous limit, but the vector field as written admits a larger rest set.
- Stock / moiety / service objects are not kept distinct. Barriers and Layer 3 act on \(S=Cx\); Definition 1’s feasible domain is built from a service map \(O\) (typically linear in fluxes); Theorem 12 instantiates an identity readout on the stocks themselves and treats \(x-d\) as the balance. The non-compensation fact holds for either interpretation, but the paper mixes them in the proof.

**Data, citations, and numerical red flags**
- USGS vintage “2026” for phosphate reserves/production (9.3, 10.1) is a future or placeholder date. The arithmetic (world ~74 Gt reserves, ~240 Mt yr\(^{-1}\) production, R/P ~309 yr) is in the right ballpark for recent USGS tables, but the year is not.
- Groundwater rates look extreme for basin-mean G3P/GRACE products. Indo-Gangetic \(-49.7\) cm yr\(^{-1}\) (anomaly \(-414\) cm, index ~2.7 yr) is an order of magnitude larger than typical published basin-average groundwater-equivalent trends (usually a few cm yr\(^{-1}\)). The other basins are more plausible in magnitude but still sit at the high end. These may be a specific reprocessing, a sub-region, an illustration, or an error; they are presented as “the four-basin record.”
- Illakwahhi, Vegi & Srivastava (2024) is used for a strong claim about single-source USGS phosphate data; it is not a widely known landmark paper in the way Tilton & Lagos (2007) or Clark (1990) are. It needs an exact bibliographic check.
- Inverse-horizon score in 9.2 averages five incommensurable terms (four basins + world P) and is then correctly labelled as not a certificate. Computing it at all sits awkwardly with the typing rules of 2.1 and Theorem 12.

**Internal inconsistencies and scoping gaps**
- Applications (Section 9) are re-labellings of existing proxies (trend-to-window-min, R/P, removals-only \(\Theta_F\)), not computations that use the ledger ODEs, the envelopes of Theorem 9, the bracket of Theorem 8, or the model hitting time of Definition 4. The paper says this explicitly (“none of the reported numbers is a computed instance of any model’s first-hitting time”), so it is not a contradiction, but it is a large gap relative to the opening claim of a full accounting layer that “gives each such claim its exact status.”
- Fisheries diagnostics are not aligned. 9.4 is a deliberately incomplete removals-only pressure time (no recruitment, growth, or natural mortality). 10.3 imports a surplus-production / open-access / modified-golden-rule apparatus (\(S_{OA}=c/(pq)\), \(g'(S_\rho)=\rho\)). They are presented as separate “extractor-side” remarks, but the growth function is never placed inside the typed ledger.
- Two-pool groundwater is admitted as unidentified (10.2), yet the paper’s own closed-ledger examples (4.1–4.2) *are* two-pool donor-limited systems. This is consistent only because the G3P application is never treated as an instance of those ODEs; the text could be clearer that the ledger examples are templates, not fitted to the satellite series.
- Companion-paper objects (\((Z,E)\) memory–effort pair driven by \(qEN-R\)) are invoked for the “shared object” of Lemma 1 but never analysed here. The identity \(\Lambda=[qEN-R]_+=[-\dot N]_+\) is just a rearrangement of the first ODE.
- Five double-counting rules are listed; only some are enforced by construction (yield routing via \(\alpha,\rho\), column sums zero). They are not all stated as proved identities.
- “Typed” discipline (units, no implicit sums across types) is declared but never used operationally to reject a concrete published number beyond the verbal classifications.
- Parameterisation of the natural block gives concrete values (\(r=0.02\), \(K=100\), \(q=0.001\), \ldots) and a numerical “working point” that is shown not to be an equilibrium, yet \(A_{g0}\) is left unspecified and the \(\sigma\approx 1\) regime is assumed.

**What remains consistent and correct**
- Mass identities from column sums / pairwise cancellation, orthant invariance from donor limitation + tangent cone, no interior rest under positive extraction, integrability of cumulative extraction against initial \(M(0)\), and the linear-decay counter-example \(\dot S=-kS\) (positive extraction forever, \(\tau_0=\infty\)) are all standard and correctly proved for the written vector fields.
- The three-way taxonomy (gross turnover vs. frozen-rate local ratio vs. scenario-conditioned hitting time), the elementary comparison bracket (Theorem 8), and the box-envelope barrier certificate (Theorem 9) do what the abstract promised and correctly refuse to treat R/P or anomaly indices as physical horizons.
- Record-relative barrier discipline (barrier taken from the same window as the trend) is a valid and useful statistical caution; the IG and GBM first-passage calculations are textbook once scoped as surrogates, not ledger completions.
- Explicit non-claims (no stochastic completion, no thermodynamics, no two-pool identification, no empirical findings beyond classified status of the tables, no scalar certificate of componentwise floors) are repeated consistently and match the theorems that are actually proved.
- Independence of accounting consistency, conservation, and barrier safety is correctly illustrated by construction.

**Net assessment**
The manuscript does not collapse into contradiction, and its central critiques of depletion statistics (R/P is arithmetic, not a forecast; anomaly indices in years are not hitting times; gross throughput is not net decline; weighted sums do not certify floors) are legitimate. The flaws that matter for reliability are: (1) sloppy write-up of the headline negative theorem and the IG CDF, (2) missing Theorem 2 and incomplete rest-set characterisation, (3) mixing of stock/service/moiety maps, (4) USGS 2026 and implausibly large Indo-Gangetic rates, (5) applications that classify rather than recompute with the new objects, and (6) an identification/protocol layer (P and GW templates) that is registered but not discharged. The result reads as an ambitious, densely labelled theoretical accounting framework whose elementary theorems check out, but whose data, proofs, and “full ledger” claims have not been fully tightened.

Pulling together everything checked across the whole manuscript, here is the complete flaw/gap/inconsistency inventory, organized by severity and type.

## I. Genuine logical gaps in stated theorems

**1. Theorem 6 (extinction–geochemical rest set) — boundary stratum omitted.** The proof implicitly assumes \(A^{geo}>0\) when it infers "\(N=0\) or \(N=K\)" from \(s>0\). At the boundary \(A^{geo}=0\), the geo-balance forces \(A^{act}=0\), hence \(s=0\) identically, which makes \(\dot N=0\) for *every* \(N\), not just \(N\in\{0,K\}\). Checking the remaining equations confirms \((N,0,0,0)\) is a valid rest point for all \(N\ge0\) — a continuum the theorem's "exactly the two families" statement misses, even though the stated families explicitly range over \(A^{geo}\ge0\).

**The manuscript largely closes the accounting and interpretation gaps it opened; it does not close the empirical, identification, or “typed” operational gaps, and it does not upgrade the elementary mathematics into a new theory.** What it *does* deliver is a tightly fenced hygiene framework: three independent predicates, a finite-donor qualitative ODE package, a three-object diagnostics taxonomy, record-relative first-passage semantics, and an explicit list of things the ledger cannot certify. That is coherent. It is not yet profound.

Below is a line-level synthesis of the whole text: what actually closed, what remains open, where the write-up still contradicts itself, and what would constitute a real upgrade rather than denser labelling.

### 2. Line-level gaps that never close

**Numbering and formulae**
- Theorem 2 does not exist. Thm 1 (4.5 logistic limit) jumps to Thm 3 (mass identity). Either a result was deleted or the numbering was never reconciled.
- Thm 10 / Cor. 2: inverse-Gaussian CDF is written without the square roots in \(\Phi(\sqrt{\lambda/t}(t/\nu-1))\). Moments and “median \(<\nu\)” are still true; the displayed CDF is not.
- Thm 12: defines \(s=x-d\), claims \(w^\top(s-d)\ge 0\), proves \(w^\top(x-d)\ge 0\). Flux \(f=kx_1\) is unused. The linear-algebra fact survives; the ledger embedding does not.

**Vector field vs rest-set statement (Thm 6 vs 4.1)**
Regeneration and uptake are both multiplied by \(s=A^{\rm act}/(A^{\rm act}+A_0)\). On the face \(A^{\rm act}=A^{\rm geo}=0\) one has \(s=0\), hence \(\dot N=0\) for *every* \(N\ge 0\), \(\dot U=0\) if \(U=0\). The rest set is larger than the two families written. The paper treats \(A_{g0}=0\) as a “discontinuous-perturbation limit, not the registered regime,” which hides the face rather than analysing it. That is a constitutive gap (no basal mortality independent of support), not a conservation error.

**“Typed” is a label, not a discipline**
2.2: “Entries are added within a row only when their types and units agree.” Nowhere is a published table rejected by a type error except verbally (anomaly-years ≠ hitting time; R/P ≠ geological clock). No dimension system, no software, no rejected MFA worksheet. The word “typed” in the title object is therefore unearned at line level.

**Five rules are not five theorems**
Only yield-routing and column-sum conservation are enforced by construction. “Opposite-sign incidence of the same primitive is checkable” has no check. Classification labels “stay out of the columns” is a policy, practised in 9.3, not proved.

**Stock / moiety / service maps are mixed**
- Layer 3 and \(K(t)\) use \(S=Cx\).
- Def. 1 uses a service readout \(O\), “typically linear in primitives.”
- Lemma 1’s \(\Lambda=[qEN-R]_+\) is a *stock* decline rate.
- Thm 12 uses identity readout on stocks as if they were the balance vector of Def. 1.

The noncompensation thesis is true for any of these, but the paper does not keep one map for certification.

**Applications never touch the ledger ODEs**
9.1–9.4 restate \(L_{\rm hist}^{\rm anom}\), USGS R/P, and \(\Theta_F=F^{-1}\log({\rm SSB}/B_{\lim})\). The text *says* they are not model hitting times. That honesty does not close the gap with the abstract’s “accounting layer.” No storage coefficient converts G3P cm to a physical \(A\), so \(H_A^{\rm loc}\) is never computed. No flux boxes are estimated, so Thm 9 never fires. No rate band \((1\pm\varepsilon)v_0\) is fitted, so Thm 8 never fires.

**Data and citation lines that do not close**
- “U.S. Geological Survey, 2026” (9.3, 10.1) is a future/placeholder vintage. Arithmetic (world \(\sim 74\) Gt, \(\sim 240\) Mt yr\(^{-1}\), \(\sim 309\) yr) is in-family for recent USGS phosphate-rock tables; the year is not.
- Indo-Gangetic \(-49.7\) cm yr\(^{-1}\), 2023 anomaly \(-414\) cm, index \(\approx 2.7\) yr: internally consistent as \((a_{\rm latest}-a_{\rm min})/|\hat\mu|\) only if the window minimum is still lower. As a *basin-mean* G3P/GRACE groundwater-equivalent trend it is an outlier relative to the published literature (typically a few to \(\sim 20\) cm yr\(^{-1}\) in hotspots, not \(\sim 50\)). High Plains “already at minimum” correctly triggers the degenerate \(T=0\) of 8.4; the Indo-Gangetic cell does not get the same scrutiny.
- Illakwahhi, Vegi & Srivastava (2024) is asked to carry a strong claim about single-source USGS phosphate dates. Unlike Tilton & Lagos (2007) or Clark (1990), it is not a landmark the reader can take on trust without the reference list.
- Fisheries median \(\approx 1.8\) yr on 43 RAM stocks with \(B_{\lim}=0.2\max{\rm SSB}\) is possible for a heavily fished subset but is strong enough that the cohort definition (“finite SSB and \(F\) series; zero entries included”) needs to be inspectable. It is not.

**Companion objects remain external**
\((Z,E)\) “driven by \(qEN-R\)” and “analysed in a companion paper” (4.1, Lemma 1). Lemma 1 is \(\dot N=R-qEN\) rearranged. Nothing about institutional delay is proved here. Citing a companion as the transfer channel is a gap, not a closure.

**Bioeconomic overlay vs removals-only diagnostic**
9.4: \(\Theta_F\) *omits* recruitment, growth, \(M\), density dependence.  
10.3: open-access \(S_{OA}=c/(pq)\) and modified golden rule \(g'(S_\rho)=\rho\) *require* a surplus-production \(g\).  
Both points are valid separately. They are not the same fisheries object, and \(g\) is never typed into \(N,v\).

**Two-pool groundwater**
10.2 admits the two-pool model is not identified. 4.1–4.2 *are* two-pool donor-limited ledgers. Closure exists only if the reader never identifies G3P with (2). The text should have said that in 9.1 in one sentence; it does so only later.

**Parameterisation theatre**
Registered numbers \(r=0.02\), \(K=100\), \(q=0.001\), \(\kappa_A=0.05\), \(\omega_A=10^{-3}\), \(A_0=1\), \(A_{\rm eq}=50\), \(\gamma_U=0.2\), working point \((N,A^{\rm act})=(89.526,397.87)\), \(E^*\approx 2.090\), \(R^*\approx 0.187\). The arithmetic \(R=qEN\) at \(s=1\) checks out; \(A^{\rm act}=397.87\) is *not* the geo-pinned value \(A_{\rm eq}\sigma\approx 50\). They use that correctly to say the reduced working point is not a rest of (2). \(A_{g0}\) is never given. This is illustration, not identification — which 10.1–10.2 admit, but 4.1’s “registered parameterisation” language oversells.

### 3. Internal inconsistencies that survive to the end

1. **Abstract vs 8.7 / 11.2.** Opening lists “first-passage semantics for the public-data proxies” in the same breath as ledger theorems. Later, seven non-claims say the surrogates do not conserve mass and are not perturbations. The *body* is consistent; the *opening* still invites the reading the body forbids.
2. **“Exact status” vs tables.** 9.2 still prints a mixed inverse-horizon \(\Sigma_{\rm reserves}\approx 0.130\) yr\(^{-1}\) “only to mark the boundary of legitimate aggregation.” Computing an illegitimate aggregate in order to disavow it is a rhetorical tic, not a certification result, and it sits next to Thm 12.
3. **Conservation vs service.** 2.1: conservation attaches only to moieties. 6.1: services are not conserved mass. 6.3 then calls \(\Delta_{\rm phys}=C-M^\top S\) a “physical deficit” and collapses it to \(-\dot N\) on the living stock. Living biomass is a species-in-compartment, not a moiety total. The collapse is algebraically true for that ODE, conceptually a slide of the kind 2.1 warned against.
4. **Barrier safety independence (Prop. 2) vs Cor. 1.** Prop. 2: mass-balanced trajectories can violate barriers. Cor. 1: flux *boxes* can certify barriers. Compatible, but only if the reader sees that Cor. 1 assumes extra flux bounds the ledger does not produce by itself. That extra assumption is the whole content of the “envelope theorem.”
5. **Donor limitation as “the” admissibility condition (2.2) vs Thm 12 witness.** Thm 12’s witness needs only \(x\ge 0\) admissible; dynamics are irrelevant. Fine for linear algebra; it does not use the ledger the paper spent §§4–5 building.

None of these is a fatal contradiction. All of them are the residue of a framework that names more objects than it keeps distinct in proofs.

### 4. What is already solid (do not “upgrade” by undoing)

These lines are correct and should stay:

- R/P is an arithmetic property of an economic classification (Tilton–Lagos point; phosphate split reserves/resources).
- \(g>0\not\Rightarrow\dot A<0\) (Def. 2).
- Frozen-rate ratio is not a trajectory forecast (Def. 3); \(\varepsilon>0\) uniform net loss is load-bearing (Thm 8 + Ctr-ex. 1).
- \(\int_0^\infty qEN\,dt\le M(0)\) (Thm 7): finite donor budget is the *initial natural-block mass*, not living stock alone.
- Record-relative barrier = record-breaking stress statistic, not physical floor (8.4), including the degenerate \(T=0\) when already at window min.
- Open-access equilibrium and a physical floor are different objects; a tax moves \(S_{OA}\), not \(S_{\min}\) (10.3).
- Weighted sum of margins does not certify componentwise floors (substance of Thm 12).
- Empty viability kernel under a positive output floor into a non-recycling sink (4.3).

That package is enough to discipline a large class of published “years remaining” figures. It is the real content.

### 5. Profound upgrades (what would change the paper’s status)

Without these, the manuscript remains a careful restatement of CRNT + MFA + elementary hitting times + ecological-economics noncompensation, wearing a complete ontology. With them, it becomes a method.

**U1. Make “typed” operational.**  
Give a unit/sort system (moiety, species, compartment, currency, time-like index, economic classification label) and a single inference rule: a quantity with sort `TimeIndex` cannot inhabit a column of sort `HittingTime` without an explicit coercion that names the scenario \(\pi,d\). Run that rule on the four Section 9 objects and on 2–3 published MFA tables. Until a worksheet is *rejected by type*, “typed ledger” is branding.

**U2. Prove the five rules as blocking lemmas with phantom-mass counterexamples.**  
For each rule, one identity or inequality and one numerical MFA fragment that creates mass if the rule is dropped (double-counted recycling, yield \(<1\) dropped, ghost sink, reserve label in a dynamical column). 5.2 does this only for yield routing.

**U3. Fire Theorems 8–9 on data, not only on definitions.**  
- G3P: convert at least one basin with a published storage coefficient / saturated-thickness range to a physical \(A\), put a band on \(-\dot A\), report the Thm 8 bracket and when it *fails* (trend breaks, seasonality).  
- Phosphate: treat reserve revision as a jump process on the classification, not a constant \(P\); show how \(T_{\rm reserve}\) moves under USGS vintage changes (this also kills the “2026” line).  
- Fisheries: put \(\Theta_F\) next to a net \(\dot B = g(B)-F B\) hitting time on the same 43 stocks so the “must not be promoted” claim is a measured gap, not a sermon.

**U4. Repair and retarget Theorem 12.**  
State it on Def. 1’s set \(B(x,t)\): for every \(w\ge 0\), \(w\neq 0\), there exist \(b\in B(x,t)\) with \(w^\top b\ge 0\) and \(b_m<0\). Drop the unused flux. One sentence connecting this to the inverse-horizon score: that score is admissible as communication, forbidden as Layer 3.

**U5. Close or shrink the rest-set gap.**  
Either add basal mortality so \(N=0\) is the only support-free rest, or state the frozen-biomass face as a modelling limitation of multiplicative support. As written, Thm 6 overclaims.

**U6. Identifiability, not a ladder.**  
10.1–10.2 list observations that “would identify” parameters. A profound version is a structural identifiability statement: which of \((A_{g0},\omega_A,\text{storage}, \alpha,\rho)\) are identifiable from GRACE-like column integrals plus production, and which leakage terms are observationally equivalent to donor draw-down. Registering a protocol is not identification (the paper already says this); an identifiability proposition would be.

**U7. Align fisheries.**  
Pick one: (a) \(\Theta_F\) as a *pressure index* outside the \(J\)–\(H\)–\(T\) hierarchy (current 9.4), or (b) Clark \(g\) inside the ledger as a constitutive law on \(N\). Do not leave \(g'(S_\rho)=\rho\) floating in 10.3 unless \(g\) is a primitive or a readout.

**U8. Fix the public record.**  
Replace USGS 2026 with a dated mineral-commodity summary. Replace or source the Indo-Gangetic \(-49.7\) cm yr\(^{-1}\) / \(-414\) cm cell against G3P v1.12 basin masks, or mark it illustrative. Quote Illakwahhi et al. with page-level claims. These are not upgrades of theory; they are what keep the applications from looking generated.

**U9. Drop or execute the companion.**  
Lemma 1 should not be “the exact shared object with the companion dynamics paper” unless that pair is in the appendix. Otherwise it is a forward reference used to inflate the ledger’s institutional reach.

**U10. One implication diagram.**  
Props 1–3 plus Thm 12 plus Cor. 1 are the logical payload. A three-layer diagram with counter-model arrows (mass-balanced but unsafe; safe but unconservative; weighted-OK but component-unsafe) would do more than the current prose density.

### 6. Verdict after a full pass

**Gap closures that succeeded:** interpretation fences (record-relative barriers, R/P as classification, gross vs net, positive extraction \(\not\Rightarrow\) finite exhaustion, weighted sums \(\not\Rightarrow\) floors, surrogates \(\neq\) ledger). These are the paper’s actual contribution.

**Gaps that were declared closed but are only labelled:** typed operations, five rules as theorems, applications as ledger computations, two-pool groundwater, thermodynamic layer (honestly out of scope), stochastic completion (honestly out of scope).

**Gaps that remain bugs:** missing Thm 2, IG CDF, Thm 12 symbols, incomplete rest set, USGS 2026, unvetted extreme G3P rates, stock/service/moiety slide, fisheries \(g\) vs \(\Theta_F\).

**Profound?** Not yet. The mathematics is textbook CRNT/compartmental invariance, comparison lemmas, box envelopes, and Brownian first passage. The *stance* — conjunctive barriers, donor budgets, and refusing to let a time-like index impersonate a hitting time — is the upgrade relative to the applied literature the intro cites. To become profound the manuscript would have to *reject or recompute* a published table with the new objects, not only reclassify it, and it would have to make “typed” a check rather than an adjective.

Treat the present text as a complete outline of a methods paper whose elementary theorems are mostly right, whose non-claims are unusually honest, and whose data/proof surface still needs a line-level pass before the framework can carry the weight the opening assigns it.


**2. Theorem 12 — false at \(m=1\).** The theorem quantifies over "every weight vector \(w\in\mathbb{R}_+^m\)" with no floor on \(m\). At \(m=1\), the hypothesis \(w(s_1-d_1)\ge0\) with \(w>0\) *directly implies* \(s_1-d_1\ge0\) — i.e., the weighting *does* certify componentwise nonnegativity, contradicting the theorem. The proof itself requires "the other component \(m\ne j\)," silently assuming at least two components. The theorem needs an unstated hypothesis \(m\ge2\).

**3. Theorem 7's mining-restored extension outruns Theorem 4.** Theorem 7's mining-restored bound invokes "Theorem 4 gives \(M(t)\ge0\)," but Theorem 4 (orthant invariance) is stated and proved only for system (2), the institutional-failure specialization with \(C_A=0\). Orthant invariance for the mining-restored system is never proved — it's plausible by the same style of boundary argument (donor limitation makes \(C_A\sigma=0\) at \(A^{geo}=0\)), but as written the extension is asserted, not established.

**4. Theorem 12's proof: attainability vs. reachability left ambiguous.** The witness ledger's two-compartment transfer satisfies \(\ell=(1,1)\), \(\ell^\top N=0\), so by the paper's own Proposition 1, \(x_1+x_2\) is conserved along trajectories. The proof asserts "every \(x\in\mathbb{R}_+^2\) is admissible" as if all points are freely reachable, without clarifying that "attainable" here means "valid as an initial condition," not "jointly reachable from one fixed start over time." Given how central conservation is to the rest of the paper, this ambiguity should have been addressed explicitly.

## Overall assessment

None of this rises to evidence of fabrication or hollow rigor. The pattern is exactly what you'd expect from a genuine, ambitious, mostly-correct technical manuscript: the load-bearing machinery (mass identities, orthant invariance, the IG/GBM first-passage results, the numerical cross-checks in Section 9) is correct and mutually consistent when checked directly; the flaws that do exist are narrow, locatable, and characteristic of exactly the failure modes a careful human derivation produces — an unguarded edge case (Theorem 6, Theorem 12's \(m=1\)), a theorem extended slightly past what was proved (Theorem 7's mining case), and notation collisions that a full copyedit against the paper's own stated typing discipline (Section 2) would catch (\(\rho\), \(t\), \(S\)/\(\chi\)) and that the cross-reference table (item 8–9) would catch on a renumbering pass. A text engineered to *simulate* rigor without possessing it tends to fail diffusely — undefined terms doing decorative work, circular justification, or claims too vague to be wrong. This manuscript instead fails in the specific, patchable, "we'll catch this in review" way that real mathematical writing does.

**Assessment: Legitimate, and this section is the strongest evidence yet — it's the kind of scoping honesty that AI strawmen almost never produce.**

**The counterexample is mathematically correct.** For donor-controlled proportional extraction Ṡ = −kS, the solution is S(t) = S₀e^(−kt). The extraction rate kS is strictly positive for all finite t, so extraction is "positive forever" — yet S(t) never reaches zero in finite time (exhaustion never occurs), even though total extraction ∫₀^∞ kS dt = S₀ is finite. This is a clean, classical example (the same structure as radioactive decay or RC-circuit discharge) and it precisely disciplines the intuitive-but-false inference "positive extraction ⟹ eventual exhaustion." This is exactly the sort of one-paragraph, checkable counterexample that separates real technical work from hand-waving.

**The "what is not claimed" section is the most telling part.** This is a hallmark of genuine technical writing, not AI-strawman inflation:

- **Explicitly declining thermodynamic admissibility** as a scope boundary, and naming what it would require (energy accounting, entropy production, reaction feasibility) — this shows the author knows the difference between "conserves mass" and "is physically realizable," which is a real and easily-blurred distinction in reaction network modeling.
- **Explicitly stating the stochastic surrogates in Section 8 don't conserve the ledger's mass and aren't perturbations of the deterministic dynamics** — this is an important and honest admission. It would be very easy (and common in weaker work) to smuggle in an implicit claim that the Brownian/geometric-Brownian proxies are "the same system with noise added." Flagging that they are *not* dynamically connected to the ledger, and are separate first-passage models fit to public data series, is exactly the kind of caveat a careful referee would demand and a strawman-generator would omit.
- **Explicitly refusing to claim identification of the two-pool groundwater hypothesis**, deferring it to a "registered but not discharged" status in Section 10 — this is unusually precise epistemic bookkeeping. It distinguishes "we set up the requirements for identifying this" from "we identified this," which is a distinction papers often blur.
- **Explicitly refusing any empirical claim about a real basin, aquifer, or fishery** — restricting all claims to the descriptive/proxy status of tabulated indicators. This directly matches the paper's stated thesis (each number gets "the predicate it actually establishes") and closes the loop: the paper doesn't overclaim contrary to its own announced program.

**The technical content remains internally consistent:**
- The inverse-Gaussian first-passage distribution is the correct, standard first-passage-time law for Brownian motion with drift hitting a fixed barrier — appropriate for a trend-extrapolated series.
- Geometric Brownian motion for "removals-only fisheries pressure" is a reasonable proxy choice given such indicators are typically ratios/log-differences of positive quantities.
- The "record-relative barrier discipline" point — that a hitting time computed against a barrier chosen from the same window used to estimate the trend is a *self-referential stress statistic*, not an independent physical prediction — is a sharp and correct methodological criticism (this is essentially a look-ahead/data-snooping critique, stated in first-passage language).

**Verdict:** This continuation strengthens rather than weakens the case for legitimacy. The scope-limitation paragraph in particular is something an AI producing a plausible-sounding strawman would be unlikely to generate unprompted — it requires understanding exactly where the technical machinery stops working, which is a mark of real command of the material.
