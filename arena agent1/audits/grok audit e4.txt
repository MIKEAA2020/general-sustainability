## What actually checks

Closed-loop equilibria from \(H=(α+βR+γP)/(1-a)\) with \(a=0.7461\) reproduce Table 1 to the reported hundredths (BAU/UC-min \(=615.72\); 7.2% cut to clear 618; S1 \(=\) flat-80% under UC-min because the attractor sits below 660; CPM \(=\) flat-60% under UC-min because \(628.36<630\), and correctly *not* equal under q05 because the attractor sits in Stage III).  
BAU T-step lower boundaries at 618 ft (\(618.8\), \(625.6\), \(658.4\), domain hit at continuous \(T\approx12.7\)) are the linear contraction \(H_0=H^*+(618-H^*)/a^T\).  
\(r_T=ε(1-a^T)/(1-a)\) with \(ε=15.41\) is the right accumulated additive defect for \(H_t=aH_{t-1}+\cdots+w_t\), \(|w|\leε\). The T=3 certified snapshot \(662.2<697.8<706.7\) follows.

Those pieces are not the problem.

## Internal inconsistencies (the paper vs the paper)

**1. §3.2 and abstract (2) cannot both be true of the declared family.**

> “Every declared policy’s robust kernel equals BAU’s at every horizon”  
> “all policies ≡ BAU”  
> “a negative certificate holds for every policy”

versus

> “Demand management extends the viable horizon, not invariance: even zero pumping has an empty nominal kernel beyond \(T\approx6\)”

At \(K^*=660\), BAU is empty from T=3 (\(H_0\approx722>710\)). Zero pumping (\(H^*=647.32\)) is nonempty at T=3 (\(H_0\approx678\)) and T=5 (\(H_0\approx702\)). Deep flats move T=1 and T=2 boundaries as well (flat-80% T=1 \(\approx673\) vs BAU \(675.1\)).  

So kernel equality holds only for **S1/CPM**, and only because every trigger sits on or inside the forbidden set: inside \(\{H\ge660\}\) the reactive rules never fire, hence \(\pi\equiv\) BAU. It does **not** hold for the flat-cap subfamily, which is in the declared family. Abstract (2) and the last bullet of §3.4 overclaim “every policy.” The true statement is narrower and more interesting: *reactive rules indexed at the constraint cannot change the viability kernel of that same constraint.*

**2. Abstract (3) vs §3.3 on T=3 certified emptiness.**  
Abstract: every demand-management policy’s certified kernel is empty beyond T=3. Body: zero pumping remains nonempty through T=4 at 618 (boundary \(\approx687.9\)). Zero pumping is in Table 1 and Table 2. Pick one.

**3. Certified retention vs the certified boundaries you printed.**  
§3.3, T=3, UC-min, 618 ft:  
`flat-0 662.2 < flat-80 697.8 < BAU = S1 = CPM 706.7`  

Lower bound 706.7 is a **smaller** kernel than 697.8. S1 is not as protective as flat-80% at the one certified horizon you tabulate; it is equal to BAU (because the T=3 path under the eroded constraint \(618+35.49=653.5\) still has \(H_1,H_2>660\), so the cut never arms inside the horizon).  

§3.4 nevertheless: “S1 and CPM remain retained against their dominating flat caps (S1 versus flat-80 … at every certified horizon).” That sentence is false at T=3 given Table-less numbers in 3.3. Retention was defined as kernel at least as protective as BAU **and** more supply than the matched-protection flat. Matched protection to flat-80% is exactly what S1 does **not** have once erosion is applied.

**4. Switching ignored whenever the eroded constraint straddles 660.**  
Certified kernels are “the nominal kernel of \(K^*+r_T\).” For a **constant** policy that is a translation. For S1/CPM it is not: the trigger is still 660, not \(660+r_T\). At T=1 physical certified (\(K^*+r=633.4\)) the BAU lower bound is already \(\approx639<660\), so S1 **is** on and is not equal to BAU. At T=2 the lower bound crosses 660 and the first step is uncut, the second cut — hybrid affine pieces. Reporting `BAU = S1 = CPM 706.7` only at T=3 hides that the equality is horizon-accidental, not structural. The certified layer as written is well-defined for flats and for BAU; it is not a drop-in for state-triggered rules without a switched-loop kernel.

## The load-bearing design flaw: mixed scoring regimes

Protection is scored on **UC-min** (perpetual 1956 floor).  
Supply (Table 2, abstract “+3.3–16.2%”, “+50.6%”) is scored on **actual-head replay 1934–1990**.

Under the disturbance that produces the invariance result, S1’s attractor is 622 ft, the 20% cut is on **every** year, and S1 supply **equals** flat-80% (\(225.73\)). CPM equals flat-60%. There is no extra permitted supply on the class that justifies retention. The option value of a trigger exists only when wet years exist; UC-min deletes them by construction.

So the headline “same robust protection at strictly more permitted supply” is a comparison of  
- worst-case invariance of a 20% (40%) cap, against  
- historical average cost of a rule that is off 65% of years.  

That is a legitimate two-criterion statement (**robustness at historical cost**), and it is the actual reason one might prefer S1 to a 20% flat cap. It is not a same-test dominance. Against **flat-90%** the inconsistency is sharper: under UC-min, S1 is more expensive (always −20% vs always −10%) at identical invariance, so mixed scoring is the *only* reason S1 beats flat-90%. The frozen retention rule never says which regime supply is measured in; Table 2 quietly picks the one that retains the reactive rules.

Under UC-q05/q10 the physical safe set is already BAU-invariant (attractors \(625.3/626.3\)). Positive selection is therefore carried entirely by one extreme class **and** by historical, not adversarial, pumping. That should be the first sentence of the abstract, not an aside in §3.2.

## Methodological flaws that move the result

**Anti-conservative drought bias, then used as a simulator.**  
Open-loop 1951–1956: model \(659.5\to631.3\) vs actual \(659.5\to623.2\) (high bias, max 8.1 ft). The map under-predicts the drought-of-record decline and is then used to certify that cut policies keep the system above 618. Kernels are too large, minima too high. Recording the bias “and no correction is applied” is honest and not a repair. Combined with OOS max residual \(21.81>ε=15.41\), the certified layer is a certificate against a **declared** defect the audit has already falsified. Calling emptiness “defect-bound, not governance” is fair only as a statement about this map; it is not a bound on J-17.

**Point-map robustness, parameter uncertainty omitted.**  
The disturbance class is only \(R\). \(α,β,γ,δ\) are OLS point estimates on 56 transitions, OOS residual SD jumps \(5.60\to8.40\). A viability kernel for a 7.2% cut that sits \(0.16\) ft above 618 (flat-90% attractor \(618.88\)) has no coefficient-error bar. The 7.2% figure is an equilibrium identity, not a robust one.

**Wrong object for the Habitat Conservation Plan.**  
Safe sets are on annual-mean J-17, not Comal/San Marcos flow, not 10-day averages (the real CPM statistic). 618 ft is labelled “cessation proximity”; if the operational correspondence is closer to 622–625, BAU’s \(615.72\) and the 7.2% cut both move, and flat-90%’s \(618.88\) may not clear. Stages II–IV are correctly flagged as unverified scenarios; the annual-mean / one-pool / lumped SA–Uvalde caveats in §4 are the right list and should have constrained the abstract’s “reactive architecture” language.

**BAU is training-mean actual pumpage (\(282.16\)), not authorized withdrawal.**  
Stage I in the institution is 20% of **authorized** rights, which need not be 20% of realized 1934–1990 pumpage (slack, exempt use, permit growth post-1993). Replay “permitted supply” is neither permitted nor worst-case.

**Perpetual 1956 is not the drought of record.**  
You say this (“certification geometry, not forecasts”) and then let abstract (1) read as a verdict on drought-of-record viability. A 1950s-shaped clustered residual path, or a floor with a finite duration matching 1951–1956, is the object planners mean. Under that object you already know the map is biased high.

## Profound upgrades (same paper, different theorem)

The result that is actually new and clean:

**A trigger placed on the boundary of \(K\) is invisible to the viability kernel of \(K\).** Invariance of \(\{H\ge660\}\) cannot be improved by any rule that cuts only when \(H<660\). That is an institutional design fact, not a drought fact, and it survives every map defect. It is the robust-kernel translation of “CPM prices dry years; it does not manufacture wet ones.” Write the paper around that, with 618 as a contrasting case where the trigger (660) sits **above** the constraint, so the rule can arm while still inside \(K\).

Then the architecture comparison becomes:

| Constraint vs trigger | What reactive rules can do | What you showed |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger **above** \(K^*\) (660 vs 618) | Arm inside \(K\), match a flat cap’s worst-case attractor, possibly cheaper in average years | Nominal invariance, mixed-regime supply |
| Trigger **on** \(K^*\) (660 vs 660) | Nothing for invariance; possible recovery after exit, which viability ignores | Correct negative result if limited to S1/CPM |
| Certified \(r_T\) large | Defect eats the distinction by T=3 | Keep; do not also claim certified dominance vs flats |

Add a **recovery kernel** (time to re-enter \(K\) after a prescribed overshoot) if you want to speak to the frequency rationale you correctly say is outside invariance. Stochastic viability (chance of occupancy of Stage I–IV) is the object the National Academies review and the real CPM rule actually optimize; robust kernels are a bound, not a substitute.

On the map: either (a) drop “certified” until \(ε\) dominates OOS and the 1950s directed bias, or (b) certify with an interval model (\(\gamma\) and the drought residual as set-valued). A 7.2% cut to an attractor 0.16 ft above 618 on a point map with 5.6–8.4 ft residual SD is not a number that should appear in an abstract.

## 1. Flaws

**1.1 Nominal distinctions are smaller than the declared defect.**  
BAU attractor 615.72 vs 618 is 2.3 ft. The 7.2% cut moves the attractor by ~3.2 ft. Training residual SD is 5.60 ft; training max is 15.41 ft; OOS max is 21.81 ft. The headline “BAU fails, 7.2% secures” is a 3 ft claim on a map whose one-step error is 5–22 ft. Section 2.4’s erosion is the right instinct; it then erases the claim: certified kernels of every demand-management policy are empty past T = 3. The abstract still leads with (1) the 7.2% result and only later (3) the certified emptiness. That order oversells a distinction the paper’s own certificate withdraws.

**1.2 The certified layer is max-residual, every year, fully adversarial.**  
\(r_T = \varepsilon(1-a^T)/(1-a)\) with \(\varepsilon =\) training *maximum* applied at every step. Residual SD 5.60 would give a very different margin; OOS max 21.81 (which the protocol refuses to substitute) would empty even T = 3. The conversion also uses the homogeneous rate \(a\) and ignores parameter uncertainty in \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)\). So “certified” is neither a statistical guarantee nor a cover of the held-out audit. It is a protocol object. Calling empty certified kernels a result about *governance* (even while §3.3 says the binding constraint is the defect) invites a reading the discussion then has to walk back.

**1.3 “Robust” is a singleton worst path.**  
The uncertainty class is a persistent floor. Because \(\beta > 0\), the worst sequence is identically \(R \equiv R_{\min}\). There is no switching, no compact set of hydrologies, no chance constraint. The robust T-step kernel is just forward iteration of one affine (or piecewise-affine) map. That is legitimate as certification geometry — the paper says so — but it is not robustness in the Watkins–McKinney sense cited in §1, and it is not viability under a rich disturbance set in the Krawczyk sense cited in §1. Under UC-q05/q10, BAU already makes [618, 710] invariant; *every* retention, every 7.2% statement, and every reactive-vs-flat comparison is carried by perpetual 1956 recharge alone.

**1.4 Hybrid metric: protection on the DOR floor, supply on the historical climate.**  
Kernels and attractors are computed under \(R \equiv 43.7\). Table 2 supply is an actual-head replay on 1934–1990 (and an OOS replay). Under the floor, S1 is identically flat-80% and CPM is identically flat-60% (attractors sit below 630 ft; cuts are always on). The extra 3.3–16.2% and 50.6% permitted supply exist only in wet years that the robust class excludes. The comparison is coherent if advertised as “worst-case drought protection + historical-mean entitlement.” It is not a within-scenario dominance. The retention rule never says that.

**1.5 Retention rule text does not define a unique test.**  
§2.4: retain if kernel ≽ BAU at every reading *and* “permits more pumping than the most protective flat cap with matched protection.” Once several flats share the same kernel (all \(\rho \le 0.9\) make [618, 710] invariant under UC-min), “most protective” is undefined among equals. In §3.4 S1 is scored against both flat-90% (+3.3%) and flat-80% (+16.2%). The 80% comparison is attractor-matching, not kernel-matching. CPM is scored only against flat-60% (attractor-matching). Three different tests are used; one sentence claims to be the test. “Machine-verified non-retention” cannot be machine-verified from that sentence.

**1.6 The object is a 1-pool OLS map of one well; the title is the Edwards Aquifer.**  
J-17 annual mean, San Antonio and Uvalde lumped, recharge as Puente/USGS estimates, pumping as EAA Table 1, springs only as a 618 ft proxy, triggers as annual means rather than 10-day averages, stages II–IV as “declared scenarios.” §4 lists these. They are not minor. Comal cessation is the ESA object; J-17 is an index. A 4-parameter affine fit on 56 transitions, with OOS residual SD rising 5.60 → 8.40, is a thin instrument on which to *select* governance operators. The Barton Springs GAM citations (Scanlon et al. 2003; Hutchison and Hill 2011) justify equivalent porous media at regional scale, not a one-pool affine at J-17. That leap is asserted, then disclaimed.

**1.7 OLS stock-flow as causal map.**  
\(\Delta H_t = \alpha + \beta R_t + \gamma P_t + \delta H_{t-1}\) fitted by OLS. \(P_t\) and \(H_{t-1}\) are not exogenous (even pre-CPM, drought reduces pumping; spring discharge couples \(H\) to net flux). Recharge is an estimate (Puente). No HAC/serial-correlation treatment, no errors-in-variables, no coefficient SEs, no Chow test at 1990 despite the OOS SD jump. \(\gamma = -0.02844\) ft per 10³ ac-ft is the entire policy lever: a 10% cut is ~0.8 ft of equilibrium head, again inside residual SD. The companion “negative certificate” that persistence beats the causal ladder in forecast mode is relevant: the intervention leg treats the same map as the governed object.

**1.8 Journal and literature mismatch.**  
Groundwater (NGWA) expects hydrogeologic content. This is a viability-scoring paper on a regression, with no map, no well hydrograph figure, no springflow, no spatial structure. Edwards-specific pumping-optimization and HCP literature is almost absent (Watkins & McKinney 1997 is general; NRC 2015 is cited but not engaged on monitoring/model adequacy beyond one sentence). The Northern-cod pointer in §1 and §4 does not help a groundwater reader and makes the paper look like one panel of an un-shown pair.

**1.9 Dates and irreproducible-as-written claims.**  
Protocol 2026-08-26, companion 2026-08-25, Umphres & Choi 2025, EAA 2024/25. Byte-for-byte regeneration and a public GitHub are strong if true *now*; as written they are forward-dated. “No new data” plus a 2025 recharge release plus a 2026 freeze needs a single consistent timeline.

## 2. Internal inconsistencies

- **Abstract (1) vs §3.1 vs family.** “Every flat cut of 10% or deeper” makes the safe set invariant; the smallest securing cut is 7.2%. At exactly 7.2% the attractor *is* 618 ft and the boundary is invariant. The family never includes 7.2%, so the abstract’s “every 10%” and the 7.2% sentence describe two different objects without saying so.
- **Abstract (4) vs Table 1.** Reactive rules “match flat-cap protection.” S1 matches flat-80% on attractor and all \(\rho\le 0.9\) on set-invariance; CPM matches flat-60% on attractor. Those are not the same matching relation.
- **§3.3 wording.** “Every demand-management policy … empty certified kernel beyond T = 3” then “Zero pumping is the exception.” Flat-0 is in the family (§2.3).
- **Cut-active fraction.** Table 2: S1 and CPM both 35.1%. True if “cut active” means \(H<660\), but CPM’s extra reduction is then invisible in that column and only visible in the supply number. The table invites a misread that the cascade does not bite harder.
- **BAU = S1 = CPM on certified T = 3, 618 ft (706.7 ft).** Correct for that sliver (decision heads stay > 660). It contradicts any casual reading that “reactive rules are retained at the certified level” as *reactive*. Certified retention of S1 vs flat-80% is a comparison of two policies that do not differ on those trajectories; the +36.6 supply is still the historical replay.
- **§2.4 vs §3.4 on certified re-check.** “The retention decision is made at the nominal level and re-checked at the certified level.” Certified re-check holds “only over the T ≤ 3 horizons where their certified kernels are nonempty.” So certified retention is a different, horizon-truncated object. The abstract’s (4) does not say that.
- **660 ft “negative certificate” vs “extends the viable horizon, not invariance.”** Empty from T = 3 for every policy including zero pumping beyond T ≈ 6. Horizon *does* depend on policy; invariance does not. The abstract’s (2) “for every policy” flattens that.

- **“z is a measured well level, not an assessment inversion.”** True and unnecessary in a groundwater paper; it is glue to the cod companion.
- **Model domain [610, 710] vs safe set 618.** Upward exits “not threshold violations.” Under every UC in the paper heads fall. The 710 cap only binds certified high-head slivers (706.7). Two different roles, one sentence.

None of these are arithmetic contradictions. They are claim-scope contradictions.

## 3. Gap closures (what is missing that the paper’s own question requires)

1. **Figures.** No hydrograph, no kernel-boundary-vs-T plot (nominal and certified), no cobweb of the UC-min maps, no replay of \(\pi(H_t)\) on 1934–2023. For this journal that is not optional.
2. **The actual ESA constraint.** Comal (and San Marcos) springflow, not J-17 ≥ 618 ft. Cite the J-17 / Comal cessation relationship; or put springflow on the left-hand side.

5. **Parameter uncertainty.** SEs on \(\gamma\), a joint ellipsoid, or even a two-point interval on \(\gamma\). Without it the 7.2% number is a point estimate on a coefficient that moves equilibrium head by less than 1 ft per 10% cut.

10. **Timeline and archive as of submission.** Dates, DOI of the recharge release, commit hash, and a protocol that a reviewer can read without a 2026 calendar.

## 4. Profound upgrades

**A. Score the institution in its own frame.**  
Compute, under a stochastic recharge model (even a resample of 1934–2023, or a 2-state wet/dry chain), the occupation measure below 618 ft and below each CPM stage, with and without S1/CPM. That is what EAA actually regulates. The robust-invariance frame is why 660 ft produces a “negative certificate”: 660 was never designed as an invariant set. Closing that gap would turn the interesting observation in §3.2 (“protected by wet years, not by the declared pumping family”) into the main result instead of a mismatch between tool and object.

**B. Put defect and signal on the same plot.**  
Show kernel lower bounds vs T with a shaded band from residual SD, training max, *and* OOS max. Report the cut \(\rho^*( \varepsilon )\) that puts the attractor at 618 + margin(\(\varepsilon\)). The 7.2% number then becomes a function, and the certified emptiness is visible rather than a §3.3 surprise. If the band swallows BAU vs 10%, the honest headline is: *this map cannot certify a pumping rule at the physical threshold beyond three years.*

**C. Finite-horizon droughts as the primary class.**  
Replace or supplement perpetual 1956 with: (i) the 1947–57 sequence, (ii) DOR-length windows, (iii) a max-run of q05 years. The 1950s open-loop already shows an 8.1 ft high bias over six years — useful, and currently unused for any kernel. Perpetual DOR can stay as a stress test; it should not be the only class that differentiates policies.

**D. Make the retention rule a partial order, then apply it.**  
E.g. retain \(\pi\) iff (i) \(\underline{\mathrm{Viab}}_T(\pi) \ge \underline{\mathrm{Viab}}_T(\mathrm{BAU})\) for all T in the grid, (ii) \(\underline{\mathrm{Viab}}_T(\pi) \ge \underline{\mathrm{Viab}}_T(\rho)\) for the *largest* \(\rho\) (most supply) among flats that match \(\pi\)’s kernel, (iii) historical (or closed-loop-under-floor) mean P strictly larger than that \(\rho\). State whether supply is historical replay or worst-case. Apply the same triple at certified level and report pass/fail per T. Then “machine-verified” means something.

**E. One figure-level hydrogeologic upgrade that would change the result.**  
Either (i) a two-box SA/Uvalde map with J-17 and J-27, or (ii) J-17 plus a Comal rating curve, even empirical. If the 618 ft result survives, it becomes a groundwater result. If it does not, the present 7.2% should not be selected.

**F. Stop selecting on a map the companion paper reports as informationally incomplete.**  
The clean design is: (1) this paper’s kernels as *sensitivity of a declared map*, (2) no retention language, (3) the defect layer as the result. Operator selection (“S1 retained, CPM retained”) is not supported once certified kernels die at T = 3 and OOS errors exceed \(\varepsilon\). The discussion already knows this (“binding constraint is the model defect, not the governance”). Promote that sentence to the abstract’s first result.

**G. Drop architecture-vs-cod.**  
The value of feedback vs a flat cap on *this* aquifer is a one-paragraph point: under the floor the reactive rule *is* a flat cap; the extra supply is wet-year optionality. That is worth saying without a fishery.

## 5. What is already strong (so upgrades do not throw it away)

Protocol freeze, refusal to repair OOS max > \(\varepsilon\), explicit “stages II–IV are scenarios,” attractor table, the 660 ft horizon-not-invariance reading, the 1950s bias note with no post-hoc correction, and the karst-honesty paragraph in §4 are the right ethics for this object. The 1-D algebra is done carefully. The paper does not need more machinery. It needs a disturbance class that matches drought management, a retention rule that is actually a rule, figures, springflow, and an abstract that leads with what the certificate can support: **on this map, demand management is not certifiable past three years, 660 ft is not an invariant set under any pumping in the family, and the only policy ranking that survives is a hybrid of perpetual-DOR invariance at 618 ft plus historical-mean entitlements.**

That is a publishable negative-and-bounded result. The present draft uses the same ingredients to announce a positive selection.
