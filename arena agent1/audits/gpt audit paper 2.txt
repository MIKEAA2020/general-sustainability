## Overall diagnosis

The manuscript has a strong organizing idea: **incomplete observation can destroy governability even when every compatible physical state is viable under full information**. Its best contribution is the identification of several distinct failure mechanisms:

\[
\text{physical exit}
\;\neq\;
\text{common-action failure}
\;\neq\;
\text{late information}
\;\neq\;
\text{uncertifiable safety}
\;\neq\;
\text{policy-class failure}.
\]

That taxonomy fits naturally into the larger sustainability architecture developed across the earlier papers and chat history. In particular, it provides several **local certificates for exclusion from the hybrid epistemic-institutional viability kernel**.

However, the current paper overstates its mathematical status. It does **not** supply “the necessity half” of output-feedback viability theory. Its results are mostly **sufficient conditions for nonviability**, not necessary conditions, and they do not characterize the complement of the epistemic kernel. This directly contradicts Section 6.5(i), which correctly admits:

> “The certificates are sufficient conditions for nonviability, not necessary-and-sufficient characterizations…”

That contradiction is the manuscript’s most important conceptual flaw.

There are also several theorem-level errors:

1. the robust/nonrobust epistemic kernels are defined identically;
2. Theorem 2 does not prove that the entire epistemic kernel is empty;
3. Theorem 3 needs a common admissibility assumption and stronger disturbance regularity;
4. Theorem 1’s measurable-selection and boundary-crossing arguments are incomplete;
5. the barrier “duality” uses inconsistent quantifier orders;
6. the appendix infers empty viability kernels from absence of equilibria, which is invalid;
7. the belief-state framework is not yet mathematically closed under continuous observations, delays, hybrid review, or history-dependent constraints.

The right upgrade is not to abandon the paper, but to reposition it as:

> **A sound obstruction calculus embedded in a greatest-fixed-point characterization of hybrid belief-state viability.**

That would turn the paper from a collection of elementary impossibility statements into a mathematically integrated component of a predictive sustainability theory.

# I. Central status correction

## 1. “Necessity theory” is currently overstated

The abstract says:

> “This paper supplies the complementary necessity theory…”

The conclusion says:

> “This paper has supplied the necessity half.”

But Section 6.5 says:

> “The certificates are sufficient conditions for nonviability, not necessary-and-sufficient characterizations…”

The latter is correct. A condition \(C(B)\) such that

\[
C(B)\Longrightarrow B\notin \operatorname{ERViab}_{\mathcal I}
\]

is a **sufficient obstruction certificate**. It is not a necessary condition for nonviability unless one also proves

\[
B\notin \operatorname{ERViab}_{\mathcal I}
\Longrightarrow C(B)
\]

for at least one certificate in a complete family.

### Required revision

Replace claims of a complete “necessity half” by:

> The paper develops a family of sound, computationally interpretable sufficient certificates for nonviability under incomplete observation. These certificates identify several distinct mechanisms by which an information state can lie outside the epistemic viability kernel. They do not exhaust the complement of that kernel.

A defensible title would be:

> **Obstruction Certificates for Robust Viability under Incomplete Observation**

or, if the fixed-point upgrade below is added:

> **Belief-State Viability and Obstruction Certificates under Incomplete Observation**

The word “calculus” becomes justified if the certificates are presented as composable rules acting on a common predecessor operator.

# II. Framework-level inconsistencies

## 2. The definitions of \(\mathrm{EViab}\) and \(\mathrm{ERViab}\) are identical

The manuscript defines:

> \(B_0\in\mathrm{EViab}\) if there exists a policy such that, for every admissible disturbance, every compatible trajectory remains safe.

Then:

> \(B_0\in\mathrm{ERViab}\) if the same holds robustly—the policy must work against every disturbance.

These are the same definition.

### Repair options

Either remove \(\mathrm{EViab}\) entirely and work only with robust epistemic viability, or distinguish the uncertainty semantics explicitly.

For example:

\[
\operatorname{EViab}_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal V)
=
\left\{
B_0:
\exists \Pi\;
\forall x_0\in B_0\;
\exists d(\cdot)
\text{ such that safety holds}
\right\},
\]

although this favorable-disturbance notion is usually not the most useful one.

A cleaner distinction is:

\[
\operatorname{ERViab}_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal V)
=
\left\{
B_0:
\exists \Pi\;
\forall x_0\in B_0\;
\forall d(\cdot)\;
x^{\Pi,d}_{x_0}(t)\in\mathcal V
\right\},
\]

and a stochastic kernel:

\[
\operatorname{PViab}^{1-\alpha}_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal V)
=
\left\{
\mu_0:
\exists\Pi,\quad
\mathbb P^\Pi
\left[
x(t)\in\mathcal V\;\forall t
\right]
\ge 1-\alpha
\right\}.
\]

For this paper, the most economical choice is to use only \(\operatorname{ERViab}\).

---

## 3. The physical-state projection \(K_{\mathcal I}\) is undefined

The hierarchy

\[
\mathrm{IRViab}_{\mathfrak I}(\mathcal V)
\subseteq K_{\mathcal I}
\subseteq \mathrm{RViab}(\mathcal V)
\subseteq \mathrm{Viab}(\mathcal V)
\]

mixes information states and physical states. The first two terms are not formally placed in the same space.

If \(\mathfrak K_{\mathcal I}\) is a set of beliefs, then at least two different physical projections are possible:

### Existential projection

\[
\operatorname{proj}_{\exists}(\mathfrak K_{\mathcal I})
=
\bigcup_{B\in\mathfrak K_{\mathcal I}} B.
\]

### Universal or certified projection

\[
\operatorname{proj}_{\forall}(\mathfrak K_{\mathcal I})
=
\left\{
x:
\exists B\in\mathfrak K_{\mathcal I}
\text{ with }x\in B
\text{ and all states in }B\text{ safely governable}
\right\}.
\]

The inclusion into the full-information robust kernel is valid only after the projection and quantifiers are specified.

### Better hierarchy

Keep all objects in their native spaces:

\[
\mathfrak K_{\mathrm{inst}}
\subseteq
\mathfrak K_{\mathrm{epi}}
\subseteq
2^{\operatorname{RViab}^{\mathrm{full}}(\mathcal V)}.
\]

Then state the physical implication:

\[
B\in\mathfrak K_{\mathrm{epi}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B\subseteq\operatorname{RViab}^{\mathrm{full}}(\mathcal V).
\]

This is clearer than comparing beliefs directly with physical kernels.

---

## 4. The information-state definition is syntactically and mathematically incomplete

The manuscript writes

\[
B_t
=
\left\{
x\in X:
O(x(\tau))=y(\tau)
\text{ for }\tau\le t
\text{ along some realization}
\right\}.
\]

Here \(x\) is used simultaneously as a current state and as an entire trajectory.

### Correct definition

Let \(\xi(\cdot)\) denote a candidate trajectory. Then

\[
B_t
=
\left\{
\xi(t):
\begin{array}{l}
\xi(0)\in B_0,\;
\dot\xi(s)=f(\xi(s),u(s),d(s)),\\
d(s)\in D(\xi(s)),\\
O(\xi(s))=y(s)\text{ for all observed }s\le t
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]

With observation noise \(v\),

\[
y(s)\in H(\xi(s),v(s)),
\]

or

\[
\|y(s)-O(\xi(s))\|\le \bar v.
\]

For hidden parameters \(\theta\), beliefs must live in the augmented space:

\[
B_t\subseteq X\times\Theta.
\]

For delay systems, consistent with the earlier sustainability paper, they must live in history space:

\[
B_t\subseteq
\mathscr H\times\Theta,
\qquad
\mathscr H=C([-\tau_{\max},0],X).
\]

This is the correct bridge to the grand unifying object.

---

## 5. “Policy depends on \(B_t\), equivalently on the record” is not generally true

The manuscript says:

> A policy is observation-based if it depends on the record only through \(B_t\) (equivalently, through the record itself).

The two are not automatically equivalent.

A policy depending on \(B_t\) is a special case of a record-based policy. The converse requires \(B_t\) to be a sufficient statistic. That can fail when:

- institutional modes depend on past events;
- action constraints depend on previously issued commitments;
- observation channels have memory;
- dwell-time or switching constraints apply;
- probability distributions matter, not merely supports;
- two histories generate the same compatible set but different legal authority;
- the physical system itself has delays.

### Correct augmented state

Use

\[
Z_t=(B_t,h_t,\chi_t),
\]

where:

- \(B_t\) is the physical/parameter compatibility set;
- \(h_t\) is institutional mode;
- \(\chi_t\) stores other necessary memory, such as commitments, review clocks, or delay history.

Only after proving Markov sufficiency may policies be restricted to \(Z_t\).

# III. The safe-control correspondence is too weak for the claimed role

## 6. Tangency to \(\mathcal V\) is not equivalent to membership in the viability kernel

The manuscript defines

\[
\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}(x)
=
\left\{
u:
\nabla q_j(x)\cdot f(x,u,d)\ge0
\quad
\forall d,\;\forall j\text{ active}
\right\}.
\]

Then it says:

> By Nagumo’s theorem … \(x_0\in\mathrm{RViab}(\mathcal V)\) iff … there is a measurable selection \(u(x)\in\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}(x)\) along the trajectory.

This statement suppresses important hypotheses and conflates two objects:

1. invariance of the whole set \(\mathcal V\);
2. viability of the maximal controlled-invariant subset \(K\subseteq\mathcal V\).

If \(\mathcal V\) itself is not controlled invariant, the correct tangency condition should generally be applied to the kernel \(K\), not merely to \(\mathcal V\):

\[
f(x,u,d)\in T_K(x)
\qquad
\forall d.
\]

At points inside \(\mathcal V\) but outside \(K\), all controls may be “instantaneously safe” while every trajectory is doomed to exit later.

### Required distinction

Define the instantaneous inward-action set

\[
\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)
=
\left\{
u\in U(x):
f(x,u,d)\in T_{\mathcal V}(x)
\;\forall d
\right\}.
\]

Then explicitly state:

\[
\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}^{\mathrm{loc}}(x)\neq\varnothing
\]

is only a local necessary condition for invariance of \(\mathcal V\), unless stronger global hypotheses hold.

For recursive viability, define

\[
\mathcal R_K(x)
=
\left\{
u\in U(x):
f(x,u,d)\in T_K(x)
\;\forall d
\right\},
\]

where \(K=\operatorname{RViab}(\mathcal V)\).

This distinction is essential because the common-action obstruction can be formulated at two levels:

- a **local boundary obstruction** using \(\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}^{\mathrm{loc}}\);
- a **recursive viability obstruction** using \(\mathcal R_K\) or a belief-state predecessor.

The latter is the more powerful and unifying object.

# IV. Theorem 1: finite-time exit certificate

## 7. The game quantifiers need formalization

The condition

\[
\sup_{u\in U(x)}
\inf_{d\in D(x)}
D^+q(x;f(x,u,d))
\le-\varepsilon
\]

does imply that every pointwise control admits an adverse disturbance. But the proof then constructs a disturbance after seeing the realized control.

That is not an ordinary fixed open-loop disturbance. It is a **nonanticipative disturbance strategy**.

### Required game semantics

State explicitly:

\[
\forall\Pi
\quad
\exists\Delta
\]

where \(\Pi\) is a causal control policy and \(\Delta\) is an Elliott–Kalton-type nonanticipative disturbance strategy that may respond to the control history without anticipating future controls.

Otherwise “the disturbance chooses after the control” is informal and can change the game value.

A clean formulation is:

```text
For every admissible causal control policy \(\Pi\), there exists a
nonanticipative disturbance strategy \(\Delta\) such that the
corresponding trajectory exits.
```

---

## 8. Closed graph alone does not justify the measurable-selection step used

The proof says the measurable-selection theorem follows from closed graph. More is usually needed to select an adverse \(d(t)\) satisfying the derivative inequality along the trajectory.

Define

\[
D_\varepsilon(x,u)
=
\left\{
d\in D(x):
D^+q(x;f(x,u,d))\le-\varepsilon
\right\}.
\]

You need this correspondence to have:

- nonempty values;
- measurable graph, or another measurable-selection property;
- preferably compact values;
- enough regularity to support existence of the closed-loop differential inclusion.

Closed graph of \(D\) and continuity of \(f\) may help, but continuity of the Dini derivative in \((x,u,d)\) is not automatic for a general \(q\).

### Simplest repair

Assume \(q\in C^1\). Then use

\[
\nabla q(x)\cdot f(x,u,d)
\]

instead of a general directional Dini derivative. With compact \(D(x)\), continuity, and measurable \(u(t)\), the adverse-control correspondence is much easier to treat.

If nonsmooth \(q\) is important, assume \(q\) is locally Lipschitz and use the Clarke derivative or contingent epiderivative under a cited comparison theorem.

---

## 9. Reaching \(q=0\) is not yet leaving \(\{q\ge0\}\)

The proof concludes:

> \(q\) reaches \(0\)… and by continuity … the state leaves \(\{q\ge0\}\) no later.

Continuity alone does not imply that reaching the boundary is followed by crossing it. The derivative inequality at the boundary can imply crossing, but that argument must be stated.

Moreover, if

\[
q(x(t))\le q(x_0)-\varepsilon t,
\]

then at

\[
t=q(x_0)/\varepsilon
\]

the estimate gives only \(q\le0\), not necessarily \(q<0\). For every \(\delta>0\),

\[
q\!\left(x\left(\frac{q(x_0)}{\varepsilon}+\delta\right)\right)
\le-\varepsilon\delta<0,
\]

provided the adverse differential inequality remains valid through the boundary.

### Better conclusion

Define the first violation time

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{viol}}
=
\inf\{t:q(x(t))<0\}.
\]

Then prove

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{viol}}
\le \frac{q(x_0)}{\varepsilon}
\]

in the sense of the infimum of violating times, or state that violation occurs before every time strictly greater than \(q(x_0)/\varepsilon\).

---

## 10. The “exact negation of a barrier condition” claim is incorrect

The manuscript says:

> Condition (1) is the exact negation of a barrier condition.

It is not. A sufficient inward-barrier condition and a sufficient outward-drift condition need not be logical complements. There can be a middle region where neither holds.

The manuscript also later writes the alternative safety order as

\[
\inf_d\sup_u D^+q\ge0.
\]

For a controller choosing an action that must withstand every disturbance, the pointwise robust order is generally

\[
\sup_u\inf_d D^+q\ge0.
\]

The order

\[
\inf_d\sup_u
\]

allows the controller’s maximizing action to depend on the disturbance realization, unless the disturbance is observed before control selection.

### Required wording

Replace “exact negation” by:

> Condition (1) is an outward-drift certificate dual in spirit, but not logically complementary, to an inward barrier condition. The two conditions leave an unresolved intermediate region.

---

# V. Theorem 2: the claimed empty epistemic kernel is not proved

## 11. The theorem statement is false under the paper’s own belief-state definition

Theorem 2 claims there are systems with

\[
\operatorname{ERViab}_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal V)=\varnothing.
\]

But if information states may include singleton beliefs, then for every known \(S_0\),

\[
B_0=\{S_0\}
\]

is viable by applying \(u=r(S)\) along the exactly known deterministic trajectory. Constant observation does not erase prior exact knowledge.

Thus the construction proves at most that the initial fibre

\[
B_0=[1,2]
\]

is nonviable. It does not prove that the entire epistemic kernel is empty.

### Correct theorem

> There exists a system for which every physical state is viable under full information, while the initial observation fibre \(B_0=O^{-1}(y_0)\) is not epistemically viable.

Formally,

\[
\mathcal V
=
\operatorname{Viab}^{\mathrm{full}}(\mathcal V),
\qquad
B_0\notin\mathfrak K_{\mathcal I}.
\]

If you want the epistemic kernel to be empty, restrict the admissible initial information states to observation fibres induced by a fixed prior. For example,

\[
\mathfrak B_0=\{O^{-1}(y):y\in O(X)\}.
\]

For constant \(O\), \(\mathfrak B_0=\{X\}\), and the admissible initial kernel can indeed be empty.

---

## 12. \(r\) must be nonconstant

The proof claims

\[
\bigcap_{S\in[1,2]}\{0,r(S)\}=\{0\}
\]

because \(r(S)>0\). Positivity is insufficient. If \(r(S)\equiv c>0\), then

\[
\bigcap_{S\in[1,2]}\{0,c\}=\{0,c\}.
\]

Require:

\[
r \text{ is nonconstant on every relevant compatible interval},
\]

or use a simpler construction with two hidden modes requiring disjoint controls.

---

## 13. Constant observation does not imply \(B_t=[1,2]\) forever

Even with no informative measurement, known dynamics and known applied controls propagate the initial set. Under \(u=0\),

\[
B_t=\Phi_t([1,2])
\]

rather than necessarily \([1,2]\).

This does not destroy the example, but the proof must use the propagated belief. The clean route is:

1. at \(t=0\), the only common admissible action is \(0\);
2. under \(u=0\), all possible states decrease;
3. show that every action admissible for all propagated states remains \(0\);
4. conclude finite exit.

Alternatively, avoid state-dependent control sets, which create unnecessary complications.

### Better hidden-mode example

Let the augmented state be \((z,\theta)\), with

\[
\dot z=\theta u,
\qquad
\theta\in\{-1,+1\},
\qquad
u\in[-1,1],
\qquad
z\ge0.
\]

At \(z=0\), robust local safety requires

\[
u\ge0 \quad\text{if }\theta=+1,
\qquad
u\le0 \quad\text{if }\theta=-1.
\]

This still has the common action \(u=0\), so to obtain disjoint safe sets add an unavoidable drift:

\[
\dot z=\theta u-c,
\qquad c>0.
\]

Then at \(z=0\),

\[
u\ge c \quad(\theta=+1),
\qquad
u\le -c \quad(\theta=-1),
\]

which are disjoint if \(U=[-1,1]\) and \(0<c\le1\). Each mode is individually viable; the unresolved belief is not.

This uses a common control set and isolates the information obstruction cleanly.

---

## 14. An observation-based policy need not select a constant action

The proof says:

> The observation record is constant, hence the policy selects a constant \(u\equiv0\).

A causal policy can depend on time and its own past actions even when observations are constant. It may select an open-loop time-varying control.

The relevant conclusion is not “constant observation implies constant action,” but:

\[
u(t)\in \bigcap_{x\in B_t}U(x)
\]

for robust admissibility at every time. If that intersection is \(\{0\}\) for all reachable beliefs, then \(u(t)=0\) almost everywhere.

---

# VI. Theorem 3: common-action obstruction

## 15. The theorem requires common admissibility before common safety

The proof lets \(a=\pi(B)\), then argues that empty intersection of \(\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}(x)\) produces a state where \(a\) is unsafe.

But if \(a\notin U(x)\) for some compatible state, then \(a\) is not even admissible there. This is itself an obstruction, but it must be treated separately.

Define:

\[
U^B(B)=\bigcap_{x\in B}U(x).
\]

Then distinguish:

### Admissibility obstruction

\[
U^B(B)=\varnothing.
\]

No single action is admissible at every compatible state.

### Safety obstruction

\[
U^B(B)\neq\varnothing,
\qquad
\bigcap_{x\in B}\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}(x)=\varnothing.
\]

Every common admissible action is unsafe for at least one compatible state.

The proof should start with \(a\in U^B(B)\).

---

## 16. “Emptiness must be caused by the boundary” is not valid as written

The proof says:

> Since \(\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}(x)=U(x)\) at interior points, emptiness must be caused by the boundary.

That only follows if the selected action belongs to every \(U(x)\). Otherwise varying state-dependent admissibility sets at interior states can empty the intersection.

With the correction

\[
a\in U^B(B),
\]

the argument becomes valid: an interior state cannot reject \(a\) through the local safety correspondence because there \(\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}(x)=U(x)\).

---

## 17. A negative derivative at one instant is not enough under arbitrary measurable policies

The proof selects an action \(a\), obtains

\[
\nabla q_j(\bar x)\cdot f(\bar x,a,\bar d)<0,
\]

and concludes that the trajectory exits before the next observation. But an arbitrary policy could change its action immediately as a function of time, even without receiving new information.

The theorem needs one of the following:

1. **sample-and-hold:** the selected action is held over \([t_k,t_{k+1})\);
2. **positive dwell time:** no new action may be selected for a declared interval;
3. **uniform common-action failure over time:** every time-varying common action generates outward drift for some indistinguishable branch;
4. **nonanticipative adversarial branch construction:** nature continuously chooses a compatible branch defeating the current common action.

Given the manuscript’s sustainability motivation, sampled review is the cleanest formulation.

### Correct sampled theorem

Let \(a_k\) be held on \([t_k,t_{k+1})\). If for every common admissible \(a\),

\[
\exists x\in B_k\cap\partial\mathcal V,\quad
\exists d\in D(x),\quad
Dq(x;f(x,a,d))\le-\eta<0,
\]

and the adverse branch remains observation-compatible for a positive interval, then \(B_k\) is not one-step tube-safe.

This connects directly to the predecessor operator.

---

## 18. Closed graph of \(D\) does not justify extending \(\bar d\) as a constant disturbance

The proof says:

> Let the disturbance realize the constant selection \(\bar d\) on a small interval.

If \(D(x)\) is state-dependent, \(\bar d\in D(\bar x)\) need not imply

\[
\bar d\in D(x(t))
\]

for nearby \(x(t)\).

You need:

- state-independent \(D\); or
- lower semicontinuity and a local selection \(d(x)\); or
- a differential-inclusion argument using a disturbance correspondence preserving strict outward drift.

A corrected assumption is:

\[
D_\eta(x,a)
=
\left\{
d\in D(x):
\nabla q_j(x)\cdot f(x,a,d)\le-\eta
\right\}
\]

has nonempty compact values and admits a measurable local selection.

---

## 19. The theorem should be formulated through tube safety, not only instantaneous tangency

The deeper object is not

\[
\bigcap_{x\in B}\mathcal R_{\mathcal V}(x),
\]

but the set of actions that keep the entire reachable tube safe until the next observation:

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{tube}}(B,\Delta)
=
\left\{
a:
\operatorname{Reach}_{[0,\Delta]}(B,a)
\subseteq\mathcal V
\right\}.
\]

Then the obstruction is exact for one review period:

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{tube}}(B,\Delta)=\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B\notin\operatorname{Pre}(\mathfrak Q)
\]

for every target collection \(\mathfrak Q\).

The instantaneous common-action condition is the \(\Delta\downarrow0\) boundary approximation.

This is the principal mathematical bridge to the unifying kernel.

# VII. Theorem 4: delayed-information obstruction

## 20. The theorem is currently close to a restatement of its hypothesis

It assumes that for every policy there already exists a compatible state and disturbance such that

\[
D^+q\le-\varepsilon
\]

until violation. The conclusion that every policy fails then follows immediately by comparison.

The useful scientific content is therefore not yet a theorem about the observation structure. It is the task of verifying the hypothesis.

### Stronger and more operational formulation

Define the pre-observation indistinguishability class

\[
\mathcal C_{[0,T_{\mathrm{obs}})}(B_0,\Pi)
\]

of all state-disturbance branches producing the same observation record before \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\).

Then define the worst-case safety time:

\[
T_{\mathrm{safe}}(B_0)
=
\sup_{\Pi}
\inf_{\substack{x_0\in B_0\\ d\in\mathcal D\\
\text{branch observation-compatible}}}
\inf\{t:q(x(t))<0\}.
\]

A clean theorem is:

\[
T_{\mathrm{obs}}>T_{\mathrm{safe}}(B_0)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B_0\notin\operatorname{ERViab}_{\mathcal I}.
\]

The drift estimate supplies a computable upper bound:

\[
T_{\mathrm{safe}}(B_0)
\le
\frac{\inf_{x\in B_0}q(x)}{\varepsilon}.
\]

This separates:

- the conceptual obstruction;
- the drift-based certificate used to estimate it.

---

## 21. The minimum-margin state may not exist

The theorem assumes a state \(x^*\) with

\[
q(x^*)=\inf_{x\in B_0}q(x).
\]

This requires compactness of \(B_0\) and lower semicontinuity of \(q\), or an explicit attainment assumption.

A more robust version uses \(\delta\)-minimizers:

\[
q(x_\delta)
\le
\inf_{x\in B_0}q(x)+\delta.
\]

Then violation occurs by

\[
\frac{\inf q+\delta}{\varepsilon},
\]

and \(\delta\downarrow0\).

---

## 22. “No informative observation” needs formal definition

With continuous exact output \(y(t)=O(x(t))\), different hidden states may become distinguishable immediately through the output trajectory or its derivative, even if \(O(x_1)=O(x_2)\) initially.

The manuscript needs one of:

- sampled observations only at \(t_k\);
- delayed observations \(y(t)=O(x(t-\ell))\);
- observation-equivalence over an interval;
- an explicit condition that all adverse branches generate identical output histories before \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\).

Define:

\[
x_1\sim_{[0,T)}^\Pi x_2
\]

if there exist admissible disturbances such that the two branches under policy \(\Pi\) produce the same observation record on \([0,T)\).

Then “no informative observation arrives” means the adverse branches remain in the same equivalence class until \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\).

---

## 23. The claimed limiting relation to Theorem 3 is inaccurate

The manuscript says:

> The common-action obstruction is its instantaneous limit \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\to\infty\) with \(\inf q=0\).

The relevant limit is not \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\to\infty\). If \(\inf q=0\), any strictly positive observation delay can be too late under a uniform outward drift. The common-action obstruction is better described as the **zero-margin, one-step boundary case** of the delayed tube-safety obstruction.

Use:

\[
\inf_{x\in B}q(x)=0,
\qquad
T_{\mathrm{obs}}>0.
\]

---

# VIII. Certification theorem

## 24. The fibre criterion is correct, but its scope must be sharpened

Theorem 5 is mathematically sound as a set-theoretic statement:

\[
C\circ O=\mathbf 1_K
\]

exists if and only if \(K\) is saturated under the equivalence relation induced by \(O\).

On the admissible domain \(Z\), the equivalent statement should be written:

\[
K
=
Z\cap O^{-1}(O(K)).
\]

Without the \(Z\cap\), the equality may include inadmissible points outside the declared domain.

---

## 25. Measurability is omitted

The certifier is defined merely as a function. If \(C\) must be measurable, then fibre constancy alone may not suffice under arbitrary measurable spaces, because \(O(K)\) need not be measurable.

Add either:

- “exact certifier” means a set-theoretic function; or
- assume \(O(K)\) is measurable in \(Y\); or
- work with standard Borel spaces and appropriate regularity.

A measurable version is:

```text
A measurable exact certifier exists iff \(K\) is \(O\)-saturated and
\(O(K)\) is measurable, in which case \(C=\mathbf 1_{O(K)}\).
```

---

## 26. “Per-floor observation is the only observation structure…” is too strong

Exact certification of every floor does not require literally observing each floor separately. It requires the observation to refine the safe/unsafe partition induced by the floor.

A nonlinear or vector-valued statistic may certify multiple floors without reporting each underlying state component.

The exact condition is:

\[
O(z_1)=O(z_2)
\Longrightarrow
\mathbf 1_{\{q_j\ge0\}}(z_1)
=

# X. Literature-positioning problems

## 30. “Perfect-measurement viability has a complete theory” is too broad

The classical theory is mature, but “complete” is too strong, especially for:

- nonconvex controls;
- hybrid and impulsive systems;
- delay equations;
- state constraints with nonsmooth boundaries;
- stochastic systems;
- games with asymmetric information;
- infinite-dimensional systems;
- computationally high-dimensional kernels.

Use:

> Under standard finite-dimensional regularity assumptions, full-state viability has a well-developed kernel and tangency theory.

---

## 31. “Incomplete observation has had half a theory” is not defensible

Imperfect-information differential games, belief-state control, set-membership estimation, discriminating kernels, partially observed stochastic control, and output-feedback safety already include both existence and nonexistence results in various forms.

The novelty should be framed more narrowly:

> The paper organizes several elementary but operationally useful nonviability certificates into a common belief-state viability language, with explicit attention to sustainability monitoring design.

That is credible and still useful.

---

## 32. The treatment of estimation tubes is internally unstable

The manuscript says estimation-space reduction gives equality of value functions, but then says:

> “what it does not supply is a common prescription.”

If the imperfect-information problem is correctly reduced to a belief-state game with equal values, admissible belief-state controls should already be common controls applicable to all compatible states under the declared semantics. Otherwise the reduction would not be exact.

The correct relation is:

> The common-action obstruction is not outside the estimation-space programme; it is a local certificate that the belief-state predecessor is empty.

That is a stronger internal connection and avoids claiming a gap that may not exist.

---

## 33. Barrier certificates do not “have nothing to say” about information

The statement that barrier methods presuppose full-state feedback is too categorical. There are observer-based barriers, output-feedback barrier constructions, robust barrier functions under estimation error, and belief-space safety certificates.

The genuine distinction is:

- a state-space barrier certifies safety under a specified controller/information assumption;
- an epistemic obstruction certifies that no common safe action exists for a belief;
- a belief-space barrier can in principle certify safety directly in information space.

The grand theory should explicitly allow a barrier functional

\[
\mathcal B(B,h)
\]

on information states, not only a barrier \(b(x)\) on physical states.

---

## 34. Absence of a barrier certificate does not imply unsafety

The discussion says:

> “a system for which no barrier certificate exists and for which the exit certificate holds is unsafely classified by two independent arguments.”

Under a restricted certificate class, failure to find a barrier is not an argument for unsafety. Under a genuine converse theorem with all hypotheses satisfied, safety would imply existence, so nonexistence could imply unsafety—but that requires proving nonexistence in the complete class, not merely failing to construct one.

Delete this sentence or replace it with:

> When a converse barrier theorem applies, the outward-drift certificate can be compared with the failure of the corresponding inward condition. Outside converse settings, absence of a found barrier has no diagnostic force.

# XV. Add a dual-certificate layer

The paper’s strongest path toward a genuine “calculus” is to give computational duals for predecessor emptiness.

## 47. Polyhedral common-action obstruction via Farkas

Suppose, for a finite or polytopically represented belief, safe actions satisfy

\[

\qquad i=1,\dots,m.

Stack these as

\[
Au\le b.
\]

\[
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{com}}(B)=\varnothing
\]

if and only if there exists a Farkas multiplier \(\lambda\ge0\) such that

\[
\lambda^\top A=0,
\qquad
\lambda^\top b<0.
\]

This is a finite, independently checkable certificate of epistemic infeasibility.

It connects directly to the material substitution certificate already discussed:

- material feasibility is separation in flow space;
- common-action feasibility is separation in control space;
- exact certification is saturation in observation space;
- viability is a greatest fixed point in belief space.

These are not disconnected techniques. They are different projections of the same feasibility logic.
