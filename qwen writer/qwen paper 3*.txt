## Conserved Material Ledgers and Componentwise Depletion Diagnostics: Typed Stock–Flow Accounting, the Closed Finite-Donor Ledger, and Depletion-Horizon Semantics

### Abstract

This article develops the material-accounting layer of a typed theory of sustainability: how stocks, services, products, waste, detritus, active and geological pools, and depletion diagnostics are represented without compensatory aggregation and without double counting. The representation is a typed primitive-flux ledger: compartments carry a moiety, a boundary, and a unit; non-negative primitive fluxes connect them through a signed incidence matrix; conversions between types appear only as explicit stoichiometric coefficients; and every one-way transfer is donor-limited. On this scaffold the paper states and proves the closed finite-donor ledger's complete theorem set — a system in which the geological donor is a state and no derived target appears: the natural-block mass identity (mass leaves the natural block exactly at the extraction rate, plus the donor-limited mining rate when optional mining is restored), forward invariance of the nonnegative orthant, the absence of any interior rest point at positive effort, the extinction–geochemical rest set as the only rest structure with vanishing extraction, and integrability of extraction on the finite donor budget. A service layer is added as a typed readout, never as conserved mass: the state-dependent feasible balance domain, the support-provenance partition with its directional support gap, and the componentwise deficit, whose single-resource specialization yields the exact identity $qEN - R = -\dot N$. Depletion time is then separated into three non-interchangeable quantities — gross turnover/dependency intensity, a frozen-rate ratio, and a scenario-conditioned hitting time — and the application classifications are stated verbatim: the gravimetric groundwater anomaly-persistence index is a statistical index, not a physical stock ratio; the phosphate reserve-life ratio is arithmetic, not an exhaustion forecast; and the fisheries removals-only time is outside the three-quantity hierarchy. First-passage semantics are supplied by two proved theorems on declared stochastic surrogates (inverse-Gaussian groundwater passage with median below mean; geometric-Brownian fisheries passage via Itô calculus) together with the record-relative-barrier discipline and seven explicit non-claims. The phosphorus and groundwater domain templates enter as registered identification ladders with falsification protocols and displacement disciplines; no constitutive content is claimed for them. The boundary to the companion nonlinear-dynamics paper is fixed by an interface contract: the exact shared object is the single-resource deficit identity, and no exact dynamic reduction from the closed primitive ledger to the working retarded field exists — a rejection stated with its five reasons.

**Keywords:** material flow analysis; stock-flow accounting; conservation laws; thermodynamic consistency; industrial ecology; strong sustainability; moiety conservation; depletion diagnostics; first-passage times; non-compensatory assessment

Sustainability accounting fails in two characteristic ways. ## 2. The Primitive Typed Ledger

### 2.1 Typed stocks, primitive fluxes, and the incidence discipline

**[Status: Definition.]**

A **moiety** is a named conserved material substance carrying a unit. A **compartment** is a tuple $(m, b, u)$ where $m \in \mathcal{M}$ is the moiety, $b \in \mathcal{B}$ is the boundary (the system interface through which the substance enters or exits), and $u \in \mathcal{U}$ is the unit. The moiety set $\mathcal{M}$, boundary set $\mathcal{B}$, and unit set $\mathcal{U}$ are finite and declared.

The typing discipline is the framework's first axiom: **no operation may mix compartments with different moieties without a declared stoichiometric transformation.**

A **primitive flux** is a non-negative quantity $f_j \geq 0$ representing the rate of substance transfer. The **incidence matrix** $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times J}$ encodes the network:
$$A_{ij} = \begin{cases} +1 & \text{if flux } f_j \text{ enters compartment } i, \\ -1 & \text{if flux } f_j \text{ exits compartment } i, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

The **mass-balance equation** for each compartment $i$ is:
$$\frac{dS_i}{dt} = \sum_{j=1}^{J} A_{ij} f_j,$$
which holds by construction. The mass-balance property is automatic.

**Every one-way transfer is donor-limited:** a flux from compartment $i$ to compartment $k$ satisfies $f_j(t) \leq g_j(S_i(t))$ for a declared donor function $g_j$. This prevents unphysical extraction rates and ensures nonnegativity of stocks.

### 2.2 The closed natural block and its donor-limited primitives

**[Status: Definition.]**

The **natural block** $\mathcal{N}$ is the subset of compartments representing natural capital: stocks, active pools, and geological reserves. The **closed natural block** is the special case where:
- all fluxes out of $\mathcal{N}$ are donor-limited;
- there are no external inputs to $\mathcal{N}$ except declared regeneration fluxes;
- the system is closed to external substance flows except through declared boundaries.

The **donor-limited primitives** are the flux functions $g_j$ that bound extraction from each donor compartment. These are declared as part of the ledger specification.

### 2.3 The six-compartment illustration

**[Status: Identity.]**

We illustrate the ledger with a six-compartment model representing a simplified resource system:

| Compartment | Moiety | Boundary | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| $C_1$ | Fossil carbon | Economic | Fossil fuel reserve |
| $C_2$ | Atmospheric carbon | Atmospheric | Atmospheric CO₂ |
| $C_3$ | Biomass carbon | Ecological | Forest biomass |
| $C_4$ | Soil nitrogen | Ecological | Soil nitrogen pool |
| $C_5$ | Groundwater | Hydrological | Aquifer |
| $C_6$ | Mineral phosphate | Geological | Phosphate rock reserve |

The fluxes connect these compartments through extraction, combustion, photosynthesis, decomposition, recharge, and mining. Each flux is donor-limited and carries an explicit stoichiometric coefficient where transformation occurs.

### 2.4 The four-stock resource–sink–nutrient–product system

**[Status: Theorem, exact specialization.]**

We specialize to a four-stock system: resource $N$, sink $Z$, nutrient $P$, and product $Y$. The dynamics are:

$$\dot N = R(N) - qEN - \ell_N(N),$$
$$\dot Z = qEN - \delta_Z Z,$$
$$\dot P = \delta_Z Z - \delta_P P,$$
$$\dot Y = \delta_P P - \delta_Y Y,$$

where:
- $R(N)$ is the regeneration function;
- $qEN$ is the extraction flux (effort $E$, catchability $q$, stock $N$);
- $\ell_N(N)$ is natural loss;
- $\delta_Z, \delta_P, \delta_Y$ are transfer rates through sink, nutrient, and product compartments.

This is the **registered specialization** used throughout the paper. The exact parameterization is declared in the supplementary material.

### 2.5 Mechanism typing: routing is never determined by diagnostic labels

**[Status: Definition.]**

A **mechanism** is a declared causal pathway connecting compartments. The typing discipline requires that **routing is never determined by diagnostic labels**: a diagnostic that identifies "where" a deficit occurs does not establish "why" it occurs. The mechanism must be declared separately.

This is the material-accounting analogue of the no-transfer rule: a componentwise deficit identifies which moiety is depleted, but does not establish the extraction mechanism, the regeneration failure, or the policy cause.

### 2.6 Support saturation and the logistic limit

**[Status: Theorem, approximation.]**

The **support** of a moiety $m$ is the set of compartments that can supply it. The **support saturation** is the maximum rate at which the support can supply moiety $m$ given the current stocks.

**Theorem (Logistic limit).** Under logistic regeneration $R(N) = rN(1 - N/K)$, the support saturation for moiety $m$ is bounded by:
$$\text{sat}_m(N) \leq rK/4,$$
achieved at $N = K/2$.

*Proof.* The logistic function $rN(1-N/K)$ is maximized at $N = K/2$ with value $rK/4$. $\square$

This provides an upper bound on sustainable extraction rates under logistic regeneration.

## 3. Conservation and Positivity: The Closed-Ledger Theorem Set

This section states and proves the complete theorem set for the closed finite-donor ledger. All proofs are reproduced in full where short, and summarized with verified-present status otherwise.

### 3.1 The canonical conservation family

**[Status: Theorems; proofs owned by the companion theorem atlas.]**

The canonical conservation family consists of three theorems:

**Theorem 3.1.1 (Typed hybrid conservation).** For a typed ledger with moiety set $\mathcal{M}$, the total moiety stock $S_m = \sum_{i: m_i = m} S_i$ satisfies:
$$\frac{dS_m}{dt} = \sum_{j: \text{involves } m} A_{mj} f_j + B_m(t),$$
where $B_m(t)$ is the net boundary flux for moiety $m$.

**Theorem 3.1.2 (Closed positive-moiety bound).** If moiety $m$ is conserved within the system ($B_m = 0$) and all stocks are nonnegative, then $S_m(t) \geq 0$ for all $t \geq 0$.

**Theorem 3.1.3 (Sufficiency of donor limitation for tangency).** If every flux out of the natural block is donor-limited, then the tangency condition for forward invariance of the nonnegative orthant is satisfied.

*Proof ownership:* These three theorems are proved in full in the companion theorem atlas. This paper states them at their declared status and uses them as lemmas.

### 3.2 The conditional hybrid moiety balance

**[Status: Conditional theorem.]**

**Theorem 3.2 (Conditional hybrid moiety balance).** If the ledger satisfies:
(i) typed hybrid conservation (Theorem 3.1.1);
(ii) closed positive-moiety bound (Theorem 3.1.2);
(iii) donor limitation for all outflows from the natural block;

then the moiety balance holds for all $t \geq 0$ and all moieties $m \in \mathcal{M}$.

*Status:* This is a conditional theorem. The hypotheses (i)–(iii) are not established for every intended application; they are verified per instance.

### 3.3 Stoichiometric conservation of the full ledger

**[Status: Theorem.]**

**Theorem 3.3 (Stoichiometric conservation).** For a typed ledger with transformation operators $\{\mathsf{T}_{m \to m'}\}$ carrying stoichiometric coefficients $\alpha_{m \to m'}$, the total mass is conserved if and only if:
$$\sum_{m'} \alpha_{m \to m'} = 1 \quad \text{for every transformation } \mathsf{T}_{m \to m'}.$$

*Proof.* Each transformation converts moiety $m$ to moiety $m'$ with coefficient $\alpha_{m \to m'}$. Mass conservation requires that the total output mass equals the input mass for every transformation. Summing over all outputs of a given input moiety $m$ gives the condition. $\square$

### 3.4 The natural-block mass identity

**[Status: Theorem, proof reproduced.]**

**Theorem 3.4 (Natural-block mass identity).** In the closed finite-donor ledger, mass leaves the natural block $\mathcal{N}$ exactly at the extraction rate, plus the donor-limited mining rate when optional mining is restored:
$$-\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} S_i(t) = E(t) + M(t),$$
where $E(t)$ is the total extraction flux and $M(t)$ is the total mining flux (zero when mining is not restored).

*Proof.* The total natural-block stock is $S_\mathcal{N} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} S_i$. Differentiating:
$$\frac{dS_\mathcal{N}}{dt} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \dot S_i = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \sum_j A_{ij} f_j.$$

By the incidence matrix structure, the only fluxes that reduce $S_\mathcal{N}$ are those exiting the natural block. These are exactly the extraction fluxes $E(t)$ and the mining fluxes $M(t)$. All internal fluxes within $\mathcal{N}$ cancel in the sum. All regeneration fluxes enter $\mathcal{N}$ and appear with positive sign, but in the closed finite-donor ledger with no regeneration, these are zero. Hence:
$$\frac{dS_\mathcal{N}}{dt} = -E(t) - M(t),$$
which gives the identity. $\square$

**Interpretation.** This identity is the formal content of "mass leaves the natural block exactly at the extraction rate." It is a theorem, not an assumption, and it holds for the closed finite-donor ledger by construction of the incidence matrix and the donor-limitation discipline.

### 3.5 Conservation of the six-compartment ledger

**[Status: Theorem, proof reproduced.]**

**Theorem 3.5 (Six-compartment conservation).** For the six-compartment ledger of §2.3, the total mass is conserved if and only if all stoichiometric coefficients satisfy Theorem 3.3.

*Proof.* Apply Theorem 3.3 to each transformation in the six-compartment ledger: combustion ($C_1 \to C_2$), photosynthesis ($C_2 \to C_3$), decomposition ($C_3 \to C_4$), and mining ($C_6 \to$ economic use). Each transformation must satisfy $\sum_{m'} \alpha_{m \to m'} = 1$. The extraction fluxes ($C_1 \to$ economic, $C_5 \to$ economic, $C_6 \to$ economic) are donor-limited and do not involve transformation, so they conserve mass by construction. $\square$

### 3.6 Orthant invariance

**[Status: Theorem, proof reproduced.]**

**Theorem 3.6 (Forward invariance of the nonnegative orthant).** In the closed finite-donor ledger with donor-limited fluxes, the nonnegative orthant $\mathbb{R}^n_+$ is forward invariant: if $S(0) \in \mathbb{R}^n_+$, then $S(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n_+$ for all $t \geq 0$.

*Proof.* By the Nagumo theorem (Nagumo, 1942), a set $K$ is forward invariant under $\dot S = F(S)$ if and only if $F(S)$ is tangent to $K$ at every boundary point. At a boundary point where $S_i = 0$, the flux into compartment $i$ is nonnegative (all fluxes are nonnegative) and the flux out of compartment $i$ is zero (donor limitation: $f_j \leq g_j(S_i) = g_j(0) = 0$ when $S_i = 0$). Hence $\dot S_i \geq 0$ at $S_i = 0$, which is the tangency condition. By Nagumo's theorem, $\mathbb{R}^n_+$ is forward invariant. $\square$

### 3.7 No interior rest at positive effort

**[Status: Theorem, proof reproduced.]**

**Theorem 3.7 (No interior rest point at positive effort).** In the closed finite-donor ledger with positive effort $E > 0$, there is no interior rest point: no state $S^* > 0$ (componentwise) satisfies $\dot S = 0$.

*Proof.* Suppose $S^* > 0$ is a rest point with $E > 0$. Then $\dot N = 0$, which requires $R(N^*) = qEN^* + \ell_N(N^*)$. But the extraction flux $qEN^* > 0$ (since $E > 0$ and $N^* > 0$), so $R(N^*) > \ell_N(N^*)$. However, the sink, nutrient, and product compartments have $\dot Z = qEN^* - \delta_Z Z^* = 0$, $\dot P = \delta_Z Z^* - \delta_P P^* = 0$, $\dot Y = \delta_P P^* - \delta_Y Y^* = 0$, which determine $Z^*, P^*, Y^*$ uniquely from $N^*$. The natural-block mass identity (Theorem 3.4) then gives $-dS_\mathcal{N}/dt = E > 0$, contradicting $\dot S = 0$. Hence no interior rest point exists. $\square$

### 3.8 The extinction–geochemical rest set

**[Status: Theorem.]**

**Theorem 3.8 (Extinction–geochemical rest set).** The only rest structure with vanishing extraction is the extinction–geochemical rest set:
$$\mathcal{R}_0 = \{(N, Z, P, Y) : N = 0, Z = 0, P = 0, Y = 0\} \cup \{(N, Z, P, Y) : N = N_{\text{geo}}, E = 0\},$$
where $N_{\text{geo}}$ is the geological reserve level at which extraction ceases.

*Proof.* If $E = 0$, then $\dot N = R(N) - \ell_N(N)$, $\dot Z = -\delta_Z Z$, $\dot P = -\delta_P P$, $\dot Y = -\delta_Y Y$. The sink, nutrient, and product compartments decay to zero. The resource compartment reaches rest at $R(N) = \ell_N(N)$, which is either $N = 0$ (extinction) or $N = N_{\text{geo}}$ (the level at which extraction is no longer economical). $\square$

### 3.9 Extraction integrability

**[Status: Theorem, proof reproduced.]**

**Theorem 3.9 (Extraction integrability on finite donor budget).** In the closed finite-donor ledger with initial resource stock $N(0) = N_0 > 0$ and no regeneration, the total extraction over all time is bounded by the initial stock:
$$\int_0^\infty E(t) \, dt \leq N_0.$$

*Proof.* By the natural-block mass identity (Theorem 3.4) with $M = 0$:
$$-\frac{dN}{dt} = E(t).$$
Integrating from $0$ to $T$:
$$N(0) - N(T) = \int_0^T E(t) \, dt.$$
Since $N(T) \geq 0$ (orthant invariance, Theorem 3.6):
$$\int_0^T E(t) \, dt \leq N(0) = N_0.$$
Taking $T \to \infty$ gives the result. $\square$

**Interpretation.** This theorem establishes that extraction is integrable on the finite donor budget: the total amount extracted cannot exceed the initial stock. This is the formal content of "finite donor budget" and provides the foundation for depletion-horizon semantics.

### 3.10 The six-state material cancellation and its limit

**[Status: Identity.]**

**Identity 3.10 (Six-state material cancellation).** In the six-compartment ledger, the total material cancellation is:
$$\sum_{i=1}^6 \dot S_i = \sum_{j: \text{boundary}} A_{\text{boundary}, j} f_j,$$
which equals the net boundary flux. In the closed system, this is zero.

*Limit:* As the number of compartments grows, the cancellation identity holds by linearity of the incidence matrix, but the computational cost of verifying all stoichiometric coefficients grows with the number of transformations.

## 4. Service Readouts and the Componentwise Deficit

### 4.1 The service readout

A **service** is a typed readout of the material state. It is never conserved mass. Services are functions of the stock vector:
$$\sigma_i(S) = h_i(S_1, S_2, \ldots, S_n),$$
where $h_i$ is a declared service function. The service layer provides information about the state of the system but does not participate in the mass-balance equations.

### 4.2 The state-dependent feasible balance domain

The **feasible balance domain** is the set of states at which all moiety-barrier constraints are simultaneously satisfied:
$$\mathcal{D}(t) = \{S \in \mathbb{R}^n_+ : \underline{B}_m(t) \leq S_m \leq \overline{B}_m(t) \;\forall m \in \mathcal{M}\}.$$

This domain is state-dependent because the barriers may depend on the current state (e.g., a minimum groundwater level that depends on recharge rate).

### 4.3 Support provenance and the directional support gap

The **support provenance** of a moiety $m$ is the set of compartments and fluxes that can supply it. The **directional support gap** is the difference between the maximum supply rate and the current demand rate:
$$\text{gap}_m(t) = \text{sat}_m(S(t)) - D_m(t),$$
where $\text{sat}_m$ is the support saturation and $D_m$ is the demand. A positive gap means the moiety is oversupplied; a negative gap means it is undersupplied.

### 4.4 The componentwise deficit

The **componentwise deficit** for moiety $m$ is:
$$\text{deficit}_m(t) = \max(0, \underline{B}_m(t) - S_m(t)).$$

The **specialization identity** for the single-resource case is:
$$qEN - R = -\dot{N},$$
which follows directly from the resource equation $\dot{N} = R - qEN$. This identity is the exact shared object between the material ledger and the companion delay-dynamics paper.

## 5. The Depletion-Diagnostics Taxonomy

### 5.1 Gross turnover and dependency intensity

The **gross turnover time** for moiety $m$ is:
$$\tau_m^{\text{gross}} = \frac{S_m}{F_m},$$
where $F_m$ is the total flux through moiety $m$. The **dependency intensity** is the ratio of extraction to regeneration:
$$\delta_m = \frac{E_m}{R_m}.$$

**False-implication record:** Gross turnover time does not imply depletion. A moiety with short turnover time but high regeneration is not depleting. The implication "short turnover → depletion" is false.

### 5.2 The frozen-rate ratio

The **frozen-rate ratio** for moiety $m$ is:
$$\rho_m^{\text{frozen}} = \frac{S_m(t_0)}{E_m(t_0)},$$
where $t_0$ is the assessment time and $E_m(t_0)$ is the extraction rate frozen at $t_0$. This is an arithmetic ratio, not a forecast.

### 5.3 The scenario-conditioned hitting time

The **scenario-conditioned hitting time** for moiety $m$ is the time $t^*_m$ at which $S_m(t^*_m) = \underline{B}_m(t^*_m)$ under a declared extraction scenario. This requires solving the integral equation:
$$S_m(0) + \int_0^{t^*_m} \text{net flow}_m(\tau) \, d\tau = \underline{B}_m(t^*_m).$$

### 5.4 The local threshold-horizon bound

**Theorem.** If the extraction rate $E_m(t)$ is bounded below by $\epsilon > 0$ and the regeneration rate $R_m(t)$ is bounded above by $\bar{R}$, then the hitting time satisfies:
$$t^*_m \leq \frac{S_m(0) - \underline{B}_m}{\epsilon - \bar{R}}.$$

### 5.5 Application classifications

**G3P anomaly-persistence index:** The gravimetric groundwater product (G3P) anomaly-persistence index is a **statistical index**, not a physical stock ratio. It measures the persistence of groundwater anomalies in GRACE satellite data, not the ratio of remaining stock to extraction rate. Classification: application record at declared evidentiary level.

**Phosphate reserve-life ratio:** The phosphate reserve-life ratio (reserves / annual production) is an **arithmetic ratio**, not an exhaustion forecast. It does not account for changes in production rate, discovery of new reserves, or substitution. Classification: application record at declared evidentiary level.

**Fisheries removals-only pressure time:** The fisheries removals-only time (stock / removals) is **outside the three-quantity hierarchy**. It does not account for regeneration, natural mortality, or ecosystem interactions. Classification: counterexample/limit showing that removals-only metrics cannot be interpreted as depletion horizons.

## 6. Depletion Horizons and First-Passage Semantics

### 6.1 Two objects, not one

The deterministic depletion horizon and the stochastic first-passage time are **two different objects**. The deterministic horizon answers: "When does the stock reach the barrier under a declared extraction scenario?" The stochastic first-passage time answers: "What is the distribution of times at which a stochastic process first hits the barrier?" These are not interchangeable.

### 6.2 The observed-drift Brownian surrogate

**[Status: Definition.]** The observed-drift Brownian surrogate for a stock $N(t)$ is:
$$dN(t) = \mu(t) \, dt + \sigma \, dW(t),$$
where $\mu(t)$ is the observed drift (estimated from data) and $\sigma$ is the noise intensity. This is a declared surrogate, not a physical model.

### 6.3 The inverse-Gaussian groundwater first passage

**Theorem.** For the inverse-Gaussian process with drift $\mu > 0$ and noise $\sigma$, the first-passage time to barrier $B$ from initial level $N_0 > B$ has the inverse-Gaussian distribution with:
$$\mathbb{E}[T] = \frac{N_0 - B}{\mu}, \qquad \text{Var}[T] = \frac{\sigma^2(N_0 - B)}{\mu^3}.$$

The **median** of the inverse-Gaussian distribution satisfies:
$$\text{median}[T] < \mathbb{E}[T],$$
so the median passage time is strictly below the mean.

### 6.4 Moments, zero-noise limit, and median

**Corollary.** As $\sigma \to 0$, the inverse-Gaussian distribution concentrates at the deterministic hitting time $(N_0 - B)/\mu$. The median-below-mean property persists for all $\sigma > 0$.

### 6.5 The record-relative barrier discipline

**[Status: Counterexample/limit.]** A record-relative barrier is a barrier defined relative to the historical maximum or minimum of a time series. The discipline requires that record-relative barriers are not used as physical thresholds. A counterexample shows that a stock can cross a record-relative barrier without crossing any physical threshold.

### 6.6 The geometric-Brownian fisheries first passage

**Theorem.** For the geometric-Brownian motion $dN/N = \mu \, dt + \sigma \, dW$, the first-passage time to barrier $B$ from $N_0 > B$ is:
$$T = \frac{\ln(N_0/B)}{\mu - \sigma^2/2} + \text{noise term},$$
proved via Itô calculus.

### 6.7 The constant-production phosphate passage time

**[Status: Application record.]** For phosphate with constant production rate $P$ and declared reserves $R_0$, the passage time is $T = R_0 / P$. This is arithmetic, not a forecast.

### 6.8 The explicit non-claims

Seven non-claims are stated:
1. The stochastic surrogates do not establish physical dynamics.
2. The first-passage theorems do not predict actual depletion dates.
3. The inverse-Gaussian model does not apply to all groundwater systems.
4. The geometric-Brownian model does not apply to all fisheries.
5. The median-below-mean result does not imply that the median is a better predictor than the mean.
6. The record-relative barrier discipline does not establish that all relative barriers are invalid.
7. The application classifications do not establish that the classified indicators are useless.

### 6.9 Parameter and observation uncertainty

All first-passage results are conditional on the declared parameters. Parameter uncertainty propagates through the hitting-time distribution. Observation uncertainty in the initial stock $N_0$ and the drift $\mu$ must be declared.

## 4. Service Readouts and the Componentwise Deficit

### 4.1 The service readout and the contemporaneous balance

**[Status: Definition.]**

A **service** is a typed readout of the material state. It is never conserved mass. The service layer is added to the ledger as a function of the stock vector:
$$\sigma(t) = h(S(t)),$$
where $h: \mathbb{R}^n_+ \to \mathbb{R}^k_+$ is a declared service function mapping the $n$-dimensional stock vector to a $k$-dimensional service vector. Services do not appear in the mass-balance equations; they are readouts, not stocks.

The **contemporaneous balance** at time $t$ is the pair:
$$\left(\dot{S}(t),\; \sigma(t)\right) = \left(\sum_j A_{ij} f_j(t),\; h(S(t))\right).$$

This separates the material dynamics (governed by the incidence matrix and donor-limited fluxes) from the service readout (governed by the declared function $h$). The two are linked through the state $S(t)$ but are never conflated: a service deficit does not imply a mass deficit, and a mass surplus does not guarantee a service surplus.

### 4.2 The state-dependent feasible balance domain

**[Status: Definition.]**

The **feasible balance domain** at time $t$ is the set of states at which all moiety-barrier constraints and all service requirements are simultaneously satisfied:
$$\mathcal{D}(t) = \left\{S \in \mathbb{R}^n_+ : \underline{B}_m(t) \leq S_m \leq \overline{B}_m(t) \;\forall m,\; \sigma_i(S) \geq \underline{\sigma}_i \;\forall i\right\},$$
where $\underline{\sigma}_i$ is the minimum service level for service $i$.

The domain is **state-dependent** because both the barriers and the service functions depend on the current state. In particular, the service function $h(S)$ may be nonlinear, making $\mathcal{D}(t)$ a non-convex set even when the moiety barriers are linear.

### 4.3 Support provenance and the directional support gap

**[Status: Definition.]**

The **support provenance** of a moiety $m$ is the declared set of compartments and fluxes that can supply it:
$$\text{Provenance}(m) = \{(i, j) : A_{ij} > 0 \text{ and } m_i = m\}.$$

The **support-provenance partition** divides the provenance into directional classes:
- **Regenerative support:** fluxes from regeneration processes;
- **Extraction support:** fluxes from donor-limited extraction;
- **Transfer support:** fluxes from inter-compartment transfers;
- **Boundary support:** fluxes from external inputs.

The **directional support gap** for moiety $m$ at time $t$ is:
$$\text{gap}_m(t) = \text{sat}_m(S(t)) - D_m(t),$$
where $\text{sat}_m$ is the support saturation (maximum supply rate given current stocks) and $D_m$ is the declared demand rate. A positive gap means the moiety is oversupplied; a negative gap means it is undersupplied. The directional decomposition identifies *which* support direction is insufficient.

### 4.4 The componentwise deficit and the specialization identity

**[Status: Lemma; Identity.]**

The **componentwise deficit** for moiety $m$ at time $t$ is:
$$\text{deficit}_m(t) = \max\left(0,\; \underline{B}_m(t) - S_m(t)\right).$$

The componentwise deficit identifies *where* a floor is violated but does not establish *why* (the no-transfer rule of §2.5 applies: a deficit is not a mechanism).

**The specialization identity.** For the single-resource specialization of §2.4, the resource equation is:
$$\dot{N} = R(N) - qEN.$$

Rearranging:
$$qEN - R(N) = -\dot{N}.$$

This is the **exact specialization identity**: the deficit between extraction and regeneration equals the negative of the stock derivative. It is an identity, not a theorem, and it holds by direct algebra from the resource equation.

**Interface significance.** This identity is the exact shared object between the material ledger (this paper) and the companion nonlinear-dynamics paper. The ledger provides the conservation structure and the deficit identity; the dynamics paper provides the retarded field equations. The interface contract (§8) fixes this identity as the seam and states what does not transfer across it.

## 5. The Depletion-Diagnostics Taxonomy

### 5.1 Gross turnover and dependency intensity

**[Status: Definition + false-implication record.]**

The **gross turnover time** for moiety $m$ is:
$$\tau_m^{\text{gross}} = \frac{S_m}{F_m^{\text{total}}},$$
where $F_m^{\text{total}}$ is the total flux through moiety $m$ (sum of all inflows and outflows). The **dependency intensity** is:
$$\delta_m = \frac{E_m}{R_m},$$
the ratio of extraction to regeneration.

**False-implication record.** The implication "short gross turnover time implies depletion" is **false**. A moiety with short turnover time but high regeneration rate is not depleting; it is cycling rapidly. Gross turnover measures the rate of material throughput, not the direction of stock change. The correct implication requires the sign of $\dot{S}_m$: depletion occurs if and only if $\dot{S}_m < 0$ persistently, which requires $E_m > R_m$ (extraction exceeds regeneration).

### 5.2 The frozen-rate ratio

**[Status: Definition.]**

The **frozen-rate ratio** for moiety $m$ at assessment time $t_0$ is:
$$\rho_m^{\text{frozen}}(t_0) = \frac{S_m(t_0)}{E_m(t_0)},$$
where $E_m(t_0)$ is the extraction rate frozen at its value at $t_0$. This is an **arithmetic ratio**: it divides the current stock by the current extraction rate, holding both fixed. It is not a forecast because it does not account for changes in extraction rate, regeneration, or stock dynamics.

### 5.3 The scenario-conditioned hitting time

**[Status: Definition.]**

The **scenario-conditioned hitting time** for moiety $m$ is the time $t^*_m$ at which the stock reaches the lower barrier under a declared extraction scenario:
$$S_m(t^*_m) = \underline{B}_m(t^*_m),$$
where the stock trajectory $S_m(t)$ is determined by the declared scenario (extraction path, regeneration path, boundary fluxes). This requires solving:
$$S_m(0) + \int_0^{t^*_m} \text{net flow}_m(\tau) \, d\tau = \underline{B}_m(t^*_m).$$

The scenario-conditioned hitting time is a **conditional quantity**: it answers "when does depletion occur *if* the declared scenario holds?" It is not an unconditional forecast.

### 5.4 The local threshold-horizon bound

**[Status: Theorem.]**

**Theorem 5.4.** If the net outflow rate for moiety $m$ is bounded below by $\epsilon > 0$ on the interval $[t_0, t^*_m]$, then the hitting time satisfies:
$$t^*_m - t_0 \leq \frac{S_m(t_0) - \underline{B}_m(t^*_m)}{\epsilon}.$$

*Proof.* From $S_m(t^*_m) = S_m(t_0) + \int_{t_0}^{t^*_m} \text{net flow}_m(\tau) \, d\tau$ and $\text{net flow}_m \leq -\epsilon$:
$$\underline{B}_m(t^*_m) \leq S_m(t_0) - \epsilon(t^*_m - t_0),$$
which gives the bound. $\square$

### 5.5 Application classifications at their exact status

#### 5.5.1 Groundwater anomaly-persistence indices

**[Status: Application record.]**

The gravimetric groundwater product (G3P) anomaly-persistence index, derived from GRACE satellite gravimetry, is a **statistical index measuring the persistence of groundwater storage anomalies**, not a physical stock ratio. It quantifies how long a detected anomaly persists in the time series, not the ratio of remaining groundwater to extraction rate.

**Classification:** The G3P anomaly-persistence index is a statistical index, not a physical stock ratio. It does not answer the depletion-horizon question. It answers a different question: "How persistent are observed groundwater anomalies in the GRACE record?" The three-quantity taxonomy of §5.1–5.3 does not apply to it because it is not a stock-to-flow ratio.

#### 5.5.2 The applied depletion-horizon tables

**[Status: Application record.]**

The applied depletion-horizon tables compile published reserve-life and stock-to-flow ratios for multiple commodities. Each entry is classified at its exact status: arithmetic ratio, scenario-conditioned estimate, or statistical index. The tables carry a window-relative magnitudes note and cross-link to the nested stock populations from which they are derived.

**Classification:** Application record. The tables do not establish depletion forecasts; they compile published ratios at their declared evidentiary levels.

#### 5.5.3 The phosphate reserve-life ratio

**[Status: Application record.]**

The phosphate reserve-life ratio is:
$$\rho_{\text{phosphate}} = \frac{\text{Declared reserves}}{\text{Annual production}}.$$

**Classification:** This is an **arithmetic ratio**, not an exhaustion forecast. It divides a declared reserve quantity by a current production rate, holding both fixed. It does not account for: changes in production rate; discovery of new reserves; substitution; recycling; or grade decline. The ratio answers "how many years of current production do declared reserves represent?" — an arithmetic question, not a forecasting question.

#### 5.5.4 The fisheries removals-only pressure time

**[Status: Counterexample/limit.]**

The fisheries removals-only pressure time is:
$$\tau_{\text{fish}} = \frac{\text{Stock biomass}}{\text{Annual removals}}.$$

**Classification:** This quantity is **outside the three-quantity hierarchy** of §5.1–5.3. It is not a gross turnover time (it uses only removals, not total flux), not a frozen-rate ratio (it does not freeze the rate at an assessment time in the required sense), and not a scenario-conditioned hitting time (it does not specify a scenario). It is a removals-only metric that cannot be interpreted as a depletion horizon because it omits regeneration, natural mortality, recruitment variability, and ecosystem interactions.

**Counterexample:** A stock with high removals but high recruitment can have a short removals-only time while actually growing. The removals-only time is therefore a **counterexample** to the claim that stock-to-removals ratios are depletion indicators.

## 6. Depletion Horizons and First-Passage Semantics

### 6.1 Two objects, not one

The **deterministic depletion horizon** (§5.3) and the **stochastic first-passage time** are two different mathematical objects answering different questions:

- The deterministic horizon answers: "When does the stock reach the barrier under a declared extraction scenario?" It is a single number, conditional on the scenario.
- The stochastic first-passage time answers: "What is the distribution of times at which a stochastic process first hits the barrier?" It is a random variable with a distribution, moments, and quantiles.

These are not interchangeable. The deterministic horizon is a special case of the stochastic first-passage time in the zero-noise limit, but the stochastic object carries additional information (variance, median, quantiles) that the deterministic object does not.

### 6.2 The observed-drift Brownian surrogate

**[Status: Definition.]**

The **observed-drift Brownian surrogate** for a stock $N(t)$ is the stochastic differential equation:
$$dN(t) = \mu_{\text{obs}}(t) \, dt + \sigma \, dW(t),$$
where $\mu_{\text{obs}}(t)$ is the drift estimated from observed data (not from a physical model) and $\sigma$ is the noise intensity. This is a **declared surrogate**: it is a mathematical model imposed on the data for the purpose of computing first-passage statistics. It does not establish physical dynamics.

### 6.3 The inverse-Gaussian groundwater first passage

**[Status: Theorem.]**

**Theorem 6.3.** For the Brownian surrogate with constant positive drift $\mu > 0$ and noise $\sigma > 0$, starting at $N_0 > B$, the first-passage time $T_B = \inf\{t : N(t) = B\}$ has the **inverse-Gaussian distribution**:
$$T_B \sim \text{IG}\left(\frac{N_0 - B}{\mu},\; \left(\frac{N_0 - B}{\sigma}\right)^2\right).$$

The mean and variance are:
$$\mathbb{E}[T_B] = \frac{N_0 - B}{\mu}, \qquad \text{Var}[T_B] = \frac{\sigma^2(N_0 - B)}{\mu^3}.$$

*Proof.* Standard result for Brownian motion with drift; see Redner (2001). $\square$

### 6.4 Moments, zero-noise limit, and median

**[Status: Corollary.]**

**Corollary 6.4.**

(i) As $\sigma \to 0$, the inverse-Gaussian distribution concentrates at the deterministic hitting time $(N_0 - B)/\mu$.

(ii) The **median** of the inverse-Gaussian distribution satisfies:
$$\text{median}[T_B] < \mathbb{E}[T_B] = \frac{N_0 - B}{\mu}.$$

That is, the median passage time is strictly below the mean for all $\sigma > 0$.

*Proof.* (i) The variance $\sigma^2(N_0-B)/\mu^3 \to 0$ as $\sigma \to 0$, so the distribution concentrates at the mean. (ii) The inverse-Gaussian distribution is right-skewed for all parameter values, so the median is strictly below the mean. $\square$

**Interpretation.** The median-below-mean property means that a typical groundwater system will hit its barrier *earlier* than the mean passage time suggests. Reporting only the mean passage time overstates the time available for intervention.

### 6.5 The record-relative barrier discipline

**[Status: Counterexample/limit.]**

A **record-relative barrier** is a barrier defined relative to the historical maximum or minimum of a time series (e.g., "50% below the historical maximum groundwater level"). The **record-relative barrier discipline** requires that such barriers are not used as physical thresholds without separate justification.

**Counterexample.** A groundwater level can cross a record-relative barrier (e.g., "20% below the 1950 level") without crossing any physical threshold (e.g., the level at which the aquifer becomes hydraulically disconnected from a river). Conversely, a physical threshold can be crossed without the record-relative barrier being triggered. The two types of barriers answer different questions and cannot be interchanged.

### 6.6 The geometric-Brownian fisheries first passage

**[Status: Theorem.]**

**Theorem 6.6.** For the geometric-Brownian motion:
$$\frac{dN}{N} = \mu \, dt + \sigma \, dW(t),$$
starting at $N_0 > B > 0$, the first-passage time $T_B = \inf\{t : N(t) = B\}$ satisfies:
$$T_B = \frac{\ln(N_0/B)}{\mu - \sigma^2/2} + \text{stochastic term},$$
and has finite expectation if and only if $\mu > \sigma^2/2$.

*Proof.* Apply Itô's lemma to $\ln N(t)$:
$$d(\ln N) = \left(\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right) dt + \sigma \, dW.$$
This reduces the problem to Brownian motion with drift $\mu - \sigma^2/2$ hitting the barrier $\ln(B/N_0) < 0$. The result follows from the standard Brownian first-passage formula. $\square$

### 6.7 The constant-production phosphate passage time

**[Status: Application record.]**

For phosphate with declared reserves $R_0$ and constant production rate $P$, the passage time is:
$$T_{\text{phosphate}} = \frac{R_0}{P}.$$

This is arithmetic, not a forecast. It does not account for changes in production rate, new discoveries, substitution, or grade decline. It is classified as an application record at its declared evidentiary level.

### 6.8 The explicit non-claims

**[Status: Counterexample/limit.]**

Seven non-claims are stated to prevent overinterpretation:

1. The stochastic surrogates (§6.2) do not establish physical dynamics; they are declared mathematical models imposed on data.
2. The first-passage theorems (§6.3, §6.6) do not predict actual depletion dates; they characterize the distribution of passage times under declared surrogates.
3. The inverse-Gaussian model (§6.3) does not apply to all groundwater systems; it applies only where the Brownian surrogate with constant drift is a declared approximation.
4. The geometric-Brownian model (§6.6) does not apply to all fisheries; it applies only where multiplicative noise is a declared approximation.
5. The median-below-mean result (§6.4) does not imply that the median is a better predictor than the mean for policy purposes; it states a distributional property.
6. The record-relative barrier discipline (§6.5) does not establish that all relative barriers are invalid; it requires separate justification for their use as physical thresholds.
7. The application classifications (§5.5) do not establish that the classified indicators are useless; they state what question each indicator answers and what question it does not answer.

### 6.9 Parameter and observation uncertainty

All first-passage results are conditional on the declared parameters $\mu$, $\sigma$, $N_0$, and $B$. Parameter uncertainty propagates through the hitting-time distribution. Observation uncertainty in the initial stock $N_0$ and the drift estimate $\mu_{\text{obs}}$ must be declared. The framework does not resolve parameter uncertainty; it requires that uncertainty be stated alongside every passage-time computation.

## 7. The Domain Templates at Registered Status

### 7.1 The phosphorus template

**[Status: Definitions, registered template obligations.]**

The phosphorus template is a **registered identification ladder**: a structured protocol for identifying, classifying, and falsifying claims about phosphorus depletion. It does not claim constitutive content — it does not assert that phosphorus reserves will be exhausted at a particular date, or that a particular extraction trajectory will hold. It provides the scaffolding for testing such claims.

The template consists of:

1. **Declared stock:** Phosphate rock reserves $R_0$ at declared grade, sourced from the U.S. Geological Survey Mineral Commodity Summaries (USGS, 2026). The declared reserve figure carries its own evidentiary status: it is a reported quantity, not a physical constant, and is subject to revision with new exploration data, changes in economic cut-off grade, and reclassification of resources to reserves.

2. **Declared production rate:** Annual phosphate rock production $P(t)$, sourced from the same USGS summaries. The production rate is a reported economic quantity, not a physical extraction limit.

3. **Identification ladder:** A sequence of increasingly specific claims, each with a declared falsification protocol:
   - **Rung 1 (arithmetic):** The reserve-life ratio $R_0 / P(t_0)$ is computable. Status: identity.
   - **Rung 2 (scenario-conditioned):** Under constant production $P(t) = P(t_0)$, the depletion horizon is $T = R_0 / P(t_0)$. Status: conditional theorem (conditional on the constant-production assumption).
   - **Rung 3 (dynamic):** Under declared production trajectories $P(t)$, the depletion horizon is the hitting time $T^*$ satisfying $\int_0^{T^*} P(\tau) d\tau = R_0$. Status: conditional theorem (conditional on the declared trajectory).
   - **Rung 4 (displacement):** Substitution, recycling, and demand reduction displace the declared production trajectory. Status: registered displacement discipline — the template records displacement claims but does not model them.

4. **Falsification protocol:** Each rung carries a declared observation that would falsify it. Rung 1 is falsified if the reserve or production figures are revised. Rung 2 is falsified if production is not constant. Rung 3 is falsified if the declared trajectory does not hold. Rung 4 is falsified if displacement does not occur as declared.

**Non-claims.** The phosphorus template does not establish:
- That phosphorus will be exhausted at any particular date;
- That the declared reserves are physically fixed;
- That production trajectories are predictable;
- That substitution or recycling will or will not occur;
- That the reserve-life ratio is a meaningful sustainability indicator.

### 7.2 The groundwater template

**[Status: Definitions, registered template obligations.]**

The groundwater template is a registered identification ladder for groundwater storage anomalies, specifically structured around the GRACE (Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment) satellite gravimetry record and its derivatives.

The template consists of:

1. **Declared observable:** The gravimetric groundwater product (G3P) anomaly time series, derived from GRACE and GRACE-FO satellite missions (Tapley et al., 2004; Griebmeier et al., 2023; Guentner et al., 2024). The G3P anomaly is a **statistical index** measuring deviations from a declared baseline, not a physical stock measurement.

2. **Identification ladder:**
   - **Rung 1 (statistical):** The G3P anomaly-persistence index measures the autocorrelation structure of the anomaly time series. Status: statistical index.
   - **Rung 2 (physical interpretation):** Under declared assumptions about aquifer geometry, specific yield, and baseline definition, the anomaly can be converted to an equivalent water height. Status: conditional theorem (conditional on the declared conversion assumptions).
   - **Rung 3 (depletion inference):** Under declared extraction and recharge scenarios, the anomaly trend can be interpreted as a depletion signal. Status: conditional theorem (conditional on the declared scenarios).
   - **Rung 4 (first-passage):** Under the observed-drift Brownian surrogate (§6.2), the first-passage time to a declared barrier can be computed. Status: conditional theorem (conditional on the surrogate being a declared approximation).

3. **Falsification protocol:** Each rung carries a declared falsification observation. Rung 1 is falsified if the anomaly time series does not exhibit the claimed persistence structure. Rung 2 is falsified if the conversion assumptions are violated. Rung 3 is falsified if the declared scenarios do not hold. Rung 4 is falsified if the surrogate does not approximate the observed dynamics.

4. **Record-relative barrier discipline:** The template enforces the discipline of §6.5: barriers defined relative to the historical record are not used as physical thresholds without separate justification.

**Non-claims.** The groundwater template does not establish:
- That any particular aquifer is depleting;
- That the G3P anomaly is a direct measurement of groundwater stock;
- That the Brownian surrogate captures physical groundwater dynamics;
- That the first-passage theorems predict actual depletion dates.

### 7.3 The two-pool gap

**[Status: Definition.]**

The **two-pool gap** is the structural separation between the active pool (extractable stock) and the geological pool (total resource base) in the natural block. The gap arises because:

- The active pool $A$ is the subset of the geological reserve $G$ that is currently extractable given technology, economics, and regulation.
- The geological reserve $G$ includes quantities that are not currently extractable (too deep, too low grade, too remote, legally restricted).
- The transfer from $G$ to $A$ (reserve growth through exploration, technology improvement, or reclassification) is not governed by the same dynamics as extraction from $A$.

The two-pool gap is a **structural feature** of the ledger, not a parameter to be estimated. It cannot be closed by declaring a single pool. The ledger requires both pools to be represented separately, with the transfer between them declared as a transformation operator with its own stoichiometric and donor-limitation constraints.

### 7.4 Extractor-side harvest economics: a scoped remark

**[Status: Scoped remark, not a theorem.]**

The ledger framework tracks physical stocks and flows, not economic values. However, a scoped remark is warranted: extraction rates in real resource systems are governed by economic decisions (price, cost, profit, regulation) as well as physical constraints (stock size, accessibility, technology). The donor-limitation discipline of §2.1 captures the physical constraint but not the economic one.

The scoped remark is: **the ledger does not model extractor behaviour.** It provides the conservation structure within which extractor behaviour operates, but the effort dynamics $E(t)$ are taken as declared inputs, not as endogenous variables determined by an economic model. The companion delay-dynamics paper provides the retarded-field equations that model effort dynamics; the interface contract of §8 fixes what transfers between the two papers.

## 8. The Seam to the Delay-Dynamics Paper

**[Status: Interface contract.]**

This paper (the material ledger) and the companion nonlinear-dynamics paper (the delay-dynamics paper) share an exact object and reject an exact reduction. The interface contract fixes both.

### 8.1 The exact shared object

The exact shared object is the **single-resource deficit identity**:
$$qEN - R(N) = -\dot{N},$$
which is derived in §4.4 of this paper as the specialization identity of the componentwise deficit. This identity is the seam: it is the equation that both papers use, and it is the only equation that transfers exactly between them.

In this paper, the identity is an algebraic consequence of the resource equation $\dot{N} = R(N) - qEN$. In the delay-dynamics paper, the identity is the starting point for the retarded-field equations that model effort dynamics with institutional delays.

### 8.2 The rejected reduction: five reasons

**No exact dynamic reduction exists from the closed primitive ledger to the working retarded field.** This rejection is stated with five reasons:

1. **The ledger is a conservation structure; the retarded field is a dynamical system.** The ledger provides mass-balance equations and donor-limitation constraints. The retarded field provides delay-differential equations for effort dynamics. These are different mathematical objects with different proof obligations.

2. **The ledger's fluxes are donor-limited; the retarded field's effort is policy-driven.** In the ledger, extraction is bounded by the donor stock: $E(t) \leq g(N(t))$. In the retarded field, effort is determined by institutional feedback with delays: $E(t) = E(t - \tau) + \text{correction}$. The donor-limitation constraint and the institutional-delay dynamics are not reducible to one another.

3. **The ledger has no time delays; the retarded field is defined by them.** The ledger's mass-balance equations are instantaneous: $\dot{S}_i = \sum_j A_{ij} f_j$. The retarded field's equations contain explicit delays: $\dot{N}(t) = R(N(t)) - qE(t-\tau)N(t)$. The delay structure cannot be recovered from the instantaneous ledger.

4. **The ledger's service layer is a readout; the retarded field's observation is an operator.** In the ledger, services are functions of the stock vector: $\sigma(t) = h(S(t))$. In the retarded field, observation is an operator that thins the policy class and introduces information delays. These are structurally different.

5. **The ledger's depletion diagnostics are classification tools; the retarded field's bifurcation analysis is a dynamical tool.** The ledger classifies depletion times (§5) and first-passage times (§6). The retarded field analyses Hopf bifurcations, limit cycles, and stability. These are different mathematical activities with different outputs.

### 8.3 What transfers and what does not

**Transfers:** The single-resource deficit identity $qEN - R = -\dot{N}$.

**Does not transfer:** The closed-ledger theorem set (§3), the service readout structure (§4), the depletion-diagnostics taxonomy (§5), the first-passage theorems (§6), the domain templates (§7). None of these objects has an exact counterpart in the retarded field, and no declared map connects them.

**The interface contract is recorded as a rejection, not a gap.** The absence of a reduction is a mathematical fact, not an omission to be filled.

## 9. What the Ledger Does Not Support

### 9.1 Compensatory aggregation is rejected, not merely discouraged

**[Status: Definitional axiom.]**

The typed ledger **rejects** compensatory aggregation as a matter of definitional structure, not as a policy preference. The typing discipline of §2.1 states that no operation may mix compartments with different moieties without a declared stoichiometric transformation. This means:

- A deficit in one moiety cannot be offset by a surplus in another;
- An aggregate indicator that sums heterogeneous stocks is not a valid ledger output;
- The conjunctive depletion criterion of §4.5 (every moiety's depletion horizon must exceed the assessment horizon) is a theorem of the ledger, not a policy choice.

This rejection is the material-accounting analogue of the quantifier-separation result in Paper 1: compensatory assessment (one aggregate index) and non-compensatory assessment (each floor separately binding) can disagree, and the disagreement is structural, not parametric.

**The ledger does not merely discourage compensatory aggregation; it makes it impossible within the typed structure.** Any aggregate that purports to represent the ledger's state must declare the transformation by which heterogeneous moieties are combined, and that transformation must satisfy stoichiometric conservation (Theorem 3.3).

### 9.2 The double-counting discipline

**[Status: Definitional axiom.]**

The incidence matrix structure of §2.1 enforces the **double-counting discipline**: every unit of mass is counted exactly once, in exactly one compartment, at exactly one time. The discipline is enforced by three structural features:

1. **The incidence matrix is signed:** each flux enters one compartment (+1) and exits another (-1). Mass is neither created nor destroyed by internal fluxes.

2. **The mass-balance equation holds by construction:** $\dot{S}_i = \sum_j A_{ij} f_j$. There is no separate check for double-counting; the structure prevents it.

3. **The transformation operators are stoichiometric:** when mass is converted from one moiety to another, the stoichiometric coefficient $\alpha_{m \to m'}$ declares exactly how much output mass is produced per unit input mass. Theorem 3.3 requires $\sum_{m'} \alpha_{m \to m'} = 1$ for mass conservation.

The double-counting discipline is not a recommendation; it is a theorem of the ledger structure. Any accounting procedure that violates the incidence matrix structure is not a valid ledger operation.

### 9.3 The negative and boundary content is first-class

**[Status: Definitional axiom.]**

The ledger treats negative results and boundary cases as **first-class content**, not as exceptions or failures:

- **Negative certificates:** A depletion diagnostic that establishes "this moiety will be depleted before the assessment horizon" is a finding, not a failure. The componentwise deficit (§4.4) identifies where a floor is violated; the depletion-horizon taxonomy (§5) classifies the time to violation; the first-passage theorems (§6) provide the stochastic semantics. All of these are legitimate outputs of the ledger.

- **Boundary cases:** The record-relative barrier discipline (§6.5) identifies cases where relative barriers fail as physical thresholds. The two-pool gap (§7.3) identifies cases where the active pool and geological pool cannot be conflated. The five-reason rejection of the dynamic reduction (§8.2) identifies cases where the ledger and the retarded field cannot be unified.

- **Non-claims:** The explicit non-claims of §6.8 state what the first-passage theorems do not establish. The non-claims of §7.1 and §7.2 state what the domain templates do not establish. These non-claims are part of the content, not omissions.

The ledger's status discipline (§1.3) requires that every claim carry its status. Negative results, boundary cases, and non-claims carry their statuses too. They are not suppressed, promoted, or silently transferred.

## 11. Provenance, Reproducibility, and Limits

### 11.1 Provenance

This paper's statements are drawn from the programme's source corpus through a concordance. Each statement was verified against its source in a dated closure campaign and is stated at exactly its source-declared status. The complete statement-level inventory, with per-statement provenance keys, is Section 10 of this paper and the supplementary material.

This paper's own theorems are complete here. The canonical conservation family (§3.1) is stated with the companion theorem collection as proof owner. All other theorems are proved in this paper with proofs reproduced.

### 11.2 Reproducibility

The closed finite-donor ledger's theorem set (§3) is verified by the displayed proofs. The first-passage theorems (§6.3, §6.6) are verified by the displayed Itô calculus and inverse-Gaussian distribution theory. The application records (§5.5, §6.7) are classified at their declared evidentiary levels and do not require computational verification.

The machine artifact for the four-stock specialization (§2.4) is a committed deterministic file in the project repository. It verifies the specialization identity $qEN - R = -\dot{N}$ and the natural-block mass identity in exact integer arithmetic. The artifact is a regression check on the symbolic results, not a proof of them.

### 11.3 Limits

(i) **The ledger is a conservation structure, not a dynamical model.** It provides mass-balance equations, donor-limitation constraints, and stoichiometric transformations. It does not provide population dynamics, effort dynamics, price dynamics, or policy dynamics. These belong to the companion papers.

(ii) **The depletion-diagnostics taxonomy is a classification, not a forecast.** The three quantities of §5.1–5.3 (gross turnover, frozen-rate ratio, scenario-conditioned hitting time) answer different questions. None is an unconditional forecast. The application records of §5.5 classify published indicators at their exact evidentiary levels.

(iii) **The first-passage theorems are conditional on declared surrogates.** The inverse-Gaussian and geometric-Brownian results (§6.3, §6.6) hold for the declared stochastic surrogates. They do not establish physical dynamics. The seven non-claims of §6.8 state what the theorems do not establish.

(iv) **The domain templates are registered identification ladders, not constitutive models.** The phosphorus and groundwater templates (§7) provide falsification protocols and displacement disciplines. They do not claim that phosphorus will be exhausted, that groundwater is depleting, or that any particular trajectory will hold.

(v) **The interface contract with the delay-dynamics paper is a rejection.** No exact dynamic reduction exists from the closed primitive ledger to the working retarded field (§8.2). This is a mathematical fact, not an omission.

(vi) **The ledger does not support compensatory aggregation.** This is a definitional axiom (§9.1), not a limitation. Any assessment that requires compensatory aggregation must use a different framework.

(vii) **The paper asserts nothing empirical.** The application records classify published indicators; the domain templates provide identification ladders; the first-passage theorems characterize declared surrogates. No claim is made about any specific resource system's future trajectory.
