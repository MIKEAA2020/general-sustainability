# Audit of the revised manuscript

## Overall verdict

The manuscript is substantially sharper than the earlier version, and the central separation idea is potentially publishable. The key identity

\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)=\bigcap_{w\in C}E_w(z)
\]

together with the distinction

\[
\exists a\,\forall w
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\forall w\,\exists a_w
\]

is mathematically meaningful and provides a clean dynamic separation mechanism.

However, the manuscript is **not yet journal-ready**. The main problem is no longer the absence of a formal architecture; it is that several claims are stronger than the displayed definitions or proofs support. The most important defects are concentrated in Section 4:

1. “endpoint-only” is defined using exact tubes;
2. the false-positive region is inconsistently defined;
3. the geometric description of the false-positive set is wrong;
4. the machine verification cannot, as described, verify a continuum theorem over all prices;
5. the policy implications overstate what the theorem proves.

There are also serious but repairable issues in the governance, intergenerational, composition, computational, and planetary-boundaries sections.

The manuscript should be repositioned as:

> a formal counterexample and quantifier-separation paper, with a typed framework surrounding the main theorem,

rather than as a fully established “domain-agnostic framework” whose extensions are all mathematically completed.

---

# 1. Principal mathematical contribution: what is valid

The strongest part of the paper is the following structure.

For each action \(a\), define a trajectory-dependent floor surplus \(s\). With

\[
C=\mathbb R_+^n\setminus\{0\},
\]

the pointwise identity

\[
s\ge0
\iff
w\cdot s\ge0\quad \forall w\in C
\]

is correct. Consequently, for a **fixed action** and a common action set,

\[
a\in E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)
\iff
a\in E_w(z)\quad\forall w\in C.
\]

The genuine separation is then:

\[
\underbrace{\exists a\;\forall w}_{\text{one action safe under every price}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{\forall w\;\exists a_w}_{\text{each price has some safe action}},
\]

with the converse generally false.

That is the correct mathematical contribution. It should be stated more narrowly and made the organizing theorem of the paper.

The paper should avoid suggesting that scalarization itself is pointwise blind under the full cone. The manuscript correctly recognizes this in Lemma 1. The blindness arises from **policy dependence**, not from the closed-cone scalarization at a fixed trajectory.

# 2. Fatal or near-fatal issues in Section 4

## 2.1 “Endpoint-only physical” is not endpoint-only

The definition is

\[
E_{\mathrm{phys}}(z)
=
\{a:\forall d,\;
\mathrm{Tube}(a,d)\subseteq S^{\mathrm{phys}}
\text{ and }
\mathrm{Succ}(a,d)\subseteq G^{\mathrm{phys}}\}.
\]

This is an exact-tube physical assessment. It is not endpoint-only.

If the intended doctrine is endpoint accounting, the definition should be something like

\[
E_{\mathrm{end}}(z)
=
\{a:\forall d,\;
\mathrm{End}(a,d)\subseteq S^{\mathrm{phys}}
\text{ and }
\mathrm{Succ}(a,d)\subseteq G^{\mathrm{phys}}\}.
\]

If only the final state matters, write

\[
\mathrm{End}(a,d)\subseteq S^{\mathrm{phys}}
\]

rather than using “endpoint” ambiguously for an entire successor set.

### Consequence

Theorem B(3) is currently labelled an endpoint-only result but is proved using an action—FAST—that is physically tube-safe anyway because \(S^{\mathrm{phys}}\) constrains only \(x\). The distinction is therefore not demonstrated by the displayed definition.

To establish the claimed strict hierarchy, define two separate operators:

\[
E_{\mathrm{end}},
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}.
\]

Then show

\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}
\subseteq
E_w
\subseteq
E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}
\subseteq
E_{\mathrm{end}},
\]

where the last inclusion is generally strict.

## 2.2 The false-positive set is inconsistently defined

Theorem B(4) states

\[
\mathrm{FP}
=
\{x<1,\;s_1<2,\;s_2<2,\;s_1+s_2\ge2\}.
\]

This is the correct strong-versus-aggregate false-positive region.

But the prose repeatedly defines the “triangle” with \(x\) unrestricted:

\[
\mathrm{FP}_0
=
\{s_1<2,\;s_2<2,\;s_1+s_2\ge2\}.
\]

That region is not entirely false positive, because for \(x\ge1\), STAGED is typed-admissible. It is a **price-dependent-plan region** or **aggregate-versus-direct-floor region**, not necessarily a strong-sustainability false-positive region.

Use three distinct sets:

\[
\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}}
=
\left(\bigcap_{w\in C}P_w\right)\setminus P_{\mathrm{typ}},
\]

\[
\mathrm{FP}_0
=
\{s_1<2,\;s_2<2,\;s_1+s_2\ge2\},
\]

and

\[
R
=
\mathrm{FP}_0\cap\{x\ge1\},
\qquad
I
=
\mathrm{FP}_0\cap\{x<1\}.
\]

Then

\[
\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}}=I,
\]

assuming the definitions in Theorem B.

This would make the rescue/impossibility distinction precise:

- \(R\): aggregate price-dependent region, but ultimately typed-transformable;
- \(I\): genuine aggregate false-positive region.

## 2.3 The figure specification is geometrically wrong

The proposed false-positive geometry says:

> “the triangle with vertices \((0,2),(2,0),(0,0)\), minus the region below the budget line.”

The triangle with those vertices is exactly

\[
s_1\ge0,\quad s_2\ge0,\quad s_1+s_2\le2.
\]

Removing the region below the budget line removes the entire triangle.

The intended region, with

\[
0\le s_1<2,\qquad
0\le s_2<2,\qquad
s_1+s_2\ge2,
\]

is the upper-right triangle in the square \([0,2]^2\), with vertices approximately

\[
(0,2),\quad(2,0),\quad(2,2),
\]

subject to the strict boundaries \(s_i<2\).

The corrected description should be:

> The aggregate-versus-floor discrepancy region is the portion of the square \(0\le s_1,s_2<2\) lying on or above \(s_1+s_2=2\). Its relative interior is \(0<s_1,s_2<2\) and \(s_1+s_2>2\).

Also, \(x=0\) and \(x=1\) cannot be shown as two regions in the same \((s_1,s_2)\)-plane unless they are presented as separate panels or explicitly labelled slices. At fixed \(x=0\), the region is the genuine impossibility region. At fixed \(x=1\), it is the rescue region. They are not spatially separated “left” and “right” portions of the same plane.

## 2.4 Theorem B(2) needs a careful treatment of zero-coordinate boundaries

The argument is correct in the relative interior where \(s_1,s_2>0\). On the boundary, special cases occur:

- \(s_1=0\);
- \(s_2=0\);
- \(s_1=2\);
- \(s_2=2\);
- \(s_1+s_2=2\).

For example, if \(s_1=0\) and \(s_2=2\), SLOW is typed-safe because its minimum \(s_1\) remains \(0\), while FAST is not typed-safe. This point belongs to the typed set under the displayed formula because \(s_2\ge2\).

The theorem should distinguish:

- closed-set identities;
- relative interiors;
- boundary classification;
- price-ratio formulas where division by \(s_2\) or \(2-s_2\) is undefined.

The formulas

\[
\rho_1=\frac{2-s_1}{s_2},
\qquad
\rho_2=\frac{s_1}{2-s_2}
\]

are valid only when

\[
s_2>0,\qquad s_2<2.
\]

State boundary conventions separately.

## 2.5 “No price vector rescues the assessment” is too broad

The manuscript says:

> “no choice of prices — and no family of price vectors — recovers the noncompensatory predecessor.”

This requires a quantifier qualification.

It is true for the construction

\[
\bigcap_{w\in W}\{z:E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\},
\]

where each weight may choose its own action. It is not true if a family of prices is evaluated with a **common-policy requirement**:

\[
\{z:\exists a\;\forall w\in W,\;a\in E_w(z)\}.
\]

That common-policy formulation recovers the typed assessment when \(W=C\), and often already does so for the coordinate weights \(e_i\).

The paper must distinguish:

\[
\bigcap_w\{z:E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}
\]

from

\[
\{z:\bigcap_wE_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

The first is the weak per-weight assessment. The second is the strong common-plan assessment.

## 2.6 Theorem A’s set notation is confusing

The expression

\[
\{P_{\mathrm{typ}}\}
\]

appears to mean the set of states accepted by the assessment, but \(P\) was already defined as the policy class in the canonical tuple. This is a type collision.

Use, for example,

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{typ}},
\qquad
\mathcal V_w,
\qquad
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{phys}},
\]

for accepted-state sets, and reserve \(P\) for policy classes.

Then write:

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{typ}}
=
\{z:E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)\ne\varnothing\},
\]

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{weak}}
=
\bigcap_{w\in C}
\{z:E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

This makes the central noncommutation visually explicit:

\[
\{z:\bigcap_wE_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}
\subsetneq
\bigcap_w\{z:E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

# 3. Theorem C: propagation through backward induction

The basic monotonicity result is plausible, but the statement needs tightening.

## 3.1 The first inclusion is valid under a common typed recursion

If all three recursions use:

- the same action correspondence;
- the same disturbance sets;
- the same exact tube maps;
- the same reset semantics;
- nested safe sets;

then

\[
W_j^{\mathrm{typ}}
\subseteq
W_j^w
\subseteq
W_j^{\mathrm{phys}}
\]

holds by backward induction.

However, the manuscript writes

\[
W_j^{\mathrm{typ}}
\subseteq
\bigcap_{w\in C}W_j^w.
\]

This is fine, but it should not be interpreted as

\[
W_j^{\mathrm{typ}}
=
\bigcap_{w\in C}W_j^w.
\]

The strict gap is caused by the same stagewise \(\exists a/\forall w\) issue, and the proof should explicitly identify where equality fails.

## 3.2 The hold-prefix argument needs the action domain specified

The statement that HOLD is the unique action and therefore pulls the witness region backward is valid only if:

1. HOLD is available at every prefix state;
2. the hold tube is exactly \(\{z\}\);
3. the prefix safe sets contain the relevant witness states;
4. the terminal and successor architecture labels remain unchanged;
5. no additional reset or disturbance branches are introduced.

These assumptions should be stated rather than embedded in prose.

## 3.3 General multi-interval strictness requires more than prefixing holds

The claim that strictness holds for every multi-interval datum does not follow from the one-interval theorem. It is true for the constructed hold-prefixed datum, but not necessarily for arbitrary multi-stage systems.

A later stage can erase the gap if:

- every aggregate-feasible state has a common typed-safe continuation;
- the weak and typed terminal sets coincide on the reachable subset;
- earlier constraints eliminate the witness region;
- actions become coupled across stages.

The correct formulation is:

> Strictness persists under hold-prefix extension of the witness datum.

Do not generalize this to arbitrary multi-interval data without a separate persistence theorem.

# 4. Machine verification and reproducibility

## 4.1 Finite grid verification does not prove the continuum theorem

The manuscript says that all claims of Theorems 1–3 are “machine-checked” over a 29,791-state grid and finite critical weight sets.

That can verify:

- the finite discretized witness;
- selected rational points;
- the exact action classifications on the grid;
- the reported counts.

It cannot by itself verify the continuous identities

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{typ}}
=
\{x\ge1\}\cup\{s_1\ge2\}\cup\{s_2\ge2\},
\]

or

\[
\bigcap_{w\in C}\mathcal V_w
=
\{x\ge1\}\cup\{s_1+s_2\ge2\},
\]

unless the manuscript separately proves that:

1. every state belongs to a finite cell with constant action classification;
2. every weight ratio is reduced to finitely many critical inequalities;
3. boundary cases are handled analytically;
4. the exact tube predicates are constant on each cell.

The continuum proof is already largely present in Theorem B. The code should be described as a **finite artifact validating the symbolic theorem**, not as proving the symbolic theorem.

## 4.2 The “dense critical weight sets” language is misleading

A finite set cannot be dense in the full continuum \(C\). It may be dense relative to a finite grid or contain all analytically identified critical ratios for the discretized states.

Replace “dense critical weight sets” with:

> the finite verification set contains the analytically identified critical weight ratios for every enumerated grid state, together with the boundary weights and midpoint tests.

## 4.3 Scale 40 is not automatically exact integer arithmetic

Clarify what “scale 40” means:

- If it means denominator \(40\), only rational quantities compatible with that denominator are represented exactly.
- If it means a decimal fixed-point scale \(10^{40}\), then exactness holds for represented decimal rationals, not for arbitrary real parameters.
- If all witness quantities are rational, provide the common denominator or integer encoding.

The claim “no tolerances” is useful, but exactness applies to the finite representation, not automatically to the underlying real-valued datum.

## 4.4 Verification certification levels are conflated

The manuscript calls the finite artifact “certified,” but its hierarchy says certification requires interval or rigorous arithmetic. Exact integer arithmetic is rigorous for the finite rational computation, but the continuous theorem still depends on symbolic proof.

Use two labels:

- **finite computation:** exact rational/integer verification;
- **mathematical theorem:** analytic proof over the stated continuum.

The strongest claim is:

> The artifact exactly verifies the finite rational instance and regression-tests the symbolic classification; the continuum statements are established by the displayed proofs.

## 4.5 The artifact is not currently auditable from the text

A journal paper should provide:

- repository URL;
- commit hash or release DOI;
- software version;
- execution command;
- expected hashes of output files;
- license;
- machine-readable input datum;
- treatment of boundary and degenerate cases.

“Committed alongside” is not enough without a stable identifier.

# 5. Conceptual and terminology issues

## 5.1 The paper sometimes treats “weak sustainability” too narrowly

A scalarized feasibility operator with nonnegative weights is one formal model of compensatory assessment. It is not identical to the entire weak-sustainability literature.

Weak sustainability may involve:

- aggregate wealth;
- constant total capital;
- nondeclining comprehensive consumption;
- substitutability assumptions;
- discounting;
- shadow prices;
- intertemporal investment conditions.

The paper should say:

> “the scalarized aggregate doctrine as formalized here”

rather than equating the operator with weak sustainability without qualification.

Likewise, strong sustainability is not always equivalent to requiring every stock coordinate to remain nonnegative at every instant. Critical natural capital frameworks may use thresholds, safe operating spaces, irreversibility, resilience, or minimum-service conditions.

## 5.2 “Prices” are being used as arbitrary assessment weights

The set

\[
w\in\mathbb R_+^n\setminus\{0\}
\]

contains assessment weights, but calling all of them “prices” is stronger. Actual prices may be:

- strictly positive;
- endogenous;
- constrained by market structure;
- dynamic;
- state-dependent;
- dimensionally heterogeneous.

Use “nonnegative scalarization weights” in the theorem and reserve “prices” for the interpretive section.

## 5.3 The “weakest/intermediate/strongest” doctrine hierarchy needs qualification

The set inclusion

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{typ}}
\subseteq
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{weak}}
\subseteq
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{phys}}
\]

is correct for the constructed operators. It does not establish a universal ranking of all endpoint, weak, and strong sustainability doctrines. The ranking depends on:

- common action and disturbance classes;
- safe-set inclusion;
- common horizon;
- common tube semantics;
- common terminal condition.

State the hierarchy as a theorem about the defined operators, not a general doctrinal ordering.

# 6. Governance section: internal inconsistencies and upgrades

## 6.1 Constructors do not change exactly one component

The manuscript defines a constructor as a map that:

> “changes exactly one named component.”

But the examples contradict this:

- \(Cap(Q)\) may change the action correspondence \(U\), the dynamics \(F\), and the observation or enforcement model.
- \(Tax(\tau)\) changes the effort law, potentially the policy class and equilibrium.
- \(Leak(h)\) changes realized fluxes and therefore \(F\), possibly the observation model.
- \(Obs(I,\Psi)\) changes both \(O\) and the feedback law.
- \(Rest(\cdot)\) may alter actions, policies, or institutional admissibility.

Replace “exactly one component” with:

> A primitive constructor has a declared support: the set of tuple components it may alter. Any induced changes outside that support must be represented explicitly or prohibited.

Define

\[
\operatorname{supp}(\mathsf C)
\subseteq
\{T,Z,S_{st},B_{out},V,\Gamma,O,A,C,R,D,K,P\}.
\]

Then constructor composition becomes auditable.

## 6.2 “Vocabulary rewrites” are not generally mathematically inert

A tax is not merely a price entry if it changes:

- agent incentives;
- equilibrium controls;
- compliance;
- enforcement;
- revenue and redistribution;
- the disturbance law.

A predator exclusion can alter trophic dynamics, not merely restrict action. An observation reform can change the admissible policy class, not only rename \(O\).

Section 6.2 should be reframed as:

> Some instruments admit a canonical representation by primitive constructors; the representation is valid only after proving the induced component changes.

## 6.3 Action-set monotonicity is correct but should be separated from policy monotonicity

The statement

\[
U_1(x)\subseteq U_2(x)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{Viab}(U_1)\subseteq\operatorname{Viab}(U_2)
\]

is correct for existential viability under the same dynamics and information structure.

But the policy ladder

\[
P_{\mathrm{impl}}\subseteq P_{\mathrm{inst}}\subseteq P_{\mathrm{tech}}\subseteq P_{\mathrm{theor}}
\]

is not automatically induced by action-set inclusion. Policy classes also depend on:

- information;
- timing;
- authority;
- strategic equilibrium;
- observability;
- implementation mechanisms.

These should be treated as separate restrictions.

## 6.4 Commons Obstruction theorem needs a boundary-exit correction

From

\[
\dot S\le-\varepsilon
\]

inside

\[
[S_{\min},S_{\min}+a],
\]

one obtains reaching \(S_{\min}\) within at most \(a/\varepsilon\) from the upper edge. Reaching the boundary is not identical to exiting below it.

A corrected statement is:

> Every trajectory entering the strip reaches \(S_{\min}\) within at most \(a/\varepsilon\). If the same strict negative drift condition persists at the boundary and solutions are forward complete, the trajectory subsequently exits below \(S_{\min}\).

Alternatively assume the initial state lies in

\[
[S_{\min},S_{\min}+a)
\]

and use a margin that remains strictly negative through the boundary.

The theorem also needs a defined scalar stock dynamics and a clear quantifier over Nash trajectories.

## 6.5 “Monitoring bounds the observation error so state feedback applies” is not automatic

Observation error does not by itself make a desired feedback law implementable. One needs a robust-feedback theorem:

\[
\|\hat x-x\|\le\delta
\]

and a control law whose robust admissibility is certified for all states in the observation fibre. Otherwise monitoring may improve knowledge without producing a safe common prescription.

# 7. Intergenerational section

## 7.1 The nested-impossibility proof should use an \(\omega\)-limit argument explicitly

The conclusion is broadly correct under the stated compactness assumptions, but the proof should be written carefully.

If

\[
x(t)\in V^{(k)}
\quad\text{for all }t\ge t_k,
\]

and the trajectory is contained in a compact set, there exists an \(\omega\)-limit point \(x^*\). Since each \(V^{(k)}\) is closed,

\[
x^*\in V^{(k)}
\quad\forall k,
\]

so

\[
x^*\in\bigcap_kV^{(k)},
\]

contradicting emptiness.

The manuscript should state:

- forward completeness;
- existence of an \(\omega\)-limit point;
- confinement to one common compact set;
- closedness of every \(V^{(k)}\).

## 7.2 “Every finite segment satisfiable” is not implied by the theorem

The theorem proves no infinite path exists when the intersection is empty. It does not prove that every finite horizon is satisfiable.

If the intended phenomenon is:

\[
\operatorname{Viab}_N\ne\varnothing
\quad\forall N,
\qquad
\operatorname{Viab}_\infty=\varnothing,
\]

that requires a separate explicit example, such as

\[
V^{(k)}=[0,1/k]
\]

with a trajectory that can remain in each finite prefix but no point belongs to all sets, together with dynamics and initial conditions making every finite prefix feasible.

## 7.3 Stationary equivalence requires aligned boundaries and policy timing

The statement is immediate only if:

- generation intervals cover the same infinite horizon;
- the policy class is unchanged across generations;
- there are no resets or inherited variables;
- \(V^{(k)}=V\) exactly;
- the intergenerational criterion is simply \(x(t)\in V^{(k)}\).

If inheritance, reset, age structure, or generation-specific policy restrictions exist, stationary set equality alone may not reduce the model to ordinary viability.

# 8. Composition and transformation section

## 8.1 Effort-scale invariance requires the exact equations

The claimed transformation

\[
E'=aE,\quad E'_{\max}=aE_{\max},\quad q'=q/a,\quad \delta_0'=a\delta_0
\]

may be correct, but no dynamics are displayed. Invariance cannot be assessed without the registered equations.

Provide the transformed equations and verify term by term that:

\[
(N'(t),Z'(t))=(N(t),Z(t))
\]

or specify which observables remain invariant.

Also distinguish:

- parameter nonidentifiability;
- state-coordinate scaling;
- observational equivalence;
- dynamical conjugacy.

These are not identical.

## 8.2 The soft-minimum bound is unsupported as written

The inequality

\[
\pi_j\le w_{\min}^{-1}e^{-\rho\Delta_y}
\]

requires definitions of:

- \(w_{\min}\);
- \(\pi_j\);
- \(\Delta_y\);
- the yield functions;
- the normalization;
- the domain;
- the assumptions under which the exponential estimate holds.

The subsequent error estimate

\[
\|X-X^k\|\le C_T\varepsilon_c
\]

requires a stability or Lipschitz theorem, initial-condition matching, a finite time horizon, and a specified norm.

This should be converted into a proposition with a complete assumption list and proof, or marked as a conditional source result.

## 8.3 “Coupling creates viability” is not reproducible

The paper gives only:

\[
g_i(s)=s(1-s),\qquad d=0.2,
\]

and refers to “equilibrium-defined harvest floors.” That is insufficient to reproduce the example. Supply:

- the coupled equations;
- the control/action set;
- the disturbance set;
- the safe set;
- the exact floor definition;
- the decoupled comparison system;
- the kernel calculation;
- the proof or exact numerical certificate.

Otherwise this belongs in a motivating example, not in the formal framework.

# 9. Planetary Boundaries addition

## 9.1 Avoid claiming that the Planetary Boundaries framework has ten boundaries

The manuscript says:

> “nine (now ten) Earth-system processes”

and lists atmospheric oxygen concentration as the tenth.

This is likely to be challenged. The commonly cited Planetary Boundaries framework is organized around nine processes, although individual boundary formulations and proposed extensions may introduce additional variables. Atmospheric oxygen is not ordinarily presented as one of the standard nine planetary boundaries.

Use:

> “nine planetary-boundary processes in the current standard formulation, with additional proposed indicators and extensions in the wider literature.”

If a ten-boundary formulation is intended, cite the exact source and explain its status.

## 9.2 The noncompensatory interpretation needs qualification

It is reasonable to say that the Planetary Boundaries framework motivates separate boundary constraints. It is too strong to state without qualification that the framework’s “operational logic is noncompensatory.”

Planetary-boundary assessments involve:

- uncertainty;
- control variables;
- regional disaggregation;
- interactions;
- justice considerations;
- aggregation of risk;
- boundary transgression severity.

The paper should say:

> The typed operator provides a formal idealization of the separate-boundary reading.

## 9.3 The theorem does not justify the planetary-boundary stance

Theorem B shows that a particular compensatory assessment can accept a transition rejected by a typed assessment. It does not prove that planetary boundaries ought to be noncompensatory. That is a normative and scientific-design question.

Replace:

> “provides a formal justification for this noncompensatory stance”

with:

> “provides a formal model of one reason a noncompensatory stance may be adopted.”

## 9.4 “Funding the bridging resource” does not generalize to planetary boundaries

The witness has an explicit STAGED action and a scalar stock \(x\) that funds it. Many planetary-boundary violations do not have a single identifiable bridging resource. The policy implication should remain conditional on the presence of a typed rescue action.

# 10. Policy implications

Section 5.4 is useful but currently overstates the theorem.

## 10.1 Correct first implication

Instead of:

> “aggregate indices cannot certify transitions,”

write:

> “aggregate indices alone cannot certify noncompensatory transition safety under the present assessment semantics.”

A scalarized assessment can certify its own criterion. What it cannot certify is typed safety unless an additional bridge theorem is supplied.

## 10.2 “Fund the bridge, not the price” is conditional

The witness shows that changing prices cannot solve the displayed false positive and that STAGED solves it when \(x\ge1\). It does not establish a universal policy law that typed false positives are always resolved by funding one resource.

Replace with:

> “When the typed recursion identifies an admissible rescue action whose feasibility is controlled by a resource margin, investment in that margin is a candidate remedy; reweighting alone cannot substitute for it within the witness semantics.”

## 10.3 Reporting standard should be expanded

The proposed minimum report should include at least:

1. typed floors and provenance;
2. aggregate weights or weight family;
3. policy quantifiers;
4. disturbance set;
5. observation and implementation model;
6. exact versus conservative tube status;
7. terminal maintainability;
8. action-exhaustion status;
9. uncertainty and approximation bounds;
10. normative premises.

Otherwise the recommendation may reproduce the paper’s own hidden assumptions.

# 11. Data requirements: missing empirical requirements

Section 5.5 is directionally correct, but it omits several load-bearing data requirements.

## 11.1 Need action completeness or an explicit lower-bound status

A negative certificate over a finite action set proves impossibility only relative to that action set. The empirical application must state whether \(A\) is:

- exhaustive;
- a registered policy menu;
- a sampled subset;
- an inner approximation of available actions.

If \(A\) is incomplete, the result is not “no safe transition exists,” but:

> no safe transition exists among the registered actions.

## 11.2 Need calibration and identifiability data

For empirical use, specify:

- parameter estimates;
- confidence or uncertainty sets;
- structural alternatives;
- validation data;
- observation error;
- model discrepancy;
- missing-data treatment;
- disturbance dependence.

A declared \(D\) is necessary but not enough. The set must be justified or the verdict should be marked conditional on model credibility.

## 11.3 Need policy and authority data

Because the paper emphasizes implementation, empirical admission must include:

- who may select each action;
- what information they possess;
- decision timing;
- enforcement assumptions;
- compliance uncertainty;
- strategic responses;
- resource and legitimacy constraints.

## 11.4 Need a safety semantics for uncertain thresholds

A physical or normative floor is rarely known exactly. The paper should distinguish:

\[
s_i(z)\ge0
\]

from robust threshold safety:

\[
s_i(z;\theta)\ge0
\quad\forall\theta\in\Theta,
\]

and probabilistic or confidence-level versions if admitted.

# 12. Limitation (vi): tractability claim needs correction

The new computational limitation says the exact recursion is polynomial in the number of states, actions, and disturbance realizations for fixed horizon.

That is true only for a finite explicitly enumerated transition graph with constant-time predicate evaluation. It is not a general statement about continuous exact-tube viability.

For continuous systems:

- exact tube inclusion may be undecidable or computationally hard;
- action and disturbance sets may be infinite;
- reachability can be exponential in dimension;
- hybrid mode combinations can produce exponential growth;
- partial observation introduces belief-state dimension;
- robust quantifiers can require quantified real arithmetic or differential games.

A more accurate formulation is:

> On a finite explicit state-action-disturbance graph, the backward recursion is polynomial in the graph size and horizon. For continuous, hybrid, or belief-state models, the graph itself may be exponentially large, infinite, or only approximately representable; exact verification may be computationally intractable or undecidable for broad model classes.

Also, “state space grows multiplicatively with typed floors” is not universally correct. If floors are coordinates, dimension grows additively while grid cardinality can grow exponentially:

\[
N_{\mathrm{grid}}
=
\prod_{i=1}^nN_i.
\]

If architectures and histories are cross-product modes, multiplicative growth is appropriate there. State the representation explicitly.

# 13. Claims of novelty and literature positioning

## 13.1 The dynamic quantifier result may be familiar in adjacent literatures

The identity

\[
\exists a\,\forall w
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\forall w\,\exists a
\]

is elementary, but its application to exact-tube sustainability-transition assessment may still be novel. The paper should not call it simply:

> “the first formal dynamic separation”

unless a systematic literature review supports that priority claim.

Safer wording:

Statements such as:

need exact citations to results with matching:

- hybrid resets;
- exact tubes;
- robust disturbances;
- finite architecture transitions;
- terminal maintainability;
- policy quantifiers.

General viability citations establish related machinery, not necessarily the exact typed theorem. Use “instantiates standard predecessor constructions” unless an exact theorem match is documented.

The criterion

is primarily a projectability, semiconjugacy, or factor-dynamics condition. It is not specifically a theorem “on viability kernels.” Cite flow semiconjugacy or dynamical-systems sources, and separately cite viability sources for kernel transfer.

# 14. Recommended formal upgrades

## Upgrade 1: Define accepted-state operators explicitly

Introduce:

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{end}}
=
\{z:E_{\mathrm{end}}(z)\ne\varnothing\},
\]

\[
\mathcal V_w
=
\{z:E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\},
\]

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{typ}}
=
\{z:E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)\ne\varnothing\},
\]

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{weak}}
=
\bigcap_{w\in C}\mathcal V_w.
\]

Then the central result becomes:

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{typ}}
=
\{z:\bigcap_{w\in C}E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}
\subseteq
\bigcap_{w\in C}\{z:E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}
=
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{weak}}.
\]

This is cleaner than using \(P_{\mathrm{typ}}\) for accepted-state sets.

## Upgrade 2: State a general quantifier-separation proposition

Let \(\{E_\lambda(z)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\) be action sets. Then always

\[
\{z:\bigcap_{\lambda}E_\lambda(z)\ne\varnothing\}
\subseteq
\bigcap_{\lambda}\{z:E_\lambda(z)\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

Equality holds if and only if every family \(E_\lambda(z)\) has the finite-intersection property together with a compactness/closedness condition, or if the family is otherwise known to admit a common selector.

This would deepen the paper beyond the two-action witness. The witness then proves strictness.

## Upgrade 3: Add a price-family theorem

For a restricted price family \(W\subseteq C\), define

\[
\mathcal V_W
=
\bigcap_{w\in W}\mathcal V_w.
\]

Then:

\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{typ}}
\subseteq
\mathcal V_W
\subseteq
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{phys}},
\]

and if \(W_1\subseteq W_2\),

\[
\mathcal V_{W_2}\subseteq\mathcal V_{W_1}.
\]

This makes precise the claim that restricting prices enlarges the weak accepted set.

## Upgrade 4: Add stability of the gap under perturbations

The manuscript repeatedly calls the gap “structurally robust.” Nonempty interior proves topological robustness in the state variables only if the exact inequalities are strict. It does not prove robustness to perturbations of:

- dip depths;
- reset gains;
- bridge cost;
- dynamics;
- disturbance set;
- action trajectories.

Add a perturbation theorem. For example, if

\[
s_1+s_2\ge2+\delta,
\qquad
s_i\le2-\delta,
\qquad
x\le1-\delta,
\]

then the classification persists under parameter perturbations bounded by a specified function of \(\delta\).

## Upgrade 5: Separate exact, finite, and empirical status

Use a three-level table:

| Claim layer | What is proved |
|---|---|
| Symbolic theorem | Exact continuum inequalities for the declared witness |
| Machine artifact | Exact finite rational regression checks |
| Empirical implication | Conditional design requirement only |

This would eliminate the current machine-verification overclaim.

## Upgrade 6: Formalize rescue as an action synthesis problem

Instead of saying the recursion “names the binding resource,” define a resource-augmentation map:

\[
\mathsf{Aug}_r:
(x,s,A)\mapsto(x+r,s,A\cup\{\mathrm{STAGED}_r\}).
\]

Then prove the minimal rescue threshold:

\[
r^*
=
\inf\{r:\exists a\in A_r,\;a\in E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)\}.
\]

For the witness,

\[
r^*=1-x
\]

for STAGED, subject to \(x<1\). This would turn the policy implication into a real theorem rather than an interpretation.

## Upgrade 7: Formalize constructor supports

For each governance constructor \(\mathsf C\), record:

\[
\operatorname{supp}(\mathsf C),
\qquad
\mathsf C(S),
\qquad
\text{preserved components},
\qquad
\text{induced policy changes}.
\]

Then prove commutation or noncommutation results for constructor composition:

\[
\mathsf C_1\circ\mathsf C_2
\ne
\mathsf C_2\circ\mathsf C_1
\]

when, for example, observation changes the implementable policy class before versus after an action restriction.

# 17. Suggested revised abstract claim

The abstract’s central claim could be made more precise as follows:

> The paper proves a quantifier-separation result for a typed exact-tube transition datum. For each scalarization weight \(w\), an action may be selected that satisfies the aggregate floor, while no single action satisfies all typed floors simultaneously. Thus
> \[
> \bigcap_w\{z:E_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}
> \supsetneq
> \{z:\bigcap_wE_w(z)\ne\varnothing\}.

> On an explicit rational witness, the strict gap contains a relatively open state region. A separate resource-controlled action partitions the broader aggregate-versus-floor discrepancy region into a rescue set and a genuine impossibility set.

This says exactly what is mathematically established without overextending it to all weak and strong sustainability theories.
