## Quantifier Noncommutativity in Sustainability Assessment: A Formal Separation of Compensatory and Noncompensatory Transition Doctrines

### Abstract

Sustainability assessments aggregate heterogeneous capital floors into single indices, implicitly assuming that a deficit in one floor can be compensated by a surplus in another. This paper proves a quantifier-separation result for a typed exact-tube transition datum that shows when this compensation assumption fails dynamically. For each nonnegative scalarization weight $w$, an action may be selected that satisfies the aggregate floor $w \cdot s \geq 0$, while no single action satisfies all typed floors $s_i \geq 0$ simultaneously. Thus the per-weight accepted set strictly contains the common-plan accepted set:
$$\bigcap_{w \in C}\{z : E_w(z) \neq \varnothing\} \;\supsetneq\; \{z : \textstyle\bigcap_{w \in C} E_w(z) \neq \varnothing\}.$$
On an explicit rational witness, the strict gap contains a relatively open state region. A separate resource-controlled action partitions the broader aggregate-versus-floor discrepancy region into a rescue set and a genuine impossibility set. The continuum statements are established by displayed proofs; a committed exact-integer artifact regression-checks the finite rational instance. The paper defines the typed framework surrounding this result and states, at their actual status, the governance, intergenerational, and composition extensions it motivates.

**Keywords:** sustainability assessment; viability theory; weak and strong sustainability; scalarization; quantifier noncommutativity; compensatory aggregation

### 1. Introduction

#### 1.1 The problem

Two failure modes motivate this paper. The first is *commensurability drift*: assessments aggregate stocks, services, liabilities, and floors into single indices whose compensation principles are rarely stated as explicit mathematics. The second is *status drift*: conceptual frameworks state aspirations as theorems and conditional results circulate as unconditional ones. This paper addresses the first with a precise theorem and guards against the second by labeling every claim.

The weak-sustainability doctrine, as formalized in genuine-savings and inclusive-wealth frameworks (World Bank, 2011; Neumayer, 2013; Boos, 2015), treats capital forms as substitutable through prices. The strong-sustainability doctrine holds that certain floors are separately binding (Ekins et al., 2003). This debate is mature in the policy and multi-criteria-decision literatures (Cinelli et al., 2014; Schär et al., 2025; Hanley et al., 1999; Usubiaga-Liaño, 2025). What has received less attention is the *dynamic* question: during a sustainability transition, when can a compensatory aggregate certify a trajectory that a noncompensatory assessment rejects?

#### 1.2 The central result

The paper's organizing result is a quantifier noncommutation. Fix a typed transition datum. For each scalarization weight $w$ in the nonnegative cone $C$, let $E_w(z)$ be the set of actions admissible at state $z$ under the aggregate floor $w \cdot s \geq 0$. Two acceptance criteria differ by quantifier order:

- **Common-plan acceptance (noncompensatory):** there exists one action lying in every $E_w(z)$ —
  $$\exists a\;\forall w:\; a \in E_w(z).$$
- **Per-weight acceptance (compensatory):** for each weight there exists some admissible action, possibly depending on the weight —
  $$\forall w\;\exists a_w:\; a_w \in E_w(z).$$

The first implies the second; the converse fails. The failure is not a defect of any particular weight: it is the noncommutativity of the existential (action) and universal (weight) quantifiers. The paper proves the always-valid inclusion, gives a general proposition isolating the mechanism, and supplies an explicit exact-tube witness in which the gap has nonempty interior.

#### 1.3 Scope and contribution

**Claimed.** (i) A general action-set identity linking noncompensatory floors to scalarization over the full nonnegative cone (Theorem 2). (ii) A general quantifier-separation proposition (Proposition 1). (iii) A price-family monotonicity theorem (Theorem 3). (iv) An explicit rational witness with a relatively open region of strict separation, split into rescue and impossibility sets (Theorem 4). (v) Monotonicity of the witness hierarchy under hold-prefix extension (Theorem 5).

**Not claimed.** A universal hierarchy of all weak and strong sustainability doctrines; a proof that planetary boundaries ought to be noncompensatory; an empirical result about any specific resource system; or completion of the governance, intergenerational, and composition extensions, which are stated at their actual (partial) status in Sections 6–8.

To our knowledge this provides an explicit exact-tube witness for this dynamic separation in a compensatory/noncompensatory assessment setting; we do not assert priority over the elementary quantifier fact itself, which is standard.

### 2. The Typed Framework (Minimal Working Realization)

This section fixes the typed framework the theorem needs. It is a minimal realization, not a completed universal architecture.

#### 2.1 Types and physical state

Physical state is typed: a state variable denotes a moiety (a named conserved substance) with a unit, and typed fluxes connect typed stocks. Conservation claims are per-moiety; the framework does not authorize adding biomass, money, and biodiversity into one conserved scalar. Services, thresholds, information states, and institutional variables are separate types. No claim mixes types without a declared bridge.

#### 2.2 The canonical tuple

The canonical object is the tuple
$$S = (T, Z, S_{st}, B_{out}, V, \Gamma, O, A, C, R, D, K, P)$$
comprising a type system $T$; state space $Z$; stock–flux structure $S_{st}$; boundary interface $B_{out}$; constitutive laws $V$; service/technology correspondence $\Gamma$; observation operator $O$; assessment operator $A$; command architecture $C$; deployment/reset architecture $R$; disturbance class $D$; safe-and-just set $K$; and policy class $P$. **$P$ is reserved for the policy class throughout; accepted-state sets use a distinct notation (§4.1).** A model is a specified tuple; a claim is a statement about a tuple with a status; an application is a tuple plus data.

#### 2.3 Uncertainty levels and the quantifier discipline

Uncertainty is stratified into four declared levels — parameter, observation/assessment, process disturbance, and structural model uncertainty — each with a fixed quantifier discipline. Every application poses its safety question in one of three forms: actual-policy safety (specified $(\pi_0, d_0)$ stays in $K$); viability (some $\pi \in P$ stays in $K$); robust viability (one causal policy works for every $d \in D$). The order is fixed: $\exists \pi\,\forall d$, not $\forall d\,\exists \pi$. Section 4 studies what happens when a second quantifier — over scalarization weights — interleaves with this one.

#### 2.4 Model maps

Cross-model claims are licensed by four declared maps — specialisation, exact projection (semiconjugacy), approximation (declared residual), singular reduction — which are not interchangeable. This paper needs only the taxonomy and the discipline it enforces.

#### 2.5 Claim-status discipline

Every statement carries a status: axiom/definition, identity, theorem, conditional theorem, conjecture, or counterexample. Two rules govern: no promotion (a conditional theorem is never stated as a theorem), and no silent transfer (a status proved for one model class does not transfer without a declared map).

### 3. The Assessment Operators

#### 3.1 Four operators, correctly separated

The earlier draft conflated an endpoint-only operator with an exact-tube operator. We define them separately. Fix a typed exact-tube datum with transition-safe sets and destination set. For a state $z$ and action $a$, let $\mathrm{Tube}(a,d)$ be the full set of states visited during the interval under disturbance $d$, let $\mathrm{End}(a,d)$ be the endpoint values visited, and let $\mathrm{Succ}(a,d)$ be the successor set after any endpoint reset.

The **noncompensatory typed** operator (each floor separately binding):
$$E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z) = \{a : \forall d,\; \mathrm{Tube}(a,d) \subseteq S \text{ and } \mathrm{Succ}(a,d) \subseteq G\},$$
where $S = S^{\mathrm{phys}} \cap \{s_i \geq 0,\; i=1..n\}$ and $G = G^{\mathrm{phys}} \cap \{s \geq 0\}$.

The **scalarized aggregate** operator at weight $w \in C$:
$$E_w(z) = \{a : \forall d,\; \mathrm{Tube}(a,d) \subseteq S^w \text{ and } \mathrm{Succ}(a,d) \subseteq G^w\},$$
where $S^w = S^{\mathrm{phys}} \cap \{w \cdot s \geq 0\}$ and $G^w = G^{\mathrm{phys}} \cap \{w \cdot s \geq 0\}$.

The **exact-tube physical** operator:
$$E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}(z) = \{a : \forall d,\; \mathrm{Tube}(a,d) \subseteq S^{\mathrm{phys}} \text{ and } \mathrm{Succ}(a,d) \subseteq G^{\mathrm{phys}}\}.$$

The **endpoint-only physical** operator:
$$E_{\mathrm{end}}(z) = \{a : \forall d,\; \mathrm{End}(a,d) \subseteq S^{\mathrm{phys}} \text{ and } \mathrm{Succ}(a,d) \subseteq G^{\mathrm{phys}}\}.$$

All four retain the same disturbance quantifier $\forall d$; they differ only in constraint structure. Because $\mathrm{End}(a,d) \subseteq \mathrm{Tube}(a,d)$, the endpoint operator is the weakest. The chain is
$$E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z) \;\subseteq\; E_w(z) \;\subseteq\; E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}(z) \;\subseteq\; E_{\mathrm{end}}(z),$$
with the last inclusion generally strict, though it collapses in the witness of §4 because the witness's physical constraint touches only the monotone reserve stock $x$.

#### 3.2 Typed failure classes

An assessment verdict must name the failure class it establishes. The taxonomy (definitional, not predictive): material inconsistency; physical infeasibility; epistemic/common-prescription infeasibility; authority infeasibility; implementation infeasibility; temporal infeasibility; recovery failure; architecture-transition failure; model-credibility failure; normative incompatibility. The rule is to identify the earliest discharged obstruction rather than relabel an institutional failure as physical impossibility.

### 4. The Separation Theorem

This is the paper's citable core. Proofs are complete; the machine artifact is a finite regression check, not a proof of the continuum statements (§4.8).

#### 4.1 Accepted-state operators (Upgrade 1)

To avoid the collision between $P$ (policy class) and accepted-state sets, define for any operator $E$:
$$\mathcal{V}[E] \;=\; \{z : E(z) \neq \varnothing\}.$$
Thus $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} = \mathcal{V}[E_{\mathrm{typ}}]$, $\mathcal{V}_w = \mathcal{V}[E_w]$, $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}} = \mathcal{V}[E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}]$, $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{end}} = \mathcal{V}[E_{\mathrm{end}}]$, and the compensatory accepted set
$$\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}} \;=\; \bigcap_{w \in C} \mathcal{V}_w.$$
With this notation the central noncommutation is visually explicit:
$$\underbrace{\{z : \textstyle\bigcap_w E_w(z) \neq \varnothing\}}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}\;\text{(common plan)}} \;\subseteq\; \underbrace{\bigcap_w \{z : E_w(z) \neq \varnothing\}}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}\;\text{(per weight)}}.$$

#### 4.2 A general quantifier-separation proposition (Upgrade 2)

**Proposition 1.** Let $\{E_\lambda(z)\}_{\lambda \in \Lambda}$ be a family of action sets. Then always
$$\{z : \textstyle\bigcap_{\lambda} E_\lambda(z) \neq \varnothing\} \;\subseteq\; \bigcap_{\lambda} \{z : E_\lambda(z) \neq \varnothing\}.$$
Equality holds if and only if, for every $z$ in the right-hand set, the family $\{E_\lambda(z)\}_\lambda$ admits a common selector — equivalently, the family has the finite-intersection property together with a compactness/closedness condition guaranteeing $\bigcap_\lambda E_\lambda(z) \neq \varnothing$.

*Proof.* If $a \in \bigcap_\lambda E_\lambda(z)$, then each $E_\lambda(z)$ is nonempty, so $z$ lies in the right-hand set. For the equality condition: the inclusion fails at $z$ precisely when each $E_\lambda(z)$ is nonempty but $\bigcap_\lambda E_\lambda(z) = \varnothing$, i.e. when no common selector exists. $\square$

Proposition 1 isolates the mechanism: the witness of §4 proves the inclusion is strict by exhibiting a $z$ where the nonemptiness witnesses differ by $\lambda$.

#### 4.3 The closed-cone identity and the price-family theorem

**Lemma 1 (closed-cone pointwise equivalence).** For $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$: $v \geq 0$ componentwise $\iff$ $w \cdot v \geq 0$ for every $w \in C = \mathbb{R}^n_+ \setminus \{0\}$.

*Proof.* ($\Rightarrow$) $w \geq 0$, $w \neq 0$, $v \geq 0$ gives $w \cdot v = \sum_i w_i v_i \geq 0$. ($\Leftarrow$) If $v_k < 0$, take $w = e_k \in C$: $w \cdot v = v_k < 0$. $\square$

This lemma says that at a *fixed* trajectory the full-cone aggregate is lossless. The separation is therefore entirely dynamic (quantifier-order), not static scalarization blindness.

**Theorem 2 (assessment identity and hierarchy).**

*(i) Hierarchy.* For every $w \in C$: $E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z) \subseteq E_w(z) \subseteq E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}(z) \subseteq E_{\mathrm{end}}(z)$.

*(ii) Localization.* For every $z$: $E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z) = \bigcap_{w \in C} E_w(z)$. Hence $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} = \{z : \bigcap_w E_w(z) \neq \varnothing\}$.

*Proof.* (i) Let $a \in E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)$. For every $d$, $\mathrm{Tube}(a,d) \subseteq S = S^{\mathrm{phys}} \cap \{s \geq 0\}$, and by Lemma 1 every tube point satisfies $w \cdot s \geq 0$ for every $w$, so $a \in E_w(z)$. The remaining inclusions follow from $S^w \subseteq S^{\mathrm{phys}}$, $S^{\mathrm{phys}} \cap \{\cdot\} \subseteq S^{\mathrm{phys}}$, and $\mathrm{End} \subseteq \mathrm{Tube}$. (ii) $\subseteq$ by (i). $\supseteq$: if $a \in \bigcap_w E_w(z)$, then for every $d$ and tube point $p$, every $w$ gives $w \cdot s(p) \geq 0$, so $s(p) \geq 0$ by Lemma 1; hence $\mathrm{Tube} \subseteq S$, and similarly $\mathrm{Succ} \subseteq G$. $\square$

Theorem 2(i) is constraint-set monotonicity of viability/reachability sets (Aubin, 1991; Frankowska, 1989) applied to nested safe sets; the claim of this paper is (ii) and the witnessed strictness.

**Theorem 3 (price-family monotonicity, Upgrade 3).** For a weight family $W \subseteq C$ define $\mathcal{V}_W = \bigcap_{w \in W} \mathcal{V}_w$. Then
$$\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{V}_W \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}},$$
and if $W_1 \subseteq W_2$ then $\mathcal{V}_{W_2} \subseteq \mathcal{V}_{W_1}$.

*Proof.* The second inclusion is Theorem 2(i) intersected over $W$. The monotonicity is because intersecting over more sets can only shrink the result. The first inclusion: $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} = \{z : \bigcap_{w \in C} E_w(z) \neq \varnothing\} \subseteq \{z : \bigcap_{w \in W} E_w(z) \neq \varnothing\}$, and the latter contains $\bigcap_{w \in W} \mathcal{V}_w$ by Proposition 1 applied to the subfamily $W$… — **correction:** by Proposition 1, $\{z : \bigcap_{w \in W} E_w(z) \neq \varnothing\} \subseteq \mathcal{V}_W$, so the inclusion $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} \subseteq \mathcal{V}_W$ follows since $\bigcap_{w \in C} E_w \subseteq \bigcap_{w \in W} E_w$ makes the common-plan set over $C$ a subset of the common-plan set over $W$, which is a subset of $\mathcal{V}_W$. $\square$

Theorem 3 makes precise the claim that *restricting* the price family *enlarges* the compensatory accepted set, so the full cone is the strictest compensatory reading.

#### 4.4 The witness datum

Two architectures — extraction $q=0$, regenerative $q=1$ — one review interval $[0,1]$, phase state $(q,x,s_1,s_2)$: a physical reserve stock $x$ and two typed floors $s_1$ (protected-group service surplus) and $s_2$ (remediation-liability coverage), floors normalized to 0. $S_0 = \{x \geq 0, s_1 \geq 0, s_2 \geq 0\}$; $G = \{(1,x,s) : x \geq 0, s \geq 0\}$ (destination maintainability witnessed by the destination hold policy, a declared datum). Disturbance set $\{\beta, \alpha\}$ scales dip depth (worst-case dip 2); destination reset gains $e = (1/4, 1/4)$; rescue cost $c = 1$. The four meta-actions from any initial $(0,x,s)$ with $x \geq 0, s \geq 0$:

| Action | Within-interval trajectory | Successor |
|---|---|---|
| NO-SWITCH | state constant | $\{(0,x,s)\}$ — misses $G$ |
| FAST | $s_1$ dips to $s_1 - 2$ mid-interval (adverse); $s_2, x$ constant | $\{(1,x,s+e)\}$ |
| SLOW | $s_2$ dips to $s_2 - 2$ mid-interval; $s_1, x$ constant | $\{(1,x,s+e)\}$ |
| STAGED | floors grow; $x$ spends linearly to $x-1$ | $\{(1,x-1,s+e)\}$ |

Every trajectory is piecewise linear on breakpoints $\{0, \tfrac12, 1\}$ and monotone per piece, so all tubes are exact visited sets — no outer approximation.

#### 4.5 Theorem 4 (witnessed separation, corrected set definitions)

On the witness datum, over initial states $X_0 = \{(0,x,s) : x \geq 0, s \geq 0\}$:

**(1)** $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} = \{x \geq 1\} \cup \{s_1 \geq 2\} \cup \{s_2 \geq 2\}$.

**(2)** $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}} = \bigcap_{w \in C} \mathcal{V}_w = \{x \geq 1\} \cup \{s_1 + s_2 \geq 2\}$.

**(3)** $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}} = \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{end}} = X_0$ (in this witness the physical constraint touches only the monotone $x$, so tube and endpoint coincide).

Define three sets, distinguished precisely:
- $\mathrm{FP}_0 = \{s_1 < 2,\; s_2 < 2,\; s_1 + s_2 \geq 2\}$ — the aggregate-versus-direct-floor discrepancy region, $x$ unrestricted. This is **not** entirely a false positive, because for $x \geq 1$ STAGED is typed-admissible.
- $R = \mathrm{FP}_0 \cap \{x \geq 1\}$ — the **rescue set**: aggregate-feasible and typed-transformable (witnessed by STAGED).
- $I = \mathrm{FP}_0 \cap \{x < 1\}$ — the **impossibility region**.
- $\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}} = \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}} \setminus \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}$ — the genuine compensatory-versus-noncompensatory false-positive set.

**(4)** $\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}} = I$. The genuine false-positive region is exactly the impossibility region; it is nonempty with relatively open interior $\{0 < s_1, s_2 < 2,\; s_1 + s_2 > 2,\; x < 1\}$.

**(5)** Both hierarchy inclusions are strict: every point of $I$ lies in $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}} \setminus \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}}$, and the point $(x,s_1,s_2) = (\tfrac12, \tfrac1{10}, \tfrac1{10})$ lies in $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}} \setminus \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}$.

**(6) Per-weight plan disagreement.** On the relative interior of $\mathrm{FP}_0$ (where $s_2 > 0$ and $s_2 < 2$), the FAST-certifying weight ratios are exactly $\{r = w_2/w_1 \geq \rho_1\}$ and the SLOW-certifying ratios exactly $\{r \leq \rho_2\}$, with
$$\rho_1 = \frac{2 - s_1}{s_2}, \qquad \rho_2 = \frac{s_1}{2 - s_2}, \qquad \rho_2 \geq \rho_1 \iff s_1 + s_2 \geq 2.$$
High-$s_2$-weight assessors ($r > \rho_2$) license FAST only; low-$s_2$-weight assessors ($r < \rho_1$) license SLOW only; intermediate assessors license both; and **no single action serves every weight**, i.e. $\bigcap_w E_w = E_{\mathrm{typ}} = \varnothing$ there (Theorem 2(ii)). *Boundary convention:* the formulas for $\rho_1, \rho_2$ require $s_2 > 0$ and $s_2 < 2$; on the boundary faces ($s_2 = 0$, $s_2 = 2$, $s_1 = 0$, $s_1 = 2$, $s_1 + s_2 = 2$) the classification is stated separately and reduces to the closed-set identities of (1)–(2).

**(7) The rescue split.** $R$ is typed-transformable, witnessed by STAGED (bridging at physical cost $c$, keeping both floors intact, landing in $G$). $I$ is aggregate-feasible for every cone weight yet admits **no** typed-admissible action, with four exhibited violations: FAST violates the $s_1$ floor under the adverse disturbance; SLOW violates the $s_2$ floor; STAGED drives $x$ negative; NO-SWITCH misses $G$.

*Proof sketch.* (1) NO-SWITCH fails $G$; FAST's worst-case $s_1$-tube is $[s_1-2, s_1]$, safe iff $s_1 \geq 2$; SLOW symmetrically; STAGED's $x$-tube is $[x-1,x]$, safe iff $x \geq 1$. (2) For $x \geq 1$, STAGED is aggregate-safe for every $w$. For $s_1 + s_2 \geq 2$ with $s_1, s_2 < 2$: FAST is aggregate-safe iff $r \geq \rho_1$; SLOW iff $r \leq \rho_2$; and $\rho_2 \geq \rho_1 \iff s_1 + s_2 \geq 2$, so every $r \in [0,\infty]$ is covered. Conversely if $x < 1$ and $s_1 + s_2 < 2$, at $w = (1,1)$ no action is aggregate-safe. (4) $\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{agg}} = \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}} \setminus \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} = [\{x \geq 1\} \cup \{s_1+s_2 \geq 2\}] \setminus [\{x \geq 1\} \cup \{s_1 \geq 2\} \cup \{s_2 \geq 2\}] = \{x < 1, s_1 < 2, s_2 < 2, s_1+s_2 \geq 2\} = I$. (7) STAGED's tubes on $R$ stay in $S$ and land in $G$; on $I$ the four exhibited violations exhaust the action set. $\square$

**Remark.** The compensatory assessment's binding condition is the total budget $s_1 + s_2 \geq 2$; the noncompensatory assessment requires one floor to survive its own worst-case dip or the bridge to be funded. The region between them is where the compensatory doctrine certifies — per weight, with weight-dependent plans — what the noncompensatory doctrine rejects.

#### 4.7 Theorem 5 (propagation, corrected scope)

Extend the datum to $m \geq 2$ intervals by prepending hold intervals (sole action HOLD: constant tube $\{z\}$, successor $\{z\}$, safe set $S_0$; the last interval carries the witness). Define each assessment's backward recursion with its own terminal set. Assume: HOLD is available at every prefix state; the hold tube is exactly $\{z\}$; the prefix safe sets contain the witness states; terminal and successor architecture labels are unchanged; and no additional reset or disturbance branches are introduced.

*(i) Hierarchy.* For every stage $j$: $\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{typ}}_j \subseteq \bigcap_{w \in C} \mathcal{V}^w_j \subseteq \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{phys}}_j$. This inclusion holds for every multi-interval typed exact-tube datum (it is constraint monotonicity under backward induction).

*(ii) Persistence of strictness.* The stage-0 regions are the witness regions pulled back through the holds, so both strictness witnesses persist **under this hold-prefix extension**.

*(iii)* The displayed separation is not an artifact of the one-interval framing **for the constructed hold-prefixed datum**. Strictness for arbitrary multi-stage systems does not follow without a separate persistence theorem (a later stage can erase the gap if every aggregate-feasible state has a common typed-safe continuation, if weak and typed terminal sets coincide on the reachable subset, or if actions couple across stages).

*Proof.* (i) Downward induction; base $G \subseteq G^w \subseteq G^{\mathrm{phys}}$ by Lemma 1; step by Theorem 2(i) and the induction hypothesis. (ii) HOLD is the unique action, admissible iff $z$ lies in the safe set and the next-stage accepted set. $\square$

## 5. Interpretation

### 5.1 The doctrinal reading, with corrected terminology

The scalarized operators $\{E_w\}_{w \in C}$ formalize **one** compensatory assessment doctrine: a single aggregate index $w \cdot s$, nonnegative scalarization weights on capital forms, substitution across floors permitted at those weights, disturbances respected. We refer to this as *the scalarized aggregate doctrine as formalized here*, not as "weak sustainability" simpliciter. The weak-sustainability literature is broader than this operator: it encompasses aggregate wealth, constant total capital, nondeclining comprehensive consumption, substitutability assumptions, discounting, shadow prices, and intertemporal investment conditions (Neumayer, 2013; World Bank, 2011; Boos, 2015). Likewise, strong sustainability is not always equivalent to requiring every stock coordinate to remain nonnegative at every instant; critical-natural-capital frameworks may use thresholds, safe operating spaces, irreversibility, resilience, or minimum-service conditions (Ekins et al., 2003). The typed operator $E_{\mathrm{typ}}$ is a formal idealization of the separately-binding-floor reading.

Within this precise scope, Theorem 4 reads: **the two formalized doctrines can disagree on the same transition datum with the same robustness standard and the same action set, in the direction compensatory-accepts / noncompensatory-rejects, on a relatively open region — and the disagreement is not an artifact of one bad weight: every weight accepts, each licensing a different physical transition.** The plans are genuinely different transitions (FAST and SLOW violate different floors at different times), which is the dynamic formalization of substitution across floors. By Theorem 2(ii), the disagreement's precise seat is the noncommutativity of "choose an action" with "for all weights." At the static level the closed-cone aggregate is lossless (Lemma 1), so the compensatory doctrine's blind spot is not the existence of an aggregate index but the *policy dependence* of the aggregate-feasible transition.

Throughout the theorem we use "nonnegative scalarization weights" for elements of $C$. We reserve the word "prices" for the interpretive discussion, because actual prices may be strictly positive, endogenous, dynamically determined, state-dependent, or dimensionally heterogeneous, and the theorem does not require any of those properties.

### 5.2 Positioning against established theory

**Conceded as established.** The backward recursion of §3 is a typed instance of established robust-predecessor, reach-avoid, capture-basin, and hybrid-reachability constructions (Aubin, 1991; Aubin, Bayen, and Saint-Pierre, 2011; Saint-Pierre, 1994; Lygeros, Tomlin, and Sastry, 1999). Theorem 2(i) is constraint-set monotonicity of viability kernels and reachability sets (Aubin, 1991; Frankowska, 1989). Static scalarization limitations are established: weighted sums cannot reach nonconvex parts of Pareto fronts (Das and Dennis, 1997) — a different mechanism (frontier geometry under a single optimization) from the present action-quantifier mechanism. Compensability analysis is established in multi-criteria decision analysis, including the explicit mapping of compensatory aggregation to weak sustainability and outranking methods to strong sustainability (Cinelli et al., 2014; Schär et al., 2025). Scalarization-dependent optimal policies are a staple of multi-objective optimization and reinforcement learning.

**Claimed.** (i) The action-set identity $E_{\mathrm{typ}} = \bigcap_{w \in C} E_w$ with the closed-cone choice isolating the separation as purely dynamic (Theorem 2(ii)). (ii) The general quantifier-separation proposition (Proposition 1). (iii) The price-family monotonicity theorem (Theorem 3). (iv) The explicit exact-tube witness with a relatively open region of strict separation and the rescue/impossibility split (Theorem 4). (v) Monotonicity of the witness hierarchy under hold-prefix extension (Theorem 5).

**Novelty qualification.** To our knowledge, this paper provides an explicit exact-tube witness for this dynamic separation in a compensatory/noncompensatory assessment setting. We do not assert priority over the elementary quantifier fact $\exists a\,\forall w \neq \forall w\,\exists a_w$, which is standard. Whether an equivalent dynamic separation appears in adjacent literatures (multi-objective robust control, viability theory, MCDA) is a bounded absence in our review, not an established universal negative.

### 5.3 What the theorem does not say

- **No aggregate blindness at fixed trajectories.** Lemma 1 establishes the opposite: at a fixed trajectory, the full-cone aggregate is lossless.
- **No separation on every datum.** Where a single action is safe for all weights, the assessments coincide. The theorem is an existence separation with a relatively open region, plus the always-valid hierarchy and localization.
- **No infinite-horizon, stochastic, partial-observation, or endogenous-event extension.**
- **No claim that the full nonnegative cone is the only reasonable weight family.** Theorem 3 shows restricting the family enlarges the compensatory accepted set, so the full cone is the strictest compensatory reading.
- **No welfare claim about prices.** The weights model an assessment doctrine, not normative endorsement.
- **No transfer to empirical systems.** The theorem is about assessment operators on a declared datum. It asserts nothing about any fishery or aquifer.
- **No universal doctrinal ranking.** The set inclusion $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} \subseteq \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}} \subseteq \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ is a theorem about the defined operators under common action and disturbance classes, common safe-set inclusion, common horizon, common tube semantics, and common terminal condition. It does not establish a universal ranking of all endpoint, weak, and strong sustainability doctrines.

### 5.4 Relation to Planetary Boundaries (corrected)

The typed operator $E_{\mathrm{typ}}$ provides a **formal idealization** of the separate-boundary reading underlying the Planetary Boundaries framework (Rockström et al., 2009; Steffen et al., 2015), which is organized around **nine boundary processes in the current standard formulation**, with additional proposed indicators and extensions in the wider literature. (We do not assert a ten-boundary formulation; atmospheric oxygen is not ordinarily presented as one of the standard nine.)

The framework's operational logic is reasonably read as treating each boundary separately, but planetary-boundary assessments also involve uncertainty, control variables, regional disaggregation, interactions, justice considerations, aggregation of risk, and boundary-transgression severity. We therefore state only that the typed operator provides a formal model of the separate-boundary reading, not that it captures the framework's full operational logic.

**What the theorem does and does not contribute.** Theorem 4 shows that a particular compensatory assessment can accept a transition rejected by a typed assessment. It provides **a formal model of one reason a noncompensatory stance may be adopted** — namely, that per-weight compensation can license weight-dependent plans no single one of which respects all floors. It does **not** prove that planetary boundaries ought to be noncompensatory; that is a normative and scientific-design question. Nor does the witness's rescue mechanism generalize automatically: the witness has an explicit STAGED action funded by a scalar stock $x$, whereas many boundary violations have no single identifiable bridging resource. The rescue implication is conditional on the presence of a typed rescue action controlled by a resource margin.

### 5.5 Policy implications (qualified)

The separation theorem has three implications, each stated at its actual strength.

**First.** Aggregate indices alone cannot certify noncompensatory transition safety under the present assessment semantics. A scalarized assessment can certify its own criterion (aggregate feasibility); what it cannot certify is typed safety unless an additional bridge theorem is supplied. This follows from Theorem 4: the compensatory accepted set strictly contains the noncompensatory accepted set, and the excess region is where the two criteria diverge.

**Second.** When the typed recursion identifies an admissible rescue action whose feasibility is controlled by a resource margin, investment in that margin is a candidate remedy; reweighting alone cannot substitute for it within the witness semantics. In the witness, STAGED is funded by $x$ at cost $c=1$, and the minimal rescue threshold is $r^* = 1 - x$ for $x < 1$ (see Upgrade 6, §5.6). This is a theorem about the witness, not a universal policy law: it applies where a resource-controlled rescue action exists.

**Third.** Per-floor reporting alongside aggregate reporting is necessary for detecting this class of discrepancy. It is not universally necessary for every sustainability judgment, but where the question is whether a transition respects separately-binding floors, an aggregate index alone cannot distinguish the rescue region from the impossibility region. A minimum report for such an assessment should include: (1) typed floors and provenance; (2) aggregate weights or weight family; (3) policy quantifiers; (4) disturbance set; (5) observation and implementation model; (6) exact versus conservative tube status; (7) terminal maintainability; (8) action-exhaustion status; (9) uncertainty and approximation bounds; (10) normative premises.

### 5.6 Upgrade 6: rescue as action synthesis, and data requirements

**Rescue as an action-synthesis theorem.** Rather than saying the recursion "names the binding resource," define a resource-augmentation map
$$\mathsf{Aug}_r : (x, s, A) \mapsto (x + r,\; s,\; A \cup \{\mathrm{STAGED}_r\})$$
and the minimal rescue threshold
$$r^* = \inf\{\, r : \exists a \in A_r,\; a \in E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z)\,\}.$$
On the witness, for the STAGED action and $x < 1$, $r^* = 1 - x$: the exact resource increment that converts an impossibility-region state into a typed-transformable one. This turns the second policy implication from an interpretation into a theorem.

**Data requirements for empirical application.** The theorem is a result about assessment operators on a declared datum. Empirical application requires, in addition to the typed floors, disturbance set, action set, tube model, and destination maintainability witness named in Part 1 §4.4, the following load-bearing data:

1. **Action-set completeness status.** A negative certificate over a finite action set proves impossibility only relative to that set. The application must state whether $A$ is exhaustive, a registered policy menu, a sampled subset, or an inner approximation. If incomplete, the verdict is "no safe transition exists among the registered actions," not "no safe transition exists."
2. **Calibration and identifiability data.** Parameter estimates, uncertainty sets, structural alternatives, validation data, observation error, model discrepancy, missing-data treatment, disturbance dependence. A declared $D$ is necessary but not sufficient; it must be justified or the verdict marked conditional on model credibility.
3. **Policy and authority data.** Who may select each action, what information they possess, decision timing, enforcement assumptions, compliance uncertainty, strategic responses, resource and legitimacy constraints.
4. **Threshold-uncertainty semantics.** A physical or normative floor is rarely known exactly. Distinguish the deterministic floor $s_i(z) \geq 0$ from robust threshold safety $s_i(z;\theta) \geq 0$ for all $\theta \in \Theta$, and from probabilistic or confidence-level versions if admitted.
5. **Destination maintainability status.** Whether the destination maintainability witness is physical, simulated, or merely declared.

## 6. Framework Extension: Governance, Authority, and Implementation

> **Status declaration.** This section is a **framework extension**, not completed mathematics. The constructor algebra, implementability ladder, and obstruction results are stated at their actual (partial) status. Where a proof is summarized from a source rather than displayed, the status is marked. The separation theorem of §4 does not depend on this section.

### 6.1 Constructors with declared support (corrected)

Governance instruments enter the framework as **constructors**: maps on the system data. The earlier draft's claim that a constructor "changes exactly one named component" is too strong: a harvest cap can alter the action correspondence, the dynamics, and the enforcement model; a tax can alter the effort law, the policy class, and the equilibrium; an observation reform can alter both the observation operator and the feedback law. We replace that claim with **declared support**.

Each primitive constructor $\mathsf{C}$ carries a declared support
$$\operatorname{supp}(\mathsf{C}) \;\subseteq\; \{T, Z, S_{st}, B_{out}, V, \Gamma, O, A, C, R, D, K, P\},$$
the set of tuple components it may alter. Any induced change outside the declared support must be represented explicitly or prohibited. This makes constructor composition auditable: for constructors $\mathsf{C}_1, \mathsf{C}_2$, one may prove commutation or noncommutation
$$\mathsf{C}_1 \circ \mathsf{C}_2 \;\neq\; \mathsf{C}_2 \circ \mathsf{C}_1$$
when, for example, an observation change alters the implementable policy class before versus after an action restriction.

The primitive constructors: $\mathsf{Cap}(Q)$ (upper harvest bound), $\mathsf{Floor}(H_{\min})$ (output floor), $\mathsf{Tax}(\tau)$ (price entry in the effort law), $\mathsf{Excl}$ (excluding a competing predator or agent), $\mathsf{Leak}(h)$ (unreported/illegal harvest), $\mathsf{Obs}(I, \Psi)$ (replacing the observation map and feedback law), $\mathsf{Rest}(\cdot)$ (any other restriction of the control correspondence). Each must declare its support.

**Action-set monotonicity.** Restriction of the action correspondence obeys a one-sided monotonicity: $U_1(x) \subseteq U_2(x)$ for every $x$ implies $\mathrm{Viab}(V; U_1, F) \subseteq \mathrm{Viab}(V; U_2, F)$ for existential viability under the same dynamics and information structure. The family of correspondences with nonempty existential viability kernel is upward closed and closed under unions; intersections are not generally preserved. This monotonicity is about the **action correspondence**. It is separate from monotonicity of the **policy class**, which also depends on information, timing, authority, strategic equilibrium, observability, and implementation mechanisms.

**The implementability ladder.** The action correspondence is layered:
$$U_{\mathrm{impl}}(z) \;\subseteq\; U_{\mathrm{inst}}(z) \;\subseteq\; U_{\mathrm{tech}}(z) \;\subseteq\; U_{\mathrm{theor}}(z),$$
with the parallel policy-class ladder $P_{\mathrm{impl}} \subseteq P_{\mathrm{inst}} \subseteq P_{\mathrm{tech}} \subseteq P_{\mathrm{theor}}$. Within-architecture viability quantifies over the implementable class; a viability result over a technological or theoretical class does not transfer downward, because restriction of the correspondence can empty the kernel even where enlargement preserves it.

### 6.2 Management vocabularies as representations (corrected)

The earlier draft claimed management vocabularies are "rewrites, not theorems" and "mathematically inert." That is too strong. A tax is not merely a price entry if it changes incentives, equilibrium controls, compliance, enforcement, revenue and redistribution, or the disturbance law; a predator exclusion can alter trophic dynamics; an observation reform can change the admissible policy class. We reframe: **some instruments admit a canonical representation by primitive constructors; the representation is valid only after proving the induced component changes.** A total allowable catch is representable as $\mathsf{Cap}$; a harvest control rule as $\mathsf{Obs}$; a landing subsidy as $\mathsf{Tax}(-\sigma)$; unreported harvest as $\mathsf{Leak}$; a closed season as a periodic $\mathsf{Cap}$. Each representation is valid conditional on its declared support being discharged.

### 6.3 The commons obstruction (boundary-exit corrected)

The safe aggregate-harvest capacity is declared through the tangent requirement $g(S) - H \geq 0$. The obstruction is a finite-time result, stated with the boundary-exit correction.

**Theorem (Commons Obstruction, corrected).** Suppose a scalar stock $S$ with dynamics $\dot S = g(S) - H$, and that under the implemented Nash feedback there exist $a > 0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ such that
$$g(S) - H^{\mathrm{Nash}}(S) \;\leq\; -\varepsilon \qquad \text{for every } S \in [S_{\min},\, S_{\min} + a],$$
and every relevant trajectory enters this strip. Then every trajectory entering the strip reaches $S_{\min}$ within at most $a/\varepsilon$ time. **If the same strict negative-drift condition persists at the boundary and solutions are forward complete, the trajectory subsequently exits below $S_{\min}$.** Reaching the boundary is not identical to exiting below it.

*Status.* Summarized from the source's proof; the uniform margin $\varepsilon > 0$ is load-bearing (strictly negative drift without a uniform margin does not by itself imply finite-time exit). The theorem requires a defined scalar stock dynamics and an explicit quantifier over Nash trajectories.

**Monitoring is not automatic.** The earlier draft stated that "monitoring bounds the observation error so state feedback applies." This is not automatic: observation error $\|\hat x - x\| \leq \delta$ does not by itself make a desired feedback law implementable. One needs a robust-feedback theorem certifying a control law whose robust admissibility holds for all states in the observation fibre. Otherwise monitoring may improve knowledge without producing a safe common prescription.

## 7. Framework Extension: Intergenerational Structures

> **Status declaration.** This section is a **framework extension**. The nested-impossibility result is stated with its proof conditions made explicit. The "every finite segment satisfiable" phenomenon requires a separate example and is not implied by the theorem as stated.

### 7.1 Generation structure

A generation structure is a sequence $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \cdots \to \infty$ of generation boundaries with closed per-generation constraint sets $V^{(k)}$; intergenerational viability requires $x(t) \in V^{(k)}$ for $t \in [t_k, t_{k+1})$.

### 7.2 The stationary equivalence (with conditions)

If $V^{(k)} = V$ for all $k$, intergenerational viability reduces to ordinary viability. **Conditions.** This immediate equivalence holds only if: the generation intervals cover the same infinite horizon; the policy class is unchanged across generations; there are no resets or inherited variables; $V^{(k)} = V$ exactly; and the intergenerational criterion is simply $x(t) \in V^{(k)}$. If inheritance, reset, age structure, or generation-specific policy restrictions exist, stationary set equality alone may not reduce the model to ordinary viability.

### 7.3 The nested-impossibility theorem (ω-limit argument made explicit)

**Theorem (Nested Impossibility).** Assume (i) trajectories are forward complete; (ii) every trajectory is confined to one common compact set $K$; (iii) each $V^{(k)}$ is closed; and (iv) the generation sets are nested decreasing with empty intersection, $\bigcap_k V^{(k)} = \varnothing$. Then no intergenerationally viable path exists.

*Proof.* Suppose a viable path $x(t)$ exists with $x(t) \in V^{(k)}$ for all $t \geq t_k$. Since the trajectory is confined to the compact set $K$ and is forward complete, it has an $\omega$-limit point $x^*$. Because each $V^{(k)}$ is closed and the trajectory lies in $V^{(k)}$ for all sufficiently large $t$, $x^* \in V^{(k)}$ for every $k$. Hence $x^* \in \bigcap_k V^{(k)}$, contradicting emptiness. $\square$

**What the theorem does not establish.** It proves no infinite path exists when the intersection is empty. It does **not** prove that every finite horizon is satisfiable. The phenomenon
$$\mathrm{Viab}_N \neq \varnothing \;\; \forall N, \qquad \mathrm{Viab}_\infty = \varnothing$$
requires a separate explicit example — for instance $V^{(k)} = [0, 1/k]$ with dynamics and initial conditions making every finite prefix feasible but no point belonging to all sets. Until such an example is supplied, the claim is only the infinite-horizon impossibility.

## 12. Limitations (corrected)

(i) The assessment-separation theorem is an existence result with a relatively open region, not a claim that the gap is always large; on dominated-action data the assessments coincide.

(ii) Its novelty verdicts at no-match-found are bounded-search absences; if external review overturns a verdict, the claim is weakened to the supported subset.

(iii) The framework covers no infinite horizons, no partial observation, no stochastic chance constraints, and no endogenous event times at the transformation-operator level.

(iv) The paper asserts nothing empirical; the conjecture set of §11 is declared at non-executed conjecture and design status.

(v) The framework extensions of Sections 6–8 are stated at partial / conditional status and are not completed mathematics.

(vi) **Computational tractability (corrected).** On a finite explicit state-action-disturbance graph with constant-time predicate evaluation, the backward recursion is polynomial in the graph size and horizon. For continuous, hybrid, or belief-state models, the graph itself may be exponentially large, infinite, or only approximately representable; exact tube inclusion may be computationally hard or undecidable; reachability can be exponential in dimension; hybrid mode combinations can produce exponential growth; partial observation introduces belief-state dimension; and robust quantifiers can require quantified real arithmetic or differential games. Grid cardinality grows as $N_{\mathrm{grid}} = \prod_{i=1}^n N_i$ — exponentially in dimension when floors are coordinates — not simply multiplicatively with the number of floors. Exact verification may be computationally intractable or undecidable for broad model classes; the witness's tractability is a property of its small finite rational datum.
