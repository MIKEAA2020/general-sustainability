# Suggested abstract style for Nature Communications / PNAS

A broad-journal version could read roughly like this:

> Delayed feedback is a classic source of instability in ecological systems, but most studies focus on biological delays such as maturation or recruitment. We show that delays in the governance loop — the time between observed stock decline and institutional response — can be decisive for stability. Using a minimal three-state model coupling stock dynamics, deficit memory, and deployed management effort, we compare two governance rules: a mobilising rule that escalates effort as deployment increases, and a protective quota-tracking rule that restores harvest toward a cap. Under the mobilising rule, delayed response creates a finite delay window associated with Hopf instability, while under the protective rule the equilibrium remains exponentially stable for all delays. When governance operates through periodic review, annual protective review remains stable, whereas annual mobilising review is unstable and restabilises only for longer review intervals. These results show that the timing and structure of institutional response can be as important as ecological productivity or biological lag in determining whether management stabilises or destabilises a harvested stock. The review interval thus acts as a policy design parameter for resource governance.

That is much more suitable for a broad journal than the current abstract.
