\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The composition mask and what the aggregate cannot identify}\label{sec:mask}

The per-hectare value of capital land $\Ups(q)$ is a concave quadratic in $q$ with an interior maximum at $q^*=q_{\max}/2$, where $\Ups(q^*)=b_c+b_{G,c}\rho_c q_{\max}/4=0.066\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$ at the baseline; the regenerative term there ($0.016\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$) is about one third of the direct yield ($0.05\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$; ratio $0.32$), so capital value is only modestly enhanced by regeneration. The quantity that governs what conversion does to the aggregate is the \emph{composition diagnostic}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dconv}
\dconv(q,D)=b_f(D)-\Ups(q),
\end{equation}
the difference between what a hectare yields in the fast book and what it is worth in the capital book. At the baseline $\dconv=+0.787,\ +0.784,\ +0.794\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$ at $q=0.3,\,0.6,\,0.9$: the conversion contribution is robustly positive, although other terms still determine the net account change. The three regimes $\dconv>0$ (premium: conversion contributes positively to $B$, so a conversion-driven mask is possible), $\dconv=0$ (first-order neutrality, non-generic) and $\dconv<0$ (discount: conversion contributes negatively to $B$, so conversion alone cannot produce that mask) are the only shape distinction the quality-based model admits; because $Y_c$ is linear in $A_c$ at fixed $q$, there is no area-based capacity maximum and no regime split of the kind an area-based regeneration law would produce.

\begin{proposition}[Composition identity, mask and non-identifiability]\label{prop:mask}
Let $T_b\equiv0$, $R_{rc}\equiv0$ and hold $D$ fixed, so that $b_f$ is constant. Along any solution of \eqref{eq:B}--\eqref{eq:q},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
\dot B=\dconv(q)\,u_c+b_f\big(\mu A_r-\eta_f A_f\big)+b_{G,c}\,G_c'(q)\,A_c\,\dot q .
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Whenever $u_c>0$, the conversion contribution to $\dot B$ is $\dconv(q)u_c$ and is positive if and only if $\dconv(q)>0$; with $R_{rc}=0$, the same transfer gives $\dot A_c=-u_c<0$. At any state where the maintenance--retirement imbalance and quality-growth terms vanish ($\mu A_r=\eta_f A_f$ and $\dot q=0$), the net derivative is $\dot B=\dconv(q)u_c$, so its sign has this same condition.
\item In the reduced frozen-demand, fixed-$D$ case in which $\mu A_r=\eta_f A_f$ and $\dot q=0$ throughout, and with $\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1$, $\dconv>0$ implies $\dot S=-\dot B=-\dconv u_c<0$ whenever $S>0$. Thus the shortfall decreases to zero if the balance is reached first; if the floor constraint becomes active first, $A_c$ approaches $\Acmin$ asymptotically and the shortfall can persist. This monotonicity is not asserted when the other terms in the full model are nonzero.
\item At fixed $b_f>0$ and fixed $q$ (so that $\Ups(q)>0$), the observation $B=b_fA_f+\Ups(q)A_c$ is one scalar constraint on two land areas. At an interior feasible state it leaves a local continuum of decompositions: a sufficiently small $\delta A_c$ can be offset by $\delta A_f=-(\Ups/b_f)\delta A_c$, with $\delta A_r=-\delta A_f-\delta A_c$, while preserving $B$ and the area total, provided the area bounds remain satisfied. Thus an aggregate-only observation does not locally identify $A_c$. For a finite observed change $\Delta B$, the algebraic offset $\Delta A_f=(\Delta B-\Ups\Delta A_c)/b_f$ is feasible only when the resulting land areas respect their bounds; the admissible range depends on the state and need not be the whole interval from $\Acmin-A_c(0)$ to zero. If $b_f$ and $A_f$ are independently observed, the residual $C=B-b_fA_f=\Ups(q)A_c$ identifies that product, not $\Ups$ and $A_c$ separately. An independent observation of $A_c$ would identify the effective value $\Ups(q)=C/A_c$; even then, $b_c$, $b_{G,c}$, $\rho_c$, and $q$ are not separately identified from a single value. With $q$ independently observed over a sufficiently informative range, $\Ups(q)$ can at most identify $b_c$ and the product $b_{G,c}\rho_c$, not $b_{G,c}$ and $\rho_c$ separately.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:B} with $b_f$ constant and substitute \eqref{eq:Ac}--\eqref{eq:Af} with $R_{rc}=0$: $\dot B=b_f(u_c+\mu A_r-\eta_fA_f)-\Ups u_c+b_{G,c}G_c'(q)A_c\dot q$, which is \eqref{eq:identity}. (i) Follows from \eqref{eq:identity} with $R_{rc}=0$; when the maintenance--retirement imbalance and quality-growth terms vanish, only the conversion term remains. (ii) Under the reduced-case conditions in the statement, $S=E-B$ and $\dot S=-\dot B=-\dconv u_c<0$ on $S>0$. Since $S$ is bounded below by zero and $A_c$ is bounded below by $\Acmin$, conversion either closes the shortfall or is limited by the floor. (iii) At fixed $b_f$ and $q$, the observation map is linear; an interior feasible point admits the stated small perturbations along a level set of $B$, while finite changes must also obey the area bounds. The residual $C$ identifies only $\Ups(q)A_c$ unless $A_c$ is independently observed; the parameter combination follows from the definition of $\Ups(q)$.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:model}a shows the mask at the baseline under frozen demand $E=0.825$\,gha (restoration off, $A_{f0}=0.6$\,ha, $A_{c0}=1.5$\,ha, dimensionless $q_0=0.9$, $A_r(0)=0.4$\,ha). Biocapacity rises from $0.594$ to $0.825$\,gha ($+39\%$) while capital land area $A_c$ falls from $1.500$ to $0.808$\,ha ($-46\%$) and fast land $A_f$ rises from $0.600$ to $0.923$\,ha. The dimensionless quality state $q$ rises from $0.9$ to $1.0$, but because $q_0=0.9>q^*=q_{\max}/2=0.5$, this rise lies on the descending side of the concave per-hectare function $\Ups(q)$: its value falls from $0.05576$ to $0.05000$\,gha\,ha$^{-1}$. Accordingly, the modeled capital contribution $\Ups(q)A_c$ to $B$ falls from $0.08364$ to $0.04038$\,gha (by $51.7\%$, about $52\%$), distinct from the $46\%$ decline in capital-land area. The area changes are one-to-one ($\Delta A_f+\Delta A_c+\Delta A_r=0$ to machine precision). The dynamics are fast at first and then asymptotic: because the deficit-side land subsystem is affine (Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}(ii)), the approach to the balance point is exponential at the slow rate $|\lambda_1|=0.054\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ (e-folding $\approx19$\,yr), so within $25$\,yr $98\%$ of the rise in $B$ and $85\%$ of the fall in $A_c$ have occurred ($B=0.820$\,gha, $A_c=0.913$\,ha; $97\%$ of the fall by $50$\,yr), while the strict inequalities $\dot B>0$ and $\dot A_c<0$ persist for $\approx150$\,yr at numerical resolution. The slow tail is reserve equilibration: retirement of fast land to the reserve lowers the fast flow, re-opens a small shortfall, and converts a little more capital, until the reserve reaches its equilibrium share $A_r/A_f=\eta_f/\mu$. The window is therefore not a fixed horizon: its scale is set by $\lambda_1$, which depends on $\tconv$, $\mu$, $\eta_f$ and the yield ratio $b_c/b_f$ (SI~\S S2.2), and its amplitude by $\dconv$ and the initial deficit.

Two consequences of Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}(iii) frame the empirical analysis of Section~\ref{sec:empirical}. First, the composition signature ($B$ rises while $A_c$ falls) is contemporaneously observable only in the two-book series; a monitor who sees the aggregate alone cannot distinguish a composition mask from broad growth. Second, independent proxies for the fast book ($A_f$, $b_f$) convert the aggregate into a measured fast book and a residual, which is the most that can be identified without independent ecological valuation of the capital book. We call the net two-book signature $B$ rising while $A_c$ falls the \emph{composition mask}; its conversion component is positive when $\dconv>0$, but the net sign of $\dot B$ also depends on the other terms in \eqref{eq:identity}. The alternative \emph{liquidation mask} is $E$ steady while $A_c$ falls, which does not require a positive conversion contribution. The two-book series makes these readings distinguishable.

\subsection{The drawdown is a demand-driven floor collision, not a fold}\label{sec:ceiling}

With endogenous population there is no interior steady state with active conversion: at a demographic equilibrium $P=K=B/e$, so $E=B$ and $S=0$. Conversion is therefore a transient regime, and its steady states exist only under a frozen exogenous demand pinned above the flow. The frozen-demand system is where the question of a catastrophic shift must be posed, and it has a complete answer.

\begin{proposition}[Balance point, demand ceiling and affine deficit-side dynamics]\label{prop:ceiling}
Assume frozen demand $E$, $T_b\equiv0$, $R_{rc}\equiv0$, $q=q_{\max}$ (the attractor of \eqref{eq:q} for $q(0)>0$) and $D=0$. Let $k=\mu/(\mu+\eta_f)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Beq}
B_{eq}(A_c)=b_{f0}\,k\,(A_{tot}-A_c)+b_cA_c=b_{f0}kA_{tot}-(b_{f0}k-b_c)A_c .
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Equilibria of the land subsystem on the no-conversion face $S=0$ satisfy $A_f=k(A_{tot}-A_c)$, $A_r=(1-k)(A_{tot}-A_c)$ and $B_{eq}(A_c)\ge E$. If $b_{f0}k>b_c$, $B_{eq}$ is strictly decreasing, so the balance point $B_{eq}(A_c^*)=E$ is unique,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Acstar}
A_c^*(E)=\frac{b_{f0}kA_{tot}-E}{b_{f0}k-b_c},
\end{equation}
The algebraic root lies in the feasible land interval $A_c\in[\Acmin,A_{tot}]$ exactly when $B_{eq}(A_{tot})\le E\le\Eceil:=B_{eq}(\Acmin)$; it lies strictly above $\Acmin$ iff $E<\Eceil$. For $E<B_{eq}(A_{tot})$, the root lies above $A_{tot}$ and feasible no-conversion equilibria already satisfy $B_{eq}\ge E$.
\item On the deficit side ($S>0$, $S/b_f<A_c-\Acmin$) the land subsystem is affine in $z=(A_f,A_c)$, $\dot z=Jz+c$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
J=\begin{pmatrix}-\tconv^{-1}-\mu-\eta_f & -\mu-\dfrac{b_c}{b_f\tconv}\\[8pt] \tconv^{-1} & \dfrac{b_c}{b_f\tconv}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det J=\frac{\mu+\eta_f}{\tconv}\Big(k-\frac{b_c}{b_f}\Big),
\end{equation}
and $\operatorname{tr}J<0$.
$J$ does not depend on $E$ or on the state, $\det J>0$ exactly when $B_{eq}$ is decreasing, and the fixed point of the affine system is the balance point of (i). For $B_{eq}(A_{tot})\le E<\Eceil$ the balance point lies above the typed floor and every trajectory that remains in this regime converges to it at the constant rates given by the eigenvalues of $J$; for $E>\Eceil$ the affine fixed point lies below the floor, so the trajectory approaches the face $A_c=\Acmin$ asymptotically, with a limiting persistent shortfall $S^*=E-\Eceil>0$. The ceiling is a collision of the equilibrium with a face of the admissible set, not a bifurcation of the vector field: there is no second branch, no fold, no hysteresis, and the leading eigenvalue does not tend to zero as $E\to\Eceil$.
\item Beyond the ceiling, with degradation active, the debt converges to the unique root $D^*(E)$ of $\eta D=E-B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)$, where $B_{eq}(\cdot\,;D)$ uses $b_{f0}e^{-\alpha D}$, provided $\eta>\alpha b_{f0}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)$; $D^*$ is increasing in $E$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) With $S=0$ and $R_{rc}=0$, \eqref{eq:Af} gives $\mu A_r=\eta_fA_f$, hence $A_r/A_f=\eta_f/\mu$ and, with $A_r=A_{tot}-A_f-A_c$, $A_f=k(A_{tot}-A_c)$; substituting into \eqref{eq:B} with $q=q_{\max}$ ($\Ups=b_c$) and $D=0$ gives $B=B_{eq}(A_c)$, and $S=0$ requires $B\ge E$. Strict monotonicity and \eqref{eq:Acstar} follow from $b_{f0}k>b_c$. (ii) On the deficit side $u_c=(E-b_fA_f-b_cA_c)/(b_f\tconv)$ is affine in $z$; substituting into \eqref{eq:Ac}--\eqref{eq:Af} with $A_r=A_{tot}-A_f-A_c$ gives $J$ and a constant vector $c$ by direct differentiation; $\det J$ follows by expansion, and $\operatorname{tr}J=-\tconv^{-1}(1-b_c/b_f)-\mu-\eta_f<0$ for $b_c<b_f$. The fixed point solves $u_c=0$ and $\mu A_r=\eta_fA_f$, i.e.\ it is the balance point. Because $J$ is constant, convergence rates are constant along the branch. (iii) The map $D\mapsto\eta D+B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)-E$ is continuous, negative at $D=0$ when $E>\Eceil$, tends to $+\infty$, and is strictly increasing when $\eta>\alpha b_{f0}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)\ge-\partial_DB_{eq}$; hence it has a unique root, which increases with $E$.
\end{proof}

At the baseline $k=0.5455$, the slope $b_{f0}k-b_c=0.414\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$, and $\Eceil=1.138\,\mathrm{gha}$; the balance points $A_c^*(E)=1.110,\,0.626,\,0.385,\,0.143,\,0.070\,\mathrm{ha}$ at $E=0.70,\,0.90,\,1.00,\,1.10,\,1.13\,\mathrm{gha}$ agree with the integrated equilibria (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}b). The eigenvalues of $J$ are real and negative, $\lambda_1=-0.0537\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ and $\lambda_2=-0.998\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$; $\lambda_1$ varies only weakly with the fast-land yield, from $-0.0522\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ at $b_f=0.60$ to $-0.0556\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ at $b_f=2.00$ (Table~\ref{tab:model}a), and never changes sign. There is no universal scalar stability index: the conversion-loop gain is the state- and parameter-dependent $\dconv$, of order $1\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$, and the return rate is $|\lambda_1|$ of order $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$. Beyond the ceiling the debt fixed points are $D^*=1.19$ and $5.38$\,gha\,yr at $E=1.158$ and $1.238$\,gha (integrated values $1.21$ and $5.39$\,gha\,yr); the monotonicity condition of (iii) holds with margin ($\alpha b_{f0}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)=0.034\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}<\eta=0.05\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$), so under frozen demand the debt saturates rather than running away.

The statement ``no critical slowing down'' is precise here. By critical slowing down we mean that the least-negative eigenvalue of the relevant linearization tends to $0^-$ as a parameter approaches a fold, so that the return time $T_{ret}\approx-1/\lambda_1$ diverges. In Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} the linearization is constant along the entire branch, so neither $\lambda_1$ nor $T_{ret}\approx19$\,yr changes as $E\to\Eceil$. The only early-warning signal the model offers is a \emph{boundary} signal, the capital book approaching its typed floor, which is observable only in the two-book series.

With endogenous demand the same structure appears as a continuous drawdown. Figure~\ref{fig:model}c shows the minimum capital land reached during a run as a function of initial population $P_0$ for four initial capital-land areas $A_{c0}$: the curves are continuous and monotone, the floor is approached asymptotically (because $u_c\le(A_c-\Acmin)/\tconv$, the distance to the floor decays exponentially during a sustained deficit and is never crossed), and higher initial capital-land area supports higher demand before the floor is approached. The ``collapse thresholds'' of the earlier literature on this model are therefore operational quantities: Table~\ref{tab:model}b reports the initial demand at which the floor is approached to within $10^{-2}$, $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-6}$\,ha of its value. The $10^{-6}$\,ha floor-proximity thresholds ($P_0\approx2.41,\,2.70,\,2.98,\,3.14$\,cap for $A_{c0}=0.40,\,0.70,\,1.00,\,1.18$\,ha) are obtained by bisection on that tolerance criterion; they rise monotonically with initial capital, as do the looser criteria, and no criterion exhibits a jump. Two robustness checks bear on the predictions of Section~\ref{sec:predictions}: the initial-population floor-proximity threshold at $A_{c0}=1.0$\,ha is identical to three decimals for $\tau_g=5$, $20$ and $40$\,yr, and identical to three decimals with the restoration coefficient $\chi_r=0.10$\,gha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ and $\chi_r=0$\,gha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ (SI~\S S4). The first reflects the small leverage of the regenerative term at the baseline and the monotone quality dynamics; the second reflects the fact that a surplus-gated flow is inert during the shortfall that produces the drawdown.

\subsection{The mask is bounded: technology gain is parameter-bounded, debt erosion is shortfall-bounded}\label{sec:bounded}

Both the technology wave and the degradation act on the same coefficient, the fast-land yield $b_f$ of \eqref{eq:bf}, and they do so multiplicatively. This yields a structural asymmetry.

\begin{proposition}[Boundedness asymmetry]\label{prop:bounded}
Under \eqref{eq:bf}--\eqref{eq:Tb}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the technology factor satisfies $1\le(b_{f0}+T_b(t))/b_{f0}\le1+\Delta b/b_{f0}$ for all $t$, so the logarithmic gain of the yield channel is bounded by $\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})$ uniformly in time ($0.78$ at the baseline);
\item the debt satisfies $D(t)\le\max\{D(0),\ \sup_{s\le t}\pos{E-B}(s)/\eta\}$, and $D\to S^*/\eta$ whenever the shortfall settles at $S^*$; the degradation factor $e^{-\alpha D}$ is therefore bounded below only through the sustained shortfall;
\item the degradation loss exceeds the maximal technology gain once the sustained shortfall exceeds $S^\dagger=(\eta/\alpha)\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})$, equal to $1.30\,\mathrm{gha}$ at the baseline.
\end{enumerate}
Under an additive degradation law $b_f=b_{f0}+T_b-\alpha D$ the two channels enter symmetrically and (iii) reduces to a comparison of $\Delta b$ with $\alpha D^*$; the asymmetry is a property of the multiplicative form.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) is $0\le T_b\le\Delta b$. (ii) From \eqref{eq:D}, $\dot D\le\bar S-\eta D$ with $\bar S=\sup_{s\le t}\pos{E-B}$, so $D(t)\le\max\{D(0),\bar S/\eta\}$ by comparison; if $\pos{E-B}\to S^*$ then $D\to S^*/\eta$. (iii) compares $\alpha S^*/\eta$ with $\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})$.
\end{proof}

The proposition corrects a tempting overstatement: debt does not compound without bound under a fixed shortfall -- it saturates at $S^*/\eta$ -- but its ceiling is set by the shortfall rather than by a parameter, and the shortfall is itself raised by demand growth. At the baseline the crossover shortfall $S^\dagger\approx1.3\,\mathrm{gha}$ exceeds the baseline biocapacity, so at these parameters the debt channel is a slow erosion rather than a reckoning; what bounds the mask at the baseline is the balance-point mechanism of Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}(ii), with debt erosion as the second, slower limit. A second coupling is possible: because $K=B/e$, a technology-driven rise in $B$ can raise carrying capacity and, after the demographic delay, demand. Whether this produces a larger all-time debt peak depends on the initial state and the timing of the response; it is not a sign-general theorem. In the controlled matched experiment in SI~\S S8, with $P_0=1.0$\,cap, the active $T_b$ run reaches a global $D$ maximum of $0.02020$\,gha\,yr versus $0.00813$\,gha\,yr without the wave (ratio $2.49$). At the reference $P_0=1.2$\,cap, the all-time peak is instead lower with the wave ($0.05104$ versus $0.06122$\,gha\,yr), while the maximum over $t\ge t_{wave}=40$\,yr is higher ($0.02518$ versus $0.01085$\,gha\,yr; ratio $2.32$). This delayed-rebound response is conditional on the declared parameters and initial demand, and the global maximum must not be conflated with the post-wave-center maximum. The active-wave experiment is a diagnostic extension, not a forecast or an empirical estimate.

\subsection{Recovery requires an explicit, gated restoration flow}\label{sec:recovery}

The model is structurally asymmetric: $A_c\to A_f$ proceeds through conversion, but no term in \eqref{eq:Ac}--\eqref{eq:Ar} returns land to the capital book except the restoration flow $R_{rc}$. Fast-land retirement sends land to the reserve, not to capital. Reducing population, footprint or shortfall therefore does not reverse prior conversion: with $\chi_r=0$ the capital book stays where the shortfall left it. In the endogenous-demand run with $A_{c0}=1.0$\,ha and $P_0=2.0$\,cap, capital land falls to $0.398$\,ha within four years and remains there without restoration, whereas with the surplus-gated flow ($\chi_r=0.10$\,gha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$) it recovers to $0.828$\,ha, a dimensionless recovery fraction $\mathcal R_c=(A_c(T)-A_c^{\mathrm{deg}})/(A_c^{\mathrm{init}}-A_c^{\mathrm{deg}})=0.71$ (SI~\S S4.3). The sign of the gate matters. A surplus-gated flow, active when $B>E$, is the ecologically sign-correct rewilding: it retires land only when the system has spare capacity, but it is inert during the shortfall it must eventually address -- which is why the drawdown thresholds of Table~\ref{tab:model}b do not depend on $\chi_r$ -- and once a surplus opens it can over-restore while the supported population re-equilibrates downward, so a target-level stopping rule is needed for a clean $\mathcal R_c=1$ (SI~\S S4.4). A deficit-gated flow, active when $E>B$, is the one gate that acts during the shortfall, but it retires productive land exactly when provision is short and can deepen the near-term deficit; at the tested rates conversion outpaces it, so it is necessary but insufficient and must be sized or capped. Neither gate is an unqualified good, and the gate-sign asymmetry is phase-dependent rather than a universal ranking.

\subsection{Measured composition of aggregate biocapacity, 1961--2022}\label{sec:empirical}

The NFA world series records annual account values $B,E$ in gha and population $P$ in cap; the per-capita levels $B/P,E/P$ are in gha\,cap$^{-1}$. Indices, ratios and logarithmic changes below are dimensionless. The identity $d\ln B=d\ln(B/P)+d\ln P$ applied to the NFA world series gives the aggregation face of the result (Fig.~\ref{fig:aggregation}). Aggregate biocapacity rose $+23.0\%$ ($\Delta\ln B=+0.207$, $+0.339\%\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$); per-capita biocapacity fell $-52.9\%$ ($\Delta\ln(B/P)=-0.752$, $-1.23\%\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$); the per-capita footprint rose $+16.9\%$; and the biocapacity ratio $R_B=E/B$ rose from $0.718$ to $1.782$, crossing $1$ in 1971. Because the identity is exact (dimensionless residual $<10^{-16}$) the split is unambiguous: population contributed $+0.959$ ($+464\%$ of $\Delta\ln B$) and the per-capita term $-0.752$ ($-364\%$). This is the extensive/intensive split, not a causal claim (biocapacity is computed from area and yield independently of $P$), and it is not yet the two-land composition mask: the same aggregate that hides a per-capita halving is the one that would hide a capital-land drawdown.

To advance past the aggregate we apply the observation operator of Section~\ref{sec:data}. With $A_f$ from FAOSTAT cropland area and $b_f$ from the normalized crop-yield index calibrated at 1961, the exact Divisia decomposition \eqref{eq:divisia} attributes the $+0.207$ aggregate log-change to a yield channel $+0.393$, a cropland-area channel $+0.056$ and a residual $-0.242$ (Table~\ref{tab:divisia}). Raw component log-changes ($+1.128$, $+0.160$, $-0.404$) are changes in the components, not contributions, and cannot be summed; the log-mean weights $w_X=0.349$, $w_C=0.600$ make the identity exact, whereas average shares ($0.375$, $0.625$) would give $+0.231$. The yield channel dominates the growth of the aggregate, cropland area is a small positive, and the residual -- all biocapacity not captured by the cropland proxy -- is a large negative. The result is robust to the unidentified base-year cropland share: over the dimensionless share $\phi_0\in[0.10,0.34]$ the weighted identity sums to $+0.207$ exactly and the yield channel is the dominant positive term at every $\phi_0$, although the split is not pinned ($w_X$ ranges $0.18$--$0.62$); for $\phi_0\ge0.339$ the residual is no longer positive at the endpoint and the level decomposition $\Delta B=\Delta X+\Delta C$ (reproducing $\Delta B=+2.242\times10^{9}$\,gha) carries the same sign statement (SI~\S S5.2). The nominal anchor $\phi_0=0.19$ can be replaced by the measured one, $b_f(1961)=B_{\mathrm{cropland,NFA}}(1961)/A_f(1961)\approx0.93\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$ (cropland share $\approx0.128$), which lies inside the robustness band and leaves the decomposition unchanged. The instantaneous fast-book share $s_X$ rises from $0.19$ to $0.56$ over the period; only the growth and sign statements are robust, and $b_c$, $b_{G,c}$, $\rho_c$ and the yield/area split of the residual remain unidentified, exactly as Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}(iii) says they must.

The measured land-type accounts make the composition explicit without any proxy (Fig.~\ref{fig:composition}). In the land-type vintage the aggregate grew $+24.5\%$ ($\Delta\ln B=+0.219$), of which cropland contributed $+23.8$ points and built-up land $+7.3$ points, while forest products, grazing land and fishing grounds together contributed $-6.6$ points; the component ratios are cropland $2.85\times$ (area $1.17\times$, per-hectare yield $2.43\times$), built-up $8.63\times$, forest products $0.90\times$, grazing $0.98\times$, fishing $0.96\times$. Cropland accounts for essentially all of the net aggregate growth ($\approx97$--$103\%$ depending on vintage), and the non-cropland book is flat at the endpoint ($1.008\times$; intra-period index up to $1.024\times$). The identifiability limit is recursive: the near-flat non-cropland total is itself the net of declining forest-products, grazing and fishing biocapacity (together $\approx-6.4\times10^{8}$\,gha) and an $8.6\times$ rise in built-up land. These categories are accounting flows and land classes, not direct measurements of the model's effective area state $A_c$ or its $q$-dependent contribution $\Ups(q)A_c$; they do not demonstrate a measured decline in $A_c$, but they do show that aggregate summary statistics conceal composition at more than one level.

\subsection{Falsifiable predictions}\label{sec:predictions}

The model's testable content, framed against two-book series ($B$, $A_c$, $A_f$ and land-cover/yield proxies) rather than the aggregate, is the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=P\arabic*.,leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Mask signature.} In the baseline frozen-demand run, the model predicts a finite interval with $\dot A_c<0$ and $\dot B>0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}a). The conversion contribution is positive exactly when $\dconv>0$, but the net sign of $\dot B$ also depends on maintenance/retirement, quality growth, debt, technology, and restoration. With baseline shares $\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1$, active conversion requires $R_B=E/B>1$; in the reserved-flow extension with $\sigma_f,\sigma_c<1$, conversion can occur even when $R_B\le1$, which is not a baseline result. A two-book series distinguishes the net composition signature ($B$ rises while $A_c$ falls) from a liquidation mask ($E$ steady while $A_c$ falls) (Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}).
\item \textbf{Window scale.} The duration of the mask is set by the slow eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ (through $\tconv$, $\mu$, $\eta_f$, $b_c/b_f$) and its amplitude by $\dconv$ and the deficit, not by a fixed horizon; the bulk of conversion precedes the closing of the deficit by one to two e-folding times.
\item \textbf{No critical-slowing precursor.} The return time does not grow as demand approaches the ceiling, and the drawdown depth is continuous in demand (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}c); the observable warning is the capital book approaching its typed floor (Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}). This is a negative prediction: a catastrophic-shift early-warning indicator is not expected to precede the two-land drawdown.
\item \textbf{Demand threshold, not delay threshold.} The operational initial-demand threshold for approaching the capital floor rises with initial capital (Table~\ref{tab:model}b) and is insensitive to the regeneration delay (identical to $10^{-3}$ for $\tau_g=5$--$40$\,yr), because the regenerative share of capital value is small and quality is monotone; a one-stock regeneration cliff is not expected.
\item \textbf{Recovery requires a gated flow.} Reducing demand does not restore the capital book; recovery requires an explicit restoration flow whose gate sign determines whether it is inert during the shortfall (surplus-gated; thresholds unchanged by $\chi_r$) or deepens it (deficit-gated).
\end{enumerate}
The identifiability statement of Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}(iii) is not a prediction but a constraint on testing: P1--P5 can be tested only with independent $A_c$ and $b_f$ proxies, never with the aggregate alone.
