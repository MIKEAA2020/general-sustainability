Abstract
Aggregate biocapacity is a value-weighted total over heterogeneous accounts; the total
alone does not identify their composition.      We formulate a two-land delay-dierential
model in which account shortfall triggers one-way conversion from ecological-capital to
fast-provisioning area, while the capital-quality state q evolves separately. The conversion
contribution to aggregate biocapacity is positive when fast-land yield exceeds the eective
per-hectare value of capital land. In a reduced frozen-demand equilibrium relation with
q = qmax , debt and technology wave o, and restoration disabled, the balance point is
unique and the oor collision has no fold or critical slowing down. This branch result does
not eliminate trajectory dependence:     conversion without restoration leaves realized Ac
below its pre-overshoot level after conversion stops, but does not imply perpetual decline
at equilibrium. In a representative conditional run, Ac falls 46.2%, Υ(q) falls 10.3%, and
the modeled capital contribution Υ(q)Ac falls 51.7% while aggregate B rises 39%; these
are model outputs, not empirical stock estimates. In Edition 2026 of the National Foot-
print and Biocapacity Accounts, world biocapacity rises 24.9% from 1961 to 2023 while
B/P falls 52.7% and the global production footprint per person rises 16.7%. Cropland
contributes +24.06 percentage points to the total change, Forest biocapacity −5.95, and
built-up land +7.44 as an accounting class, not ecological capital. NFA component val-
ues are accounting signals, not measurements of biomass, soil condition, wetland extent,
sh biomass, or Ac . No comparable global physical-stock inventory is assembled; the pro-
posed scoreboard remains an unpopulated, nonadditive vector pending defensible common
boundaries, valuation, normalization, and substitution rules.

1. Introduction
   Carrying capacity is one of ecology's most enduring and most elusive
ideas.   In classical population biology it is a xed ceiling; imported into
global human ecology it is usually treated as a static boundary, the maxi-
mum number of people the Earth can support (cf. Cohen, 1995). Treating
the ceiling as xed overlooks a feedback that matters:      a population that
overshoots its resource base degrades the base and lowers the future ceiling.
This paper is concerned with a subtler failure than a xed ceiling. It concerns
the measurement on which monitoring and policy depend.
   The standard aggregate measure of annual regenerative supply is bioca-
pacity, expressed in the National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts (NFA)
in global hectares (gha) for each annual account. Under the Global Footprint
Network (GFN) convention, annual Ecological Footprint and biocapacity val-
                                   −1
ues are reported in gha, not gha yr   (Global Footprint Network, 2009, 2019).
A global hectare is a productivity-normalized hectare whose value incorpo-
rates yield and equivalence factors (Borucke et al., 2013; Lin et al., 2018).
Because the weighting sits inside the unit, an aggregate biocapacity series
does not tell a monitor whether capacity rose because land became more
productive, because more land was brought into provision, or because eco-
logical capital was converted into provisioning land. The composite-indicator
literature warns that additive aggregation turns weights into trade-os, so
that a decit in one dimension can be oset by a surplus in another (Nardo
et al., 2008; Becker et al., 2017; Fischer et al., 2022); biocapacity is exactly
such a composite, and the footprint framework has been criticized on re-
lated grounds (Blomqvist et al., 2013; Giampietro and Saltelli, 2014; van den
Bergh and Grazi, 2015; Galli et al., 2016). The aggregate remains useful as
a headline, but only if its components are reported alongside it. What has
been missing is a dynamical account of how a rising aggregate and a falling
capital base can coexist, for how long, and what would be needed to tell
them apart.
   Coupled humanenvironment models have largely treated the environ-
ment as a single stock or a single aggregate balance:      the World3 system
(Meadows et al., 1972, 1974, 2004), the RicardoMalthus models of Easter
Island (Brander and Taylor, 1998; Anderies, 2000), and more recent bidirec-
tionally coupled Earth-system frameworks (Motesharrei et al., 2016; Donges
et al., 2017; Calvin and Bond-Lamberty, 2018; Henderson and Loreau, 2018).
Estimates of Earth's carrying capacity span several orders of magnitude (Co-



                                       2
hen, 1995; van den Bergh and Rietveld, 2004), some authors reject the notion
for humans altogether (Simon, 1996), and others let the ceiling itself vary lo-
gistically (Meyer, 1994; Meyer and Ausubel, 1999) or treat natural capital
as a depreciating asset (Dasgupta, 2021). Demographic projections used in
most global assessments assume that population unfolds independently of
environmental constraints (United Nations, 2022). Within this literature the
environment has one book; the question of what an aggregate over two books
with dierent yields and regeneration times can conceal has not been posed
as a modeling problem, although the land-use literature has documented the
historical operator  conversion of forest, grassland and wetland into cropland
 in detail (Haberl et al., 2007; Krausmann et al., 2013; Erb et al., 2017).
   Consider an orchard with two kinds of ground.        Fast provisioning land
 cropland and intensively managed systems  yields much and is quickly
restored, but has little standing regenerative value. Ecological-capital land
 forest, soil, wetland, long-rotation systems  has a large standing value
and a regenerative ow that is slowly rebuilt. Demand is evaluated against
the human-available annual account total; any remaining account shortfall
triggers conversion from capital land into fast provision. Because aggregate
biocapacity is a value-weighted sum over the two land books, conversion
can raise the account total while capital-land area Ac shrinks.      A harvest
that looks stable, or a rising aggregate measure of capacity, can therefore
mask a shrinking ecological-capital account.      Neither the orchard nor its
harvesters respond instantly  forest loss, soil degradation and demographic
adjustment unfold over decades  which is why societies can prosper for
long periods while quietly undermining their productive base, only to decline
abruptly (Tainter, 1988; Diamond, 2005). The companion ledger work calls
the broader phenomenon the productivity illusion (Abaee, 2026c); this paper
isolates a distinct compositional mechanism:     conversion can reweight the
books so that the aggregate rises while ecological-capital area falls, even
with the technology wave o.
   We address three questions.   (Q1) Under what conditions does conversion
of ecological-capital land raise the aggregate while capital-land area Ac falls,
how long does the mask last, and can the composition be identied from
the aggregate?   (Q2) Is the resulting capital drawdown a catastrophic shift
in the sense of the alternative-stable-states literature (Scheer et al., 2001),
with a fold, hysteresis and early-warning signals, or something structurally
dierent?   (Q3) Is the mask self-limiting, and what does recovery require?
   The paper makes four contributions. First, it formulates a minimal two-


                                       3
land model (Section 2) in which area is conserved one-to-one across three
land books, per-hectare capital quality is a separate state, conversion is a
rate with an explicit time constant, and degradation acts on the surviving
yield through an accumulated ecological debt; the model is a delay dieren-
tial equation with ecological (regeneration) and demographic delays (Kuang,
1993; Hutchinson, 1948). Second, it proves three propositions (Section 3): a
composition identity that gives the exact sign condition for the conversion
contribution and shows that an aggregate-only observation does not locally
identify the capital loss (Proposition 1); the uniqueness of the frozen-demand
balance point, the location of the demand ceiling, and the absence of a fold
and of critical slowing down, derived from the ane structure of the decit-
side dynamics (Proposition 2); and a boundedness asymmetry between the
technology and debt channels that makes the mask transient (Proposition 3).
Third, it reports NFA Edition 2026 world account movements for 19612023,
with a same-edition 19612022 sensitivity, and separates these accounting
results from physical-stock evidence (Section 3.5).    A supplementary area
cereal-yield proxy is explicitly exploratory: its residual is an accounting con-
struction, not ecological capital. Fourth, it states the falsiable content of
the model as ve predictions and draws the reporting implication for sustain-
ability indicators (Sections 3.6 and 4). The model is deliberately simple and
most of its parameters are representative rather than tted; it is not a fore-
cast. Its purpose is to make the composition logic of aggregate biocapacity
explicit and to state which outcomes are structural and which are conditional
on eective parameters that are not separately identied.

2.2. Parameters and their status
   Table 1 lists the baseline registry.   The capital book is a strategic re-
duction: ecological capital is physically heterogeneous, with land types that
dier in extent, condition, stock, yield, and regeneration time, while the
model represents them as one eective class. Most registry values are there-
fore representative, eective quantities, not tted estimates.   Independent
recovery data bound a broad regeneration-response timescale band across
secondary forests, collapsed sh stocks, and soils (Poorter et al., 2016; RAM
Legacy, cf. Hutchings and Reynolds, 2004; Neubauer et al., 2013; Poeplau et
al., 2011; SI S5.5); these anchors bound a broad eective timescale band of
about 2533 yr (representative value about 29 yr; forest t50 ≈ 30 yr, shery
t50 ≈ 5.6 yr). They concern regeneration response, not the delay τg , and do
not identify a single global rate. The NFA cropland biocapacity-per-area quo-
tient and a supplementary FAOSTAT cereal-yield/cropland-area proxy are
descriptive account diagnostics, not tted values of model bf or a physical-
capital observation operator (SI S5).    The external proxy uses an explicit
1961 NFA account anchor, but its cereal-yield and area boundaries do not
constitute a validated mapping to the model's fast book. The baseline sits
in the composition-premium regime, ∆bconv = bf − Υc (q) > 0, which permits
a positive conversion contribution (Section 3.1); the registry xes a regime,
not a prediction.

4. Discussion
4.1. What the two-land structure changes, and what it removes
   The results depart from the single-stock, single-aggregate reading of the
environment that dominates the resource and sustainability literature (Schae-
fer, 1954; May, 1973; Meadows et al., 1972, 2004; Wackernagel and Rees,



                                         23
1996; Brander and Taylor, 1998) in three respects, each of which is a nega-
tive result as much as a positive one. First, the conversion contribution can
raise aggregate capacity while capital-land area Ac falls, but this bounded
composition eect does not by itself determine the net change in the aggre-
gate: Proposition 1 gives the exact sign condition for the conversion contribu-
tion, and Proposition 3 explains why the technology/debt mask cannot per-
sist. Second, in the reduced frozen-demand case of Proposition 2 (q = qmax ,
D = 0, technology wave o, and restoration disabled), the balance relation is
strictly monotone and its oor collision is not a fold. There is no hysteresis
of that equilibrium relation or critical slowing down; these are branch-level
statements, not claims that realized states are independent of history. With
conversion but no return ow, Ac remains below its pre-overshoot value after
conversion stops, while the equilibrium itself has Ȧc = 0 (Table 4). Third,
recovery is not the reverse of degradation; it requires an explicit, gated ow,
and the gate sign is a design decision with phase-dependent consequences.
   The negative results have a direct bearing on early-warning practice.
Generic indicators of critical slowing down (rising autocorrelation and vari-
ance) are motivated by the approach to a fold (Scheer et al., 2001). In a
two-book system in which the drawdown is a boundary collision, such indi-
cators would not be expected to re, and their silence should not be read as
safety. The informative signal is the capital book itself, which requires the
two-book series.
   Several quantities that circulate for models of this kind are deliberately
not claimed here.   The two-gain Hopf classication of which delay desta-
bilizes, the associated critical delays, and the reference eigenvalue of order
+0.6 yr−1 belong to a single-stock fastslow reduction that we retain only
as a comparator in SI S7, where its equations, equilibrium family and g-
ures are given; none of its features is carried into the two-land results. The
1820 yr regeneration cli  of the one-stock comparator does not survive
the two-land structure (Table 4). What is structural in the two-land model
is the composition logic and the identiability limit; what is conditional on
eective parameters is the sign and size of ∆bconv and the location of the
ceiling.

4.3. Policy operators
   Three operators follow from the typed-oor structure and are developed in
SI S6. Freezing conversion  capping Af or ooring Ac  is the single opera-
                           min
tor that guarantees Ac ≥ Ac ; a governor that observes only the composite B
cannot guarantee a capital-land oor, and the failure is informational rather
than dynamical.    Restoration is protective only when surplus-gated, and a
decit-gated ow can deepen the shortfall it is meant to relieve; this is the
protective, as opposed to mobilizing, institutional signature of the delay com-
panion (Abaee, 2026a). A Half-Earth reservation (Wilson, 2016), E ≤ σB
with σ < 1, succeeds without liquidation on the nominal path, but this is a
nominal-path consequence until it is certied against a declared disturbance
class under a preregistered retention rule, and we state it as such.

   The     accounting   layer      the   closed   typed-ux   ledger,   conservation

4.5. Limitations
   The capital land is an eective class, not a single land type; the sin-
gle regeneration timescale, the composition diagnostic and the ceiling are
eective-parameter statements, and the full vector decomposition is cited
rather than re-derived. Most parameters are representative rather than es-
timated; bc , bG,c and ρc are not identiable from a cross-section, and the
regeneration anchors bound a band rather than calibrate     ρc (the anchors
concern the regeneration rate, not the lag τg ). The per-capita footprint is
held constant to isolate the stockowdemand feedbacks; endogenizing e

is an extension.   The composition window is bounded and demand-driven;
without a sustained shortfall or a technology/demand feedback it closes at
the balance point. Stability at S = 0 is one-sided because the positive part is
exact. Drawdown thresholds are tolerance-dependent bands; a small margin
next to that band is reported as such and not as a sharp boundary. The

debt-repayment rate is set physical and non-singular (η = 0.05 yr ); the
η → 0 case removes the debt equilibrium and is excluded by a oor, and
outcomes are insensitive to η over a modest positive range.     The accounts
do not separate yield from area, which is the identiability limitation the
paper is about rather than an estimation error.     Finally, the reported tra-
jectories use the delayed-regeneration term exactly as specied in Eq. (10).
A current-quality implementation is retained only as a numerical sensitivity
check; it changes the demonstration mask window by 1.3 yr and the initial-

SI S3 remain unchanged at their displayed precision (Section 2.4).



5. Conclusions
   Aggregate biocapacity alone does not identify its composition.       In the
two-land model, conversion contributes positively to B when fast-land yield
exceeds the eective per-hectare value of capital land, while Ac falls; the net
change in B depends on the other terms (Proposition 1). Under the stated
reduced frozen-demand assumptions, the equilibrium relation is single-valued
and has no fold, hysteresis, or critical slowing down. That result is distinct
from path dependence of the realized state: with restoration disabled, one-
way conversion leaves Ac below its pre-overshoot value after the shortfall
closes, but the equilibrium itself has Ȧc = 0. In the representative run, Ac
falls 46.16%, Υc (q) falls 10.33%, and Υc (q)Ac falls 51.72% while model B
rises 39%; q evolves separately, and none of these are empirical global-stock
estimates.
   The Edition 2026 NFA world accounts provide a dierent result: from
1961 to 2023, total B rises 24.86%, B/P falls 52.71%, and the global pro-
duction footprint per person rises 16.73%. Cropland contributes +24.06 per-
centage points to the total biocapacity-account change; Forest biocapacity
contributes −5.95 points, while built-up land contributes +7.44 points as
an accounting class, not ecological capital. These are account-composition
signals, not measurements of biomass, soil condition, wetland extent, sh
biomass, or Ac . The 19612022 sensitivity uses the same NFA edition and



                                      29
population series.     No comparable global physical-stock inventory was as-
sembled, so the scoreboard is an unpopulated specication. Heterogeneous
physical-stock measures remain a nonadditive vector unless common spatial
and temporal boundaries, normalization or valuation, and substitution rules
are declared. The supplementary C = B − b̂f Af is only a proxy residual; it is
not physical capital or the model's Υc (q)Ac . Report NFA components along-
side their aggregate, and do not translate account movements into physical-
stock decline without independent, boundary-consistent observations and a
validated mapping.
