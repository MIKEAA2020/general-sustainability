Abstract
Aggregate biocapacity is a value-weighted total over heterogeneous accounts; the total
alone does not identify their composition.      We formulate a two-land delay-dierential
model in which account shortfall triggers one-way conversion from ecological-capital to
fast-provisioning area, while the capital-quality state q evolves separately. The conversion
contribution to aggregate biocapacity is positive when fast-land yield exceeds the eective
per-hectare value of capital land. In a reduced frozen-demand equilibrium relation with
q = qmax , debt and technology wave o, and restoration disabled, the balance point is
unique and the oor collision has no fold or critical slowing down. This branch result does
not eliminate trajectory dependence:     conversion without restoration leaves realized Ac
below its pre-overshoot level after conversion stops, but does not imply perpetual decline
at equilibrium. In a representative conditional run, Ac falls 46.2%, Υ(q) falls 10.3%, and
the modeled capital contribution Υ(q)Ac falls 51.7% while aggregate B rises 39%; these are
model outputs, not empirical stock estimates. In Edition 2026 of the National Footprint
and Biocapacity Accounts, world biocapacity rises 24.9% from 1961 to 2023 while B/P falls
52.7% and the global production footprint per person rises 16.7%. Cropland contributes
+24.06 percentage points to the total change, Forest biocapacity −5.95, and built-up land
+7.44 as an accounting class, not ecological capital. NFA component values are accounting
signals, not measurements of biomass, soil condition, wetland extent, sh biomass, or
Ac . No common-boundary global physical-stock series for the relevant components was
assembled; accordingly, no physical-stock composite or decline nding is reported.      The
physical-stock scoreboard is an unpopulated specication; heterogeneous measures require
declared boundaries, valuation or normalization, and substitution rules.

1. Introduction
    Carrying capacity is central to ecology but dicult to estimate. Classical
population models treat it as a xed ceiling, and global human-ecology mod-
els often represent it as a static upper bound on population (Cohen, 1995).
Such formulations can omit feedbacks through which overshoot degrades a
resource base and changes future capacity. A related problem concerns the
information contained in aggregate measures of regenerative supply.
    The National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts (NFA) report annual
biocapacity and Ecological Footprint values in global hectares (gha). A global
hectare is a hectare normalized to world-average biological productivity for a
specied year; the annual basis of the account is implicit in the unit (Global
Footprint Network, 2009, 2019). Biocapacity represents annual regenerative
supply, and the Ecological Footprint represents annual demand on biologi-
cally productive area. Because yield and equivalence factors are embedded in
this productivity-normalized area, the aggregate does not distinguish changes
in productivity, total area, and land-class composition. Composite-indicator
analyses show that additive aggregation embeds trade-os, allowing a decit
in one component to be oset by a surplus in another (Nardo et al., 2008;
Becker et al., 2017; Fischer et al., 2022); related critiques address the inter-
pretation of the Ecological Footprint (Blomqvist et al., 2013; Giampietro and
Saltelli, 2014; van den Bergh and Grazi, 2015; Galli et al., 2016). Aggregate
biocapacity remains informative as a summary measure, but its interpreta-
tion depends on the component values and the processes that generate them.
    Coupled humanenvironment models have represented environmental
change through single stocks or aggregate balances, including the World3 sys-
tem (Meadows et al., 1972, 1974, 2004), RicardoMalthus models of Easter
Island (Brander and Taylor, 1998; Anderies, 2000), and bidirectionally cou-
pled Earth-system frameworks (Motesharrei et al., 2016; Donges et al., 2017;
Calvin and Bond-Lamberty, 2018; Henderson and Loreau, 2018). Estimates
of Earth's carrying capacity span several orders of magnitude (Cohen, 1995;
van den Bergh and Rietveld, 2004); other work questions the concept for
human populations (Simon, 1996), models a variable ceiling (Meyer, 1994;
Meyer and Ausubel, 1999), or treats natural capital as a depreciating asset
(Dasgupta, 2021). Demographic projections used in global assessments com-
monly specify population independently of environmental constraints (United
Nations, 2022). The land-use literature documents conversion of forest, grass-
land, and wetland to cropland (Haberl et al., 2007; Krausmann et al., 2013;


                                       2
Erb et al., 2017). These studies establish land conversion as a relevant pro-
cess; the question here is how a value-weighted aggregate responds when land
classes dier in yield and regeneration, and whether that aggregate reveals
the composition of the productive base.
    A stylized orchard distinguishes the annual ow of apples from the stock
of trees and soil that supports future harvests. Taking fruit at or below
its renewal rate uses the ow; cutting trees to meet demand beyond that
ow draws down productive capital and may reduce later harvests. In this
analogy, rapid replanting describes turnover of annual provision, not a large
standing ecological stock or rapid recovery of all supporting conditions. The
model applies this distinction to two eective land classesfast-provisioning
land and ecological-capital landthat dier in annual yield, eective per-
hectare value, and regeneration dynamics. If shortfall induces conversion
from capital land to fast-provisioning land, aggregate biocapacity can rise
even as capital-land area Ac falls. This is a conditional model mechanism;
NFA account categories do not map directly to Ac or measure the physical
stocks in the analogy. The companion material-ledger analysis discusses this
broader owstock masking as a productivity illusion; the present analysis
focuses on its composition-specic form (Abaee, 2026c).
    A complementary elevator analogy concerns observability. A lift rated for
ten passengers might continue to operate temporarily when carrying four-
teen; continued service does not establish that repeated overloading leaves
the cable's safety margin unchanged. This analogy isolates the distinction
between observed service and asset condition. It does not assert that fail-
ure is inevitable or predict an abrupt failure in the model, whose reduced
frozen-demand branch reaches a typed oor without a fold.
    The analysis addresses three questions. (1) Under what conditions can
conversion raise aggregate biocapacity while capital-land area Ac falls, how
long can this signature persist, and can the composition be identied from
the aggregate? (2) Does the resulting drawdown involve a catastrophic shift,
fold, hysteresis, or early-warning signal, or does it arise through a dierent
structure? (3) Under what conditions is the drawdown self-limiting, and
what mechanisms permit recovery?
    The analysis makes three contributions: a two-land delay model with
conserved area, separate capital quality, and shortfall-triggered conversion;
an exact sign condition for the conversion contribution, a local aggregate-
only non-identiability result for Ac , and a frozen-demand equilibrium anal-
ysis that distinguishes branch uniqueness from trajectory dependence; and

                                      3
an accounting decomposition of NFA Edition 2026 component movements,
paired with a separate crop-proxy Divisia diagnostic and an explicit account
of what those data do and do not measure. Five falsiable predictions follow
from the model. Parameter values are representative rather than tted, and
simulated trajectories describe conditional mechanisms rather than forecasts.

    The empirical analysis uses the ocial National Ecological Footprint and
Biocapacity Accounts, Edition 2026, results and metadata version 1.0 (Lo
et al., 2026). The primary window is the complete world series 19612023;
rows for 20242025 are estimated and excluded. The 19612022 sensitivity
uses the same 2026 edition and population series; both analysis windows
therefore use a single data vintage. From the workbook's world_data sheet,
B is biocap_total_gha; the empirical footprint series is efp_total_gha, the
Global Ecological Footprint of Production, denoted Eprod ; and population
is pop_1000_persons, dened in the workbook as UN PopStat population.
Per-capita quantities are recomputed by dividing each total account value
by that population in persons, rather than using the rounded workbook per-
capita columns. Ratios, indices, and logarithmic changes are dimensionless.
    The component series retain the workbook labels Cropland, Grazing land,
Forest biocapacity, Fishing grounds, and Built-up land. These are NFA ac-
count values, not measurements of biomass, soil condition, wetland extent,
sh biomass, or the model's eective area Ac . Built-up land is an NFA ac-
counting class and is not ecological capital. Accordingly, the land-type move-
ments are used as accounting-composition signals only. Variable denitions
and endpoint calculations are summarized in SI S5.
    A supplementary proxy sensitivity uses OWID's FAOSTAT-derived se-
ries for world cropland area (arable land plus permanent crops) and cereal
yield as separate physical inputs (Our World in Data, 2026a,b). The cereal
series is a sentinel, not an all-crop yield measure or an NFA yield factor. Its
base-year account level is anchored explicitly to 1961 NFA cropland bioca-
pacity; the proxy Xt = b̂f,t Af,t and residual Ct = Bt − Xt are therefore an
assumed observation operator, not a measured partition of physical capital.
C is an accounting residual only and cannot be identied with Υ(q)Ac with-
out independent, boundary-consistent inputs and a declared mapping and
valuation. The proxy and its calibration sensitivity are reported in SI S5;
no physical-stock trend is inferred from it.
    The aggregate-versus-per-capita comparison uses the exact identity
∆ ln B = ∆ ln(B/P )+∆ ln P . This extensive/intensive split is an accounting
identity, not a causal decomposition of biocapacity production.

3.2. The frozen-demand balance branch ends at a oor collision, not a fold
    With endogenous population there is no interior steady state with active
conversion: at a demographic equilibrium P = K = B/e, so E = B and
S = 0. Conversion is therefore a transient regime, and its steady states exist
only under a frozen exogenous demand pinned above the ow. The frozen-
demand system provides a reduced setting in which the equilibrium response
can be characterized.

4. Discussion
4.1. Structural implications of land-class heterogeneity
    The two-land model yields three results relevant to aggregate representa-
tions of humanenvironment systems (Schaefer, 1954; May, 1973; Meadows
et al., 1972, 2004; Wackernagel and Rees, 1996; Brander and Taylor, 1998).
First, conversion can increase aggregate biocapacity while capital-land area
Ac declines, although the conversion contribution alone does not determine
the net change in B ; the exact sign condition is given by Proposition 1, and
Proposition 3 bounds the technology/debt mechanism. Second, under the
reduced frozen-demand assumptions of Proposition 2 (q = qmax , D = 0, tech-
nology wave o, and restoration disabled), the balance relation is strictly
monotone and its collision with the typed oor is not a fold. The absence
of hysteresis and critical slowing down applies to this reduced equilibrium
relation, not to the history dependence of realized states. With conversion
but no return ow, Ac remains below its pre-overshoot value after conversion
stops, while Ȧc = 0 at equilibrium (Table 4). Third, recovery requires an
explicit restoration ow, whose gate has phase-dependent consequences.
    The one-stock comparator in SI S7 is a separate formulation with a state
A in comparator-specic stock units. In its debt-free constant-parameter
equilibrium, P = B(A)/e forms a one-parameter family; the production
relation is B(A) = (b+bG ρ)A−(bG ρ/Amax )A2 . At the illustrated comparator
baselines, bG ρ < b, so this relation is monotone; an interior maximum requires
a dierent parameter regime. The comparator's neutral family and selected
delay-dependent trajectories are formulation-specic and do not establish a
fold, a universal critical delay, or a collapse mechanism in the two-land model.
    These equilibrium results qualify generic expectations for early-warning
indicators. Rising autocorrelation and variance are associated with approach
to a fold (Scheer et al., 2001). When drawdown instead occurs through
a typed-oor collision, such indicators need not rise before the oor is ap-
proached. Their absence is not evidence of safety; the relevant signal is the
trajectory of Ac , which is not identied by the aggregate alone.

composition of its underlying land classes. For example, a 20% increase in

    The typed-oor analysis distinguishes three policy operators. A conver-
sion stop triggered directly by the typed oor Ac = Amin   c  guarantees that
constraint. An Af ceiling does not by itself guarantee a specied capital-land
oor unless its stop rule is linked to the oor and remaining-area constraints.
A governor that observes only aggregate B cannot guarantee the capital-land
oor because B does not identify Ac . Surplus-gated restoration is inactive
during shortfall, whereas a decit-gated ow acts during it and can reduce

4.4. Relation to related work
    Related work addresses typed material-ow ledgers and compensatory ag-
gregation (Abaee, 2026b,c), and distinguishes mobilizing from protective in-
stitutional feedback (Abaee, 2026a). The present model focuses on ecological-
regeneration and demographic delays; institutional review delays are not in-
cluded.

4.5. Limitations
    Ecological-capital land aggregates heterogeneous assets, so its single re-
generation state, composition diagnostic, and demand ceiling are eective
model quantities. Most parameters are representative rather than esti-
mated. The values bc , bG,c , and ρc are not separately identied by a cross-
section; recovery observations span dierent systems and provide timescale
context rather than a calibration of ρc ; they concern regeneration response,
not the lag τg . The per-capita footprint is held constant to isolate stock
owdemand feedbacks; endogenizing e is an extension. The composition
window is demand-driven and bounded under the stated mechanisms. Sta-
bility at S = 0 is one-sided because the positive-part operator is exact.
Drawdown thresholds depend on the oor-proximity tolerance and are re-
ported as bands, not sharp boundaries. The debt-repayment rate is xed
at η = 0.05 yr−1 ; the limit η → 0 removes the debt equilibrium and is ex-
cluded. NFA accounts do not separate physical area from yield, so they
cannot identify those components independently. Under current-quality re-
generation Gc (q(t)), the demonstration mask window is 1.3 yr longer and the
initial-population threshold shifts by at most 0.001 cap relative to the delayed
specication Gc (q(t − τg )) (SI S3).

5. Conclusions
  Aggregate biocapacity does not identify its composition. In the two-land
model, conversion contributes positively to B when fast-land yield exceeds

the eective per-hectare value of capital land, while Ac declines; the net
change in B depends on the other terms (Proposition 1). Under the stated
reduced frozen-demand assumptions, the equilibrium relation is single-valued
and has no fold, hysteresis, or critical slowing down. The realized trajectory is
nevertheless path-dependent: with restoration disabled, one-way conversion
leaves Ac below its pre-overshoot value after the shortfall closes, while Ȧc =
0 at the balance point. In the representative run, Ac falls 46.16%, Υc (q)
falls 10.33%, and Υc (q)Ac falls 51.72% while model B rises 39%. These are
conditional model outputs, not empirical global-stock estimates.
    The Edition 2026 NFA world accounts show that from 1961 to 2023 to-
tal B rises 24.86%, B/P falls 52.71%, and the global production footprint
per person rises 16.73%. Cropland contributes +24.06 percentage points to
the total biocapacity-account change; Forest biocapacity contributes −5.95
points, and built-up land contributes +7.44 points as an accounting class, not
ecological capital. These results describe account composition, not changes in
biomass, soil condition, wetland extent, sh biomass, or Ac . The 19612022
sensitivity uses the same NFA edition and population series. A common-
boundary global physical-stock series for the relevant components was not
assembled; accordingly, no physical-stock composite or global decline nd-
ing is reported. The SI scoreboard remains an unpopulated specication,
and heterogeneous physical-stock measures remain a nonadditive vector ab-
sent common spatial and temporal boundaries, normalization or valuation,
and explicit substitution rules. The proxy residual C = B − b̂f Af is nei-
ther a physical-capital measure nor the model contribution Υc (q)Ac . The
orchard analogy summarizes the reporting implication: an aggregate harvest
or capacity total alone does not show whether the assets supporting future
ows are stable; resolving that question requires component-level informa-
tion and, for physical-stock claims, independent observations with a validated
mapping.
