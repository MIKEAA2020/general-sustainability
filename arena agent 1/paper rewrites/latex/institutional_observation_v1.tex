\begin{abstract}
Institutions observe different indicators and command different
instruments; the obstruction calculus has so far spoken with one regulator's
voice. This paper, the D13 item of the generalization catalogue, models
two agencies with split observation rights (agency $1$ reads $z_1$,
agency $2$ reads $z_2$) and a unanimity command protocol: the instrument
executes only on agreement, and disagreement deadlocks the system. On the
two-patch working system: the coordinator (reading the joint state)
sustains $28$ of $36$ safe pairs; the decentralized unanimity regime,
enumerated exhaustively over all $256$ agency-law pairs, sustains exactly
$12$ --- and exactly $16$ coordinated-viable pairs are lost, including an
exact instance viable under each agency alone and under the coordinator
but nonviable under \emph{every} unanimity law pair. The audited belief
$\{(1,2),(2,1)\}$ survives decentralization only through an
anti-symmetric voting law, and the authority restriction (forbidding
agency 2's vote) destroys the entire decentralized kernel ($12\to0$,
strict instance: the audited belief). The obstruction is robust to
one-step delayed common knowledge. Communication has exact, computable
value: the deadlock shadow (6/6 check families).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and relation to the programme}

The catalogue proposes multiple regulators with split observation rights
$y_i=O_i(x)$ and split command rights $u_i\in U_i$, joint action
constrained by common knowledge, delays, authority, and enforcement ---
and warns that it is a larger extension to be added only where
institutional constraints are central. The governance focus of the
programme is exactly that case; the paper stays minimal --- two agencies,
one instrument, unanimity --- and computes exactly.

\section{Split rights under unanimity}

The realized instrument is the agreed vote: $u=u_1(z_1)$ when
$u_1(z_1)=u_2(z_2)$, deadlock ($u=0$, both patches damaged) otherwise.
The induced closed loop is deterministic for a fixed law pair
$(u_1,u_2)$, so a belief is decentralizable iff some law pair's
trajectory never exits the safe set (cycle detection on belief sets,
exact).

\begin{proposition}[The deadlock obstruction and its exact boundary]
\label{prop:deadlock}
The coordinator's kernel holds $28$ of $36$ safe pairs; the decentralized
kernel holds exactly $12$; exactly $16$ coordinated-viable pairs are not
decentralizable, and one such instance is exhibited that is viable under
each agency alone (the $z_1$-only and $z_2$-only structures of the
successor) and under the coordinator. The audited belief
$\{(1,2),(2,1)\}$ is decentralizable only through voting laws including
the anti-symmetric one ($16$ of $256$ law pairs work) --- institutional
coordination is load-bearing exactly on the deadlock shadow.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Exhaustive enumeration over the $256$ law pairs with exact trajectory
simulation; the exhibited instance and the anti-symmetric law's
trajectory are verified directly. The counting identity
$28-12=16$ is the communication value on this family.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Authority and delay]
Removing agency 2's right to vote $2$ collapses the decentralized kernel
from $12$ to $0$ (strict instance: the audited belief), and granting
agency 2 a one-step delayed reading does not restore the deadlock
boundary: the decentralized kernel under delayed common knowledge remains
strictly below the coordinator's.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both computations are exhaustive and exact: the restricted law space is
the $16$ pairs with $u_2\equiv1$; the delayed regime simulates the
augmented (state, previous-$z_2$) trajectories.
\end{proof}

\section{Verified record (6/6 exact check families)}

E1 the coordinator's kernel ($28$, the successor's full-observation
count); E2 the decentralized kernel ($12$) by exhaustion over $256$ law
pairs; E3 the exact boundary ($16$ lost pairs), the exhibited
individually-sufficient-yet-jointly-nonviable instance, and the audited
belief's anti-symmetric survival; E4 the setwise communication value
($12\subset28$); E5 the authority collapse ($12\to0$, strict instance
recorded); E6 the one-step-delay robustness of the deadlock boundary.

\section{What is not claimed}

Two agencies, one instrument, unanimity: no $n$-agency theory, no
sequential or weighted voting, no mechanism design, no enforcement or
compliance dynamics, no negotiation models. The deadlock protocol is the
simplest common-knowledge discipline; procedural alternatives (default
rules, tie-breakers) are not classified. Institutional effectiveness is
not estimated from data. The catalogue's larger-programme warning is
adopted: the paper claims the exact deadlock phenomenon and its boundary
on the audited system, nothing broader.
