\begin{abstract}

The viable-selector framework reads every obstruction certificate as the
emptiness of a response correspondence at an information state; its closing
section previewed the stochastic counterpart, in which the information state
is a distribution and viability is the value-one level of a belief-state
safety recursion. This paper develops that theory. The belief-state safety
value is the stochastic selector, and its finite-horizon values are
piecewise linear with exactly representable witnesses: over rational input
data, the backward recursion propagates rational alpha-vectors, so all
values are exact rationals computable without floating point. At the
deterministic limit the recursion degenerates to a survived-mass formula
over blind action sequences, recovering the robust verdicts of the
companion papers and implying their deficit bounds. Obstruction
certificates are read systematically as deficit lower bounds: the
chance-constrained common-action bound, the degenerate minimal-mass bound,
and monotonicity. On the audited delayed-regime class the theory is carried
exactly: the alpha-vector recursion reproduces the closed-form values on
all forty-eight audited cells, the certificate partition coincides with the
value boundaries, the value's kink locus is exactly the certificate
boundary, the deficit is constant on the certified region, and the class
declaration is quantified --- the cells covered only by the timing
certificate are precisely those where in-window adaptivity lifts the value
to one. All computations run in exact rational arithmetic.

\end{abstract}

\subsection{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}

The companion papers develop obstruction certificates for viability under
incomplete observation --- finite witnesses that no observation-based policy
is safe --- and unify them as emptiness of a response correspondence at an
information state (Abaee, 2026a,b,c). The unifying paper closes with the
stochastic counterpart, left there as an outlook: replace the
set-valued information state by a distribution, the correspondence's
nonemptiness by the value-one level of a safety recursion, and the
certificate by a lower bound on the deficit from unity. This paper
delivers that theory, seeded by the worked instance already carried in the
calculus paper's probabilistic section and its coverage audit.

Four results. First, the \emph{exact representation}: belief-state safety
values are piecewise linear in the belief at every finite horizon, and over
rational data the witnessing alpha-vectors are rational and propagated
exactly by the backward recursion (Section~3, Propositions~\ref{prop:pl}
and~\ref{prop:degen}) --- no floating point enters the value theory, in
keeping with the companions' verification discipline. Second, the
\emph{degeneration}: at the deterministic limit the recursion collapses to
a survived-mass formula over blind action sequences, which recovers the
companion papers' robust verdicts and proves their deficit bound
(Proposition~\ref{prop:degen}). Third, the \emph{certificate reading}:
obstruction certificates are deficit lower bounds, systematically
(Proposition~\ref{prop:deficit}). Fourth, the \emph{agreement theorem} on
the audited classes: on the delayed hidden-regime grid the alpha-recursion,
the closed form, the certificate partition, and the kink locus of the value
coincide cell by cell, with a constant deficit on the certified region and
a quantified class declaration (Section~5, Theorem~\ref{thm:agree}).
Everything is verified in exact rational arithmetic
(Section~\ref{verified}).

\subsection{2. The stochastic selector}\label{model}

Let \(X, A, Y\) be finite, with transition \(T(x' \mid x, a)\) and
observation likelihood \(g(y \mid x, a, x')\). Augment \(X\) with an
absorbing unsafe state \(\bot\): for \(x \in \mathcal{V}\),
\(T(\bot \mid x, a) = 1 - \sum_{x' \in \mathcal{V}} T(x' \mid x, a)\) and
\(T(\bot \mid \bot, a) = 1\). A belief is a distribution \(b\) over
\(X \cup \{\bot\}\), updated by Bayes' rule
\(\tau(b, a, y)\), with
\(\mathbb{P}(y \mid b, a) = \sum_{x, x'} b(x)\, T(x' \mid x, a)\,
g(y \mid x, a, x')\). The \emph{safety value} at horizon \(k\) is the
maximal survival probability
\[
V_{k}(b) = \max_{\pi}\; \mathbb{P}_{\pi}\bigl( x_{t} \in \mathcal{V}
\text{ for } t = 0, \dots, k \;\big|\; b \bigr).
\]

In the selector framework this is the distributional counterpart of the
recursive correspondence: the information state is \(b\), a response is a
policy segment, and the \emph{stochastic selector at} \(b\),
\[
\Gamma^{\mathrm{s}}_{k}(b) = \Bigl\{ a \in A : \sum_{y} \mathbb{P}(y \mid
b, a)\, V_{k-1}\bigl(\tau(b, a, y)\bigr) = V_{k}(b) \Bigr\},
\]
collects the actions attaining the value. Nonemptiness of the recursive
selector corresponds to \(V_{k}(b) = 1\); obstruction certificates
correspond to deficits \(1 - V_{k}(b) > 0\).

\begin{theorem}[belief-state value recursion]\label{thm:recursion}
\(V_{0}(b) = b(\mathcal{V})\) (the horizon counts the current state), and
for \(k \ge 0\),
\[
V_{k+1}(b) = \max_{a \in A}\; \sum_{y \in Y} \mathbb{P}(y \mid b, a)\;
V_{k}\bigl(\tau(b, a, y)\bigr).
\]
The recursion is exact on every finite horizon; it is the Smallwood--Sondik
belief-state value iteration specialized to the absorbing safety reward
(Smallwood and Sondik, 1973).
\end{theorem}

The proof is standard value iteration for the belief-state Markov decision
process, with the absorbing state making \(V_{k}\) the \(k\)-step survival
probability.

\subsection{3. Exact piecewise-linear values}\label{exact}

\begin{proposition}[rational alpha-vectors]\label{prop:pl}
At every horizon \(k\), \(V_{k}\) is piecewise linear and convex:
\(V_{k}(b) = \max_{\alpha \in \Gamma_{k}} \alpha^{\top} b\) for a finite
witness set \(\Gamma_{k} \subset \mathbb{Q}^{|X|+1}\), constructed by the
backup
\[
\alpha^{a,\,\gamma_{\cdot}}(x) = \sum_{x'} T(x' \mid x, a)
\sum_{y} g(y \mid x, a, x')\, \gamma_{y}(x'),
\quad \gamma_{y} \in \Gamma_{k},
\]
with \(\Gamma_{0} = \{ \mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{V}} \}\) and
\(\Gamma_{k+1} = \{ \alpha^{a, \gamma_{\cdot}} : a \in A,\ \gamma_{\cdot}
\text{ a selection} \}\), duplicates removed by exact equality. Over
rational \(T\), \(g\), and priors, every \(\alpha \in \Gamma_{k}\) is an
exact rational vector, every \(V_{k}(b)\) at a rational belief is an exact
rational, and the attaining witness is part of the computation. The
worst-case growth is \(|\Gamma_{k+1}| \le |A|\,|\Gamma_{k}|^{|Y|}\);
deduplication and pruned selections are the practical controls, and the
exponential law is the honest complexity statement for the general class.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof sketch.} The PL structure is Smallwood--Sondik's; the point
here is the exact-arithmetic discipline: the backup maps rational vectors
to rational vectors by finite sums and products, selections are finite, and
equality tests for deduplication are exact on rationals.
\hfill\(\square\)

\begin{proposition}[deterministic-limit degeneration]\label{prop:degen}
Let the kernels be deterministic, \(x^{+} = F(x, a, d)\), with disturbance
support \(\{F(x,a,d) : d \in D\}\) read adversarially within its support,
and let the window be blind with declared sequential class \(\Pi_{B}\).
Then the value degenerates to the \emph{survived-mass formula}
\[
V_{k}(b) = \max_{(u_{0},\dots) \in \Pi_{B}}\;
\sum_{x \in \mathrm{supp}\, b} b(x)\,
\mathbf{1}\bigl[ x \text{ survives } (u_{0}, \dots) \bigr],
\]
a maximum of rational linear functions of \(b\) (the surviving indicators
are the degenerate alpha-vectors). Consequently: if the support \(B\)
carries a jointly surviving blind sequence, \(V_{k}(b) = 1\); and if every
sequence loses some branch, then \(V_{k}(b) \le 1 - \min_{x \in B} b(x)\)
--- the robust common-action verdicts and the minimal-mass deficit bound of
the companion papers, recovered as exact values.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof sketch.} With deterministic kernels the posterior support
follows the set-valued recursion, survival from a branch under a fixed
sequence is a deterministic event, and prior mass sums over surviving
branches; maximizing over sequences gives the formula, from which both
consequences are immediate (a lost branch carries at least the minimal
mass). \hfill\(\square\)

\subsection{4. Certificates as deficit bounds}\label{deficit}

Write \(\Delta_{k}(b) = 1 - V_{k}(b)\) for the deficit.

\begin{proposition}[deficit lower bounds]\label{prop:deficit}
Each companion certificate is a computable lower bound on the deficit:
(i) \emph{chance-constrained common action:} if the one-step violation
probabilities satisfy \(\sum_{x} b(x)\, p(x, a) \ge \delta\) for every
\(a \in A\), then \(\Delta_{k}(b) \ge \delta\) for every \(k \ge 1\);
(ii) \emph{degenerate minimal mass:} at the deterministic limit, if the
support misses the \(k\)-step robust kernel,
\(\Delta_{k}(b) \ge \min_{x \in \mathrm{supp}\, b} b(x)\);
(iii) \emph{monotonicity:} \(V_{k+1}(b) \le V_{k}(b)\), so the deficit is
nondecreasing in the horizon. For finite horizons the exact deficit
\(1 - \max_{\alpha \in \Gamma_{k}} \alpha^{\top} b\) is computable, and the
bounds are witnesses of it, not surrogates.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof sketch.} (i) is the chance-constrained obstruction of the
companion paper: every action loses probability at least \(\delta\) at the
first step and survival probabilities never increase. (ii) is
Proposition~\ref{prop:degen}'s second consequence. (iii) holds since
\(V_{k+1} = \max_a \mathbb{E}[V_{k} \circ \tau] \le \max_a \mathbb{E}
[V_{k-1} \circ \tau] = V_{k}\) by induction.
\hfill\(\square\)

\subsection{5. Agreement on the audited classes}\label{agreement}

The delayed hidden-regime instance of the calculus paper --- regimes
\(\theta \in \{-1, +1\}\), blind window \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\), declared hold
class \(\{u \equiv \pm 1\}\), prior \((\tfrac12, \tfrac12)\) from
\(z_{0}\) --- is the agreement test bed.

\begin{theorem}[certificate--value agreement]\label{thm:agree}
On the audited grid (\(z_{0} \in \{1.0, \dots, 2.5\}\),
\(T_{\mathrm{obs}} \in \{1,2,3\}\); 48 cells), over the declared hold
class: (a) the alpha-vector recursion of Proposition~\ref{prop:pl} returns
exactly the closed form
\begin{gather*}
V_{k}(b_{0}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\mathbf{1}[z_{0} - k \ge 1] +
\mathbf{1}[z_{0} + k \ge 1]\bigr) \quad (k \le T_{\mathrm{obs}}),\\
V_{k}(b_{0}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1 + \mathbf{1}[z_{0} \ge 1 +
T_{\mathrm{obs}}]\bigr) \quad (k > T_{\mathrm{obs}});
\end{gather*}
(b) the certificate partition coincides with the value boundaries:
``viable'' iff \(V_{k} \equiv 1\); common-action-certified iff
\(V_{1}(b_{0}) < 1\); timing-certified iff \(V_{1}(b_{0}) = 1\) but
\(V_{T_{\mathrm{obs}}+1}(b_{0}) < 1\);
(c) the attaining alpha-vector switches exactly at \(z_{0} = 2\): the
value's kink locus is the certificate boundary;
(d) the deficit equals \(\tfrac12\) on every nonviable cell --- the
minimal-mass bound attained with the minimal prior; and
(e) the declaration is quantified: over the unrestricted sequential blind
class the value is \(1\) exactly on \(\{ z_{0} \ge 2 \}\) for
\(k \ge 2\) --- the timing cells are precisely the cells where in-window
adaptivity lifts the declared-class value.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof sketch.} The two-regime blind POMDP has, at each window
horizon, exactly two alpha-vectors \(\alpha^{u} = (\mathbf{1}[z_{0} - u t
\ge 1\ \forall t \le \cdot],\ \mathbf{1}[z_{0} + u t \ge 1\ \forall t \le
\cdot])\), \(u = \pm 1\); evaluating \(\max_{u} \alpha^{u} b_{0}\) at the
uniform prior gives (a), from which (b)--(d) read off against the
companion paper's certificate conditions, and (c) compares the attaining
witness across the boundary. For (e), the deterministic degeneration
(Proposition~\ref{prop:degen}) over all alternating sequences keeps both
branches within \(z_{0} \pm 1\). The complete computations are
machine-verified in exact rational arithmetic. \hfill\(\square\)

\subsection{6. Verified re-derivations}\label{verified}

\texttt{paper2\_stochastic\_selector\_v1\_verify.py} (standard library;
exact rational arithmetic; deterministic) re-derives every claim:
\emph{two-floor instance:} the backup produces exactly
\(\Gamma_{1} = \{(1,0), (0,1)\}\), \(V_{k}(b_{0}) = \tfrac12\) for all
probed horizons, the chance bound \(\delta = \tfrac12\) attained, and
piecewise linearity confirmed at eleven simplex beliefs against the direct
value \(\max(b_{1}, b_{2})\); \emph{degeneration:} the exact survived-mass
value equals brute-force evaluation at the probed beliefs, and at
\((z_{0}, T) = (\tfrac32, 2)\) every blind sequence loses a branch, so the
deficit is at least the minimal mass; \emph{agreement:} the recursion
matches the closed form on all 48 cells and horizons \(k \le 4\); the
partition coincidence holds cell by cell; the attaining witness switches at
\(z_{0} = 2\) for every \(T_{\mathrm{obs}}\); the deficit is exactly
\(\tfrac12\) on all 42 nonviable cells; the unrestricted class attains
value \(1\) exactly on \(\{z_{0} \ge 2\}\) for \(k \ge 2\); \emph{books:}
384 alpha-vectors generated across the campaign, all entries exact
rationals (in this instance all in \(\{0,1\}\)), and deficit monotonicity
at every probed belief of both instances. Ten check families, all passing.

\subsection{7. Delimitations and outlook}\label{scope}

The semantics here is stochastic expectation: the value is a survival
\emph{probability}, and the robust quantifiers of the companion papers are
recovered only at the deterministic degeneration
(Proposition~\ref{prop:degen}); the stochastic and robust readings of a
genuinely stochastic plant differ, and this paper does not conflate them.
The alpha-set growth is exponential in the worst case; the agreement
theorem's classes are two-witness instances precisely because the audited
systems are. General information structures beyond history partitions
(control-dependent observations, active sensing), continuous-time exit-time
value functions, and the five-layer successor architecture are the next
items of the programme; the deficit-bound reading of
Proposition~\ref{prop:deficit} is the interface through which this theory
attaches to the certificate families already established.
