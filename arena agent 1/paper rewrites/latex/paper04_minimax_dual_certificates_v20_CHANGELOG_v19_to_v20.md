## 2. New mathematical content

* **Theorem 3.1 with exactness.** The duality is stated with the quantitative identity best certificate margin = −(lower value), and Remark 3.2 separates the lower value (common action) from the upper value (full information, the Isaacs reading); the Sion hypotheses are verified in SI S1.1.
* **Theorem 3.3 (sparsity by Helly).** The support bound is `min(dim aff U, rank{v_a}) + 1`, obtained from Helly's theorem for compact families plus finite LP duality (v19: "k + 1 by Carathéodory", which gives k + 2 for the pooled point in ℝ^{k+1}; tightness claimed only in dimensions 1–2). The bound is attained **for every k** by the family `u_i − 1 ≥ 0 (i ≤ k)`, `(k−1) − Σu_i ≥ 0` on `[0,k]^k`, realised by an explicit plant (SI S2.2), with lower value −1/(k+1); the rank reduction and the infinite-family case are proved in SI S1.2–S1.3.
* **Remark 3.4 (random study).** Minimal infeasible subfamily sizes for random integer rows (m = k + 3, k = 1…4, and a rank-one variant): the bound k + 1 is attained for k ≤ 3 with decreasing frequency, no size-5 witness among 65 infeasible k = 4 systems, rank-one maximum 2 (SI S4; checks S3.1–S3.6).
* **Proposition 3.5 (non-convex action sets).** Soundness holds for any action set; a certificate exists iff the convexified atom is obstructed; the parity instance (U = {0,1}) has lower value −1, convexified value 0 and no certificate. Both the soundness direction and the parity gap are machine-checked.
* **Theorem 3.6 and Remark 3.7 (polyhedral recovery).** The Farkas pair `(λ, ν)` of the polyhedral case is derived from LP duality with the facet multipliers `ν` identifying the binding constraints of U; on the benchmark at Y = 6 the multiplier ν = 1/2 sits on the demand facet (margin 3/50). Remark 3.7 contrasts the one-LP construction with the `Σ_{j≤s} C(m,j)` feasibility tests of subfamily search.
* **Proposition 4.1 / Example 4.2 (taxonomy as tests).** Physical, epistemic and institutional are defined as tests on the safe-action sets of an atom, shown to overlap rather than partition, and instantiated on a two-patch codex (9 safe states, kernel of 8, the atom {(1,2),(2,1)} epistemic, (1,1) physical, every atom institutional under a single-action codex).
* **Proposition 5.1 (fibre margin law).** For every Y the fibre margin is `ε*(Y) = (27 − 5Y)/50`, attained at the Y-independent action (29/25, 21/25) with the unique certificate (1/2, 1/2); the Farkas pair and the four-atom check (the LP charges only the worst-shock atoms) are included. v19 gave Y* = 27/5 and the margin at Y = 6 only.
* **Proposition 5.2 (demand-floor law) and Remark 5.3.** `Y*(D) = min(77/5 − 5D, 69/5)` for D ≤ 57/25, with the first branch an epistemic–institutional and the second a physical obstruction; each unit of demand floor costs five units of aggregate stock; the value of resolving the aggregate is the band (27/5, 69/5].
* **Definition 6.1 / Theorem 6.2 / Corollary 6.3 (finite review envelope).** Review trees with observation maps that may depend on the action prefix; the node recursion equals the supremum over adapted policy trees (v19's Theorem 4 fixed the observation partition in advance, which did not cover its own two-window instance where the second action is taken before d₂ is seen); tower and martingale identities along any tree; refinement monotonicity; committed prefixes inherit node values. Example 6.4 shows that posterior-only evaluation is not an envelope (1/2 versus 1).
* **Proposition 6.5 (robust values over a coupling class).** Lower and upper robust values `R⁻ ≤ R⁺ ≤ Q₀` over the class M of couplings with fixed one-step marginals; R⁺ is certified by an exact pair (worst coupling, mixture of trees). On the two-window sweep R⁺ equals the envelope at seven of the nine distances and is strictly below it at z₀ = 1 and 5; at z₀ = 2 the certified worst coupling is the product prior itself, an **interior** point of M (six extreme couplings, barycentre = product), so no vertex enumeration would have found it; the dynamic obstruction at z₀ = 4 defeats every one of the 171 trees with probability at least 3/8 (Example 6.6, Table 1, Fig. 3).
* **Proposition 6.7 (integrated certificate).** Stated for finite-horizon trajectories with adapted controls and an information schedule, with the sign convention `h_U(−Σλ_ℓ k_ℓ)`; on the three-branch instance `Γ_h(τ) = 7/50 − τ` reproduces the companion's threshold, and SI S6 relates Γ_h to the companion's Γ (= −Γ_h on the instance) and shows on a translated hexagon that the convention is not vacuous.

## 3. Statements corrected or sharpened relative to v19

* The v19 abstract filed the dynamic envelope under "conjectures" while the body proved the finite case; v20 proves the finite envelope (Theorem 6.2) and lists the continuous-time coupling law as Open Problem 8.3.
* v19's Proposition 1 counted "k + 1 by Carathéodory in ℝ^k" — the pooled point lives in ℝ^{k+1}; the correct route is Helly (Theorem 3.3), and the related-work claim that the bound is "not recoverable from classical results" is withdrawn (it is Helly plus LP duality; the same count is the support-constraint bound of the scenario approach).
* v19 tabulated 8 192 and 19 683 policies for instances of the campaign script; the script enumerates 2^14 = 16 384 and 3·3^4 = 243 (SI S5.3); the two-window instance has 171 adapted trees (reachable cells), not 81.
* v19 wrote the integrated certificate with `h_U(+Σλk)`; the slack `F = β − ∫k·u` requires `h_U(−Σλk)` (check G1.3); on the centrally symmetric hexagon the numbers do not change.
* The feasible point (6/5, 4/5) quoted at Y = 5 is not the maximiser of the fibre programme; the maximiser is (29/25, 21/25) for every Y.
* The benchmark action set is now compact (`[0,2]²` with the demand facet), as Theorem 3.1 requires; "viable" is stated as *first-order viable at the atom* (a necessary condition), with the fibre-invariance caveat.
* The two conflicting check-family counts ("eight" versus G1–G6) are replaced by one 66-check list with stable identifiers quoted in the text.
* The consistent prior is no longer required to be a product measure; the hidden sign s is part of Ω and the defeat statement is pointwise in ω.
