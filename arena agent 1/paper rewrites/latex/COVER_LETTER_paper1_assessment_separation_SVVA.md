Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, **"The Limits of Compensatory Aggregation: A Formal Separation of Weak and Strong Sustainability Assessment,"** for consideration in *Set-Valued and Variational Analysis*.

**What the paper does.** Sustainability assessment aggregates heterogeneous capital stocks and floors into single composite indices, on the premise that a deficit in one dimension can be compensated by a surplus in another. This paper formalises that premise and shows it can fail in a way that is *structural* rather than a matter of weighting. On a typed transition datum — per-moiety floors plus an action menu — under exact-tube semantics (a transition is safe only if every state along the path, not merely the endpoint, satisfies the constraints), I prove a quantifier-separation theorem: for every non-negative scalarisation weight there is some action that keeps the *aggregate* floor non-negative along its worst-case tube, while **no** single action keeps all *typed* floors non-negative along theirs. The per-weight accepted set strictly contains the common-plan accepted set, and the discrepancy region splits by a resource threshold into a rescue region and a nonempty-interior impossibility region. The genuine acceptance gap is exactly that impossibility region. Companion results bound the separation: an erasure datum on which a later interval removes the gap; a blend theorem under which menu convexification closes the gap precisely at the compensatory region; and a converse showing that discrete time-sharing (whose visited set is the union of the action tubes) does *not* close it — so the separation is a property of the convexity of the action space, not of temporal mixing. Continuum statements are proved in full, and the rational witness is verified by exact-integer computation.

**Why it matters.** The weak-versus-strong-sustainability debate is usually conducted as a clash of normative doctrines on substitutability. This paper shows the divergence survives translation into assessment mechanics, at the level of a theorem about the standard aggregation operators on a common action menu. Its concrete consequence is: an index can be sound at the level of accounting and still over-certify at the level of assessment, because certification is a quantifier statement about *transitions* rather than a property of a single number. Where separately-binding floors matter, per-floor reporting is therefore a detection requirement and not merely a presentation preference — the aggregate alone cannot distinguish the rescue region from the impossibility region. This is a formal contribution to a live, high-stakes methodological debate that spans ecological economics, indicator design, and policy.

**Why it is appropriate for *Set-Valued and Variational Analysis*.** The substance of the paper is set-valued and variational: the accepted-state identities are intersections and unions of set-valued maps; the separation of the aggregate and typed certification is an inclusion/quantifier statement about families of sets parameterised by a weight; the impossibility region and the rescue set are the kind of set-theoretic decomposition that falls naturally out of viability and tangent-cone analysis; and the blend-collapse theorem and its converse are precisely about convexification, non-convexity of the action space, and how temporal (as opposed to spatial) convexification behaves. The machinery — closed-set identities, nonempty-interior region arguments, exact-finite verification — sits squarely within the journal's remit. I have also chosen the MSC codes (49J53, 93B03) to reflect the set-valued/multivalued and control-theoretic character of the results.

**Fit and originality.** The paper is positioned honestly: it claims a separation theorem for a dynamic, exact-tube certification setting, and it explicitly disclaims a universal ranking of sustainability doctrines, any claim about particular environmental boundaries, and any empirical result about a specific resource system. To my knowledge this is the first explicit exact-tube witness for this dynamic separation in a compensatory/non-compensatory assessment setting; the necessary novelty qualifications are stated in the paper itself.

The manuscript is self-contained (with a supplementary package for the partial-status extensions), and I would be glad to provide the exact-integer verification scripts and any further material on request. I confirm the work is not under consideration elsewhere, is original, and I have no conflicts of interest.
