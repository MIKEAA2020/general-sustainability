**Author:** Amin Abaee (Independent Researcher)

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, **"Governance delay and the stability of harvested stocks: mobilising and protective feedback rules, and the review interval as a design parameter,"** for consideration in *Theoretical Ecology*.

**What the paper does.** The delays that destabilise renewable-resource systems have been studied almost exclusively inside the ecology — maturation, recruitment, self-limitation. This paper analyses the delay that sits elsewhere: **governance delay**, the lag from an institution's observation of a decline to the response that finally acts on it. A three-state model — harvested stock, filtered deficit memory, bounded effort — compares two feedback rules: the **mobilising rule** (effort grows as more effort is deployed) and the **protective rule** (a quota-tracking law restoring harvest toward a cap), first under a continuous deployment delay, then under periodic review, where the review interval becomes a sample-and-hold control.

**The results are sharp and, in places, counter-intuitive.** For the mobilising rule, *intermediate* delay stabilises the equilibrium: two subcritical Hopf crossings — interval-certified near 3.7 and 150 yr — bound the delay window in which the lag itself holds the loop stable. For the protective rule the loop gain stays below one at the calibrated point: the equilibrium is exponentially stable at every delay — a no-Hopf theorem. Under annual review the two rules respond oppositely: protective review remains stable — its apparent 2.3-yr threshold is a discretisation artefact — while mobilising review is unstable, restabilising only above 6.5 yr through a Neimark–Sacker-type crossing. The attractor topology is five-regime, with two folds certified at the discrete collocation level. The practical message for managed renewable resources: *more frequent assessment is not always safer* — the review interval is a local spectral design parameter.

**Why *Theoretical Ecology*.** The paper extends a modelling tradition this journal has published directly: Adamson and Hilker (2020, *Theoretical Ecology* 13, 425–434) showed that delayed *knowledge* of the harvested population state destabilises bioeconomic equilibria and induces resource-harvester cycles. This manuscript moves the delay one step further along the governance loop — from what harvesters know to what institutions do about it — and makes the review cadence itself the analysed control. The framework is stock-agnostic by construction; the documented northern-cod timeline (annual assessment, the 1992 moratorium, the 2024 reopening) grounds scales, not coefficients.

The manuscript is complete at 45 pages including proofs and one figure, with an electronic supplement carrying the certification records; every computation is deterministic and re-executable from the deposited code and data. The paper is a mathematical parameterisation, not a calibration to a named fishery, and is explicit about which statements are certified, which are numerical records, and which remain open. I confirm the work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and I have no conflicts of interest.
