\begin{abstract}
The obstruction calculus says, given a monitoring structure, why viability
fails; governance needs the converse --- what must be measured for viability
to be possible. This paper, the D8 item of the generalization catalogue,
develops the design reading on the programme's working systems. The fibre
condition --- every observation fibre must contain states whose
floor-servable instrument sets intersect --- is proved necessary and, in the
intersection form, necessary-and-sufficient for one-step admissibility of
every singleton belief (verified over all $15$ partitions of the four-state
target region); the pairwise form of the rule is shown by an exact
three-codex instance to be \emph{necessary but not sufficient} off
convexity --- the one-dimensional Helly protection fails for non-convex
instrument codices, with the intersection certificate surviving. Three
design rules follow as exact identities: the delay rule (an indicator is
acceptable only with period beating worst-case exit --- viability at
deadline $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is exactly the identity
$z_0\ge1+T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, equivalently
$T_{\mathrm{obs}}<\sigma^\ast(z_0)=z_0-1$, evaluated exactly on all $48$
grid cells; the rule rejects wherever exit beats the review period), the
bias rule (a biased reading is acceptable only under correction: the
canonical uncorrected law's $\tfrac{21}{100}$ drift bound, the corrected
law's drift identically zero), and the aggregation rule (an aggregate index
is acceptable only if its fibres do not merge policy-incompatible states:
the audited belief is aggregate-nonviable, $z_1$-only-viable). The coarsest
admissible monitoring of the target region costs exactly two cells
($7$ of $15$ partitions admissible, enumerated exhaustively), by
exhaustive enumeration (8/8 check families).
\end{abstract}

\section{The fibre condition}

For a state $x$ let $\mathcal R(x)$ be its floor-servable instrument set.
For an observation structure with fibres $F$:

\begin{proposition}[Intersection form: necessary and sufficient]
\label{prop:fibre}
A structure --- a partition of the target region --- is one-step
admissible iff every fibre $F$ of the partition satisfies
$\bigcap_{x\in F}\mathcal R(x)\neq\varnothing$; equivalently, iff every
belief the structure can present --- each fibre, viewed as the information
state of its reading --- is one-step admissible. In particular a state
$x$ whose fibre is the singleton $\{x\}$ is one-step admissible exactly
when $\mathcal R(x)\neq\varnothing$. The pairwise form
($\mathcal R(x)\cap\mathcal R(y)\neq\varnothing$ for all $x,y\in F$) is
necessary but not sufficient in general.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Necessity and sufficiency of the intersection form are the read-then-act
requirement itself: the cell's one instrument must serve all of its states,
and a belief presented by the structure is admissible exactly when its
fibre is servable. For the pairwise claim: on non-convex codices three
pairwise-intersecting sets can have empty total intersection; the exact
instance below realizes this, so pairwise compatibility cannot imply the
existence of a common instrument. (In one dimension with convex sets,
Helly's theorem would protect the pairwise form --- the instance shows
exactly where that protection ends.)
\end{proof}

On the audited target --- four states of the two-patch system (Abaee 2026a,
Section 8; instruments $u\in\{1,2\}$ protecting one patch, damage $2$ on
the other) --- the floor-servable instrument sets are
$\mathcal R(1,2)=\{1\}$, $\mathcal R(2,1)=\{2\}$,
$\mathcal R(2,2)=\{1,2\}$, $\mathcal R(3,1)=\{2\}$.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the within-target reading]
\label{rem:scope}
The structures audited here are partitions of the target region itself, so
every fibre lies within the target by construction. For a general
observation map whose fibres cross the target boundary, the intersection
test must range over the \emph{whole} fibre --- a state outside the target
bounds the admissible action exactly as one inside --- and the full fibre
criterion (Abaee 2026a, Proposition 7) governs. The within-target form is
a design-space restriction, not a claim about crossing fibres.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Dynamic caveat]
Singleton fibre-admissibility is necessary only: a structure admissible for
every singleton belief can still obstruct a multi-state belief
dynamically. On the audited target the exhaustive search records whether
such an instance exists; either outcome is an exact finding.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Successor cells re-partition, so one-step admissibility of singletons does
not compose into multi-step viability; the dynamic statement is governed by
the recursion, not the static condition.
\end{proof}

\section{Verified record (8/8 exact check families)}

\paragraph{E1.} Over all $15$ partitions of the target
$\{(1,2),(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)\}$: the intersection form is equivalent to
singleton admissibility, instance by instance. The non-convex counter-
instance: $\mathcal R_1=\{\tfrac2{10},\tfrac6{10}\}$,
$\mathcal R_2=\{\tfrac2{10},\tfrac{65}{100}\}$,
$\mathcal R_3=\{\tfrac6{10},\tfrac{65}{100}\}$ --- pairwise intersections
nonempty, total intersection empty.

\paragraph{E2 (an exact absence).} The dynamic caveat's exhaustive search
over $15$ partitions $\times$ target pairs found \emph{no} instance on
this target: the singleton rule happens to be sufficient here; the absence
is recorded as the finding (necessity-only remains the general claim).

\paragraph{E3.} The coarsest admissible monitoring costs exactly $2$ cells
($7$ of $15$ partitions admissible; costs $2,2,3,3,3,3,4$). Two $2$-cell
partitions are minimal: the isolating partition
$\{\{(1,2)\},\{(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)\}\}$ --- the demanding state $(1,2)$
alone, the rest merged --- and the crossing partition
$\{\{(1,2),(2,2)\},\{(2,1),(3,1)\}\}$, which pairs $(1,2)$ with the
flexible state $(2,2)$ instead of isolating it. The remaining five
admissible partitions are refinements of these two (the admissible set is
the upper set they generate); enumeration certifies minimality and lists
all seven.

\paragraph{E4 (delay rule).} On the $48$-cell grid with the declared hold
class: viability at deadline $T_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\{1,2,3\}$ is exactly the
identity $z_0\ge1+T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, i.e.\ $T_{\mathrm{obs}}<\sigma^\ast
(z_0)$ --- monitoring frequency must beat worst-case exit.

\paragraph{E5 (bias rule).} The trap's uncorrected reading drives the
canonical certainty-equivalence law to drift $\ge\tfrac{21}{100}$ (exit);
correction restores drift identically zero --- biased indicators are
acceptable only under correction. (The quantifier over the whole uncorrected
class is the successor architecture's layer-five reading, cited not
re-claimed.)

\paragraph{E6 (aggregation rule).} The audited belief
$\{(1,2),(2,1)\}$ is nonviable under the aggregate index and viable under
the $z_1$-only index: aggregate indicators are acceptable only if their
fibres do not merge policy-incompatible states.

\paragraph{E7 (the seven admissible partitions).} Exhaustive enumeration
lists all seven admissible partitions of the fifteen --- the two minimal
$2$-cell partitions of E3, four refinements at $3$ cells, and the discrete
partition --- and certifies the upper-set structure (every admissible
partition refines an admissible $2$-cell partition).

\paragraph{E8 (singleton equivalence, two computations).} The equivalence
of Proposition~\ref{prop:fibre} is verified by two independent code paths:
fibre-intersection tests and one-step belief-viability of each presented
fibre, instance by instance over all fifteen partitions.

\section{What is not claimed}

No sensor-cost theory is developed (the cost function is cell count); no
informational-efficiency or mechanism-design claims; the delay rule is
stated for the audited hold class, not for general exit-time values
beyond the successor's; the design search is exhaustive over the
fifteen-partition target, not over general partition lattices (Bell-number
growth is acknowledged as the combinatorial wall). Non-convexity enters
only as the recorded counterinstance --- no general non-convex design
theory is claimed. Real monitoring systems involve noise and delay
stochastics, which belong to the stochastic selector's setting.
