**Title:** Aggregate Indices and Transition Safety: A Quantifier-Order Separation Between Scalarized and Coordinate-Wise Feasibility

**Journal:** *Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control*
**JEL codes:** C61; C62; D81; Q01; Q56

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, **"Aggregate Indices and Transition Safety: A Quantifier-Order Separation Between Scalarized and Coordinate-Wise Feasibility,"** for consideration in the *Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control*.

**What the paper does.** An authority that must carry out a transition while satisfying several separately binding service constraints — floors that each must hold along the entire path, not merely at reporting dates — can be evaluated in two ways. A scalarized evaluator aggregates the floors into one weighted index; a coordinate-wise evaluator requires each floor to hold. The paper shows that the two evaluators can diverge in a way that is structural rather than a matter of weighting: on an explicit rational transition datum there is an open region of states on which *every* nonnegative weighting licenses some transition plan, while *no* single plan keeps all floors nonnegative along the whole path. The acceptance gap is exactly the set of states whose reserve cannot finance a staged transition. Convexifying the plan menu (fractional allocation of control flows) closes the gap precisely at the scalarized-feasible region, whereas discrete time-sharing does not — so the separation is a property of the convexity of the action space, not of either doctrine. The paper gives the general mechanism behind this instance: for any finite menu of plans, the acceptance gap is the part of the convex hull of the plans' required margins that no single plan dominates. Exact per-weight licensing thresholds and a closed-form reserve threshold complete the characterization. Continuum statements are proved in full (proofs collected in the appendix), and the rational witness is verified by exact-integer computation.

**The core mechanism, in plain terms.** Consider two legally separate service obligations, such as minimum staffing levels in two hospital wards. A rapid reorganization temporarily draws down one ward's surplus staffing; the alternative rapid reorganization draws down the other ward's; a gradual reorganization avoids both drawdowns but requires a reserve budget. A regulator who reads only a weighted aggregate of the two staffing surpluses may find that, for *any* weighting, some reorganization keeps the weighted surplus nonnegative — while no single reorganization keeps both wards above their legal minima, and the reserve budget is too small to phase the change gradually. The weighted aggregate is lossless at any fixed instant, yet it over-certifies at the level of assessment, because certification is a statement about transitions, not about a number. The paper formalizes precisely this failure, locates it, and shows that running the two reorganizations simultaneously in a fractional mix restores a single plan acceptable to every regulator, while merely alternating between them does not.

**Why JEDC.** The substance of the paper is dynamic and control-theoretic. The two criteria are compared as robust transition-safety problems on a common datum under path-wise constraints; the separation is a quantifier noncommutation between common-plan acceptance (commitment before the weight is known) and per-weight acceptance (choice after the weight is observed), the assessment-theoretic instance of the pure-versus-mixed-strategy minimax pattern, with the value of information about the evaluation weight made explicit. The result connects directly to the viability-theory and dynamic-sustainability literature that this journal has carried (Martinet, 2011; Cairns and Martinet, 2014; Doyen and Gajardo, 2020), and to the robust-optimization treatment of here-and-now versus wait-and-see decisions. The economic framing — minimum-service constraints, liquidity-financed phasing, audited-snapshot versus continuous compliance — is developed in the introduction and the policy implications.

**Method and scope.** All statements are for finite-horizon exact-tube data with specified disturbance sets and are proved analytically; proofs are collected in an appendix (general results, the benchmark economy, and the rescue threshold). The finite rational witness is independently checked by deterministic exact-integer computation (25 checks on a 31³ = 29,791-state grid); the verification code and results are archived at Zenodo (https://zenodo.org/records/22545740). The paper makes no empirical claims about any particular resource system. Related companion studies are cited formally for related work only, and no result of the paper depends on them: the manuscript is self-contained.

I confirm that the manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and that all authors have approved the submission. There are no competing interests.
