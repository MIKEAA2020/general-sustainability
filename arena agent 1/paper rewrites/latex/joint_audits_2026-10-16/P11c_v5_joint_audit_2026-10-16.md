## Scientific and editorial assessment

P11c v5 now has a coherent technical architecture: a finite belief game, explicit observation/protocol/register axes, a theorem with restricted inclusion assumptions, exact enumerations for the worked finite system, and separate rational continuous examples. The contribution is strongest where the general statement and computation meet: fixed-convention monotonicity; closed forms for the two no-repeat registers; statewise factoring under decentralized unanimity; graph adequacy with a Helly limitation; and the explicitly indexed timing/certificate examples.

The work should continue to distinguish **general theorem**, **instance-specific exact enumeration**, **analytic proof**, **Lean source declaration**, and **illustrative rational example**. The current text sometimes compresses those categories in its verification rhetoric. Correcting that rhetoric strengthens rather than weakens the paper: the exact Python census is valuable evidence in its own right, and the Lean theorem can be presented accurately without implying a build that was not performed.

## Overall conclusion

The v4 reviewers found worthwhile problems in the old draft, but v5 resolves the principal numerical disputes and nearly all structural/presentation objections. The apparent 33/43 timing contradiction compares different predicates; the 24/26/25/28 and 26/26/27/28 counts compare different register conventions; 36 safe pairs and 28 viable pairs have different populations; and the graph, Helly, dual-margin and drift limitations are now stated. The only verified current weaknesses are the overstatement of PDF-number coverage, the unrebuilt Lean layer and a mutable code-availability reference. These merit a precise disclosure/metadata correction, not a new scientific edition.
