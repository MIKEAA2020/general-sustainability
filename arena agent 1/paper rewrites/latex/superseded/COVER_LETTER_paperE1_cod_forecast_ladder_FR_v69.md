# Cover Letter — Paper E1 (v69)

**Title:** Does a surplus-production ladder improve forecasts of Northern cod? A scored retention test against persistence on NAFO 2J3KL
**Author:** Amin Abaee (Independent Researcher, Tehran)
**Journal:** *Fisheries Research*
**Article type:** Research paper (main text 33 pp. preprint format, 9 tables, 6 figures; Supplementary Information S1–S14)
**Keywords:** Northern cod; biomass forecasting; surplus production; persistence benchmark; rolling-origin evaluation; Diebold–Mariano test; model retention; limit reference point

---

Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript for publication in *Fisheries Research*.

**The question.** Prediction-skill diagnostics in stock assessment are almost always computed on the fitted survey index, whereas the quantity advice is written about is the biomass state. The paper asks the state-level question directly for the canonical hard case: does any member of a surplus-production ladder — autonomous Schaefer, depensatory, catch-driven stock-flow, autoregressive-residual, lagged-start — forecast the spawning-stock biomass of Northern cod (NAFO 2J3KL) better than last-value persistence at the one- and five-year horizons advice uses? The test is scored on two assessment series kept strictly separate, the 1983–2015 NCAM series and the 1954–2024 xteNCAM series, under a retention rule that keeps a module only if it lowers rolling-origin RMSE against both persistence and the next-simpler module at both horizons by more than a 5% band.

**The result.** No module is retained. One-year RMSE is 98 kt for persistence against 115–196 kt for the modules on the first series and 84 against 119–206 kt on the second; paired moving-block bootstrap intervals contain zero on the first and exclude it on the second. Three findings turn the negative result into something usable. (i) A period decomposition shows that the pooled deficit is carried by the 1991–1995 collapse, which no member of the class can reproduce at the recorded catch: the 1991–1993 transitions are declines larger than the removals, which a compensatory production map cannot generate for any parameter value (Proposition 1). The lower equilibrium that makes the autonomous map decline is generated by the catch, not by depensation, and the depensation threshold is unidentified. (ii) The autonomous modules forecast the numerical floor at every post-collapse origin because the training-mean catch exceeds the production of a depleted stock; the catch-supplied modules carry a linear low-biomass growth term with one-step skill on one specification only, which does not extrapolate to five years or to the other series. (iii) Under known truth (seven data-generating processes, 200 replicates each, pre-specified before the code was written), the rule retains a true autonomous module in 97% of replicates but a true stock-flow or depensation module in under 15%, and over-retains when the truth lies outside the class — so non-retention is strong evidence against the autonomous module and weak evidence against the others, and the operating characteristics are reported alongside the verdict rather than assumed.

**Why it fits the journal.** The paper is a forecast-comparison study on a policy-relevant stock with a stated decision rule, its uncertainty, its operating characteristics and a mechanism for the result. It supplies a template for the burden-of-proof question — does added structure earn its keep out of sample, on the state, against persistence? — and a negative certificate for one stock on which structural claims have been frequent. The closing section separates the forecast layer from the verdict layer: after 2015 the reference point (884.6 kt on one vintage, 276 kt on the current framework), the assessment vintage and the observing system (the 2022–2023 survey suspension) moved more than any forecast did.

**Protocol status, stated plainly.** The design is a fixed computational protocol with a recorded execution order, not a prospective registration. The five modules, the windows, the rolling-origin RMSE comparison and the persistence bar were coded before the first scoring pass and applied unchanged; the 5% tie band and the comparator declarations for the non-nested modules were formalised after the first scores were seen (neither decides any reported outcome); the variant passes were added in sequence and are listed in order in SI S1. Only the simulation study carries a dated pre-specification (archived with its commit hashes). A one-year catch-timing offset between the estimator and the forecast path was found during re-execution; its effect is quantified in SI S14, the verdict is unchanged, and the archived numbers remain the ones reported.

**Reproducibility.** All input data, scripts, result files, the simulation pre-specification and an environment record are archived (GitHub MIKEAA2020/general-sustainability; Zenodo 22553609). Results have been reproduced in three environments; a standard-library verification script regenerates every number of Section 4.7 and SI S11–S12 from the archived data and passes 76/76 checks. The manuscript cites four companion manuscripts of mine by title where they are used (DOIs given in the reference list where available).

The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and I have no competing interests to declare.
