# Paper 1 v64 — Key points (for the submission form; JMCDA has no highlights section)

* Compensatory (weight-adaptive) and noncompensatory (common-plan) acceptance of a transition differ only by quantifier order; at a fixed trajectory the full-cone aggregate is lossless.
* For any finite plan menu the acceptance gap is the part of the convex hull of the plans' required margins that no single plan dominates; convexifying the menu closes it exactly.
* On an explicit rational datum the gap has nonempty interior and splits at a closed-form reserve threshold κ* = 1 − x into rescuable and impossible states; exact weight-ratio thresholds give each plan's licensing range.
* Blending plans closes the gap; alternating them in time does not.
* Every positive-elasticity CES aggregator falsely certifies an exact interval of gap states (critical floors 1 < 5/4 < √2 < φ < √3 < … → 2); only the Leontief member is uniformly safe. A shared shock reduces the gap by an exact 1/2-cut without removing it.
* Where separately binding floors matter, per-floor reporting along the path, or a verified common plan, is a detection requirement, not a presentation preference.
