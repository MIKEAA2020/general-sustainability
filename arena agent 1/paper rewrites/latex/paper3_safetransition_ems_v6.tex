\title{SafeTransition: exact rational certification of transition safety for
sustainability assessment}

\author[aff]{Amin Abaee}
\address[aff]{Independent Researcher,
\href{https://orcid.org/0000-0002-0019-1842}{ORCID: 0000-0002-0019-1842},
\href{mailto:amin\_abaee@ut.ac.ir}{amin\_abaee@ut.ac.ir}}

\begin{abstract}
SafeTransition, a Python library for certifying the transition safety of
sustainability assessments, is presented. The library implements the
assessment-operator framework: typed floor constraints evaluated on
supplied exact or certified conservative reachability tubes, scalarized
aggregate operators at arbitrary positive
weights, and backward recursions over state and belief spaces. Infeasibility
is returned as a Farkas certificate, a nonnegative multiplier vector with
vanishing weighted constraint sum and negative weighted bound; certificates
serialize to JSON for an independent checker sharing no code with the
library. All computations run in exact rational arithmetic
with no third-party runtime dependencies; floating point appears only in
rendered graphics. Twenty-four exact checks re-derive the companion deposit's benchmark,
and a scaling study over five parameterized families verifies
closed-form answers up to 256-variable eliminations and margins with
321-bit denominators. A single-file dashboard renders
the readings with embedded verification provenance. The library is
distributed under the MIT licence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Composite indicators occupy a central place in sustainability monitoring.
Aggregates reduce heterogeneous floor constraints on income, biomass, and
social outcomes to a single weighted reading, and that reading is what
dashboards, statutory reviews, and public communications display. The
practice rests on a long modelling tradition, and its governance follows
established frameworks for good model development and evaluation practice
\citep{Jakeman2006}. It also rests on a mathematical step that
is easy to audit and easy to overlook: a weighted sum is certified positive
at every audited snapshot, and the system behind the snapshots is declared
safe.

The companion manuscripts show that this step can fail, and characterize
exactly when it fails. In the assessment-operator framework of \citet{Abaee2026a},
a noncompensatory typed operator --- which requires every floor to hold on
the full trajectory, not at endpoints alone --- can reject every plan that a
scalarized aggregate operator licenses at a given weight; the separation is
exhibited on an explicit rational witness datum and propagated through
backward recursions. A resource-transition benchmark realizes the witness as
a regulated fishery transition and verifies every quoted number in exact
rational arithmetic. Under incomplete observation, the obstruction calculus
of \citet{Abaee2026b} complements viability assessment from the other side: it
certifies that no observation-based policy is viable, through finitely
checkable certificates --- the common-action obstruction with an explicit
Farkas multiplier vector, the observation-fibre criterion, and a
finite-horizon belief-space recursion that is sound and complete in finite
systems. These results are mathematical; they describe what should be
computed. They do not yet describe a tool that computes it.

This is the gap SafeTransition addresses. Reading a composite dashboard
today, an analyst can recompute the aggregate with ordinary floating-point
tooling, but has no standard way to re-derive the safety verdict behind it,
to check that an infeasible plan menu is infeasible by certificate rather
than by solver exit code, or to see which weight ratios license which plans.
Numerical viability and control toolboxes compute kernels and invariant sets
to high precision; multi-criteria decision analysis packages aggregate and
rank; formal verification systems and satisfiability-modulo-theories solvers
certify finite-state models and general constraint systems against their own
specification semantics. What is missing in the intersection is a small,
auditable layer in which the verdicts themselves --- admissibility of a plan
at a state, licensing at a weight, emptiness of a menu, emptiness of an
epistemic kernel --- are returned as exact rational objects that a third
party can re-verify by hand or by machine, with the assessment semantics
(floors, weights, licensed plan sets, partially observed beliefs) carried by
the verdicts themselves rather than re-encoded.

This paper presents SafeTransition, a Python library that provides this
layer. The related-software positioning of the paper was compiled against a
preserved record of twelve verbatim web queries with dated result records
(q1--q12, 2026), archived in the verification deposit; Section~\ref{sec:related}
reports what the searches covered and found. The library implements the five assessment operators and their
accepted-state sets, the typed finite-graph backward recursion, the
belief-space robust epistemic recursion, and the finitely checkable
certificate family: exact rational Fourier--Motzkin elimination with
provenance tracking, returning Farkas certificates and infeasibility
margins; the finite common-action obstruction with a minimal conflicting
subfamily; and the observation-fibre criterion. Every computation runs in
exact rational arithmetic; floats appear only in rendered graphics. The
library re-derives, in twenty-four exact checks, the resource-transition
benchmark of the verification deposit, and renders the resulting readings as
a single-file dashboard. It depends on nothing beyond the Python standard
library, which makes the exactness claim structural rather than procedural:
there is no numerical backend whose rounds of approximation would need to be
audited.

The paper is a software description. Section 2 states the mathematical basis
as implemented, with proofs delegated to the companion manuscripts.
Section 3 describes the software architecture and the exact-arithmetic
discipline. Section 4 illustrates the library on the benchmark, on the
dashboard readings, and on the partial-observation demonstrations. Section 5
positions the library relative to existing software, and Section 6 records
availability and requirements. Section 7 concludes.

\section{Mathematical basis}\label{sec:math}

\subsection{Transition data and assessment operators}\label{sec:operators}

A typed transition datum comprises a phase state
$z = (q, x, s_1, \dots, s_n)$ with a phase flag $q$, fund coordinates $x$,
and typed floors $s_i$ normalized to zero; a finite action set; a set of disturbances with, for each action and
disturbance, an exact tube $\mathrm{Tube}(a, d)$ --- the set of states
visited over the review period, given here as piecewise-linear coordinate
paths on breakpoints at which every visited extreme is attained; a
transition-safe set $S$; and a destination set $G$ reached under an endpoint
reset. Actions in the library declare offsets on the breakpoints, together
with an action-indexed worst-case disturbance, so that each tube is the
exact visited set and no outer approximation enters.

Five operators are distinguished, following \citet{Abaee2026a}. For a state $z$,
the noncompensatory typed operator admits action $a$ when every floor holds
on $\mathrm{Tube}(a, d)$ for every disturbance $d$ and the successor lies in
$G$; the scalarized aggregate operator replaces the coordinate-wise
condition by $w \cdot s \geq 0$ at weight $w$ in the positive weight cone;
the exact-tube physical and endpoint-only physical operators weaken the
constraint set; and the typed-endpoint operator evaluates floors at
endpoints only. Because endpoints are contained in tubes and typed sets in
physical sets, the chain
\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z) \;\subseteq\; E_w(z) \;\subseteq\;
E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}(z) \;\subseteq\; E_{\mathrm{end}}(z)
\]
holds for every weight in the positive weight cone and for menus whose plans
share a common disturbance set --- the standing scope conditions of the
operator framework --- and the library verifies it exactly at any state on
request (Section~\ref{sec:api}). The fifth operator continues the
pattern on the typed side: $E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z) \subseteq
E_{\mathrm{end,typ}}(z) \subseteq E_{\mathrm{end}}(z)$, since typed floors
constrain more than endpoints and more than physics alone. The accepted-state set
$\mathcal{V}[E] = \{ z : E(z) \neq \emptyset \}$ and the compensatory
accepted set $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}} = \bigcap_w \mathcal{V}_w$ are
derived from the operators; the witnessed separation
$\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} \subseteq \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}$, strict in
general, is what a composite dashboard cannot see (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}).

\subsection{Recursions}\label{sec:recursions}

Two backward recursions are implemented. The typed recursion propagates
viability backwards through an explicit state--action graph: a state is
$k$-step viable when some action keeps every visited state inside the safe
set and lands in the $(k-1)$-step viable set. On explicit graphs with
constant-time predicates the recursion is polynomial in graph size and
horizon. The belief-space recursion addresses partially observed systems,
following the robust epistemic construction of \citet{Abaee2026b}: states carry
observation labels, an action is admissible for a belief (a set of states
consistent with the observations so far) when no state in its observation
fibre enters a violation en route, and the belief updates to the set of
labels reachable under the action. The recursion returns the sets
$\mathcal{W}_k$ of $k$-step viable beliefs. Memberships certify viability in
all finite systems; completeness --- a belief outside $\mathcal{W}_N$ admits
no viable observation-based policy over the horizon --- holds when the
observation is injective on reachable states, the exactness condition stated
with the interface. A failure explainer accompanies a non-viable belief with
its per-action counterexample: the exact reason each admissible-in-isolation
action fails --- a fibre state entering a violation en route, or a
post-belief that is itself not viable at the previous level.
Post-observation recourse failures --- beliefs in
$\mathcal{W}_1 \setminus \mathcal{W}_2$ --- are the signature output
(Section~\ref{sec:partial}).

\subsection{Certificates}\label{sec:certificates}

Infeasibility is certified, not merely reported. For a stacked rational
system $A u \leq b$, the library runs Fourier--Motzkin elimination with
provenance tracking: every derived row carries its multiplier vector over
the original rows, and when a contradiction $0 \leq c$, $c < 0$ is derived,
the derived multipliers $\lambda \geq 0$ with $\lambda^{\top} A = 0$ and
$\lambda^{\top} b < 0$ are returned as a Farkas certificate, normalized to
sum to one (the certificate itself is invariant to positive scaling; the
infeasibility margin $-\lambda^{\top} b > 0$ is defined by this
normalization). The certificate is
re-verified exactly before being returned. The finite common-action
obstruction fires when the safe-action sets of pairwise-compatible states
intersect emptily while each is nonempty --- every state is individually
viable under full information, yet no observation-based policy is viable;
the library reports a minimal conflicting subfamily. The observation-fibre
criterion decides whether an exact observation-only certifier can exist at
all: one exists if and only if safe-set membership is constant on
observation fibres, and a violating fibre is reported otherwise.

\subsection{The exact-arithmetic discipline}\label{sec:exactness}

Every assessment, recursion, and certificate is computed in exact rational
arithmetic. Rationals enter either as integers, as decimal strings, or as
explicit numerator--denominator pairs; no implicit conversion from binary
floating point exists anywhere in the library, so an input's exactness is
structural, not a matter of reviewer discipline. Floats occur in exactly two
places, both rendering: SVG geometry in the dashboard and matplotlib
coordinates in figure scripts. Checks never read floats. The distinction is
the library's design-centre claim: outputs are verifiable, not merely
computed. A Farkas certificate, a licensing threshold, or a benchmark check
can be re-verified by any rational-arithmetic implementation --- including
pencil and paper --- and the test suite exercises exactly this: every
certificate returned by the eliminator is re-verified against its defining
identities before being returned, and the benchmark checks re-derive each
deposited value symbolically (for example $\sigma(16/5) = 1088/125$ for the
sustained-yield quota of the Schaefer realization). The discipline is not
merely hygienic: the suite includes an adversarial instance on which exact
and floating-point verdicts disagree. The trough condition
$49 \cdot (1/49) - 1 = 0$ holds exactly and licenses the plan; its binary
floating-point evaluation yields $-1.11 \times 10^{-16}$ and would
unlicense it. The library rejects floating-point inputs structurally, so
the exact verdict is the only one it can return. Exactness also scales
gracefully where floating point degrades: on a dyadic chain whose planted
infeasibility gap is $2^{-t}$, the certified margin keeps $t + 1$
denominator bits at $t = 320$, long after binary floating point has
rounded the gap away (Section~\ref{sec:scaling}).

\subsection{Interfaces and complexity}\label{sec:api}

The application programming interface mirrors the mathematics:
\texttt{E(datum, z, mode, w)} returns the action set of an operator at a
state; \texttt{check\_chain} verifies the operator chain exactly;
\texttt{belief\_backward} returns the belief-viability sets
$\mathcal{W}_k$ and \texttt{explain\_belief\_failure} the per-action
counterexample for a belief outside them; \texttt{certify\_polyhedron}
returns a feasibility verdict with its certificate;
\texttt{weight\_partition} returns the complete licensing arrangement along
the weight ratio as a serializable certificate;
\texttt{compute} returns the dashboard readings at a
state, weight, and read plan. The typed recursion on explicit graphs is
polynomial in graph size and horizon; the Fourier--Motzkin eliminator is
exponential in the worst case, as exact elimination inherently is, which is
why the certificate family targets the small stacked systems that transition
menus produce (a handful of constraints; the witness instances eliminate in
milliseconds). Belief enumeration is bounded --- the bound is a parameter
(default 4\,096 reachable beliefs) whose exhaustion raises an exception
carrying no partial results; the bound affects exploration completeness,
never the soundness of returned memberships.

The precision--performance trade-off is measured, not asserted. A scaling
study sweeps five parameterized instance families whose answers are known
by construction and asserted at every size: stacked Fourier--Motzkin
chains with a planted infeasibility gap (chain length up to $k = 256$
variables), dyadic coefficient growth (exact margins certified at
denominators $2^{t}$ up to $t = 320$), typed recursions on layered graphs
(up to 21\,000 states at horizon 20), cloned action menus (up to 512
actions through all five operators), and belief systems whose reachable
belief count is exactly $2^{m}$ (the default enumeration bound of 4\,096
is reached exactly at $m = 12$ and exceeded at $m = 13$, where the call
raises with no partial results). The study records wall-clock runtime,
peak traced memory, eliminator row statistics (rows generated, peak
working rows), certificate sizes and maximal numerator/denominator bit
lengths, and independent-checker runtime
(Table~\ref{tab:scaling}). Growth is unremarkable in the right sense:
elimination is quadratic in the planted instances (the $k$-variable chain
generates $k$ rows and terminates at the contradiction; the certificate
JSON reaches 335\,kB at $k = 256$, with 13-bit coefficients, and the
independent checker re-verifies it in 0.29\,s); the typed recursion is
linear in the state count (21\,000 states in 2.1\,s); belief enumeration
is exponential in $m$, as the reachable set itself is, and the bound
fires where designed. Coefficient growth inflates certificate size, not
verdict times; the exact margin's closed form
$(1/5 - (k-1)/4096)/(k+1)$ is verified at every $k$.

\section{Illustrative examples}\label{sec:examples}

\subsection{The resource-transition benchmark}\label{sec:benchmark}

The benchmark realizes the witness datum as a regulated fishery transition:
stock biomass with Schaefer surplus $\sigma(B) = rB(1 - B/K)$ ($r = 4$,
$K = 10$, $B_{\mathrm{lim}} = 2$), a pulse-plus-closed-season quota plan,
a mid-season heatwave strike, and a staged rebuild financed by a fund
buy-back. Running \texttt{safetransition verify} executes twenty-four exact
checks that re-derive the deposited verification values: the witness datum
and tube tables; quota admissibility on both disturbance branches
($0 \leq H^* \leq 13$, peak $1463/125$); tube-status certification --- the
declared piecewise-linear plan tubes are exact by construction, while the
Schaefer realization's tubes are certified conservative outer enclosures,
the enclosure following from monotonicity of $\sigma$ on the visited
biomass interval ($\sigma \geq 528/125 \geq 4$ on the adverse recovery
leg); the
index blindness of the composite reading at $w = (1, 1)$, whose minimum
stays at $2/5 > 0$ along the licensed plan while the ecological floor
reaches $-4/5 < 0$ mid-transition; the licensing thresholds

\subsection{Dashboard readings}\label{sec:readings}

The \texttt{compute} interface returns, at a state and weight, the per-plan
floor minima under each plan's characteristic disturbance, the composite
index along the read plan, the licensing verdicts at the weight ratio
$r = w_2/w_1$, the rescue threshold, and the index-blindness alarm, which
fires exactly when the composite minimum stays positive while a floor
minimum is negative. In this subsection the pairs $(1/2,\, 1/2)$ and
$(3/2,\, 0)$ denote $(x, \kappa^*(x))$ --- fund level and shortfall --- not
the phase-state coordinates $(x, s_1, s_2)$ of Section~\ref{sec:api}. At the witness state $(x, s_1, s_2) = (1/2, 6/5, 6/5)$
with $w = (1,1)$, the alarm fires: the readings reproduce the benchmark
separation as a dashboard statement (Fig.~\ref{fig:readings}). The
\texttt{dashboard} module renders the readings, the benchmark status, and
any certificates as a single HTML file with inline SVG and CSS and no
external resources, so the file can be attached to a review, emailed, or
archived with a statutory record and remains readable offline. The
dashboard is an audit-delivery mechanism rather than a novelty claim, and
it is bound to the certificate schema: a verification-provenance table
embeds the library version, the independent checker's version and re-run
command, the datum identifier, and the SHA-256 hash of every
certificate's exact serialized JSON, expandable to the serialization
itself. Rendering is deterministic --- a pure function of the inputs,
with no timestamps --- so a re-render from the same inputs is
byte-identical and the embedded hashes are checkable against the emitted
certificate files. From the reviewer's perspective this means the reading
and its verification arrive together: thresholds, alarms, certificates,
hashes, and the re-run command in one file.

\subsection{Partial observation and certificates}\label{sec:partial}

The bundled demonstrations exercise the certificate family on the companion
manuscript's instances. The three-state recourse failure runs the
belief-space recursion on a system whose root belief is one-step viable and
exits at the second step under every observation-based policy: the root
belief lies in $\mathcal{W}_1 \setminus \mathcal{W}_2$, witnessed only by
the recursion. The observation-fibre criterion reports the merged fibre on
which safe-set membership varies, ruling out any exact observation-only
certifier. The stacked menu window $u \leq 2/5$, $-u \leq -3/5$ returns the
Farkas certificate $\lambda = (1/2, 1/2)$ with infeasibility margin exactly
$1/10$, re-verified against $\lambda^{\top} A = 0$ and
$\lambda^{\top} b < 0$ before being returned. The mapping to the
obstruction calculus is row-explicit: row $[1]\; u \leq 2/5$ is the first
state's menu cap, row $[-1]\; u \leq -3/5$ is the second state's floor
requirement, the certificate's multiplier vector weights the states'
restrictions equally, and the empty common menu the obstruction reports
is exactly the infeasibility these two rows certify --- one Farkas object,
addressable row by row to the states whose compatibility it denies. The pooled-kernel identity of the deposited
double-integrator instance, weights $(3/8, 5/16, 5/16)$ with
$\sum_j \lambda_j n_j = 0$, is checked exactly. On the recourse-failure
system, \texttt{explain\_belief\_failure} returns the counterexample
object: at the root belief both actions are admissible in isolation, and
each fails because its post-belief is not one-step viable --- the
machine-checkable witness behind the
$\mathcal{W}_1 \setminus \mathcal{W}_2$ verdict.

The weight-space analysis is complete rather than pointwise. The
\texttt{weight\_partition} interface returns the full arrangement of
licensed plan sets along the ratio $r = w_2/w_1$ as a serializable
certificate. For the benchmark floors the arrangement is the three regions
the readings display: SLOW-only on $[0, 2/3)$, both plans licensed on
$[2/3, 3/2]$, FAST-only on $(3/2, \infty)$. Across the rational family of
floor pairs $(s_1, s_2)$ and dip depths the arrangement has three regimes:
for $\mathrm{dip} < s_1 + s_2$ (the benchmark's) the FAST threshold
$(\mathrm{dip} - s_1)/s_2$ precedes the SLOW threshold
$s_1/(\mathrm{dip} - s_2)$; for $\mathrm{dip} > s_1 + s_2$ the thresholds
swap and an unlicensed gap opens around $r = 1$; at equality the middle
region degenerates to the single point where both plans bind
simultaneously. The independent checker re-derives, from the certificate
alone, the thresholds, region coverage and inclusivity, the licensed set at
a sampled ratio in each region, and the binding witness constraint at each
boundary.

\section{Relation to existing software}\label{sec:related}

SafeTransition complements three established families of tools, and borrows
from each. Numerical viability kernels and reachability tools (for the
viability-theoretic tradition, and for general-purpose simulation frameworks
in the environmental modelling literature, e.g.\ Raven
\citep{Craig2020} and FloPy \citep{Bakker2016}) compute set-valued safety
objects to high floating-point precision with impressive scalability; they
answer \emph{how large is the safe set}, where SafeTransition answers
\emph{is this verdict verifiable}, for the small, structured, rational
instances that assessment menus produce. Multi-criteria and sensitivity
analysis packages --- the SAFE toolbox \citep{Pianosi2015}, SALib
\citep{Herman2017}, SPOTPY \citep{Houska2015} --- share the library's
workflow-oriented design: model-independent interfaces, worked examples,
and benchmark functions; SPOTPY's demonstration that one package can carry
an entire family of methods with a minimal amount of glue code is the
presentation standard SafeTransition aims at. Modular environmental
simulators with public source code and regression suites --- Pywr
\citep{Tomlinson2020} and airGR \citep{Coron2017} --- inform the
availability discipline: permissive licensing, distribution through package
indexes, and a test suite executed on continuous integration. None of the environmental
families returns certificates or computes in exact arithmetic. General-purpose
provers do both in their own domains: the z3 satisfiability-modulo-theories
solver \citep{deMoura2008} decides linear real arithmetic exactly over the
rationals and produces checkable unsatisfiability cores, and the Fourier--Motzkin
projection library pyfme \citep{Caron2022} performs exact symbolic
elimination over SymPy rationals with Imbert acceleration; probabilistic
model checking in PRISM \citep{Kwiatkowska2011} verifies finite-state
probabilistic models against temporal-logic specifications and returns
counterexample traces at floating-point scale. What none of them ships is
assessment semantics: floors, weight-licensed plan menus, reachability tubes
for transition data, and epistemic viability sets are objects the analyst
must encode afresh, and the encoding, not the prover, then carries the
burden of exactness. Table~\ref{tab:compare} summarizes the comparison; the
twelve verbatim search queries behind it (q1--q12, with dates, result
records, and the mapping to the claims they support) are archived in the
verification deposit \citep{Deposit2026}. The comparison is functional, not
competitive: the families operate at scales and purposes for which
floating-point computation is the right engineering choice, and the exact
layer becomes decisive precisely where verdicts enter statutory or
adversarial settings and must be re-derivable by third parties.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

SafeTransition turns the assessment-operator framework and the obstruction
calculus of the companion manuscripts into auditable software: typed
operators evaluated on exact tubes, recursions over state and belief space,
and infeasibility returned as Farkas certificates with exact margins,
serialized and re-verified by an independent checker sharing no code with
the library. The library's scope is deliberate: it certifies verdicts on
small, explicit, rational instances under exact or certified conservative
tubes, and it makes no predictive claim --- the theorem-level guarantees
are those of the companion manuscripts, and on continuous or
belief-continuous systems the graph construction itself is the modelling
step. Within that scope, the tool addresses a practical gap: readings of
the kind composite dashboards display, and nonviability verdicts of the
kind that justify tightening a plan menu, can carry their certificates with
them wherever verdicts must be re-derivable by third parties. With
independent checking, the scaling evidence, and the comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:related} in place, the tool closes this practical gap
within its stated scope. Extensions
under way are continuous-state data with certified enclosures, the
extension of the weight partition to richer plan menus, and additional
certificate families from the obstruction calculus; the interface
intentionally leaves room for all three.
