\begin{abstract}
The timing certificates of the obstruction calculus assume constant adverse
drift; resource systems rarely decline linearly. This paper, the D9 item of
the generalization catalogue, extends the exit certificate along the
catalogue's three axes and verifies each as an exact identity on the
programme's grids. State-dependent drift: with per-step comparison function
$\alpha$, the last safe step is governed by the comparison integral, and
for $\alpha(q)=q/10$ the exit value recovers the successor's
$\sigma^\ast(q_0)=\max\{k: q_0\ge(\tfrac{10}9)^k\}$ exactly at the powers
--- the constant-drift theory embeds into the comparison-function theory
without remainder on the contraction instance. Nonlinear comparison: for
$\alpha(q)=q^2/10$ the integral bound $10(1-1/q_0)$ dominates the exact
iteration exit time on the whole grid, strictly off the boundary.
Multi-constraint exit: with $q_{\min}=\min_j q_j$ the last safe step of the
jointly declining two-floor system equals $\lfloor 10\,q_{\min}(0)\rfloor$
on the $240$-point grid --- ``at least one floor breached'' certified by the
minimum-slack drift. A scope instance closes the paper honestly: when
$\alpha$ vanishes at the boundary ($\alpha(q)=q/10-\tfrac1{20}$ at
$q=\tfrac12$) the iteration converges to the equilibrium and never exits
--- no finite bound exists and the comparison integral diverges; positivity
of the comparison function at the boundary is load-bearing (6/6 check
families).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and relation to the programme}

Paper 2's finite-time exit certificate supposes
$\sup_u\inf_d D^+q\le-\varepsilon$; the catalogue proposes the three
generalizations adopted here --- state-dependent drift, comparison
functions, and multi-constraint exit through the minimum slack --- noting
that they make the timing layer applicable where decline accelerates near
thresholds. The successor's exit-time values supply the object whose level
sets these bounds reproduce; nothing in the completed items is altered.

\section{Comparison-function exit bounds}

Let the floor margin $q\in(0,a]$ follow the adversarial one-step law
$q_{t+1}\le q_t-\alpha(q_t)$ with $\alpha$ positive on $(0,a]$. Then the
last safe step is bounded by the comparison integral:
\begin{equation*}
t_{\mathrm{exit}}\ \le\ \int_{q_{\mathrm{thr}}}^{q_0}\frac{dq}{\alpha(q)}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{proposition}[Contraction embedding]
For $\alpha(q)=q/10$ (per-step multiplicative decline
$q_{t+1}=(\tfrac9{10})q_t$), the last safe step below $1$ equals
$\max\{k: q_0\ge(\tfrac{10}9)^k\}$ --- the successor's exit-time value
--- with equality at the exact powers $(\tfrac{10}9)^K$ and the recorded
neighbors. The constant-drift theory is the $\alpha(q)=\varepsilon$ case.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The iterate $q_k=q_0(\tfrac9{10})^k$ satisfies $q_k\ge1$ iff
$q_0\ge(\tfrac{10}9)^k$; taking the maximum over $k$ is the successor's
value. The constant case is the arithmetic-progression analogue with the
floor identity of the verified record.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dominance and monotonicity]
For $\alpha(q)=q^2/10$ the bound $10(1-1/q_0)$ dominates the exact
iteration exit time on the grid (strictly off the boundary), and larger
comparison functions give tighter bounds: the bound under
$\alpha_2=q/10$ is at most (strictly, off the boundary) the bound under
$\alpha_1=q/20$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The integral $\int_1^{q_0}10\,q^{-2}dq=10(1-1/q_0)$ upper-bounds the
steps of any path obeying the per-step decline; pointwise dominance of
$1/\alpha$ gives the monotonicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Multi-constraint exit]
With $q_{\min}=\min_j q_j$ and joint decline $z_j'=z_j-\varepsilon$, the
system exits $\mathcal V$ exactly when $q_{\min}<0$, and the last safe
step equals $\lfloor q_{\min}(0)/\varepsilon\rfloor$: a drift condition on
the minimum slack certifies that at least one floor is breached, within a
computed budget.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\min_j$ of equal declines declines equally; the exit event of the vector
system is the negativity of the minimum slack; the identity is the
constant-drift arithmetic in $q_{\min}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Verified record (6/6 exact check families)}

E1 the constant-drift identity (last safe step $=\lfloor10(z_0-1)\rfloor$,
$48$ cells); E2 the contraction embedding at exact powers and
$\tfrac1{100}$-neighbors ($K\le3$); E3 the quadratic-comparison dominance
on the grid with strict domination recorded; E4 the $q_{\min}$ identity on
the $240$-point grid; E5 comparison monotonicity ($\alpha_2$ vs
$\alpha_1$); E6 the scope instance: $\alpha(q)=q/10-\tfrac1{20}$ vanishes
at $q=\tfrac12$, the iteration converges there from above and never
exits, the integral diverges --- no finite bound exists, and the naive
application of the bound is not merely loose but undefined.

\section{What is not claimed}

The comparison-function layer is one-dimensional in the floor margin: no
vector-valued comparison functions, no stochastic comparison theorems (the
stochastic selector's setting), and no converse --- a finite integral bound
is sufficient for exit, and nothing is claimed about necessity. The
audited instances are discrete one-step iterations of the programme's
working systems; continuous-time flows are cited through the successor's
HJB/Isaacs layer, not developed. The scope instance is a property of the
affine example, not a classification of all boundary-vanishing comparison
functions.
