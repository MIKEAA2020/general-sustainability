# Cover Letter — Paper E1

**Title:** Does a surplus-production ladder improve forecasts of Northern cod? A scored test on NAFO 2J3KL
**Author:** Amin Abaee (Independent Researcher)
**Journal:** *Fisheries Research*
**Keywords:** northern cod; biomass forecasting; surplus production; forecast evaluation; stock assessment; prediction skill; negative certificate

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, **"Does a surplus-production ladder improve forecasts of Northern cod? A scored test on NAFO 2J3KL,"** for consideration in *Fisheries Research*.

**What the paper does.** Fisheries assessment builds structure by default, but whether that structure improves out-of-sample forecasts — as opposed to in-sample fit — is a separate, testable question. This paper runs that test on the canonical demanding case, Northern cod (*Gadus morhua*) in NAFO divisions 2J3KL, using the NCAM M-shift SSB series (1983–2015; LRP 884.6 kt) and a second, unpooled specification (xteNCAM, 1954–2024). A scored seven-model surplus-production ladder is compared against last-value persistence and a fixed-window and rolling-origin naive baseline, under a stated retention rule coded before the first scoring pass. Two catch treatments and an autoregressive residual, a one-year delay, and prey-informed variants are all included so that the added structure is held to account.

**Why it matters.** The scoring outcome is stark and counter-intuitive: no structural model beats last-value persistence on the primary out-of-sample score. One-year rolling RMSE is 98 kt for persistence versus 115–206 kt for the structural ladder across both catch treatments; five-year rolling RMSE is 265 kt versus 289–488 kt. The 1991–1995 collapse is missed by every model, and a constant-productivity model with a 1992 catch drop cannot produce the crash. Extra modules that fail identification increase error. On the evidence within this estimator and ladder, the paper supports a *negative certificate* scoped to both: the scored one-step least-squares Schaefer/Allee ladder on these two unpooled series does not beat persistence on the primary score, and unidentified modules increase error. The same retention outcome holds on the unpooled xteNCAM specification.

**Why it is appropriate for *Fisheries Research*.** The paper is a forecast-comparison study with a clear falsifiable design, a frozen protocol, a transparency and reproducibility record, and a policy-relevant stock at its core. It addresses the burden-of-proof question that sits at the centre of the journal's remit: does added model structure earn its keep out-of-sample? The manuscript is deliberately candid about what it does *not* conclude — it reports a forecast comparison and does not conclude that the stock is unsustainable, nor that the evaluation framework is empirically confirmed on this stock. The machine layer verifies the recorded arithmetic and its byte-level reproducibility rather than the class-level incompatibility, which I flag explicitly so the strength of every claim is read correctly.

**Fit and originality.** The originality lies in the scored, multi-model forecast-ladder design applied to the canonical rebuilding-and-depensation case, held to a strict persistence baseline with a stated retention rule, and in the explicit negative certificate scoped to this estimator and ladder. I have kept the claims conservative throughout and stated the scope limitations in the manuscript and abstract, including that the results are specific to the fitted estimator family and the unpooled series used, and are not an empirical verdict on the stock's sustainability.

The manuscript is self-contained and all input data, analysis scripts, and result files are archived in a public repository (https://github.com/MIKEAA2020/general-sustainability), with the frozen model specification archived alongside them. I confirm the work is not under consideration elsewhere, is original, and I have no conflicts of interest.
