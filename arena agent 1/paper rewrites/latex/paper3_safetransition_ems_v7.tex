\title{SafeTransition: exact rational certification of transition safety for
sustainability assessment}

\author[aff]{Amin Abaee}
\address[aff]{Independent Researcher,
\href{https://orcid.org/0000-0002-0019-1842}{ORCID: 0000-0002-0019-1842},
\href{mailto:amin\_abaee@ut.ac.ir}{amin\_abaee@ut.ac.ir}}

\begin{abstract}
SafeTransition, a Python library for certifying the transition safety of
sustainability assessments, is presented. The library implements the
assessment-operator framework: typed floor constraints evaluated on
supplied exact or certified conservative reachability tubes, scalarized
aggregate operators at arbitrary positive
weights, and backward recursions over state and belief spaces. Infeasibility
is returned as a Farkas certificate, a nonnegative multiplier vector with
vanishing weighted constraint sum and negative weighted bound; certificates
serialize to JSON for an independent checker sharing no code with the
library. All computations run in exact rational arithmetic
with no third-party runtime dependencies; floating point appears only in
rendered graphics. Twenty-four exact checks re-derive the companion deposit's benchmark,
and a scaling study over five parameterized families verifies
closed-form answers up to 256-variable eliminations and margins with
321-bit denominators. A single-file dashboard renders
the readings with embedded verification provenance. The library is
distributed under the MIT licence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Composite indicators occupy a central place in sustainability monitoring.
Aggregates reduce heterogeneous floor constraints on income, biomass, and
social outcomes to a single weighted reading, and that reading is what
dashboards, statutory reviews, and public communications display. The
practice rests on a long modelling tradition, and its governance follows
established frameworks for good model development and evaluation practice
\citep{Jakeman2006}. It also rests on a mathematical step that
is easy to audit and easy to overlook: a weighted sum is certified positive
at every audited snapshot, and the system behind the snapshots is declared
safe.

The companion manuscripts show that this step can fail, and characterize
exactly when it fails. In the assessment-operator framework of \citet{Abaee2026a},
a noncompensatory typed operator --- which requires every floor to hold on
the full trajectory, not at endpoints alone --- can reject every plan that a
scalarized aggregate operator licenses at a given weight; the separation is
exhibited on an explicit rational witness datum and propagated through
backward recursions. A resource-transition benchmark realizes the witness as
a regulated fishery transition and verifies every quoted number in exact
rational arithmetic. Under incomplete observation, the obstruction calculus
of \citet{Abaee2026b} complements viability assessment from the other side: it
certifies that no observation-based policy is viable, through finitely
checkable certificates --- the common-action obstruction with an explicit
Farkas multiplier vector, the observation-fibre criterion, and a
finite-horizon belief-space recursion that is sound and complete in finite
systems. These results are mathematical; they describe what should be
computed. They do not yet describe a tool that computes it.

This is the gap SafeTransition addresses. Reading a composite dashboard
today, an analyst can recompute the aggregate with ordinary floating-point
tooling, but has no standard way to re-derive the safety verdict behind it,
to check that an infeasible plan menu is infeasible by certificate rather
than by solver exit code, or to see which weight ratios license which plans.
Numerical viability and control toolboxes compute kernels and invariant sets
to high precision; multi-criteria decision analysis packages aggregate and
rank; formal verification systems and satisfiability-modulo-theories solvers
certify finite-state models and general constraint systems against their own
specification semantics. What is missing in the intersection is a small,
auditable layer in which the verdicts themselves --- admissibility of a plan
at a state, licensing at a weight, emptiness of a menu, emptiness of an
epistemic kernel --- are returned as exact rational objects that a third
party can re-verify by hand or by machine, with the assessment semantics
(floors, weights, licensed plan sets, partially observed beliefs) carried by
the verdicts themselves rather than re-encoded.

This paper presents SafeTransition, a Python library that provides this
layer. The related-software positioning of the paper was compiled against a
preserved record of twelve verbatim web queries with dated result records
(q1--q12, 2026), archived in the verification deposit; Section~\ref{sec:related}
reports what the searches covered and found. The library implements the five assessment operators and their
accepted-state sets, the typed finite-graph backward recursion, the
belief-space robust epistemic recursion, and the finitely checkable
certificate family: exact rational Fourier--Motzkin elimination with
provenance tracking, returning Farkas certificates and infeasibility
margins; the finite common-action obstruction with a minimal conflicting
subfamily; and the observation-only safety-classification criterion. Every semantic computation --- assessment, recursion, threshold, and certificate arithmetic --- runs in
exact rational arithmetic; floats appear only in rendered graphics. The
library re-derives, in twenty-four exact checks, the resource-transition
benchmark of the verification deposit, and renders the resulting readings as
a single-file dashboard. It depends on nothing beyond the Python standard
library, which makes the exactness claim structural rather than procedural:
there is no numerical backend whose rounds of approximation would need to be
audited.

The paper is a software description. Section 2 states the mathematical basis
as implemented, with proofs delegated to the companion manuscripts.
Section 3 describes the software architecture and the exact-arithmetic
discipline. Section 4 illustrates the library on the benchmark, on the
dashboard readings, and on the partial-observation demonstrations. Section 5
positions the library relative to existing software, and Section 6 records
availability and requirements. Section 7 concludes.

\section{Mathematical basis}\label{sec:math}

\subsection{Transition data and assessment operators}\label{sec:operators}

A typed transition datum comprises a phase state
$z = (q, x, s_1, \dots, s_n)$ with a phase flag $q$, fund coordinates $x$,
and typed floors $s_i$ normalized to zero; a finite action set; a set of disturbances with, for each action and
disturbance, an exact tube $\mathrm{Tube}(a, d)$ --- the set of states
visited over the review period, given here as piecewise-linear coordinate
paths on breakpoints at which every visited extreme is attained; a
transition-safe set $S$; and a destination set $G$ reached under an
endpoint reset: the operators below evaluate tube constraints on the
visited path, endpoint constraints at the terminal pre-reset state
$y^{-}$, and destination constraints at the post-reset successor
$z^{+} = R(y^{-})$. Actions in the library declare offsets on the
breakpoints, together with a finite action-indexed disturbance set. Each
tube carries a status: \texttt{EXACT} (the exact visited set of its
declared piecewise-linear paths) or \texttt{CONSERVATIVE} (a certified
outer enclosure of an underlying nonlinear realization). The status
determines the logical strength of verdicts: constraints passed on an
exact tube certify the represented trajectory safe and a violation
certifies it unsafe; on a conservative enclosure, passing certifies the
realization safe, while a violation is inconclusive unless the violating
point is known reachable. Table~\ref{tab:verdicts} summarizes.

Five operators are distinguished, following \citet{Abaee2026a}; all share
the same finite disturbance set per action, the same reset, and the same
destination condition, and weights are strictly positive. For a state $z$,
writing $\mathrm{Tube}(a,d)$ for the visited set and $z^{+}(a,d)$ for the
successor: the noncompensatory typed operator admits action $a$ when
$\mathrm{Tube}(a,d) \subseteq S_{\mathrm{typ}}$ --- every floor plus the
physical constraint --- for every disturbance $d$ and $z^{+}(a,d) \in G$;
the scalarized aggregate operator replaces the coordinate-wise floors by
$w \cdot s \geq 0$ at weight $w$, the physical constraint retained; the
exact-tube physical operator drops the floors along the tube; the
endpoint-only physical operator constrains only the terminal pre-reset
state; and the typed-endpoint operator evaluates the floors at that
pre-reset endpoint. Here
$S_{\mathrm{typ}} = \{y : s_i(y) \geq 0\ \forall i,\ \mathrm{Phys}(y)\}$
and $S_{\mathrm{phys}} = \{y : \mathrm{Phys}(y)\}$; $S$ without subscript
denotes the typed set in the recursions. Because floors with the physical
constraint included imply their weighted shadow under positive weights,
typed sets include physical ones, and tubes contain their endpoints, the
chain
\[
E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z) \;\subseteq\; E_w(z) \;\subseteq\;
E_{\mathrm{tube,phys}}(z) \;\subseteq\; E_{\mathrm{end}}(z)
\]
holds for every strictly positive weight, each inclusion possibly strict:
floors imply the nonnegative weighted sum when $w > 0$; deleting floors
weakens the condition; a tube constraint implies the same constraint at
the contained pre-reset endpoint; and the typed sets include the physical
constraint by definition. The library verifies each inclusion exactly at
any state on request (Section~\ref{sec:api}). The fifth operator continues the
pattern on the typed side: $E_{\mathrm{typ}}(z) \subseteq
E_{\mathrm{end,typ}}(z) \subseteq E_{\mathrm{end}}(z)$, since typed floors
constrain more than endpoints and more than physics alone. The accepted-state set
$\mathcal{V}[E] = \{ z : E(z) \neq \emptyset \}$ and the compensatory
accepted set $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}} = \bigcap_w \mathcal{V}_w$ are
derived from the operators. The compensatory set is the
$\forall w\, \exists a$ condition --- the licensed action may depend on
the weight --- and is in general strictly larger than the
$\exists a\, \forall w$ uniform-action condition; this quantifier-order
distinction is the companion separation in accepted-set form. The
witnessed separation
$\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{typ}} \subseteq \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{weak}}$, strict in
general, is what a composite dashboard cannot see (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}).

\subsection{Recursions}\label{sec:recursions}

Two backward recursions are implemented. The typed recursion propagates
viability backwards through an explicit state--action graph: with
$\mathcal{W}_0$ the safe set, a state is $(k{+}1)$-step viable when some
action keeps every visited state inside the safe set and lands in the
$k$-step viable set, quantified over the action's finite disturbance set.
On explicit graphs, under a unit-cost predicate model, the recursion is
polynomial in the numbers of states, state--action pairs, and horizon;
bit complexity depends on rational coefficient growth and is reported
separately by the scaling study (Section~\ref{sec:scaling}).

The belief-space recursion addresses partially observed systems,
following the robust epistemic construction of \citet{Abaee2026b}. States
carry observation labels; beliefs are elements of the resulting quotient
of the state space --- sets of observation labels whose state fibres are
re-expanded at every step. An action is admissible for a belief when no
state in any of its labels' fibres enters a violation en route under any
disturbance, and the belief updates to the set of labels reachable under
the action. The recursion returns the sets $\mathcal{W}_k$ of $k$-step
viable beliefs, with $\mathcal{W}_0$ the safe labels. Soundness holds for
all finite systems and for the natural policy class: a membership
$\mathcal{B} \in \mathcal{W}_k$ certifies a belief-based
(observation-history) policy viable for $k$ steps. Completeness of the
quotient --- a belief outside $\mathcal{W}_N$ admitting no such policy ---
holds when the observation is a safety-and-action quotient (safe-set
membership and action admissibility constant on fibres, successors
label-determined); label injectivity on reachable states is the simplest
sufficient condition. Absent the quotient condition, a non-membership is
an abstraction artifact, not a certificate of policy nonexistence. A
failure explainer accompanies a non-viable belief with its per-action
counterexample: the exact reason each action fails --- a fibre state
entering a violation en route, or a post-belief that is itself not viable
at the previous level --- and each action is admissible in isolation in
the state-wise, one-step, full-information sense. The failure object
serializes as a certificate (system, horizon, per-action reason codes)
that the independent checker re-derives by re-running the recursion from
the serialized system alone. Post-observation recourse failures ---
beliefs in $\mathcal{W}_1 \setminus \mathcal{W}_2$ --- are the signature
output (Section~\ref{sec:partial}).

\subsection{Certificates}\label{sec:certificates}

Infeasibility is certified, not merely reported. For a stacked rational
system $A u \leq b$, the library runs Fourier--Motzkin elimination with
provenance tracking: every derived row carries its multiplier vector over
the original rows, and when a contradiction $0 \leq c$, $c < 0$ is derived,
the derived multipliers $\lambda \geq 0$ with $\lambda^{\top} A = 0$ and
$\lambda^{\top} b < 0$ are returned as a Farkas certificate, normalized to
sum to one. The certificate is invariant to positive scaling of the
multiplier vector; the normalized contradiction value
$-\lambda^{\top} b > 0$ is fixed by the normalization but is not
invariant under rescaling of the input rows, and is therefore reported
as a normalized certificate contradiction value, not as an intrinsic
distance to feasibility. The certificate is
re-verified exactly before being returned; feasible systems return a
bare verdict --- feasibility is decided, not witnessed. The finite
common-action obstruction fires when the safe-action sets of a mutually
possible (co-possible under the assessed observation) family of states
intersect emptily while each is nonempty --- every state is individually
viable under full information, yet no common action exists at this
horizon; with recursively viable action sets the obstruction rules out a
viable observation-based policy at that horizon. The library reports a
minimum-cardinality conflicting subfamily, found by exhaustive search in
nondecreasing size, so every strictly smaller subfamily is certified to
intersect. The observation-only safety-classification criterion decides
whether current safe-set membership can be classified exactly from the
current observation alone: such a classification exists if and only if
safe-set membership is constant on observation fibres, and a violating
fibre is reported otherwise. The criterion is about this classification
only: it does not rule out observation-history policies, belief-based
control, conservative certifiers, or three-valued procedures.

\section{Software description}\label{sec:software}

\subsection{Architecture}\label{sec:architecture}

The library is organized as nine modules, with the command-line
entry point as an interface to them and the independent checker as a
standalone repository-root script (Table~\ref{tab:modules}). The
\texttt{rational} module provides the exact rational helpers on which every
other module rests, and the \texttt{datum} module defines the transition-data interface and ships one
fully specified instance, the witness datum of the companion manuscript,
with destination gain $e = (1/4, 1/4)$ and rescue cost $c = 1$. Custom data
subclass the interface. The \texttt{operators}, \texttt{recursion}, and
\texttt{certificates} modules implement Section~\ref{sec:math}; the
\texttt{indicators} and \texttt{dashboard} modules produce the readings of
Section~\ref{sec:readings}; the \texttt{benchmark} module carries the
twenty-four exact checks of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}. A command-line
entry point exposes \texttt{verify}, \texttt{report}, \texttt{demo}, and
\texttt{certify} --- the last emits the serializable certificates of
Section~\ref{sec:partial} for the independent checker. The
\texttt{benchmarks/} directory (not a module) carries the scaling study
of Section~\ref{sec:scaling}.

\subsection{The exact-arithmetic discipline}\label{sec:exactness}

Every assessment, recursion, and certificate is computed in exact rational
arithmetic. Rationals enter either as integers, as decimal strings, or as
explicit numerator--denominator pairs; no implicit conversion from binary
floating point exists anywhere in the library, so an input's exactness is
structural, not a matter of reviewer discipline. Floats occur in exactly two
places, both non-semantic: rendering geometry (SVG in the dashboard,
matplotlib coordinates in figure scripts) and performance measurement in
the scaling study. Checks never read floats. The distinction is
the library's design-centre claim: outputs are verifiable, not merely
computed. A Farkas certificate, a licensing threshold, or a benchmark check
can be re-verified by any rational-arithmetic implementation --- including
pencil and paper --- and the test suite exercises exactly this: every
certificate returned by the eliminator is re-verified against its defining
identities before being returned, and the benchmark checks re-derive each
deposited value symbolically (for example $\sigma(16/5) = 1088/125$ for the
sustained-yield quota of the Schaefer realization). The discipline is not
merely hygienic: the suite includes an adversarial regression in which
the exact rational predicate $49 \cdot (1/49) - 1$ evaluates to exactly
zero, while under direct IEEE-754 binary64 evaluation in the tested
expression order and with a strict zero threshold the same expression
evaluates to $-1.11 \times 10^{-16}$ and would change the verdict. This
is a regression case for one direct evaluation path, not a claim that
floating-point tooling generally errs; SafeTransition rejects
floating-point inputs structurally, so the failure mode is excluded by
construction. Exactness is also stable under coefficient growth within
the tested families: on a dyadic chain whose planted infeasibility gap is
$2^{-t}$, the certified margin retains a 323-bit reduced denominator at
$t = 320$, long after binary floating point has rounded the gap away
(Section~\ref{sec:scaling}).

\subsection{Interfaces and complexity}\label{sec:api}

The application programming interface mirrors the mathematics:
\texttt{E(datum, z, mode, w)} returns the action set of an operator at a
state; \texttt{check\_chain} verifies the operator chain exactly;
\texttt{belief\_backward} returns the belief-viability sets
$\mathcal{W}_k$ and \texttt{explain\_belief\_failure} the per-action
counterexample for a belief outside them; \texttt{certify\_polyhedron}
returns a feasibility verdict with its certificate when infeasible (a
bare verdict when feasible --- feasibility is decided, not witnessed);
\texttt{weight\_partition} returns the complete licensing arrangement along
the weight ratio as a serializable certificate, for the two-floor
witness-family menu class whose per-plan trough conditions are affine in
the ratio, over the admissible parameter family $0 < s_1, s_2 <
\mathrm{dip}$ (outside it a plan is licensed at every positive ratio or
a threshold denominator is nonpositive, and the routine raises rather
than misinterprets);
\texttt{compute} returns the dashboard readings at a
state, weight, and read plan. The typed recursion on explicit graphs is
polynomial in graph size and horizon; Fourier--Motzkin elimination can
generate exponentially many intermediate inequalities in the worst case.
Exact rational linear feasibility is not inherently exponential ---
polynomial-time algorithms exist in the bit model --- but the library
uses Fourier--Motzkin elimination because the targeted assessment systems
are small and row-level provenance is straightforward to retain (a
handful of constraints; the witness instances eliminate in milliseconds). Belief enumeration is bounded --- the bound is a parameter
(default 4\,096 reachable beliefs) whose exhaustion raises an exception
carrying no partial results; the bound affects exploration completeness,
never the soundness of returned memberships.

\subsection{Scaling of the exact pipeline}\label{sec:scaling}

The precision--performance trade-off is measured, not asserted. A scaling
study sweeps six parameterized instance families whose answers are known
by construction and asserted at every size: stacked Fourier--Motzkin
chains with a planted infeasibility gap (chain length up to $k = 256$
variables), dyadic coefficient growth (exact margins certified at
denominators $2^{t}$ up to $t = 320$), a dense random stress family with
planted answers (reporting where classical row growth becomes
impractical), typed recursions on layered graphs (up to 21\,000 states at
horizon 20), cloned action menus (up to 512 actions through all five
operators), and belief systems whose reachable belief count is exactly
$2^{m}$ (largest completed $m = 12$ with all 4\,096 beliefs; the
overflow test $m = 13$ raises with no partial results). The study records
wall-clock runtime, peak traced memory, eliminator row statistics (rows
generated, peak working rows), certificate sizes with maximal
numerator/denominator bit lengths reported separately, and
independent-checker runtime (Table~\ref{tab:scaling}). Within these
structured planted-answer families, exact rational certification remains
stable as coefficient size and instance dimension grow: the chain family
generates $k$ rows and terminates at the planted contradiction (the
certificate JSON reaches 335\,kB at $k = 256$, with 13-bit coefficients,
re-verified by the independent checker in 0.29\,s), the typed recursion
is linear in the state count (21\,000 states in 2.1\,s), belief
enumeration is exponential in $m$ as the reachable set itself is, and the
bound fires where designed. For the chain family the margin has the
closed form $(1/5 - (k-1)/4096)/(k+1)$, verified at every $k$; for the
dyadic family (chain of $L = 5$ intermediate links) the margin is
exactly $2^{-320}/7$, whose reduced denominator $7 \cdot 2^{320}$ has
323 bits. Within the tested range, coefficient growth had a larger
observed effect on certificate size than on wall-clock time. The planted
families measure correctness and exact-arithmetic stability under
controlled growth; the dense stress family reports the classical row
growth without deduplication (975 intermediate rows at 3 variables;
7\,505 rows at 4 variables in 2.2\,s for the infeasible case) and the
point at which elimination becomes impractical --- the 60\,s budget
fires at 4 dense feasible variables --- so the dense-system envelope of
the eliminator is narrow, as the complexity statement above says.

\section{Illustrative examples}\label{sec:examples}

\subsection{The resource-transition benchmark}\label{sec:benchmark}

The benchmark realizes the witness datum as a regulated fishery transition:
stock biomass with Schaefer surplus $\sigma(B) = rB(1 - B/K)$ ($r = 4$,
$K = 10$, $B_{\mathrm{lim}} = 2$), a pulse-plus-closed-season quota plan,
a mid-season heatwave strike, and a staged rebuild financed by a fund
buy-back. Three layers are distinguished: the exactness claims are
relative to the supplied rational datum; the enclosure claims connect
that datum to the Schaefer realization; and the ecological adequacy of
the Schaefer model itself is outside the scope of the certification
layer. Running \texttt{safetransition verify} executes twenty-four exact
checks that re-derive the deposited verification values: the witness datum
and tube tables; quota admissibility on both disturbance branches
($0 \leq H^* \leq 13$, peak $1463/125$); tube-status certification --- the
declared piecewise-linear plan tubes are exact relative to their
declaration, while the Schaefer realization's tubes are certified
conservative outer enclosures: on the adverse recovery leg biomass lies
in $[6/5, 16/5] \subset (0, 5) = (0, K/2)$, where
$\sigma'(B) = 4 - 4B/5 > 0$, so $\sigma$ is increasing on the interval
and its minimum is the left endpoint value $\sigma(6/5) = 528/125 = 4.224
\geq 4$, which certifies the enclosure on the recovery legs; the
index blindness of the composite reading at $w = (1, 1)$, whose minimum
stays at $2/5 > 0$ along the licensed plan while the ecological floor
reaches $-4/5 < 0$ mid-transition; the licensing thresholds
$\rho_1 = 2/3$ and $\rho_2 = 3/2$, cross-checked against the thresholds
derived directly from the tube geometry; and the rescue threshold
$\kappa^* = 1 - x$ on the non-typed-viable region, truncated at zero
($\kappa^* = \max(0,\, 1-x)$) once the fund covers the buy-back, with
shortfall $1/2$ at the false-positive witness and full financing at the
rescue witness $x = 3/2$. All twenty-four checks pass in under a second;
Table~\ref{tab:qa} collects the verification artifacts. Because the
realization tubes are conservative, the benchmark's one-sided reading is:
plans whose declared tubes pass are certified safe, and the transfer to
the nonlinear realization holds by the enclosure; a plan rejected on an
enclosure violation would be not-certified-safe rather than certifiably
unsafe, unless the violating point is reachable --- the benchmark's
separation rests on the declared (exact) plan tubes, so both directions
are conclusive there.

\subsection{Dashboard readings}\label{sec:readings}

The \texttt{compute} interface returns, at a state and weight, the per-plan
floor minima under each plan's characteristic disturbance --- an
illustrative branch, displayed as such; the robust operator verdicts
quantify over all disturbances --- the composite
index along the read plan, the licensing verdicts at the weight ratio
$r = w_2/w_1$, the rescue threshold, and the index-blindness alarm, which
fires exactly when the composite minimum stays nonnegative (the
aggregate-licensed condition, boundary included) while a floor
minimum is negative. In this subsection the pairs $(1/2,\, 1/2)$ and
$(3/2,\, 0)$ denote $(x, \kappa^*(x))$ --- fund level and shortfall --- not
the phase-state coordinates of Section~\ref{sec:operators}, and the phase
flag $q$ is suppressed in the displayed triples, being fixed at the
review-phase value throughout. At the witness state $(x, s_1, s_2) = (1/2, 6/5, 6/5)$
with $w = (1,1)$, the alarm fires: the readings reproduce the benchmark
separation as a dashboard statement (Fig.~\ref{fig:readings}). The
\texttt{dashboard} module renders the readings, the benchmark status, and
any certificates as a single HTML file with inline SVG and CSS and no
external resources, so the file can be attached to a review, emailed, or
archived with a statutory record and remains readable offline. The
dashboard is an audit-delivery mechanism rather than a novelty claim, and
it is bound to the certificate schema: a verification-provenance table
embeds the library version, the independent checker's version and re-run
command, the datum identifier, and the SHA-256 hash of every
certificate's exact serialized JSON, expandable to the serialization
itself. Rendering is deterministic --- a pure function of the inputs,
with no timestamps --- so a re-render from the same inputs is
byte-identical and the embedded hashes are checkable against the emitted
certificate files. From the reviewer's perspective this means the reading
and its verification arrive together: thresholds, alarms, certificates,
hashes, and the re-run command in one file. The hashes establish
integrity and identity of the serialized inputs, not their authenticity
or external truth: a producer could embed mutually consistent false
data, and the dashboard verifies consistency with the serialized datum,
not the world.

\subsection{Partial observation and certificates}\label{sec:partial}

The bundled demonstrations exercise the certificate family on the companion
manuscript's instances. The three-state recourse failure
(Table~\ref{tab:recourse}) runs the
belief-space recursion on a system whose root belief is one-step viable and
exits at the second step under every observation-based policy: the root
belief lies in $\mathcal{W}_1 \setminus \mathcal{W}_2$, witnessed only by
the recursion. The observation-only safety-classification criterion
reports the merged fibre $\{x_1, x_2\}$ on which safe-set membership
varies, ruling out exact classification of current safe-set membership
from the current observation alone; it does not address
history-dependent procedures. The stacked menu window $u \leq 2/5$, $-u \leq -3/5$ returns the
Farkas certificate $\lambda = (1/2, 1/2)$ with infeasibility margin exactly
$1/10$, re-verified against $\lambda^{\top} A = 0$ and
$\lambda^{\top} b < 0$ before being returned. The mapping to the
obstruction calculus is row-explicit: row $[1]\; u \leq 2/5$ is the first
state's menu cap, row $[-1]\; u \leq -3/5$ is the second state's floor
requirement, the certificate's multiplier vector weights the states'
restrictions equally, and the empty common menu the obstruction reports
is exactly the infeasibility these two rows certify --- one Farkas object,
addressable row by row to the states whose compatibility it denies. A deposited
double-integrator identity (weights $(3/8, 5/16, 5/16)$ with
$\sum_j \lambda_j n_j = 0$) is checked exactly; its system, normals,
and role as a regression anchor are tabulated in the Supplementary
Material (S11). On the recourse-failure
system, \texttt{explain\_belief\_failure} returns the counterexample
object: at the root belief both actions are admissible in isolation in
the state-wise, one-step, full-information sense, and
each fails because its post-belief $\{y_{12}, y_4\}$ is not one-step
viable --- $x_4$'s only successors enter the violating $x_3$ --- the
machine-checkable witness behind the
$\mathcal{W}_1 \setminus \mathcal{W}_2$ verdict. The object serializes
as a \texttt{belief\_failure} certificate --- system, horizon, and
per-action reason codes --- which the independent checker verifies by
re-running the recursion from the serialized system alone through a
stdlib-only reimplementation; a tampered reason or post-belief is
rejected.

The weight-space analysis is complete rather than pointwise. The
\texttt{weight\_partition} interface returns the full arrangement of
licensed plan sets along the ratio $r = w_2/w_1$ as a serializable
certificate. For the benchmark floors the arrangement is the three regions
the readings display: SLOW-only on $[0, 2/3)$, both plans licensed on
$[2/3, 3/2]$, FAST-only on $(3/2, \infty)$ (weights with a zero
component are handled by projective closure: a plan is licensed at
$r = 0$ or $r = \infty$ exactly when its floor conditions degenerate so
at the closure). Across the admissible rational family --- floor pairs
$(s_1, s_2)$ and dip depths with $0 < s_1, s_2 < \mathrm{dip}$, outside
which \texttt{weight\_partition} raises (a plan licensed at every
positive ratio, or nonpositive threshold denominators) --- the arrangement
has three regimes:
for $\mathrm{dip} < s_1 + s_2$ (the benchmark's) the FAST threshold
$(\mathrm{dip} - s_1)/s_2$ precedes the SLOW threshold
$s_1/(\mathrm{dip} - s_2)$; for $\mathrm{dip} > s_1 + s_2$ the thresholds
swap and an unlicensed gap opens around $r = 1$; at equality the middle
region degenerates to the single point where both plans bind
simultaneously. The independent checker re-derives, from the certificate
alone, the thresholds, region coverage and inclusivity, the licensed set at
a sampled ratio in each region, and the binding witness constraint at each
boundary.

\section{Relation to existing software}\label{sec:related}

SafeTransition complements three established families of tools, and borrows
from each. Numerical viability kernels and reachability tools compute
set-valued safety objects to high floating-point precision with
impressive scalability; they answer \emph{how large is the safe set},
where SafeTransition answers \emph{is this verdict verifiable}, for the
small, structured, rational instances that assessment menus produce.
Modular environmental simulators with public source code --- Raven
\citep{Craig2020} for watershed modelling, FloPy \citep{Bakker2016} as a
MODFLOW model-builder --- are the nearest workflow neighbours: they
consume declared scenario data and produce scenario outputs, and a
viability or reachability layer built on top of them is where set-valued
safety computation would live. Multi-criteria and sensitivity
analysis packages --- the SAFE toolbox \citep{Pianosi2015}, SALib
\citep{Herman2017}, SPOTPY \citep{Houska2015} --- share the library's
workflow-oriented design: model-independent interfaces, worked examples,
and benchmark functions; SPOTPY's demonstration that one package can carry
an entire family of methods with a minimal amount of glue code is the
presentation standard SafeTransition aims at. Modular environmental
simulators with public source code and regression suites --- Pywr
\citep{Tomlinson2020} and airGR \citep{Coron2017} --- inform the
availability discipline: permissive licensing, distribution through package
indexes, and a test suite executed on continuous integration. None of the environmental
families just listed returns certificates or computes in exact
arithmetic (a claim about the packages surveyed in the archived novelty
search, not about all environmental software). General-purpose
provers do both in their own domains. The z3
satisfiability-modulo-theories solver \citep{deMoura2008} decides linear
real arithmetic exactly over the rationals and emits checkable
unsatisfiability cores; its default cores, however, are trace proofs of
its own inference rules, not self-contained algebraic witnesses for a
third party who does not run z3, whereas a Farkas certificate is
verifiable by thirty lines of school arithmetic. The Fourier--Motzkin
projection library pyfme \citep{Caron2022} performs exact symbolic
elimination over SymPy rationals with Imbert acceleration; probabilistic
model checking in PRISM \citep{Kwiatkowska2011} verifies finite-state
probabilistic models against temporal-logic specifications and returns
counterexamples and witnesses, at floating-point scale for numerical
queries. These tools are not certificate-free; the point is scope. What
none of them ships is
assessment semantics: floors, weight-licensed plan menus, reachability tubes
for transition data, and epistemic viability sets are objects the analyst
must encode afresh, and the encoding, not the prover, then carries the
burden of exactness. Table~\ref{tab:compare} summarizes the comparison; the
twelve verbatim search queries behind it (q1--q12, with dates,
trimmed result records, and the mapping to the claims they support) are
archived in the verification deposit \citep{Deposit2026}, the figshare
item 10.6084/m9.figshare.33764023 whose master copy doubles as the
software's verification deposit. The comparison is functional, not
competitive: the families operate at scales and purposes for which
floating-point computation is the right engineering choice, and the exact
layer becomes decisive precisely where verdicts enter statutory or
adversarial settings and must be re-derivable by third parties.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

\subsection{Limitations}

Four limitations bound the tool's use. First, exactness is relative to
the supplied datum: the certification layer validates verdicts against
declared rational data, not against the modelled system, and enclosure
claims transfer only as far as the certified enclosure
(Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}). Second, the belief recursion's
completeness is conditional on the safety-and-action quotient; outside
it, non-membership is an abstraction artifact
(Section~\ref{sec:recursions}). Third, the eliminator's dense-system
envelope is narrow --- the stress family becomes impractical already at
four dense feasible variables --- so the certificate family targets the
small stacked systems that assessment menus produce
(Section~\ref{sec:scaling}). Fourth, the weight partition covers its
stated two-floor menu family; menus outside the family require new
derivation rather than reparameterization (Section~\ref{sec:partial}).

SafeTransition turns the assessment-operator framework and the obstruction
calculus of the companion manuscripts into auditable software: typed
operators evaluated on tubes whose status is declared exact or certified
conservative, recursions over state and belief space, and infeasibility
returned as Farkas certificates with exact margins,
serialized and re-verified by an independent checker sharing no code with
the library. The library's scope is deliberate: it certifies verdicts on
small, explicit, rational instances under exact or certified conservative
tubes, and it makes no predictive claim --- the theorem-level guarantees
are those of the companion manuscripts, and on continuous or
belief-continuous systems the graph construction itself is the modelling
step. Within that scope, the tool addresses a practical gap: readings of
the kind composite dashboards display, and nonviability verdicts of the
kind that justify tightening a plan menu, can carry their certificates with
them wherever verdicts must be re-derivable by third parties. With
independent checking, the scaling evidence, and the comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:related} in place, the tool closes this practical gap
within its stated scope. Extensions
under way are continuous-state data with certified enclosures, the
extension of the weight partition to richer plan menus, and additional
certificate families from the obstruction calculus; the interface
intentionally leaves room for all three.
