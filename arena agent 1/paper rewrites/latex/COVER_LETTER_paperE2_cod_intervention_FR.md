Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, **"Robust viability of the 2J3KL limit reference point under a surplus-production map: policy scoring, expansion, and when catch cannot help,"** for consideration in *Fisheries Research* as a research article.

**What the paper does.** The governed object is the least-squares surplus-production map fitted to the 1983–2007 Northern cod (NAFO 2J3KL) SSB series (r = 0.2369; K = 5000 kt at its bound, a declared fit defect), and every statement is scoped to that map and its declared classes. A declared catch-policy family — moratorium removals, flat caps, the critical-zone rule, a cascade, and two reactive families — is scored against the 2016 reference point (884.6 kt) under persistent productivity floors, under a protocol frozen before any score was computed. The paper delivers constructive, selection, expansion, and stochastic-viability certificates at their actual strength.

**Why it matters.** The central finding is that, on this map, the LRP is protected by good years, but the margin they must supply is smaller than the frozen convention implied. Under the informative 10th-percentile class, zero catch and the moratorium hold the entire safe set and the largest robust constant catch is 91.6 kt yr⁻¹ — an analytic limit whose bootstrap 90% interval is [0, 87.1] kt and whose 20-year survival from the LRP is 0.74, so the operational reading is order 70–90 kt, not a quota. No non-BAU policy dominates BAU under the declared partial order; the surplus-proportional family (reactive, protective at φ ≤ 0.5) comes closest, registering a genuine trade-off rather than a strict improvement. The certified kernel is empty beyond seven years because the map is expansive at the reference point for every admissible K ≥ 2K* = 1769.2 kt — a property of the fitted map, not an artifact of the particular pinned value. The certificates survive the data-preferred depensatory refit, and only the class-vacuity reading reverses.

**Why it is appropriate for *Fisheries Research*.** The paper brings rigorous, certificate-based decision machinery — robust viability kernels, spatial-vs-temporal expansion analysis, layered constructive and stochastic certificates — to a concrete harvest-control-rule and limit-reference-point question for a stock of central management interest. It is a methodological contribution aimed squarely at harvest-control-rule selection and reference-point robustness, in the language and structure of the journal, with policy scores reported under a protocol frozen before any score was computed. I have made the scope and convention-dependence explicit, including the two definitional notes (the identity vacuity of the perpetual-worst floor, and the rule-level no-dominance outcome) so that no result is read as more general than it is.

**Fit and originality.** The contribution is the protocol and its layered certificate structure rather than any single number: frozen scoring, the separation of geometric from identified findings, the extension of the policy family to genuinely reactive rules, and the explicit treatment of when catch *cannot* help (the expansive-regime null result). Where a finding is convention- or scope-conditional, that is recorded as a note rather than presented as an empirical selection result. The paper is honest that the no-dominance outcome is partly a selection property of the declared partial order, and that only the perpetual-worst floor is vacuously uninformative by an arithmetic identity.

The manuscript is self-contained and all input data, analysis scripts, and result files are archived in a public repository (https://github.com/MIKEAA2020/general-sustainability). I confirm the work is not under consideration elsewhere, is original, and I have no conflicts of interest.
