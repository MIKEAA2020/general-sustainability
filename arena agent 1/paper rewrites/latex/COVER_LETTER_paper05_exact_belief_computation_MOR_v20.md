**To the Editors, *Mathematics of Operations Research***

Dear Editors,

I submit the manuscript *Exactness is not the limitation: a linear criterion for blind survivable sets on the parameter cube and complete classifications for four, five and six parameters* for consideration as a research article.

**Problem.** A manager keeps one stock above a floor while the signs of `m` model parameters are unknown. Each sign pattern is a cell of the cube `{±1}^m`; each period one sign vector (or a hold) is committed, and the stock moves by `−1/2 + (1/5)⟨u,θ⟩`. Because the cells of an information class cannot be told apart, one action sequence must serve all of them: which sets of cells can a single blind sequence keep alive for ever, from which initial slack, and what does observing the stock buy? The question sits between viability theory under output feedback, safety games solved by antichains, and point-based POMDP evaluation, and in all three literatures the answer for this instance was computed set by set.

**Contribution.** (i) A linear criterion: a set is blind-survivable from some finite slack if and only if `max_y min_{θ∈S} ⟨θ,y⟩ ≥ 5/2`, equivalently `min_q ‖Σ q_θ θ‖₁ ≥ 5/2`; both sides are exact rational certificates, and a non-survivable set loses a cell within `⌊L/(5 − 2V)⌋` steps. The criterion yields the pair bound, the Hamming-ball census at the floor and Kleitman's diameter bound for the largest survivable set, attained for every `m`; it also shows that pairwise distances and the apparent instrument strength are not the relevant quantities (a tetrahedron with all pairs survivable is never survivable). (ii) The least sufficient slack is the least common vector in the winning region of a safety game on `ℕ^S`; it is computed as an antichain with a two-sided certificate (saturated game above, exact forward reachability below), and a pinned-slack inequality explains the sets whose threshold exceeds their first-step cost. (iii) Complete classifications for `m = 4, 5, 6`: for six parameters, 774,960 of 853,680 admissible sets are survivable, with maximal families of 192 Kleitman-extremal cylinders, 1,280 sets sitting exactly on the boundary of the criterion and 160 subcubes, and every minimal obstruction has value 2, so every non-survivable set dies within `L` steps. (iv) With the stock observed the feedback game is solved exactly (slack 23 at `m = 4`, 18 at `m = 5`, against infinity blind), the probe ladder is a computed table, and total ignorance costs exactly `5T`. (v) Point-based alpha-set evaluation is shown to be exact for finite horizons and to agree with indefinite survival only beyond the longest transient. (vi) The 143,360 boundary sets of the six-cube explain why exact arithmetic is necessary: a `float64` simulation of their certified words declares a false collapse in 1,120 of the 1,280 maximal cases, depending only on how the same rational drift is coded.

**Fit.** The paper is about a minimax criterion with LP certificates, a safety game solved by antichains with certified thresholds, extremal set theory on the Hamming cube and the value of information in a stochastic-free but partially observed control problem; the theorems are stated and proved in general, and the instances are small enough to be exact and large enough (`2^64` subsets, 853,680 candidates) that exactness is a genuine claim. The standard of evidence is unusual: a 53-check exact-rational script regenerates every number, witness, count and table, the complete six-cube classification runs in two and a half minutes, and the dynamics, pair laws, four-cube classification and deadline law are machine-checked in Lean 4 (80 theorems, no `sorry`) with the scope of the formal layer stated precisely.

**Reproducibility.** The exact module, the stage scripts and their record, the verification script with its archived output, the figure and table scripts and the Lean modules accompany the manuscript; the Supplementary Material (S1–S9) carries the omitted proofs, the complete records of the three cubes with all certificates, the observation and alpha-set tables, the floating-point study, the verification record and a symbol table.
