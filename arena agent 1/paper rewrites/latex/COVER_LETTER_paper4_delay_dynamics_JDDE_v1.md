Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, **"Governance delay and the stability of harvested stocks: mobilising and protective feedback rules, and the review interval as a design parameter,"** for consideration in the *Journal of Dynamics and Differential Equations*.

**What the paper does.** The delays that destabilise renewable-resource systems have been studied almost exclusively as ecological or informational delays — maturation, recruitment, self-limitation, or the harvester's delayed knowledge of the stock. This paper analyses the delay that sits elsewhere: **governance delay**, the lag from an institution's observation of a decline to the response that finally acts on it. A three-state model — harvested stock, filtered deficit memory, bounded effort — compares two feedback rules: the **mobilising rule** (effort grows as more effort is deployed) and the **protective rule** (a quota-tracking law restoring harvest toward a cap), first under a continuous deployment delay, then under periodic review, where the review interval becomes a sample-and-hold control.

**The results are sharp and, in places, counter-intuitive.** For the mobilising rule, *intermediate* delay stabilises the equilibrium: two subcritical Hopf crossings — interval-certified near 3.7 and 150 yr — bound the delay window in which the lag itself holds the loop stable. For the protective rule the loop gain stays below one at the calibrated point: the equilibrium is exponentially stable at every delay — a no-Hopf theorem. Under annual review the two rules respond oppositely: protective review remains stable — its apparent 2.3-yr threshold is a discretisation artefact — while mobilising review is unstable, restabilising only above 6.5 yr through a Neimark–Sacker-type crossing. The attractor topology is five-regime, with two folds certified at the discrete collocation level. The practical message for managed renewable resources: *more frequent assessment is not always safer* — the review interval is a local spectral design parameter.

**Why the *Journal of Dynamics and Differential Equations*.** The paper is a dynamics study in both registers the journal's scope names: the continuous-deployment-delay analysis is the differential-equation side, and the periodic-review analysis is its discrete analog, with the review interval acting as a sample-and-hold parameter whose restabilisation crossing is of Neimark–Sacker type. The headline results are dynamical theorems for a retarded functional differential equation with a bounded nonlinearity: a delay-independent exponential-stability (no-Hopf) theorem for the protective rule; an intermediate-delay stability window for the mobilising rule, bounded by two subcritical Hopf crossings certified by interval enclosures; and a five-regime attractor topology with two folds certified at the discrete collocation level. The delay class is the novel edge: the studied lag sits in the governance loop rather than in the ecology — outside the Hutchinson–Gurney maturation/self-limitation lineage and the delayed-information harvester models — and the analysis shows that institutional form and timing, not ecological lag, decide stability. The documented northern-cod timeline (annual assessment, the 1992 moratorium, the 2024 reopening) grounds scales, not coefficients; the model class is stock-agnostic by construction.

The manuscript is complete at 45 pages including proofs and one figure, with an electronic supplement carrying the certification records; every computation is deterministic and re-executable from the deposited code and data. The paper is a mathematical parameterisation, not a calibration to a named fishery, and is explicit about which statements are certified, which are numerical records, and which remain open. I confirm the work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and I have no conflicts of interest.
