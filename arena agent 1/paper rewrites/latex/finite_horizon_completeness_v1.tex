\begin{abstract}
For sufficient certificates the obstruction calculus has been honest about
an open middle ground: its certificates prove nonviability, and their
absence proves nothing. This paper, the D5 item of the generalization
catalogue, closes that gap on the finite horizon for finite systems: the
backward belief-safety recursion $W_0=\{B\subseteq\mathcal V_T\}$,
$W_{k+1}=\operatorname{Pre}(W_k)$ is proved sound \emph{and complete} for
finite-horizon observation-based viability, nonviability is certified by a
finite counterstrategy tree extracted constructively from the failed
recursion, and on the programme's two-patch working system the stabilized
recursion coincides exactly with the successor's $H/H-2$ stability
convention --- the convention becomes a theorem. The audited family is
horizon-free (verdicts at $N=2,4,8$ coincide for every belief and
structure), and every belief outside the stable kernel admits a validated
tree: on finite systems the certificate gap of the calculus is empty. All
claims are machine-verified in exact arithmetic (6/6 check families).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and relation to the programme}

The programme's papers state sufficient certificates: adverse drift,
common-action emptiness, timing deadlines, certification limits. The source
catalogue's proposed strengthening is adopted here: on finite systems,
finite-horizon completeness --- the backward recursion is not merely sound
but \emph{equal} to the truth, and failure carries its own finite
certificate. The relation to the completed items is precise: the viable

\section{The backward recursion}

Work on the two-patch system (16 states, safe set $\mathcal V=\{z_1\ge1,
z_2\ge1\}$, one instrument per step, per-cell read-then-act semantics).
Beliefs are finite subsets of $\mathcal V$. Define
\begin{align*}
W_0 &= \{B:\ B\subseteq\mathcal V\},\\
W_{k+1} &= \{B:\ \text{some per-cell action tuple keeps every branch
in }\mathcal V\text{ and every re-partitioned successor part lies in }
W_k\}.
\end{align*}

\begin{proposition}[Monotone decrease]
$W_{k+1}\subseteq W_k$ for every $k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$W_0$ contains every safe belief. If $B\in W_{k+1}$ then some action tuple
sends every successor part into $W_k$; since each such part is safe, $B$
is safe, so $B\in W_0$; and by induction along the same action tuple the
parts lie in each earlier family, so $B\in W_k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness and completeness on the finite horizon]
\label{thm:complete}
For every belief $B$, information structure, and horizon $N$: $B\in W_N$
if and only if the regulator wins the $N$-step observation-based viability
game from $B$. Consequently, if $B\notin W_N$ the recursion yields a
finite counterstrategy tree of depth at most $N$ whose leaves are forced
exits of $\mathcal V$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induction on $N$. For $N=0$ both sides read ``$B\subseteq\mathcal V$.''
For $N+1$: if $B\in W_{N+1}$, the witnessing action tuple followed by the
winning strategies of the induction hypothesis on each successor part wins
the game. Conversely, if the regulator wins at $N+1$, the first move is a
per-cell action tuple all of whose adversary responses leave the
corresponding parts winning at $N$, i.e.\ in $W_N$ by induction; hence
$B\in W_{N+1}$. For the tree: if $B\notin W_{N+1}$, then for
\emph{every} action tuple some response reaches a part outside $W_N$; the
adversary branches over all tuples, selecting such a part each time; the
construction terminates at depth $N$ with leaves outside $W_0$, i.e.\
forced exits. Finiteness of the state, action, and observation spaces
makes the tree finite.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The stable family is the kernel; the $H/H-2$ convention]
On a finite system the chain $W_0\supseteq W_1\supseteq\cdots$ stabilizes.
Its stable family equals the infinite-horizon observation-based kernel, and
membership in it coincides with the successor harness's convention
(verdict at $H$ agreeing with the verdict at $H-2$).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Monotone decrease in a finite lattice forces stabilization. After
stabilization the recursion is a fixed point of the belief predecessor
operator, i.e.\ the greatest fixed point below $W_0$ --- the kernel. The
two-step coincidence of verdicts is exactly two stabilizer iterations.
\end{proof}

\section{Verified record (6/6 exact check families)}

\paragraph{E1.} The recursion is computed as explicit belief families over
all $2^9$ safe subsets, under three structures (blind, aggregate, full);
$W_{k+1}\subseteq W_k$ holds setwise at every step.

\paragraph{E2.} Soundness and completeness agree instance by instance: for
all $37$ audited beliefs ($36$ safe pairs plus the three-state audited
belief), $3$ structures, and $N=1,\dots,6$: membership in $W_N$ equals the
forward game value --- $666$ identifications, no exceptions.

\paragraph{E3.} The belief $\{(1,2),(2,1)\}$ under the aggregate reading
lies outside $W_2$; the constructive proof extracts its counterstrategy
tree and every leaf validates as a forced exit.

\paragraph{E4.} Each of the three chains stabilizes, and the stable family
agrees exactly with the $H=12$/$H-2=10$ verdicts of the successor harness
on all audited beliefs --- the convention is a theorem here.

\paragraph{E5 (horizon-freeness, an exact finding).} The planned search for
a horizon-necessity instance (viable at $N=2$, nonviable at $N=4$) found
none: verdicts at $N=2,4,8$ coincide for every audited belief and
structure. On this system viability is decided by depth $2$; the finding
is recorded rather than forced.

\paragraph{E6 (no asymptotic gap).} Every belief outside the stable kernel
admits a validated finite counterstrategy tree at the stabilization depth:
on finite systems, nonviability is never merely witnessed --- it is
certified.

\section{What is not claimed}

Completeness is a finite-horizon, finite-system statement: the
infinite-horizon calculus for general systems keeps its certificate gap by
design, and nothing here closes it. Continuous state spaces, stochastic
transitions (the stochastic selector's setting), and hybrid mode switching
(the programme's hybrid companion) are out of scope; the counterstrategy
tree is a game object, not a probabilistic witness. No computational
complexity claims are made: the audited systems are small, and the
recursion's practical cost is acknowledged.
