\begin{abstract}
Institutions observe different indicators, and their votes on a shared
instrument count only in agreement; the obstruction calculus has so far
spoken with one regulator's voice. This paper, the D13 item of the
generalization catalogue, models two agencies with split observation
rights (agency $1$ reads $z_1$, agency $2$ reads $z_2$) and a unanimity
command protocol: the instrument executes only on agreement, and
disagreement deadlocks the system. On the two-patch working system
(Abaee 2026a, Section 8): the coordinator (reading the joint state)
sustains $28$ of $36$ safe pairs; the decentralized unanimity regime,
enumerated exhaustively over all $256$ agency-law pairs
($256=16\times16$: each agency's law maps its coordinate's four values to
two votes), sustains exactly $12$ --- and exactly $16$ coordinated-viable
pairs are lost, including the exact instance $\{(1,2),(3,1)\}$, viable
under each agency alone and under the coordinator but nonviable under
\emph{every} unanimity law pair. The audited belief $\{(1,2),(2,1)\}$
survives decentralization only through the anti-symmetric voting
discipline (one agency's low-stock vote paired with the other's
high-stock vote; $16$ of the $256$ law pairs sustain it --- a numerical
coincidence with the $16$ lost pairs, no identity), and the authority
restriction (forbidding agency 2's vote) destroys the entire decentralized
kernel ($12\to0$, strict instance: the audited belief). The obstruction
is robust to a one-step delayed own reading (agency 2 voting on its
previous coordinate value). Communication has exact, computable value:
the deadlock shadow (8/8 check families).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and relation to the programme}

The catalogue proposes multiple regulators with split observation rights
$y_i=O_i(x)$ and split command rights $u_i\in U_i$, joint action
constrained by common knowledge, delays, authority, and enforcement ---
and warns that it is a larger extension to be added only where
institutional constraints are central. The governance focus of the
programme is exactly that case; the paper stays minimal --- two agencies,
one instrument, unanimity --- and computes exactly.

\section{Split rights under unanimity}

The realized instrument is the agreed vote: $u=u_1(z_1)$ when
$u_1(z_1)=u_2(z_2)$; otherwise the protocol deadlocks --- $u=0$ executes
no instrument, and the protocol's declared uncoordinated-extraction rule
applies, both coordinates losing one
($z^{+}=(\max(0,z_1-1),\max(0,z_2-1))$; a declared rule of this
protocol, distinct from the directed damage $2$ of an executed
instrument). The laws: agency $i$'s law maps its coordinate's four values
$z_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ to votes $\{1,2\}$, so each agency fields
$2^{4}=16$ laws and the enumeration covers $16\times16=256$ law pairs.
The induced closed loop is deterministic for a fixed law pair
$(u_1,u_2)$; the belief set is updated \emph{pointwise} under the
realized instrument (both realizations of the reading tracked), so cycle
detection on belief sets is exact. Decentralizability of a belief is an
\emph{existence certificate} over law pairs --- an institutional-design
statement (which beliefs the protocol can sustain), not a belief-indexed
controller: no agency needs knowledge of any audited belief; the
enumeration certifies what the protocol can and cannot sustain.

\begin{proposition}[The deadlock obstruction and its exact boundary]
\label{prop:deadlock}
The coordinator's kernel holds $28$ of $36$ safe pairs; the decentralized
kernel holds exactly $12$; exactly $16$ coordinated-viable pairs are not
decentralizable, and one such instance is exhibited --- the pair $\{(1,2),(3,1)\}$,
viable under each agency alone (the $z_1$-only and $z_2$-only structures
of Abaee 2026a, Section 8's structure audit) and under the coordinator,
nonviable under every unanimity law pair. The audited belief
$\{(1,2),(2,1)\}$ is decentralizable only through voting laws including
the anti-symmetric discipline --- agency 1 voting $1$ on $z_1\le1$ and
$2$ on $z_1\ge2$, agency 2 voting $2$ unless $z_2=2$ --- with $16$ of
the $256$ law pairs sustaining it: institutional coordination is
load-bearing exactly on the deadlock shadow.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Exhaustive enumeration over the $256$ law pairs with exact trajectory
simulation; the exhibited instance and the anti-symmetric law's
trajectory are verified directly. The counting identity
$28-12=16$ is the communication value on this family.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Authority and delay]
Removing agency 2's right to vote $2$ collapses the decentralized kernel
from $12$ to $0$ (strict instance: the audited belief), and granting
agency 2 a one-step delayed own reading does not restore the deadlock
boundary: the decentralized kernel under the delayed reading remains
strictly below the coordinator's. (The delay is local memory --- agency 2
voting on its own previous coordinate value --- not common knowledge of
the joint state; the terminology of the first edition is here corrected.)
Relation to the lattice companion: its institutional class (aggregate
reading with a no-repeat codex) sustains $24$ of $36$ on the same grid ---
a different institutional restriction with a different count; no ordering
between the unanimity protocol's $12$ and the codex chain's $24$ is
claimed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both computations are exhaustive and exact: the restricted law space is
the $16$ pairs with $u_2\equiv1$; the delayed regime lets agency 2 vote
on its own previous coordinate value (initialized at the current value)
and simulates the augmented (state, previous-$z_2$) trajectories
exactly.
\end{proof}

\section{Verified record (8/8 exact check families)}

E1 the coordinator's kernel ($28$, the full-observation kernel of Abaee
2026a, Section 8); E2 the decentralized kernel ($12$) by exhaustion over
$256$ law pairs; E3 the exact boundary ($16$ lost pairs), the exhibited
instance $\{(1,2),(3,1)\}$ (individually sufficient under each agency
alone and under the coordinator, jointly nonviable), and the audited
belief's anti-symmetric survival; E4 the setwise communication value
($12\subset28$); E5 the authority collapse ($12\to0$, strict instance
recorded); E6 the delayed-own-reading robustness of the deadlock
boundary; E7 the law-space identity ($256=2^{4}\times2^{4}$) with the
deadlock transition as declared ($u=0$: both coordinates lose one) and
the loss instance's three-way viability pinned; E8 the two sixteens
distinguished ($16$ lost pairs against $16$ sustaining law pairs --- a
coincidence of this grid, no identity).

\section{What is not claimed}

Two agencies, one instrument, unanimity: no $n$-agency theory, no
sequential or weighted voting, no mechanism design, no enforcement or
compliance dynamics, no negotiation models. The deadlock protocol is the
simplest agreement discipline; procedural alternatives (default rules,
tie-breakers) are not classified, and the deadlock extraction rule is a
declared modelling choice of this protocol, not a consequence of the
dynamics. Institutional effectiveness is
not estimated from data. The catalogue's larger-programme warning is
adopted: the paper claims the exact deadlock phenomenon and its boundary
on the audited system, nothing broader.
