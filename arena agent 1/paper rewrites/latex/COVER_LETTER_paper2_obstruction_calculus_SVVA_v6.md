# Cover Letter — Paper 2 (v6, synced to manuscript v26)

**Title:** An Obstruction Calculus for Viability under Incomplete Observation
**Author:** Amin Abaee (Independent Researcher)
**Journal:** *Set-Valued and Variational Analysis*
**Mathematics Subject Classification:** 49J53; 93B03; 93C41; 91B76

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, **"An Obstruction Calculus for Viability under Incomplete Observation,"** for consideration in *Set-Valued and Variational Analysis*.

**What the paper does.** Viability theory under *perfect* measurement is mature: the viability kernel exists as the largest viable subset, characterised by Nagumo-type tangency conditions. Under *incomplete* observation the theory has been asymmetric — the sufficiency side is carried by Veliov's output-feedback regulation condition and the estimation-tube programme, while the complementary direction, certifying that *no* observation-based policy is viable, has received less systematic treatment. The manuscript develops that instrument: a calculus of **obstruction certificates**, sound sufficient conditions for nonviability (equivalently, necessary conditions for viability), finitely checkable in the polyhedral, finite-fibre, and finite-horizon cases and given by analytic drift-and-timing conditions elsewhere. The certificates do not exhaust the complement of the epistemic kernel — the observation-based counterpart of the viability kernel — and the paper says so explicitly; in finite systems backward belief recursion is complete. Six mechanisms are established (finite-time exit, epistemic emptiness by admissibility, the common-action obstruction, the delayed-information obstruction, the fibre certification criterion, and a certainty-equivalence trap under a policy-class restriction), and they are organised by one selector principle, stated as a proposition in Section 2 — observation-based viability is the existence of a common response that is admissible, safe, and recursively viable for every compatible state — with the obstructions nested in a ladder and the epistemic kernel monotone in action sets, disturbances, information, and policy class. A comparison-function bound sharpens the exit-time certificate, a uniform-margin lemma connects the instantaneous and tube obstructions, and a worked Farkas certificate shows the common-action obstruction as an explicit linear feasibility test. Six schematic figures illustrate the geometric mechanisms: four for the mechanisms themselves, plus the obstruction ladder and a one-step obstruction tree.

**Position within the journal's own literature.** The paper is set-valued and variational throughout, and it engages the journal's viability-theory lineage directly. Its object is the *epistemic kernel*; its subject is the *failure side* of that kernel, which Aubin (2001) and Aubin–Catté (2002, *Set-Valued Analysis*) characterized through Poincaré's "shadow," capture basins, and discriminating kernels. The obstruction calculus is an observation-constrained analogue of that programme: the finite-time exit certificate certifies membership in the full-information shadow, and the common-action, timing, and fibre certificates extend the same question to incomplete observation. The certificates are also positioned against barrier certificates (Prajna–Jadbabaie, Prajna–Rantzer, Maghenem–Sanfelice) and against the estimation-tube reduction (Cardaliaguet–Quincampoix–Saint-Pierre), delimiting rather than contradicting both. I have chosen the MSC codes (49J53, 93B03, 93C41) to reflect the set-valued/differential-inclusion and dynamic-control character of the results.

**Why it matters.** Sustainability governance operates under incomplete observation: stocks are assessed at discrete review intervals, indicators are coarser than the state, some stocks are unobserved, and information may arrive late. The paper makes precise when failure is attributable to *information* rather than to *dynamics*, and it converts each impossibility into a concrete design specification — observation timing, coarseness, aggregation, and bias correction. Where a certificate applies, it identifies *what to change* (a faster indicator, a separating observation, per-floor measurement, or correction of a known bias).

**Fit and originality.** To my knowledge, constructive, certificate-based instruments for the nonviability side of viability under incomplete observation have not previously been collected in this form. The paper is honest about its scope: the continuous-time certificates are sound but not complete (the open middle ground is stated), completeness is claimed only where backward recursion is exact, and one failure mode — insufficient post-observation recourse — is identified as beyond the present certificates and left open. This is, I believe, the appropriate scope for a new instrument, and precisely the kind of rigorous, applicable result *Set-Valued and Variational Analysis* exists to publish.

The manuscript is self-contained, with a bounded-constructions appendix. No code or data were used; all constructions are symbolic. I confirm the work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and that I have no conflicts of interest.
