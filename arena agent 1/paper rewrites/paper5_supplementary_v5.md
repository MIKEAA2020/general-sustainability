# Supplementary Material — Periodic Review as Sampled Governance

*Accompanies: "Periodic Review as Sampled Governance: Sample-and-Hold Dynamics of Assessment-Driven Effort Control, a Selected 42-Stock Spectral Screen, and the Northern Cod Case."*

This supplementary accompanies the main paper on sampled governance — a governance loop in which state observation and control update occur at discrete review times — and consolidates the supporting material that does not fit in the main text's flow. It carries seven bodies of material: a statement inventory (S1); the remaining proofs and identification results (S2); the epistemic-layer results on observation, information, and delayed information (S3); the case-screening records at full detail (S4); the ecosystem-context and toy-test record (S5); the distributive-layer detail (S6); the empirical hypotheses with their declared tests (S7); and the reproducibility register (S8). Every item carries its evidentiary status on the line; none is stated above it.

## S2. Remaining proofs and identification results

This section records the supplementary's formal content. The two propositions referenced in S2.1 are stated and proved in full in the main text; their proofs are not repeated here. The lemma of S2.2 is also in the main text, but its retained remarks are recorded here because they fix the lemma's reading. The theorem of S2.3 is stated and proved in full below.

**Notation.** Throughout S2 and S3, $E$ denotes effort, $N$ biomass, $K$ carrying capacity, $q$ catchability, $r$ the intrinsic growth rate, $\delta$ the equilibrium memory level, $\Delta_{\rm ref}$ a reference signal scale, $\delta_0$ a baseline signal feedthrough, $\eta$ the softplus effort-shaping coefficient, $Z_{\rm ref}$ a reference signal level, $E_{\max}$ the effort scale at which the softplus term saturates, $\tau_m$ a measurement delay, and $T_{\rm obs}$ the observation interval between successive informative assessments. The softplus sharpener $\operatorname{sp}_k$ has slope $\operatorname{sp}_k'(0)=1/2$ and curvature $\operatorname{sp}_k''(0)=k/4$ at the origin. Subscripts on $\tau$ denote branches (e.g. $\tau_\pm$ for the lower and upper Hopf delays).

**S2.1 Forward invariance and the phase-line obstruction.** **Proposition S2.1** (Forward invariance; phase-line obstruction). *Both propositions are stated and proved in full in the main text (§3.1 and §2.7); their proofs are not repeated here.* The phase-line obstruction is an elementary consequence of uniqueness for scalar autonomous ODEs (standard textbook material). The proposition records the consequence needed for the cod case: no exact trajectory of a single fixed scalar autonomous model can repeatedly rise and fall across a common level.

**S2.2 Extra-loss threshold shift (conditional lemma).** **Lemma S2.2** (Extra-loss threshold shift, conditional). *The lemma is stated and proved in full in the main text (§2.7); its proof is not repeated here. The retained remarks below fix the lemma's reading and record the parallel multiplicative-mortality form.*

The lemma is conditional on the modified positive equilibria existing — the constant loss $C$ (respectively the extra mortality $M_x$) below the production maximum — and an effective threshold is not automatically a shifted structural parameter: it is a property of the depressed production function, and the coalescence/disappearance case is part of the statement. The multiplicative-mortality parallel ($f_M(S) = f(S) - M_x S$, positive equilibria solving $f(S)/S = M_x$, with $S = 0$ remaining an equilibrium in every case, unlike the constant-loss case where $f_C(0) = -C < 0$) is the same two-interval argument applied to $h(S) = f(S)/S$, whose vertex is the production maximum of the per-capita curve. **Status: conditional theorem** (proof displayed in full in the main text; the existence and production-maximum hypotheses are declared, not established for the case at hand).

**S2.3 The dimensionless identifiability chart (theorem, proof in full).** In the three-state gated core, let $e=E/E_{\max}$, $n=N/K$, and

$$
\varrho=\frac{qE_{\max}}{r},\qquad
a=\frac{\delta}{\Delta_{\rm ref}},\qquad
b=\frac{\delta_0\delta}{\eta E_{\max}(Z_{\rm ref}+\delta)} .
$$

**Theorem S2.3** (Dimensionless identifiability). Suppose (H1) the system is at an interior equilibrium of the three-state gated core, and (H2) time is scaled by $r^{-1}$. Then $e^*=(a+\sqrt{a^2+4b})/2$ and $N^*/K=1-\varrho e^*$. After scaling time by $r^{-1}$, the local Hopf delays $r\tau_\pm$ depend on $(\varrho,\lambda_F,a,b,Z_{\rm ref}/\delta,\eta/r,r\tau_m)$ and on $\operatorname{sp}_k'(0)=1/2$, where $\lambda_F=rK/\Delta_{\rm ref}$ is the flow-scale group. The four combinations $(qE^*,\ E^*/E_{\max},\ \eta E^*/\Delta_{\rm ref},\ \delta_0/\eta)$ do **not** determine the characteristic function: the coupling entry $-qN^*$ still contains $q$ separately unless effort is scaled by $E_{\max}$. Two calibrated parameter points are two points in $(\eta/r,\varrho)$, not one class.

*Proof.* At an interior rest the surplus argument vanishes, so $qEN=S(N)$ and the signal equation gives $Z^*=\Phi(0)=\delta$; substituting into $F_B=0$ with $e=E/E_{\max}$ yields the quadratic $e^2-ae-b=0$ (the softplus term contributes $\eta E(Z/\Delta_{\rm ref}-E/E_{\max})=\eta E_{\max}e(a-e)$, and the baseline contributes $\delta_0\delta/(Z_{\rm ref}+\delta)$, whence the displayed root and $N^*/K=1-\varrho e^*$ from $S(N^*)=qE^*N^*$). After the time change $s=rt$, every entry of the linearisation scales into the listed groups: the coupling $-qN^*=-r\varrho N^*/K$, the signal feedthrough $qE^*=r\varrho e^*$, the effort entries carry $\eta/r$ and $r\tau_m$, and the characteristic function is homogeneous in these entries, so its roots — the dimensionless frequencies and delays — are functions of the groups only. □

Two qualifications ride the parameters. Hopf points are invariant under the softplus sharpness $k\in\{5,10,20,40\}$ at fixed $\delta$, not at fixed $\delta/k$ (the equilibrium memory $Z^*=\delta$ itself depends on $k$ at the baseline calibration); and criticality is not $k$-invariant, since the first Lyapunov coefficient contains $\operatorname{sp}_k''(0)=k/4$. The chart is complementary to the effort-scale non-identifiability: the separate effort scale is not identifiable from the stock–signal pair alone, and the chart states the groups that do fix the equilibrium ratio and the dimensionless delays once effort is scaled by $E_{\max}$. The calibration consequence is binding for every parameter claim in the main text: two calibrated systems are comparable only through these groups, never through raw effort scales. **Status: theorem** (proof complete; the equilibrium-existence hypothesis is declared).

## S3. The epistemic layer

This section collects the observation-and-information results that lie behind the design logic of the main text. The objects of study are observation fibres (sets of latent states that share an observation), information refinements (ways of making one information structure finer than another), and the kernel recursion that fixes what an institution with finer information can guarantee. Each is stated with its proof where one is supplied in the supplementary.

**S3.1 Observation-fibre certification (theorem, proof in full).** **Theorem S3.1** (Observation-fibre certification). Suppose (H1) $D$ is a set of admissible latent states; (H2) $\mathcal O:D\to\mathcal Y$ is an observation map; (H3) $K\subseteq D$ is a safety set. Then a deterministic observation-only certificate $c:\mathcal Y\to\{0,1\}$ with $c(\mathcal O(z))=\mathbf 1_K(z)$ for every $z\in D$ exists if and only if membership in $K$ is constant on every observation fibre: $\mathcal O(z_1)=\mathcal O(z_2)$ implies $\mathbf 1_K(z_1)=\mathbf 1_K(z_2)$.

*Proof.* If $c$ exists and $\mathcal O(z_1)=\mathcal O(z_2)$, then $\mathbf 1_K(z_1)=c(\mathcal O(z_1))=c(\mathcal O(z_2))=\mathbf 1_K(z_2)$. Conversely, under fibre constancy define $c(y)=\mathbf 1_K(z)$ for any $z$ with $\mathcal O(z)=y$ — well defined by fibre constancy — and arbitrarily off the image; then $c(\mathcal O(z))=\mathbf 1_K(z)$ on $D$. □

The theorem concerns current latent-state membership certification only. It does not imply that observation-based robust control is impossible whenever a fibre contains distinct states: those states may admit a common safe action, and a dynamic impossibility theorem must show incompatibility of every admissible causal policy over observationally indistinguishable histories. Its identification content is direct: aggregate-only certification misclassifies component safety exactly when the operational aggregate's fibres cross component-safety boundaries — the content tested by the observation-aggregation hypothesis of S7.

**S3.2 Information monotonicity of recovery time (theorem, proof in full).** **Theorem S3.2** (Information monotonicity of recovery time). Suppose (H1) $\mathcal I_1\succeq\mathcal I_2$ ($\mathcal I_1$ more informative). Then $\tau_{\mathcal E}^{\mathcal I_1}(B)\le\tau_{\mathcal E}^{\mathcal I_2}(B)$ whenever both are compared from corresponding initial information states.

*Proof.* Any policy implementable under the coarser structure $\mathcal I_2$ is implementable under the finer structure $\mathcal I_1$ by applying the garbling map; hence the optimal worst-case recovery time under $\mathcal I_1$ is no larger. □

The companion statement for viability kernels: if $\mathcal I_1$ causally refines $\mathcal I_2$ while dynamics, authority, implementation, uncertainty class, and safety constraints are unchanged, then $\operatorname{IViab}^{\mathcal I_2}_T\subseteq\operatorname{IViab}^{\mathcal I_1}_T$ — a controller with finer information can ignore it and implement a coarser-information strategy. The inclusion requires the two kernels represented in a common physical initial-state space or a declared map between belief-state spaces (conditional theorem). For the sampled-governance layer these are the formal counterparts of the review-cadence question: coarsening the information structure (longer $T_{\rm obs}$, delayed assessments) can only shrink what is achievable, and by exactly how much is a kernel-level question, not a parameter drift.

**S3.3 Hidden-mode conflict (counterexample/limit).** **Example S3.3** (Hidden-mode conflict, counterexample). Let an unobserved parameter satisfy $\theta\in\{-1,+1\}$, with $\dot z=\theta u$, $u\in\{-1,+1\}$, $z\ge 0$. At $z=0$: if $\theta=+1$ only $u=+1$ is safe; if $\theta=-1$ only $u=-1$ is safe. Both states are individually robustly viable, but $B=\{(0,+1),(0,-1)\}$ admits no common safe action, so $B$ is not in the robust-viability kernel. This is a purely informational failure — no stochasticity or estimation quality is involved — and it is an impossibility witness for common-action viability, not a positive specialisation.

**S3.4 Delayed-information obstruction (counterexample/limit, proof in full).** **Proposition S3.4** (Delayed-information obstruction, counterexample). Suppose (H1) every possible action at belief $B_0$ allows a disturbance and a compatible state to reach a locally nonviable boundary point before the next informative observation time $T_{\rm obs}$. Then $B_0$ is not in the robust-viability kernel. A sufficient condition is the existence of a constraint function $q$ and $\varepsilon>0$ with $\inf_{x\in B_t}\inf_{d}D^{+}q(x;f(x,u,d))\le-\varepsilon$ throughout an uncertainty branch and $T_{\rm obs}>\inf_{x\in B_0}q(x)/\varepsilon$. Information may be accurate but arrive too late.

*Proof.* Fix an action and an uncertainty branch; the Dini inequality integrates to $q(x_t)\le\inf_{x\in B_0}q(x)-\varepsilon t$, so the constraint is violated by time $t^*=\inf q/\varepsilon<T_{\rm obs}$, before any informative observation can alter the control; since this holds for every action and branch, no observation-based policy can save $B_0$. □

The delay in this obstruction is the observation interval $T_{\rm obs}$ — information timing, the review-interval spectrum's object — not a retarded functional differential equation. The empirical reading: review cadence enters the safety question at the kernel level, and an accurate assessment delivered too late is operationally equivalent to no assessment within the window in which the constraint is lost.

**S3.5 The ideal benchmark and the kernel recursion (conditional statements).** **Proposition S3.5** (Ideal benchmark and kernel recursion, conditional). *Part A (ideal ODE benchmark).* Suppose (H1) the system is a finite-dimensional fully observed ODE module with a closed, sufficiently regular state-safe set; (H2) at every boundary state there exists a common admissible control for all active constraints and all disturbances; (H3) the safe-control correspondence admits an appropriate regular selection or viable solution concept, and solutions exist on $[0,T]$. Then standard controlled-invariance results establish full-information viability on that horizon (Aubin, 1991; Aubin, Bayen, and Saint-Pierre, 2011). This is an ideal ODE benchmark — not a theorem for delayed, sampled, partially observed institutions — and it enters the main text only as the ideal limit of the sampled-governance comparison.

*Part B (kernel recursion).* The institutional object against which the benchmark is read is the joint institutional information state $(B,h)$ — compatible physical histories and parameters together with an institutional mode — with prescription authority $a\in\Gamma(B,h)$, implementation correspondence $u\in\mathcal E(B,h,a)$, and the lower-game quantifier order: a non-anticipative prescription strategy exists against every admissible implementation, disturbance, parameter, and observation branch. The finite-horizon epistemic-institutional kernel satisfies the safe-base recursion $\mathfrak K_{n+1}=\mathcal T(\mathfrak K_n):=\mathfrak S\cap\operatorname{Pre}_{\mathfrak I}(\mathfrak K_n)$, whose monotone operator has a greatest fixed point by Tarski's theorem (Tarski, 1955); under the additional $\omega$-continuity-from-above and strategy-closure hypotheses the countable intersection $\bigcap_n\mathfrak K_n$ is that fixed point, and without them the greatest fixed point is obtained by transfinite descending iteration (conditional statement). The kernel object is one across its ODE and sampled instantiations, and its role here is the comparison structure it fixes: physical feasibility, information, authority, and implementation are jointly represented, and none is reducible to another.

**S3.6 Value of information, informational recovery, normative monotonicity (remarks).** **Remark S3.6** (Value of information, informational recovery, normative monotonicity). (i) *Robust information value.* For a noncompensatory safety margin $q(X,u)=\min_i m_i(X,u)$, define, when the optimization is well posed, $V^{\mathcal I}_T(B,h)=\sup_{\Pi\in\Pi_{\mathcal I}}\inf_{\text{compatible branches}}\min_{0\le t\le T}q(X(t),u(t))$. A nonnegative value is equivalent to finite-horizon robust safety only when the supremum is attained or the relevant viability closure is used; for an information refinement, the difference $V^{\mathcal I_1}_T-V^{\mathcal I_2}_T$ is a safety-margin value of information, not an entropy metric. (ii) *Informational recovery.* Recovery is an information-state property: a strategy keeps the physical trajectory in the emergency envelope $\mathcal E_X$ until time $T$ and reaches a viable information state $(B_T,h_T)\in\mathfrak K_\infty$. Physical recoverability therefore does not imply institutional recoverability: the belief must itself be epistemically viable, which is the requirement that makes observation architecture part of the recovery question. (iii) *Normative monotonicity.* If two declared constraint systems satisfy $\mathcal C_X(\lambda_1)\subseteq\mathcal C_X(\lambda_2)$ and $\mathcal A(\cdot;\lambda_1)\subseteq\mathcal A(\cdot;\lambda_2)$, then the corresponding viability kernels are nested in the same direction under aligned dynamics, information, and uncertainty classes. This exposes the feasibility consequences of normative choices without purporting to derive or justify them — the scope discipline that the distributive layer of S6 inherits.

**S3.7 Model objects and empirical proxies (boundary content).** **Remark S3.7** (Model objects and empirical proxies, boundary content). The model hitting time $T^{\rm dep}=\inf\{t>0:A(t)\le\varepsilon A(0)\}$ lives on trajectories of a declared conserved-material ledger or a named reduced system and is not computed in the empirical tables; the instantaneous diagnostics (gross and net active-pool horizons) measure support use and decline and are not identified with empirical regression statistics; the constructed proxies (the groundwater trend-to-window-minimum ratio, the fisheries logarithmic horizon, the reserve-life ratio) are the public-data objects. None of the three is interchangeable with the others, and no empirical statistic is identified with a model object without a declared map. Where distributional statements are attached to such proxies, their uncertainty boundary is part of the content: the first-passage laws (inverse-Gaussian groundwater passage, geometric-Brownian fisheries passage) condition on the drift, the barrier, and the noise scale; the drift is estimated from a finite, potentially autocorrelated record; the barrier is selected from that same record; measurement error, serial dependence, seasonal forcing, spatial aggregation, trend breaks, and common climatic drivers are separate uncertainties. Integrating out uncertainty in the drift, the barrier, or the noise scale gives a predictive distribution that is generally a mixture rather than a single inverse-Gaussian law, and a residual scale from the same window would not identify process noise. No calibrated predictive distribution is claimed. This boundary-only content governs every power and detectability statement of the main text (§3.6).

**S3.8 Open problem (binary-sensor threshold discretization).** **Open problem S3.8** (Binary-sensor threshold discretization). State the conditions under which the binary-sensor observation model is a threshold discretization of the continuous observation model. The problem is open; no partial result is claimed. **Status: open problem.**

## S4. Case-screening records at full detail

This section records the case-level coefficients, correlations, periods, and lag estimates that the main text reports at summary level. Each entry below is an **author calculation** from registered input series, not a source-reported value, and each carries the scope of the calculation on the line.

- **Sheridan-6 groundwater:** decline–recovery–decline pattern; precipitation explains approximately half the index-well variance ($R^2\approx0.47$); the official programme record establishes a 55 acre-inch block allocation over the five-year first period rather than a continuously adjusted feedback.
- **Bangkok:** pumping declines after 1999, but subsidence and recovery contain spatially heterogeneous consolidation and rainfall effects.
- **La Mancha Oriental:** extraction rose again in 2019–2023 to an author-calculated average of approximately 312 hm³ yr⁻¹, with crop composition, evapotranspiration, and surface-water substitution complicating attribution; the system was on the stabilising side before that relapse.

- **Icelandic haddock** (related rule): post-implementation coefficient of variation 0.143, lower despite higher recruitment variability.
- **Peruvian anchoveta:** the 1950–2019 catch series has a robust period near 3.7 yr, matching ENSO recurrence as a candidate driver; cross-correlation gives $|r|\approx0.31$ with ENSO leading catch. The 2026-09-03 battery on the Sea Around Us series behind that figure (Peru entity 604 taxon 87, `shortened.txt`, and Chile `SAU Taxa 600004 v50-1.csv`, both 1950–2019; NOAA PSL ERSSTv5 NINO1/NINO3/NINO3.4/NINO4 and PSL SOI, annual means; artifacts under `analysis/anchoveta_enso/`): the 3.7-yr peak reproduces (3.70 yr, co-dominant with 7.96 yr in a flat multi-peak spectrum; 3.63 yr on the 1960–2019 subset); Peru–SOI r = +0.513 (lag 0, p < 0.0001), +0.419 (lag 1), +0.396 (lag 2); Chile–SOI r = +0.390 (lag 2, p = 0.001) and +0.417 (lag 4); ten of ninety index–lag cells significant at BH-FDR 0.05, all of them SOI cells; Granger one-sided ENSO→catch in both stocks (Peru p = 0.00009, Chile p = 0.00016, at lag 2; reverse directions p ≥ 0.19); split-half confines the association to 1950–1984 (lag-one r = +0.42, p = 0.013; after 1985 r = +0.13, n.s., with the lag-two cell reversing sign), and the early-period strength survives exclusion of the three collapse years (r = +0.46, p = 0.010) and a reported-catches-only sensitivity on the Chilean series (r = +0.39, p = 0.024), so the post-1985 attenuation is not a reconstruction artefact. Mechanistic-scale support: the documented El Niño–anchoveta pathway (Chávez et al., 2003) and the sediment fish-scale records (Gutiérrez et al., 2009). Registered strengthening tests (§3.6): the era-split attribution as focal test, the pathway model, and the fixed SOI index–lag specification. The subannual review regime lies below the anchovy-class response region — controller nonclassification prevents that comparison from testing the mechanism.
- **Produced-capital systems:** the unconfounded delay oscillators identified (livestock and electricity-capacity cycles) do not contain the autonomously regenerating stock the resource model assumes.

## S5. Ecosystem context and toy tests

This section records two ecosystem-context facts and four toy simulations, both at exploratory status, that sit alongside the cod case but do not enter its evidentiary chain.

Between 1985–87 and 2013–15, harp seal biomass rose from 49,600 t to 161,183 t (a 3.2-fold increase) and capelin biomass fell from 13.77 to 4.97 t km⁻² (a 64% decline) in the mass-balance record (Tam and Bundy, 2019). **Status: source-reported descriptive mass-balance inputs, background only.** The statements that the predator pit is unsupported and that no capelin correlation exists would each require a defined estimator, interval, lag structure, and uncertainty quantification, and are kept untested and exploratory until reproduced.

Four toy simulations — Schaefer growth from $1.2\times10^5$ t, below-threshold decay to zero, above-threshold convergence to $K$, and the below-threshold monotone no-pulse check at $C=3\times10^3$ — execute as designed. They confirm only that the selected parameterised toy trajectories behave as intended: the test does not fit northern cod and does not validate the constrained-mortality experiment. **Status: exploratory computational record.**

## S6. Distributive-layer detail

This section records the distributive-layer content of the main text's §4.6: constituency, measured floors, instruments, the unreproduced community-level pipeline, the social-ecological bridge conjecture, and the anti-domination residue. Each is recorded with its status on the line.

**Constituency.** The worst-off relevant population is a modelling choice — a named constituency $G_t$; for the cod case the candidate constituency is the registered inshore harvesters and licence holders of NAFO 2J3KL. The declaration is not free: the displayed community-level data are all-resident employment income including aquaculture and processing, so the constituency must either be redefined as the census-subdivision populations or licence-holder microdata or administrative data obtained — the community table is not a licence-holder panel — and the geography crosswalk from census subdivisions to 2J3KL dependence is a separate declared mapping. **Status: definitions plus a registered, unreproduced pipeline.**

**Measured floors.** A measured floor is a pair $(I_k,c_k)$ — an instrument and a cutoff — a measurement object distinct from the norm that motivates it. If several floors are in force, the arrangement fails as soon as any measured margin $m_k(G,t)=I_k(G,t)-c_k$ is negative; the object of record is the vector $m(G,t)$. The conjunction is exact and admits no compensatory master scalar. Non-decline is a normative rule, not automatically an empirically justified floor: baseline, cohort, inflation and purchasing-power treatment, uncertainty, attrition, acceptable variation, authority, and structural diversification all require declaration before a floor measures anything. **Status: definitions (methodological rule).**

**Instruments.** Two external instruments are admitted as world-hooks for deprivation, each retained with release and vintage locks: the global Multidimensional Poverty Index (Alkire–Foster method, OPHI/UNDP releases) as a multidimensional deprivation instrument, and the World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform for income-axis measurement. Neither instrument exhausts the normative object — adaptive capacity held by someone, used against someone, paid for by someone — and the norm is not filled with a scalar score. The poverty-line vintage is part of the instrument declaration: the current primary international line is $3.00/day at 2021 purchasing-power parity, and the $2.15/day 2017-PPP line is available only for historically vintage-consistent analysis; any empirical use must state the line, the welfare aggregate, the PPP year, the population, and the release vintage.

## S7. Empirical hypotheses with declared tests

The architecture-level distinctions of the main text have prospective empirical consequences. This section records each hypothesis together with the test that would discriminate it; no test has been executed. **Status of all: declared hypotheses; tests declared; none executed.**

**Observation aggregation.** In systems where independent component measurements reveal safety-crossing fibres for the operational aggregate indicator, aggregate-only certification will produce nonzero component-safety misclassification under prospective validation. The test must freeze the aggregate, the component thresholds, the candidate population, the observation model, and the error metric before validation; finding no safety-crossing fibre in the declared domain counts against the hypothesis for that application. The certification limit of S3.1 is the mechanism.

**Governance phase ordering.** After controlling declared ecological and market confounders, extractive and protective controllers will exhibit opposite signed phase relations between assessed decline and realised extraction, and measured deployment delay will shift that relation in the direction predicted by the registered controller. Contradictory causal ordering, or absence of the preregistered phase relation at adequate power, rejects the mechanism for that population. The frequency-domain caveat governs short records: where a predicted endogenous period is unresolvable, the test estimates gain and phase over resolvable frequencies from exogenous excitation, a dated intervention, or a justified closed-loop identification design (Forssell and Ljung, 1999) — a stock–effort cross-spectrum alone does not identify the open-loop transfer operator or separate ecological, assessment, decision, and deployment lags.

**Substitution certificate.** For a frozen linear technology approximation, dual infeasibility certificates will predict service shortfalls under held-out resource/capacity scenarios more reliably than an unconstrained aggregate elasticity. A fair test compares out-of-sample scenarios and records pathway changes that invalidate the frozen technology matrix.

Three further architecture-level hypotheses are declared at the same status — testable through restricted models, each with its test requirement on the line, none executed. **Stage-composition discrimination.** Models with explicit juvenile compartments outperform adult-only models when recruitment suppression is the dominant pressure; the test is an out-of-sample forecast comparison across the two model classes on the same series. **Spatial-aggregation failure.** Spatially aggregated predictions fail systematically where measured variance places the nonlinear moment correction outside its uncertainty bound; the test compares aggregated and spatially explicit predictions against withheld spatial data. **Exergy/capacity constraint effects.** Exergy/capacity constraints alter deployment and threshold risk in directions predicted before fitting the outcome; the test is intervention trials with and without the capacity constraint under preregistered directional outcomes. Each hypothesis requires a registered minimal model, data-generating process, estimator, and falsification rule, and the full architecture is not fitted merely because a smaller model fails.
