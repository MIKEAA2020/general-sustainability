## 2. Register audit — what every edition still carries

The corpus is internally disciplined to a degree rarely seen — statuses are honest, negative results are first-class, no self-praise, no phantom opponents, citations for every critiqued position (e.g., Paper 1 §5.2's Das–Dennis, Cinelli et al., Neumayer, Boos, Ekins). The register violations are concentrated in one place: **programme scaffolding that surrounds the science**, exactly as the standing diagnosis says. Confirmed with line-level evidence:

**(a) Change-log headers — the single most damaging item (guide §6.1, forbidden).** Every manuscript opens with an italic block of the form:

> *"Second edition (batch-5 corrections, 2026-08-29): the declared status of the geological half-saturation… The adjudication record is `BATCH5_JOINT_AUDIT_EVALUATION.md`; no theorem, number, claim status, or row count changes."*

Paper 1 v3 carries the same in third-edition form. This is a literal change log in the byline of a journal-facing edition. It must be removed from all nine editions at the venue pass (retained only in the internal editions, where it belongs). The same block's last sentence ("no theorem… changes") is the only part with substance — fold that into the provenance section as an auditability statement if desired.

**(b) Programme-identity byline (guide §6.4).** "Paper 3 of the programme's five-paper publication architecture (general-sustainability, A001–A025)" appears as the second line of every core paper. In a journal manuscript this is project-management language. Replacement: nothing, or one neutral sentence in the cover letter. The papers' ownership declarations (§1.2 "What enters this paper", §1.5 "Relationship to the programme") must become (i) a plain statement of contribution and (ii) an SI-able provenance note — not concordance-routing narration.

**(c) Internal identifiers in the argument (guide §6.4/§6.7).** Confirmed instances that reach the reader's line of sight: `CC-A002-001` type tags on every theorem; `[A001 §13.6]` provenance pointers; "family F06/F03/F13"; "Minimal Working Realization"; "Ω_2016/Ω_xte" (E1/E2, including E2's **title**); "R04-admitted fisheries object"; "negative certificate" (fine with a first-use gloss — the standing adjudication's disposition is correct); "the atlas's to state and prove [Paper 2, family F06]" (cross-reference to an unpublished sibling); "committed deterministic artifact (`research_program/paper1_instantiation/…`)" (repository path in the methods text). Each has a standing translation already adjudicated; the venue pass executes it. The rule from the guide that governs all of them: **the paper must stand alone for a reader who has never seen the repository** — every identifier either gets an in-paper definition, a descriptive gloss, or a published anchor (the V13 fork: genericize until siblings are published/citable, then resolve).

**(d) Proof handling in Paper 2 violates SVAA's standing rule (guide §5).** The atlas states "proofs are included in full where short and summarized with verified-present status otherwise (the camera-ready version reproduces every proof verbatim from the audited sources)." SVAA's Editor-in-Chief: proofs in the main text, in the natural flow; the submitted manuscript is what referees see. "Verified-present summary" is not a proof in the submitted manuscript. The venue pass must either (i) print every retained proof in full (possible only after the 2A/2B split) or (ii) replace by citation where the proof repeats standard literature (guide §7.3 retention-by-reference), and cut the theorem count to what the paper's own contribution needs. This is the single largest piece of work in the whole venue-pass programme, and it is unavoidable for SVAA.

**(e) Status-ledger apparatus (§14 ledger, Appendix A inventories).** The two-table inventory in Paper 1 v3 Appendix A is a defensible appendix (auditability is one of the paper's actual contributions — both external audits conceded this). But the §14 status ledger of Paper 2, with columns `# | P | Statement | Manuscript | Claim status | Evidence | Primary destination`, is concordance bookkeeping — SI material at best, and only after translation ("primary destination" is a routing column; a journal reader has no use for it).

**(f) Word "typed" frequency.** The framework's own vocabulary ("typed", "moiety", "donor-limited", "noncompensatory") is legitimate — the standing adjudication rejecting the "non-compensatory viability framework" rename is right; the terms are defined at first use and do real work. No change needed beyond first-use glosses for audiences that know "donor-limited" only from chemostat literature (EM reviewers will).

### The one strategic question worth reopening

**Paper 1's theorem (§4) is elementary.** Its value is the formal separation of assessment doctrines with a closed-form witness — an EE contribution, not an SVAA one. This is correctly routed. But the surrounding apparatus (§2 typed framework, §3 operators, §6 constructors, §7 intergenerational) is more than the theorem needs, and at 9,678 words it is 1,678 over EE's cap. Two options, both honest:
- **(A)** Trim the apparatus to what Theorems A/B/C consume, move the rest to SI; single EE paper. Fits the standing plan.
- **(B)** Split: the doctrine-separation theorem + reading → EE; the typed architecture itself (types, statuses, model maps, negative-certificate methodology) → **Sustainability Science Original Article** (≤8000 words). SS's scope ("methodological advances that integrate inquiry across disciplines") is precisely the architecture's contribution, and the transdisciplinary readership is the one that can use it.
The standing architecture says EE; option (B) is registered as the fallback if EE reviewers judge the apparatus as apparatus.

## 5. What needs no work (do not touch)

- All theorem statuses, all numeric results, all scored verdicts, all negative certificates (they are the scientific content and they are honest).
- The internal editions (`manuscript_v2.md`, etc.) — they are the auditable record and correctly contain the scaffolding; the venue pass produces new journal-facing files, it does not overwrite them.
- The concordance and verification infrastructure — it is exactly what journals' reproducibility requirements are asking for, once renamed "supplementary material" instead of "the programme's".
