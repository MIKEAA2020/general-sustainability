### 4. Stock vs. Flow Pollutants in the "What If" Counterfactual
When exploring your counterfactual—*"what if humans consumed a bit less, or populated a bit more slowly?"*—you implicitly uncovered the vital distinction between **Flow Pollutants** and **Stock Pollutants**.

**Why this survives:** This distinction dictates exactly *how* a slower population/consumption trajectory would have saved us:
*   **Flow Pollutants (e.g., smog, surface water pollution, heat waves):** These dissipate quickly. A slower rate of consumption immediately reduces the peak concentration, preventing acute damage.
*   **Stock Pollutants (e.g., CO2, microplastics, biodiversity loss):** These accumulate. Slower growth only helps if it keeps the *cumulative total* below an irreversible tipping point. 
*   **The Insight:** If we had populated slower, we might have kept cumulative CO2 below the permafrost-melt threshold, or kept habitat fragmentation below the biodiversity-collapse threshold. The "what if" isn't just about having less stuff; it's about **buying time** to stay below the irreversible tripwires of stock pollutants.
