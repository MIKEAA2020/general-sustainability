## 2. Minimum corrected mathematical core for a *candidate* future draft

These are **review specifications**, not drop-in validated theorems. Each depends on a declared policy class, solution convention, disturbance information and initial-belief type.

1. **Adverse-path premise (J1/J2).** For each candidate held `a∈U^B(B)` for which `R_V^B(B)=∅`, require **some** `x_a∈B∩∂V`, active `j_a`, `τ_a>0`, and an original-system Carathéodory pair `(x_a(·),d_a(·))` with `x_a(0)=x_a`, `d_a(t)∈D(x_a(t))` a.e., and `q_{j_a}(x_a(t))≤−c_a t<0` for `0<t<τ_a`. Require this pair to be compatible with the *same pre-review observation record* and the correct disturbance strategy. Then any chosen `a` fails within every fixed `Δ>0`; no **uniform** `τ_a` is needed. Explicit H5.2 should analogously quantify **for every admissible control/history and every relevant initial state** an adverse original trajectory through a later time `T'>q(x₀)/ε`, with `q̇≤−ε` while it remains in `0≤q≤a`; only then can contradiction force strict exit before `T'`. Do not assume an adverse path after it exits merely to integrate it.
2. **Held/finite operator (J2/J5/§8).** For a fixed review duration and information model define `SafeΔ(B,a)` to mean all compatible **original** paths exist and stay safe **and action-admissible** during that review. Let `G(B,a)` be the exact set of *nonempty* posterior beliefs at review. With nonanticipative pasting/rectangularity, `Pre(C)={B⊆V:∃a SafeΔ(B,a) and G(B,a)⊆C}` and `W₀={B⊆V}`, `W_{n+1}=Pre(W_n)` give exact **finite-horizon** held-policy viability by induction. This alone does **not** show an arbitrary infinite-horizon nonviable belief has a finite-depth certificate or that Section 3's selected mechanisms are complete.
3. **One rigorous finite-depth sufficient route (optional abstract lemma).** Suppose the exact belief recursion above and a **finite action set** at each belief. If `B∈∩_n W_n`, choose an action `a_n` witnessing `B∈W_{n+1}`. Some same action occurs for arbitrarily large `n`; hence it has *every* successor in `W_m` for each fixed `m`. Thus `C=∩_n W_n` satisfies `C⊆Pre(C)`, and the largest viable fixed point equals `C`. In that **finite-action held class**, nonviability implies membership fails at some finite `W_n`. More generally, nested **nonempty closed witness-action sets** `C_m(B)={a:SafeΔ(B,a), G(B,a)⊆W_m}` in a compact action space suffice by the finite-intersection property. These facts concern a typed recursion, **not an effective finite-dimensional test** for each continuous belief or completeness of the manuscript's particular drift/LP/fibre certificates. Closedness of all `W_n` and robustness of a grid require **separate joint semicontinuity/compactness** hypotheses; no grid proof follows from compact actions alone.
4. **Transfinite fallback (optional abstract lemma, not a promoted manuscript duality theorem).** On the full set of beliefs `W₀`, iterate monotone `Pre` through successor ordinals and intersect at limits. Set-theoretic stabilization yields the greatest fixed point if `Pre` is the exact game operator. Exclusion first at a limit cannot happen; a belief initially outside `W₀` is **rank 0**, while later exclusions are successor-rank. This is an order-theoretic characterization; a well-founded rank argument may be infinitely branching/transfinite and is **not** a finite, checkable obstruction certificate or closure of the paper's Open Problem 1 as framed. The supplied abstract `s→x>0→2x→⋆` example (all finite horizons survivable by choosing ever smaller `x`, none forever) shows why closed witness sets matter; it is **not** a continuous-flow counterexample.
5. **Recourse, now actually operational.** Fix an admissible *exogenous* scenario shared by the comparison with **every** policy and the requisite blind record. Write its weighted facet objective with the correct signed gradient kernels and branch-specific transforms. If a directly evaluated admissible scenario gives `J_λ(d_*)<0`, then `Γ_h(λ)≤J_λ(d_*)<0`: that is a **computable failure witness** even when the global scenario infimum is unsolved. A negative *lower* bound does not suffice (the log's toy `inf{2,3}=2` has lower bound `−1`). Claims of exact minimization, an attainable original scenario, a finite extreme-point search, or an Euler-certified sign need a **separate, matched** model-specific proof.
