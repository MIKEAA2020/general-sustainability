## 1. Paper01's minimal construction does not follow from the displayed assumptions — **substantive; staged and live**

Staged `paper 2 family/01_obstruction/paper01_obstruction_calculus_v63.tex:1140–1171` and live `papers/paper01_obstruction_calculus_v63.tex:1139–1170` announce an **existential** example, which can be valid. But the construction merely specifies `r` to be *continuous and injective*, then concludes that under the sole common action `u=0`, `−r(S)<0` and every branch exits `[1,2]` in finite time. Those inequalities do not follow. An explicit admissible counterexample **to the proof as written, not to the existential proposition**, is `r(S)=S−1`: it is continuous, injective, and nonnegative on `[1,2]`, yet the common action `u=0` has `S(t)=1+(S(0)−1)e^(−t)`, which stays in `[1,2]` for every initial state indefinitely. In the accompanying supplement, staged `…v63_supplementary.tex:728–733` / live `papers/paper01_obstruction_calculus_v63_supplementary.tex:728–733` additionally asserts that constant `u=r(m)` drives every state toward `m` under the same hypotheses. That needs **increasing** `r`: for the positive, continuous, injective *decreasing* choice `r(S)=3−S`, the proposed law gives `Ṡ=S−m`, which repels states from `m`.

**Narrow repair:** specify one concrete increasing `r` bounded strictly above zero, for example `r(S)=S`, and make the supplement's comparison control set explicit, e.g. `[0,2]`. Then `u=0` exits through 1 and the counterfactual constant `u=m` draws all `[1,2]` trajectories into `m`. Check the precise control/admissibility and endpoint conventions when updating both texts. Do **not** report the existential theorem as disproved.
