## 1. The two supposed numerical contradictions — joint resolution

**Timing, Astra 1 and Grok 1–2: R.** The source names **two different damage models on the same 31×3 = 93-cell domain**, not one predicate with two answers. At 11c lines 691–735, free adverse switching/one-unit decline has `D_T=T`, and endpoint viability `D_T≤z₀−1` has counts `21+11+1=33` at `T=1,2,3`. At lines 741–769, the *different* two-regime model with damage 1 and 1/2 **forced to alternate** has `D₁=1, D₂=3/2, D₃=5/2` and counts `21+16+6=43`. Both counts were independently recomputed (`check.log`) and occur as expressly separated instances in the source. Therefore *do not* “correct” 33 to 43, exclude points from one grid, or add the proposed `G_all/G_admissible` distinction: the difference is the **damage-path constraint**, not grid membership. A one-sentence caption cross-reference to the alternate regime could help a hurried reader (E), but a unified emitted constant for the two models would be wrong.

The inclusive integer-deadline tests `T≤τ*=z₀−1` and `T≤σ*=⌊z₀−1⌋` are **identical**; the *strict* predicates differ. On the 33 free-switching viable cells `T<τ*` incorrectly rejects only the **three exact-boundary cells** `(2,1),(3,2),(4,3)`, while `T<σ*` rejects **21 cells**, one per initial-state row from 2.0 to 4.0. Lines 223–231, 697–712 and Figure-caption lines 835–838 already distinguish real exit time from the floored count and the strict from the inclusive predicate. Astra's “floored rule fails” and the truncated Grok point 2 conflate **strict** floored failure with the valid **inclusive** floored identity. An explicit four-predicate mini-table would be E, not a mathematical repair.

**Register semantics, Astra 2: R.** Lines 200–213 and Proposition at 525–555 declare the audited convention **pessimistic shared register, universal over admissible threads**, with kernel sizes `(I,A,C,F)=(24,26,25,28)`. The source then deliberately computes **different models**: a fixed single-thread convention `(25,26,26,28)`; an optimistic convention with codex 27; and **per-branch registers** `(26,26,27,28)`—in the latter *institutional becomes 26*, matching aggregate. These are not inconsistent measurements of one institution. The proposed semantics-indexed count table would be a useful E, provided it preserves all four conventions and does not silently relabel the audited 24 as 26. Earlier source-verifier P9/P10 and the source text distinguish the variants. They do not establish a general real-world institution semantics.

## 4. Newly confirmed issue: master-theorem axis labels are swapped

At **11c v4 lines 310–312**, directly after stating the 2×2 grid, the text reads:

> `24 ≤ 26` and `25 ≤ 28` **(observation axis, free protocol)**; `24 ≤ 25` and `26 ≤ 28` **(protocol axis at fixed aggregation)**.

This is *opposite* to its own definitions at lines 349–367 and 448–461: `I` = aggregated observation + no-repeat/shared register (24); `A` = aggregated + free (26); `C` = full observation + no-repeat/shared register (25); `F` = full observation + free (28). Thus **protocol enlargement at fixed observation** is `I24→A26`, `C25→F28`; **observation refinement at fixed protocol** is `I24→C25`, `A26→F28`. The numbers/set containments and six tabular bodies are not refuted—**their axis attribution in that sentence is wrong**. This is a confirmed source-level exposition error, not a proof that monotonicity itself fails. It was missed by the previous report and by both attached reviewer fragments. If a future edit is authorized, correct that sentence in a **new version**, align the strict-difference discussion at lines 338–346 with the two distinct axes, and explicitly tag the memory/per-branch comparison as a third, differently defined model. Do not mutate the frozen v4 source.
