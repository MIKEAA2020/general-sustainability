## 0. What checks out

- **Drift table.** The drift table and the −8 sum over 16 cells are correct.
- **Lemma 1 and Corollary 2.** The proofs of Lemma 1 (i)–(ii) and Corollary 2 are correct.
- **Lemma 3 and Proposition 4.** Lemma 3 is correct, and the 5,219 = 17 + 17² + 17³ count is right. The band arithmetic in Proposition 4 is right, including the dip at z₀ = 1.1.
- **Counts.** 120, 496 and 560 are the correct binomials. 16·2^16, 16 + 15·32, 3·32 + 13·112 = 1,552, 2^32/97 ≈ 4.43×10⁷, 33⁴ = 1,185,921 and the 14.2× and 20.4× ratios all check.
- **Five-cube classification.**
  - Maximal cliques at distance ≤ 2: I confirmed 32 balls plus 80 squares plus 80 tetrahedra, and 912 = 32 + 240 + 640 cliques of size ≤ 3.
  - Aggregate drift: the tetrahedral aggregate bound −20 + 2·8 = −4 is right.
  - Squares: the square-face survival threshold L ≥ 3 (z₀ ≥ 1.3) is right.
- **Ladders.** The ten ladder values are right.

The substance is largely sound. The problems are in framing, internal consistency, a few proof gaps, and the headline compression metric.

## 1. Substantive errors and contradictions

**1.1 The compression baseline is the power set, which makes the "headline" largely an artifact (Abstract; Prop. 9; §0.9.3; §0.11.2).**
- The stored object is compared with 2^16 (or 2^32) subsets. No algorithm, including the paper's own clique route at m = 5, enumerates that.
- The honest baseline is the downward-closed survivable family.
  - At m = 4, above the edge, it has 1 + 16 + 32 = 49 members, against 32 stored maximal sets, a compression of about 1.5×.
  - At m = 5, z₀ ≥ 1.3, it has at most 32·2⁶ + 80·2⁴ = 3,328 members (with multiplicity, empty set included), against 112 stored.
- The "four orders of magnitude better at five than at four" is just 2^16 ÷ 3.13. It reflects 2^(2^m) growth of the power set, not any property of the classification.
- Report the ratios antichain : downset : clique-candidate set : power set, and drop or heavily qualify "improving compression by four orders of magnitude".

**1.2 "Conservative" is backwards (Prop. 9 proof).** Using 16·2^16 instead of 16·(2^16 − 1) gives a larger raw number, hence a larger compression ratio. That is the less conservative figure.

**1.3 "Four orders of magnitude" at m = 4 is wrong (Intro; Conclusion; Abstract).**
- 2^16/32 = 2,048 and 2^16/16 = 4,096, i.e. about 10^3.3–10^3.6. The total ratio is 1,048,576/496 ≈ 2,114, about 10^3.3.
- The statement is only true for the five-cube comparison.

**1.4 "Raw subset evaluations" were not performed (Abstract; §0.10.2; §0.11).**
- Prop. 9's proof obtains the counts by formula (16·2^16; 16 + 15·32).
- At m = 5 the classification is by clique enumeration plus hand proofs, never by evaluating 2^32 subsets.
- §0.11 nevertheless calls 68,719,476,736 → 1,552 "measured costs of exhaustive exact classification".
- State which counts are enumerated and which are computed.

**1.5 The "safety game" does not exist in the paper (§0.9 opening; Prop. 10; Remark 12).**
- §0.9 says every claim is "settled by the safety game of Section 0.14 — a greatest-fixed-point computation on the capped nonnegative orthant". §0.14 is "Verification methods" and contains no such game.
- Prop. 11's proof contains no game; it uses explicit arguments. "Capped orthant" is never defined, and the proof says "no artificial state cap".
- The text is also contradicted elsewhere.
  - §0.11 says "there is no fixed point here" and contrasts this paper with fixed-point algorithms.
  - It also says the stored sets are "not a winning set", but maximal survivable sets are exactly a safety game's winning region.
- Either define and present the game, or delete these claims.

**1.6 "Not by an averaging bound" is contradicted by the proof (§0.9; Remark 12; §0.14).**
- The tetrahedral exclusion is an aggregate (averaging) argument.
- It is in fact Remark 6's crude instrument: Σᵢ|Σθᵢ| = 0 + 0 + 0 + 4 + 4 = 8 < 10 = (5/2)·4. The "crude" instrument excludes the tetrahedron at m = 5.
- So the instruments are incomparable, not "crude vs sharper". At m = 4 the pairwise bound beats the average; at m = 5 the average beats the pairwise bound on this orbit. Remark 12's closing sentence ("the sharper instrument inherits the failure one dimension later") is therefore wrong as stated.
- The 0.14 bullet that lists the five-cube orbit verdicts under "Simulated" is mislabeled.

**1.7 Remark 12: "everything it permits is survivable" is false at m = 4.**
- At z₀ = 1.0 the pairwise bound permits all 32 adjacent pairs, none of which is survivable (Prop. 4).
- Likewise at m = 5 the squares pass the pairwise test at z₀ < 1.3 and fail.
- The pairwise bound is level-blind, so it is at best sharp for "survivable from some level". Say that. The abstract's "sharp at four parameters" needs the same qualifier.

**1.8 Prop. 10 (deadline law).**
- *Necessity is only shown for the hold schedule.* "Under the hold every cell drifts −1/2" proves failure of that schedule, not that no pre-revelation blind sequence works.
  - The missing step is one line: the cube-accounting identity (Σ drifts = −8) implies mean stock z₀ − t/2 under any action, so the minimum cell is ≤ z₀ − T/2.
  - Use it. Also define "viability" as an existential over all blind pre-revelation sequences.
  - The Lean `deadline_law` is, per §0.13, also schedule-specific, so the "iff" over policies is neither proved nor mechanized.
- *T = 4 is off the grid.* z₀ ≥ 3 is outside {1.0, …, 2.5}, so "verified by simulation for T = 0…4" is vacuous for T = 4 (nowhere viable). T = 3 holds only at z₀ = 2.5. Say so, or extend the grid.
- The cross-reference "(Section 0.14)" for the mechanized proof should be 0.13.

**1.9 Abstract misstates the lemma.** "Every pair of distinct actions sums, over any two parameter cells…" is wrong. Lemma 1 (i) bounds one action's inner products against two cells: ⟨u,θ⟩ + ⟨u,θ′⟩ ≤ 2(m − h).

## 2. Proof and definition gaps

**2.1 Horizon conflation.**
- §0.10.1 defines Vₖ as the *k-step* safe probability. The census, Prop. 7 and Prop. 4 use *indefinite* survival, via lim-inf averages and 60-step periodic certificates.
- The 4-step α-sets at z₀ = 2.5 (545 outcome vectors) are finite-horizon survivor sets, a different family from the 32 indefinite maximal sets. The paper never relates them.
- §0.13 says the Lean theorems carry "finite-horizon conditions". Indefinite-survival claims need a horizon T large relative to z₀ (otherwise three or more cells can survive for a while). State the exact hypothesis.

**2.2 Prop. 7 formula at j = 0.** "2/2^j above the edge" gives 2 at j = 0; it must be min(1, …) or a separate case. The last rung (1 → 1) is not a doubling, though the abstract says "each additional probe doubles". Also "maximal conditional kernel mass" is never defined, and the proof leans on an unstated "companion antichain formula", so the paper is not self-contained. State the formula (value at belief b = max over blind sequences of the mass of surviving cells) in one line.

**2.3 Observation model unspecified.**
- Is z observed? §0.15 says a z-feedback policy "could branch its actions across branches (the branches' stock paths diverge)". That is an information leak: z increments reveal ⟨u,θ⟩, hence θ.
- "The blind controller sees no observation of θ" (§0.2) sits uneasily with probes (§0.4) and with feedback.
- The "open question" (does a non-blind discipline beat the pairs?) is a finite game over (belief-subset, z) on 16 cells with 17 actions. It is tractable with the paper's own machinery. Compute it instead of leaving it open, or at least delimit the information structure precisely.

**2.4 Prop. 8 is close to tautological.** The proof says both sides are the same max over the same set. The 84 "pairings" test code correctness, not a theorem. Recast it as a test with:
- the seed and distribution of the "random rational beliefs" (unspecified);
- the three levels (unspecified);
- adversarial beliefs such as uniform on edges, singletons and ties.

**2.5 "857 on the tested levels" is ambiguous.** Prop. 8 says three tested levels; §0.9.4 says maximum across all 16 levels (at z₀ = 2.2). Give a 16-row table (levels × {m = 4, m = 5}) instead of extremal values.

**2.6 "Independent" certification is weaker than stated.** The periodic search to period 3 can never find a larger set that needs period ≥ 4, so it cannot independently support "nothing larger". It also needs a one-line justification for why 60 steps suffices. A cycle with net ≥ 0 attains its minimum in the first cycle, and a negative net is at most −0.1 per cycle, which exceeds the 1.5 slack within 20 cycles.

**2.7 Table 1 and structure.**
- §0.7 is an empty heading, because Table 1 sits in §0.6.
- "Larger sets: three-set and four-set candidates fail" should say all larger sets.
- The ladder row lists no values.
- 496 appears both as the number of pairs at m = 5 and as the stored count at m = 4. This is a coincidence worth a footnote to avoid confusion.

**2.8 Model notation.**
- ż = d(u,θ) suggests continuous time, but dynamics are discrete unit steps.
- The "grid" z₀ is only the set of *initial* stocks; z itself is uncapped.
- §0.10.1's "disturbance's worst case" does not match this model, which has a prior over θ and deterministic drifts.

## 6. Suggested improvements, in priority order

1. **Replace the headline metric.** Report antichain / downset / clique-candidates / power set, per level and for m = 4, 5. Retract "four orders of magnitude" for m = 4 and the 10⁴ improvement claim, or relabel it as power-set growth.
2. **Replace the nonexistent safety game with an LP criterion.**
   - A set S is survivable from *some* stock iff there is a rational mixed action x with every cell's average drift ≥ 0. Necessity is the Cesàro argument.
   - Sufficiency: realise x by a periodic sequence, whose first-cycle minimum is the global minimum.
   - The tetrahedron fails by LP duality, which is exactly your aggregate argument. The squares pass.
   - The m = 6 gap (size-7 balls vs size-12 cliques) then becomes a 65-variable exact LP per clique rather than an unresolved "12-dimensional safety game".
   - Level thresholds can then be found by minimizing the cycle dip.
3. **Use Kleitman's diameter theorem.** Sets pairwise at distance ≤ 2r have size ≤ the radius-r ball (for even diameter), and ≤ 2× a ball in Q_{m−1} for odd diameter. This gives the clique sizes (m = 4: 2; m = 5: 6; m = 6: 12) in one citation and explains the "ball" structure. It is the appropriate classical link, not Harper.
4. **Fix the logical statuses.**
   - Lemma 1's abstract wording.
   - The Remark 12 and Remark 6 relationship (incomparable instruments).
   - Prop. 10's necessity argument (via the −8 identity), T = 4 off-grid, and cross-references (0.13/0.14).
   - The contradictions with §0.11 on fixed points and winning regions.
5. **Specify the information model** (is z observed? when do probes occur?) and settle the feedback question computationally. It is a small finite game on this instance.
6. **Make "exactness" earn the title.** Show float failures at the knife-edge thresholds, and give runtimes and memory for exact vs. float and for 2^m enumeration vs. cliques, at m = 4, 5, 6.
7. **Tidy.** Resolve the empty §0.7, strip revision history, cut §0.10.2 to what is used, align script/paper versions, and fix the reference problems above. Replace the 857/15,929 extremal numbers with per-level tables. Make Prop. 8 a stated test with seeds, or prove the alpha-set statement.
