## A. Claims the evidence doesn't carry

**1. "Expansive for every admissible K" is a tautology, and false as worded.**
- F′(K*) = 1 + r(1 − 2K*/K) exceeds 1 exactly when K > 2K*. That is the same condition as K* < K/2, i.e. the LRP lies on the ascending limb. Props 2.1–2.3 already assume it, so the "expansion obstruction" restates "LRP below B_MSY".
- The front-matter abstract drops the K ≥ 2K* qualifier. Table 4 lists in-box K of 1000, 1200 and 1500 with F′ < 1. The profile 95% set starts at 1500 kt, and 27% of joint-bootstrap replicates have K < 2K*.
- "The data select that regime" (Result 3.7, Conclusion 3) is therefore unsupported. The data permit both regimes.

**2. The "shelf life" reading of the horizon has no object.**
- The 6–7 years is computed from a start at the 10⁴-kt ceiling. At those horizons the certified kernels begin at 4,560–5,133 kt.
- Even at T = 1, the certified lower boundary under Def. 2.7 is about 1,100 kt (q10, BAU; my arithmetic). The highest stock in the fit window is 940.75 kt. For every state the stock has occupied, the certified horizon is zero.
- So there is no certificate that "expires" at six years. The IWC/NOAA cadence argument in the Discussion and Conclusion 5 ("consulting an expired certificate") have nothing to attach to.
- Report T*(S₀) for observed S₀ and for S₀ = LRP.
- The Discussion also says the 10⁴ cap is "never approached on any reported kernel path", yet the horizon is computed from a path that starts on it.

**3. The horizon is partly set by the method, contrary to the headline.**
- **Conversion looseness.** r_T applies a_max = F′(K*) = 1.153 at every step. The ceiling trajectory sits above K/2 = 2500 throughout, where F′ ≈ 0.29–0.8. Since F is increasing on the domain (your own Prop 2.4 proof) and the defect is additive, the exact worst case is the nominal recursion with floor |e| + ε. Iterating that by hand from the same ceiling for BAU, the path stays above K* for roughly 12 years (perpetual-worst + ε) and roughly 27 years (q10 + ε), against your 6 and 7.
- **Sensitivity to ε.** r_T ∝ ε, so T* shifts by about one year per 15% change in ε. At ε = 47.1 kt (the audit's own maximum), Prop 2.4's lower bound already gives ≥ 18 years (q10) and ≥ 13 years (q05). This contradicts "neither better data about the disturbance nor a more cautious catch extends it" (Discussion), and §3.4's "the defect magnitude is not the binding obstruction" is untested.
- **Double counting.** ε = 329.0 is the 1992 residual that also defines the perpetual-worst floor, and Def. 2.3 says so. The certified layer therefore stacks the same shock twice, and the consequence isn't discussed.
- **Needed:** an ablation over ceiling, ε and conversion form showing what survives of "6/6/7".

**4. "Same shape of limitation" across systems is overstated.**
- The mechanisms differ, and §3.4 itself calls them "qualitatively different". In cod, a > 1 and the margin grows without bound. In Edwards, a = 0.746 and r_∞ = 60.7 ft.
- The Edwards "3 years" is the time for the head to fall from the 710-ft ceiling to 618 ft under perpetual 1956 recharge plus a perpetual 15.4-ft defect. Because the map is affine, your certified boundary equals the exact kernel: attractor 615.72 − 60.70 = 555.0 ft, and ln(155/63)/ln(1/a) ≈ 3.1 years.
- That figure is as ceiling-relative as the 12.7-year nominal horizon you caveat in §3.2. From the observed 2023 head (635.7 ft) it is under 1 year, and under the audit defect (21.81 ft) it is about 2 years (my arithmetic).
- Both analyses share one author, one frozen protocol and the same Def. 2.7 conversion, so "independent pipelines" and "differ by a factor of two" are not independent evidence. Any persistent-adversary recursion on a bounded domain has a finite horizon.

**5. The no-dominance verdict: the abstract misstates the mechanism, and the real one is fragile.**
- **Identity argument.** The abstract says the C* identity makes the verdict "structural: a reactive rule cannot out-supply a flat cap it matches in protection". Result 3.1 says the binding failure is H1 at q05/T = ∞, with H3 secondary.
  - Prop 2.1 constrains a rule at one state, K*. Supply integrates over states.
  - Counterexample: C(S) = 91.59 for S < 1,769 and 150 above. It has the same margin at K* as flat-91.59, keeps the safe set invariant under q10 (F(1769) ≈ 1,809), and out-supplies the flat cap whenever S > 1,769.
  - The claim holds only for the training-replay supply metric. There the stock is below the LRP in 20 of 24 years, so any rule that switches off below the LRP is capped near 17% of its above-LRP catch (S1: 4/24 × 60 = 10 kt). That is a metric artifact, which you partly concede for S1. Score supply on a closed-loop stationary or post-recovery distribution.
- **H1 block margin.** BAU's q05 T = ∞ kernel exists only because g_max − C − |e_q05| = 296.09 − 5 − 287.36 ≈ 3.7 kt.
  - r below 0.2339 (1.3% lower) removes it. Interpolating Table 4, a K of about 4,900 kt or lower removes it too, and the bootstrap median r is 0.219.
  - Near the apex the boundary moves about 37 kt per kt of floor, so 2,219.6 has no stable digits.
  - §4's "identified findings survive K within the box" is contradicted by Table 4's q05 column.
- **Percentile estimator.** The two lowest residuals are −329.0 and −323.5. With NumPy-style linear interpolation on n = 24, q05 = −287.4 requires the third-lowest residual to be about −83 kt, a roughly 240-kt gap.
  - Nearest-rank, Weibull and Hazen estimators all give −323 to −328. That exceeds g_max = 296, so the class becomes vacuous again, undoing "vacuous family reduces from two classes to one".
  - Cod §2.2 never names the estimator (Edwards does). Report results across estimators.
- **Zero catch.** The Result 3.1 reasoning covers positive-catch rules only. Flat-0 is more protective than BAU at every reading and is excluded by H3 alone, so say so.

**6. "Frozen before any score" conflicts with the convention switch.**
- The headline constants all come from the post-freeze δ = 0 convention: 91.59 vs 57.61 kt, horizons 6/7 vs 5/6, vacuous classes 2 → 1, and the 60-kt boundary 884.6 vs 900.3 kt.
- The choice rests on in-sample MSE using parameters fitted under that same convention, which is circular. It also rests on 24 points where 1991–93 likely dominate.
- Conclusion 7 ("convention-dependent only in the sense…") understates a change that moves four headline quantities.
- Present δ = 0 and δ = 1 as co-equal rows, as you do for model forms. Report MSE excluding 1991–93. Add a short deviation log (frozen / changed / why / effect).

**7. "Certification, not simulation" is undercut by the paper's own results.**
- The decision-relevant outputs are simulations: the cod survival of 0.74 at C* in §3.8, the bootstraps, and the Edwards replays. The certified layers certify nothing at observed states.
- Related work concedes "complements rather than rivals", while the abstract says "a different kind of object… not stylistic".
- A certificate is universal over rules but conditional on the fitted map, floor class and declared defect. In Edwards the defect is falsified out of sample (21.81 > 15.41 ft), so there it is not a certificate.
- "Certification and simulation" fits the evidence better than "not simulation".

## B. Internal contradictions and numerical errors

1. **Table 1, Family A φ = 0.60, q10 T = ∞ (1074.8).** Prop 2.3's closed form (smaller root of 0.4·g = 80.87) gives about 1,092 kt. The table value is also below flat-120's 1082.3, although φ = 0.60 implies a larger required surplus (202.2 vs 200.9 kt). T = 1 (895.2) reproduces, so check this cell.
2. **Result 3.4, φ = 0.75 "nonemptiness margin −37.8 kt".** The stated formula (1 − φ)g_max − |e| gives −6.9 kt. The −37.8 is the safe-set margin (1 − φ)g(K*) − |e|.
3. **§3.7 "C* pinned to within 15%" (also the Fig. 9 caption).** The 27.3-kt range is 15% of g(K*) but about 29% of C* (27.3/95.2).
4. **Table 4 caption vs content.** The caption says each row uses the refit's own q10 floor. The constructive-bound column equals g(K*) − 80.87 in every row (e.g. K = 1500: 148.8 − 80.9 = 67.9), i.e. the registered floor. Also check r(K), which is non-monotone (0.5094, 0.5248, then 0.4099 at K = 1000, 1200, 1500).
5. **§3.10 bootstrap interval [0.0, 121.1].** 11.8% of replicates have a non-positive bound, so the 5th percentile must be negative. The 0.0 looks censored. The free-refit interval does report negatives.
6. **"Order 70–90 kt".** This is a heuristic summary of point estimates and medians, not an interval. Values like 7.4898 kt, 91.59 kt and 2219.6 kt are false precision given the intervals above.
7. **§3.11 vs §3.12 vs Discussion.**
   - §3.11 says the xteNCAM bound is positive (7.49 kt) and that zero catch and BAU hold the LRP.
   - §3.12 says the same fit "also failed to make its reference point self-viable".
   - The Discussion says the second specification is "reversing the informative certificate".
   - Conclusion 7 says self-viability holds.
   - The §3.12 and Discussion sentences look like leftovers from the destination-year version.
8. **Discussion, final paragraph.** "Moratorium-level removals and zero catch are the only policies that hold the safe set under the informative class" contradicts Table 1, Result 3.4, and the next clause ("largest robust constant catch of order 70–90 kt").
9. **Fig. 5 caption.** It calls C_vac = 215.2 kt "the constructive bound", but that is C* = 91.59. Table 2 reuses q10's C_vac for all three classes, although it is class-specific (about 8.7 kt for q05). Also, 120 and 150 kt exceed C* and don't shorten the horizon, so "shortens only outside the constructive bound" is wrong. Shortening starts at 180 kt.
10. **Pair counts.** §3.4 says "all 48 (rule, class) pairs… 30 constant catches and 18 declared rules". The Fig. 5 caption says "all 30 archived (catch, floor) pairs". 30 catches × 3 floors is 90.
11. **Prop 2.2 "[b∞, ∞)".** For logistic F the kernel is bounded above, since F(S) → 0 for large S. For BAU the upper edge is about 25,000 kt, not ∞ or 10⁴. Write [b∞, S̄].

## C. System I: further gaps

- **Extrapolation.** SSB is itself NCAM model output, and the LRP is the 1983–89 mean of that series. The fit sees stocks ≤ 940.75 kt with K pinned at 5000 kt. Every kernel boundary quoted (2,220–5,130 kt) lies 2.4–5.5× above any observation, and the certified paths run above K, where the logistic gives absurd declines (−2,369 kt/yr at 10⁴).
- **"The functionals the results depend on are identified."** This is true of g(K*) and C* only. g_max = rK/4, C_vac, vacuity of q05/worst, and T = ∞ boundaries all vary enormously over the profile set (g_max ≈ 154 to well over 1,000 kt along the grid).
- **Model-form comparison.**
  - The data-preferred Allee fit has MSE 7,690 against 12,772 (about 40% lower), yet it is relegated to a "sensitivity" row. It also pins r at 2.0, which is not biologically meaningful.
  - No AIC or cross-validation is reported.
  - Frozen Schaefer floors are applied to forms whose own residuals differ.
- **Stress replay (§3.5).** It holds the 1991–95 residuals fixed under counterfactual catch, so "productivity event, not a catch event" is a model assumption. Hutchings & Myers (1994), Myers & Cadigan (1995) and Myers et al. (1997) are in the reference list and never cited, though they bear directly on this claim.
- **Stochastic layer.** The 2/24 immediate-breach rate treats the two collapse residuals (−329.0 and −323.5) as independent, though they are likely consecutive transitions of one event.
- **§3.12.** It extrapolates fits from windows where the stock is far below the LRP to g(LRP). It then merges NCAM (LRP 884.6) and xteNCAM (LRP 276) windows into one "regime" statement in the abstract, after saying no verdict transfers between them.

## D. System II: further gaps

- **"Mildest floor class".** The abstract, Discussion and Conclusion (1) say retention is nominal "under the mildest floor class". Retention is scored under UC-min, the drought-of-record floor, which is the harshest. Under q05 and q10, BAU already holds 618 ft.
- **Contradiction in §3.7.** The text calls BAU's pass probability of 0.41 a "coin flip", then lists "the failure of training-mean pumping" among the "resolved parts". The Edwards Conclusion repeats this by listing "P = 0.41–0.65" as unresolvable.
- **The 618-ft "securing" claim.**
  - The 10% cut clears 618 by 0.88 ft, but §3.7 attributes it to a "2.3-ft gap" (that is BAU's shortfall).
  - The map has an 8.1-ft drought bias and a +31.8-ft closed-loop bias over 1934–1990.
- **The 660-ft verdict.** The zero-pumping attractor is only 2.1–3.1 ft below 660 under q10/q05. If the bootstrap SD is similar to Table 7's roughly 10 ft, P(attractor ≥ 660) is on the order of 40% for q10. Table 7 resolves only UC-min at 618 ft, so "protected by wet years" is unresolved for the milder floors.
- **Retention rule.**
  - Retention flips sign with the comparator grid. CPM is retained against the 10% grid, but −1.8% against the 1% grid and −2.6% against the interpolated 7.2% cut. S1 is +0.2% there.
  - S1's mean cut (20% × 35.1% = 7.0%) happens to equal the securing cut (7.2%).
  - The hybrid criterion credits protection where S1 ≡ flat-80% and supply where it behaves like BAU. Replace "retained" with a within-scenario or probabilistic criterion, e.g. resampled recharge, scoring viability probability against supply.
- **Baseline.** Everything is relative to 1934–90 pumping (282.16). At current use (382.16), S1 and flat-80% give 613.08 ft, below 618. This belongs in the abstract, not a post-freeze bullet.
- **§3.2 horizons.** "Flat-50% T ≈ 6" does not match the T_empty formula: 3.5 years under UC-min and 4.6 under UC-q10 (my arithmetic).
- **(F4) "exact".** This holds for constant policies. For S1 and CPM the closed loop has jump discontinuities (about 1.6 ft per 20% cut), so F is not monotone at the triggers and the Lipschitz argument isn't exact.
- **Missing thresholds.** The cascade's own stages (625–650 ft) are never scored. CPM's attractor of 628.36 ft holds 625 ft while S1 (622.04) and flat-90% (618.88) don't. That is where graded design should show or fail to show value, and a stage-occupancy metric fits the CPM's frequency-management purpose better than 660-ft invariance.
- **Wording.** "A 7.2% cut… about 31.5% of current use" conflates two baselines. The same absolute cap is a 7.2% cut from training-mean pumping and a 31.5% cut from current use.
