**1.1 The quantifier story is not what drives the gap, and Protocol 1 is not exempt.**
- By Theorem 5(6), for any single weight ratio r and any I-state, either r ≥ ρ1 (FAST licensed) or r ≤ ρ1 ≤ ρ2 (SLOW licensed). So every individual weight w accepts all of I. In fact V_(1,1) = {x≥1}∪{s1+s2≥2} = Vweak exactly, so the intersection over the cone is attained at one weight.

  - Table 1 and §1.2 say the separation is "Protocol 2 vs 4 … not an objection to composite indices as such (Protocol 1 … is ordinary fixed-weight assessment)". This is contradicted on the datum, because Protocol 1 at any declared w licenses a floor-violating plan on all of I.

**1.3 Theorem 6 is under-hypothesized, and the Abstract misstates it.**

**1.3 The "anchor decision" and the interior-window headline rest on a 0.2% window.**

- "Five orders inside the scan bracket (141/50, 353/125]" is wrong: t* ≈ 2.82257 lies 0.0026 above the lower end of a 0.004-wide bracket. What is meant is that the certified width, about 10⁻¹⁰, is far smaller than the bracket.
- "More certifiable" inverts the meaning. At the concentrated allocation the geometric index *falsely certifies* a typed-reject state, while the balanced rejection is correct. The ecological content is "concentration is more falsely certified", not "more certifiable".
- State the effect size and the unit dependence before drawing management conclusions.

**1.4 The related-work argument conflates blending with randomization (§6.2, §6.4, §4.10 remark).**
- *Blend is not a mixed strategy.* The blend is a deterministic convexified control with tube equal to the pointwise convex combination. A randomized plan drawn once has realizations at the vertices, so path-wise worst case gives it no convexification. This is the real reason Kuhn's theorem is irrelevant. The Kuhn paragraph argues from finite memory and worst-case criteria instead, which misses the point.
- *The minimax identification is inconsistent.* §1.2 says the payoff is binary ("a is admissible at z under w") and §4.10 says it is the worst-case aggregate. Sion/von Neumann need the margin payoff w·(s − d_a), which is linear in w and affine in the planner's mixture. With a binary payoff there is no minimax theorem. Theorem 6 is exactly that minimax statement for the margin payoff. Say so.
- *The "inversion" claim (§6.4 item 2) is a mislabel.*
  - Per-weight (adaptive) acceptance ⊇ common-plan acceptance, which is the usual adjustable ≥ static ordering, so nothing is inverted.
  - The comparison is between convexified allocation and time-alternation, not between adjustable and static. Adjustability in ARO means adapting to realized uncertainty, not temporal switching.
  - The paper's own caveat says chattering recovers the blend. "Alternating over time does not" therefore holds only for a stipulated union-of-tubes visited set, and §6.4 omits that qualifier.
- *The polyhedrality claim is wrong.* "Their polyhedrality hypothesis has no counterpart" fails: the weight set (the simplex) is polyhedral, the constraints are linear in w, and the decision set is finite. The right literature link is that static equals adjustable when the decision set is convex compact, and the gap appears for discrete decisions. That is the pure-vs-convexified fact, not a new inversion.
- Fix the references to results: "Theorem 9 shows alternating does not" should be Proposition 10, and "Theorem 4.2(ii)" does not exist.
- "We claim four things and nothing more" contradicts the 13-item contributions list in §1.3, and "(i) and (ii)" in item 3 does not match the 1–4 numbering.

**1.5 Proposition 10's proof asserts the visited set rather than deriving it (Appendix B).**
- "Any positive time on FAST visits every s₁ in [s₁−2, s₁]" contradicts the action table, where FAST's dip is a rate (4t on [0,½]). A fraction τ of the half-interval reaches depth about 4τ, not 2.
- The final clause ("typed-admissible on I only in the per-weight sense of Theorem 5(6)") is a non sequitur: typed admissibility has no per-weight sense.
- The result is true only for the stipulated plan-granular full-depth alternation. Parametrize by duty cycle d and switching period, and prove closure as d-fraction blending and period → 0.

**1.6 Proof-status mislabeling and a swapped argument in S1/S2.**
- *Theorem S1.*
  - "Finiteness: (s−1)^θ → ∞ as θ → −∞, so some finite θ rejects" proves *positivity* of σ* (some rung rejects), not finiteness.
  - "Positivity: s₁+s₂ ≥ 2 accepts at θ = 1" proves neither. Finiteness needs acceptance at some θ < 1.
  - On the diagonal this follows from strictness at θ = 1: 2s > 2 for s > 1, and continuity. Off the diagonal it is unargued.
- *Theorem S2(i)* cites "acceptance at θ = 1" for "safe under some positive elasticity", which needs a rejecting rung. The off-diagonal rejection at finite θ is unproved.
- *Theorem G and the φ-closure.*
  - "Exactly three zeros of D(s)" over a continuum where h diverges at the ends is not decided by finite enclosures. The limits paragraph concedes the end segments rest on "value anchors and elementary asymptotics".
  - These are computer-assisted results. Label them so, instead of "Theorem … every verdict is a finite rational proof".
- *Other claims.*
  - "θ = 1/2 exactly 5/2" and "θ = 2/3 exactly 2× root" assert that the diagonal minimizes the slice. For θ = 0 the minimizer leaves the diagonal, so there is a bifurcation between θ = 0 and 1/2. State where, or label the two claims as evidence.
  - "Nonincreasing in s₁, s₂" is stated informally; give the one-line monotone-plan argument.
  - Several statements ("accepting windows are intervals") rest on four-phase certificates the text never gives.

**1.7 The shared-shock extension exposes, but does not discuss, the arbitrariness of the base convention.**
- In the action-indexed class STAGED is never exposed to the heatwave and neither is the non-characteristic floor. §7.3 describes the heatwave as an excess mortality on the stock, "present or absent", which is the common-shock class.
- So the benchmark realizes the class that the theorems treat as a variant. Moreover Table 5's "(i) proven / (ii) computed" ordering understates that (i) is the least physically natural class.
- *Canonical datum.* Its total is 12/5 < 5/2, so by M3 the canonical datum (and the benchmark) has **no admissible blend** under the shared shock. §4.10 mentions only (1/2,1,1) and puts this in a closing caveat.
  - It contradicts the Highlights, the Abstract and Conclusions' headline that convexification closes the gap.
  - It also contradicts Limitation (v), which says the gap disappears by Theorem 9 if blends are available. Make (v) class-conditional and move M3 to the front of §4.10.
- *Naming in M4/§5.4.*
  - "The datum's full magnitude δ = 2" means an event equal to the whole action-indexed dip (3/2 + 1/2 = 2). It is four times the datum's own heatwave δ = 1/2.
  - "Universal rejection on I at full magnitude" and §5.4's "collapse into universal rejection" are therefore about a hypothetical, not the benchmark. At the datum's δ = 1/2, M4 itself shows deep-I states survive.
  - Rename: δ = 1/2 is the datum, δ = 2 is a stress scale.
- *CT is true by construction.* The replace reading gives both plans the same dip 2 on each floor, so the plans are indistinguishable and the menu has no choice to exploit. STAGED is exempted ("not a schedule in the cost sense"), which is ad hoc. State CT as a degeneracy remark, not as a finding.
- *M1.* "Licensing structure unshifted" holds for the upper endpoint 1/(1+ρ₁) only. The lower endpoint is now constrained by the 1/2-cut. Say so.

**1.8 The §7.3 benchmark is only half realized, and two anchoring statements are in tension.**
- *Income floor.* The Schaefer model is given for biomass only. s₂ and SLOW's income tent have no dynamics. Nothing explains how a stock heatwave becomes SLOW's depth-1/2 income dip. "Realizes the witness datum" overstates, and "certified tubes conservative for the nonlinear realization" is proved for FAST's s₁ only.
- *Parameter.* r = 4 per year is unrealistic for cod, and r clashes with r = w₂/w₁ (the notation list protects σ but not r).
- *LRP anchor.*
  - The text says the LRP is "by construction the 1983–1989 mean SSB". It then calls the opening stock (1.6 LRP ≈ 1.42 Mt) "the neighbourhood of the assessed mid-1980s spawning biomass", and computes a 1.1 Mt (1.3 LRP) decline from it.
  - If the LRP is the 1983–89 mean, the mid-1980s level is about 1 LRP and the decline to 2015 is about 0.66 LRP. One of the two statements is wrong, or "neighbourhood" hides a factor of 1.6. Verify against DFO Table A2.
- *"Adverse reporting architecture".* The text concedes that real LRP breaches trigger mandatory responses. The "action flip" in the relevance chains (closure vs no response; "the aggregator choice flips a management action") assumes the dashboard alone triggers. It is a modeling stipulation, not a finding.
- *Self-certification.* "The extension passes the relevance test" is self-assessment. Delete it, or have someone else apply the test.

## 2. Precision errors and inconsistencies

- **Cross-references.**
  - The benchmark is §7.3 but is cited as "Section 6.3" (Introduction, §5.8, Fig. 4, Limitation (vi)) and "Section 6.2" (error-bound remark).
  - §7.1's "resource threshold of Section 5.6" should be §5.7.
  - The §5.9 verifier is called "fourth" but appears after the fifth and sixth in reading order.
  - Theorem 8 uses "Stage 1/2" while Remark 7 uses "stage 0"/"stage j".
- **Harmonic circle.** The text says the circle passes through (1,2), (√2,2) and (2,1). (1,2) and (2,1) lie on it, but (√2,2) gives 18 − 4√2 ≠ 10. The diagonal point is (φ,φ). The "no accepts below total 3" claim is right, by convexity of a² + b² on the slice; the line s₁+s₂ = 3 is a chord of the circle, not a tangent.
- **θ = 2/3 critical floor.** The polynomial factors: with u = s², 27(u−1)² − (3−u)³ = u(u² + 18u − 27), so s*² = 6√3 − 9 ≈ 1.3923 and s* ≈ 1.17996. This is a quadratic irrational, not a "cubic root". "θ = 2/3 flip total exactly 2× the cubic master root" should then be 2√(6√3−9). The printed ladder "1 < 5/4 < √2 < …" omits this 1.18 rung.
- **Undefined objects.**
  - The "64-state grid" (8 × 8?), the "13 rungs", the "ten undecided cells" and "thirteen only-linear states" are never defined.
  - The 676-state and 5,000-state grids are unexplained.
  - The margin "≈ 0.218" is the unnormalized AD − BC. It is not invariant under rescaling the logs, so say what it measures.
- **Lemma B.** The collapse convention rejects a plan on a coordinate with λᵢ ≤ 0 even at weight zero on that coordinate. This only matters at the cone's boundary, because plans fail at nearby positive weights anyway. State that.
- **Theorem 8.** Stage 2 has no path constraint and an unconditional REPAIR, so every state is accepted. This is a repair-from-anywhere example, and the "terminal coincidence" framing is a stretch. The truncation gap is also very large: the cover holds iff s₁+s₂ ≥ 0, so every state is weak-accepted and the gap is the whole complement of Vtyp. Show a less degenerate example.
- **Remark 1 proof** is a tautology ("fails precisely when no common selector exists"). The sufficient conditions (FIP, compactness) are the wrong ones; Theorem 6 uses convexity.
- **Proposition 4** needs W ≠ ∅.
- **Conclusions.**
  - "The part of the convex hull … that no single plan dominates" repeats the earlier misstatement. The gap is conv(D) + ℝⁿ₊ minus the union of orthants.
  - "Per-floor reporting distinguishes rescue from impossibility" is wrong on the datum itself: R and I have identical (s₁,s₂), and the reserve x separates them.
  - "The gap measures the value of information about the weight" has no defined value.
- **Limitations** (x)–(xiii) repeat §5.8–5.9 verbatim. Replace them with one paragraph and cross-references.
- **Appendix A proof of Theorem 6(ii)** assumes w·d_a ≤ w·s is the scalarized admissibility test, the synchronization hypothesis flagged in the first-half review; state it.
