## Direct cross-checks of #1–#4

**#1 — two genuinely compatible branches, no degenerate-singleton escape.** The counterexample can use initial belief `{x1=1,x2=2}` with a constant, uninformative observation, dynamics `x'=-1+xu`, `U=[-1,1]`, safety `x≥1`, `T=1`, `τ=1/2`, and a stored label of weight one on branch `x1`. Under `u=0`, `xbar_1(s)=1-s`, `β=-1`, `X=1`, `k(s)=1-s`; the paper's formula gives `Γ_h=-1+∫_0^1(1-s)ds=-1/2<0`. Under the single admissible common control `u=+1`, `x1(t)=1` and `x2(t)=1+e^t`: *both* are safe forever. Main §3.7 assumes only `f=f0(x,d)+B(x)u`, allowing `B(x)=x` (main lines 1168–1188); Supplementary S1 lines 526–577 invokes an exact zero-control variation-of-constants identity and simultaneously calls the general control-affine expression first order with an uncharged remainder. No additional Lean hypothesis rescues that written result, because no Lean result about it exists. A theorem with **state-independent** input matrix and affine state/constraint structure, or a validated one-sided remainder, would be a different statement and needs new proof.

**#2 — theorem versus application.** The delayed-information theorem's (H3.2) is a conditional sufficient obstruction, *not* a theorem saying every hidden-regime system with no observation until `T_obs` is nonviable. Its hypothesis is false for the alternate sequence `(+,−,+)` from `z0=2` at `T_obs=3`; all intermediate levels on both branches remain `≥1`. The Git-blob-verified historical `paper2_coverage_audit.py` function `viable_exact` tests a single `u` multiplied by `T_obs`, i.e. a whole-window hold. Supplementary S1 lines 763–789 explicitly limits its probabilistic table to a hold and expressly notes that unrestricted alternating actions rescue the purported timing cells. The repair is **scope/fidelity of the application**, not rejection of the conditional theorem or of Lean's per-step recursion. A per-step control may be common across hidden branches without being constant in time.

**#3 — stated probabilistic assumptions.** The main §10 first defines `V_k` from a **fixed stochastic transition kernel** `T` (lines 1846–1859), then its deterministic-limit proposition assumes the *support* of that kernel contains all disturbance-reachable states (lines 1871–1875), while also saying the disturbance is “read adversarially.” No lower transition-probability bound, deterministic single-successor transition, or robust/minimax redefinition of `V_k` appears in that proposition. With singleton safe belief of mass one, a bad successor of probability `1/100`, a good successor of `99/100`, constant observation, and both successors in the stated support, robust `W_1` fails yet stochastic `V_1=99/100>0=1−min b`. The example **meets the support assumption**; it violates an unstated *deterministic-transition* restriction that would be needed to recover the min-mass bound. The proof additionally jumps from `B∉W_k` to a first-step loss, unjustified for `k>1`. This is a manuscript formulation/proof problem, not a Lean failure; the later probabilistic-sufficiency source v14 uses an explicit deterministic-kernel restriction and a multi-step argument.

**#4 — actual text, corrected precision.** Main Example `calc-ex:relax-gap` lines 927–940 starts with **decaying** `z[k+1]=0.9 z[k]±u[k]` and correctly derives the finite-horizon relaxed threshold `z0≥(10/9)^K`, but then states `σ*=z0−1` for the finite action set in that same example. At `z0=2` either `u=±1` immediately sends one branch to `4/5<1`, so that assertion does not follow for the decaying model. Main theorem clause (iv), lines 910–915, explicitly refers to **different non-decaying hidden-regime data** when it says `u=0` survives forever; the earlier report's wording incorrectly suggested the example itself literally asserted infinite survival under decay. Supplementary S1 lines 898–915 correctly **separates** the non-decaying held-control gap and the decaying Farkas-row computation. But the theorem's per-step declared class `Π_B={(u0,…,uK−1):u_k∈U}` does not by itself impose a whole-window hold on the finite comparison class: alternation changes that comparison as in #2. Thus keep the finite-horizon LP result, separate the examples, and state the finite implementable class precisely.

For Helly, main lines 393–400 define `V={q_j≥0}` and the **instantaneous** robust tangent set by `∇q_j(x)·f(x,u,d)≥0`; main Proposition `calc-prop:helly` lines 966–990 uses exactly those affine-in-`u` inequalities to obtain closed convex sets and applies compactness plus Helly. Supplementary S1 lines 477–522 announces a complete proof of the same proposition but switches to **discrete next-state** `q_j(F(x,u,d))≤0` and adds convexity of `q_j`, despite the main's opposite safety sign and `C¹` assumption. This is genuinely a **supplementary proof-to-statement mismatch**; the main proof is already self-contained and is not invalidated by it. No Lean theorem adjudicates that mismatch.
