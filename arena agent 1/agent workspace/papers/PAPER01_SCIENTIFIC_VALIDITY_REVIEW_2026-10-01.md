## Findings (ordered by consequence)

### 1. **Confirmed unsoundness under stated hypotheses: oracle-recourse proposition (§3.7)**

Main lines 1168–1190 assume only **control-affine** dynamics `f(x,u,d)=f_0(x,d)+B(x)u`, but equate the actual facet value with an integral kernel computed along the **zero-control trajectory**. State-dependent `B(x)` invalidates that equality; Supplementary S1 lines 526–577 itself describes the expression as only first-order in the general control-affine case, mentions a remainder, but never bounds or adds one to the certificate. The support-function upper bound therefore need not bound the true facet value.

An exact counterexample satisfying the written form: scalar `x'=-1+xu`, `U=[-1,1]`, no disturbance, safe set `x>=1` (`q=x-1`), initial compatible branches `x1=1`, `x2=2` with an uninformative observation, one stored label from `x1` (weight 1) at `t=T=1`, blind interval `tau=1/2`. Under zero control on that branch, `xbar(s)=1-s`, `beta=q(xbar(1))=-1`, `X(1,s)=1`, `k(s)=1-s`. Hence the paper's displayed `Gamma_h=-1+integral_0^1(1-s)ds=-1/2<0` **fires**, allegedly certifying nonviability. But the admissible observation-independent control `u=+1` keeps both branches safe for all time (`x1(t)=1`, `x2(t)=1+e^t`). This is a **false-positive nonviability certificate**, not a missing numerical check. Remedy: restrict the result to genuinely linear state dynamics with state-independent control matrix and affine safety rows, or derive and validate a one-sided nonlinear remainder bound, then rerun the test on the enlarged class. Do not cite §3.7's general-control-affine soundness meanwhile.

### 2. **Confirmed policy-class mismatch: the delayed hidden-regime “ground truth” and coverage audit (§§3.3, 8–10)**

Main lines 1772–1810 use `z[k+1]=z[k]+theta*u[k]`, `theta,u in {±1}`, no regime observation for `T_obs` **steps**, and claim every blind policy drives a branch downward at rate one, giving `sigma*=z0-1`, `z0>=1+T_obs` for viability, and **42** nonviable grid cells, **12** allegedly requiring a timing certificate. This holds if one action is **held for the entire blind window**; it does **not** hold for the time-varying blind actions permitted by the main's statement of the example and its general delayed-observation hypothesis (main lines 839–845 say “every measurable ... blind-window control”). A policy can select the same action for both hidden branches at each step yet alternate it across steps. From `z0=2`, `T_obs=3`, the blind sequence `(+1,-1,+1)` keeps both branches above `1` at every step; repeat alternation for arbitrarily many blind steps. The observation is never used. In fact the unrestricted finite-step class is viable exactly when `z0>=2` for every `T_obs>=1`. Exact enumeration of all 48 grid cells for `T_obs=1,2,3` gives **30 nonviable**, rather than 42; the 12 orange timing cells are viable in that class.

This is **not** a counterexample to the delayed-information theorem under its actual hypothesis (H3.2): the alternating policy shows that H3.2 does not hold for this example in the unrestricted class. It is a counterexample to the paper's *application* of that theorem and its unqualified ground-truth/coverage claim. The historical `paper2_coverage_audit.py` explicitly tests only a **single held action** (`viable_exact` loops over one `u` and evaluates `z0+theta*u*T_obs`); it does not run general backward recursion. Supplementary S1 lines 763–789 correctly calls its calculation the **declared hold class** and even explicitly notes alternation rescues the other class. Main §8 line 1755 mentions the hold class, but main §9's model, table/caption and `ERViab` conclusion do not impose it. Repair requires an explicit whole-window hold constraint throughout the result and caption, plus separate identification of the unrestricted-class boundary; a script that proves the intended class, not a change to the general theorem. Source v53–56 already carried this mismatch; it was not introduced by the paper01 merge.

### 3. **False probability bound as stated: deterministic-limit proposition (§10)**

Main lines 1871–1875 define the stochastic `V_k(b)` in the preceding theorem and claim that, when stochastic transitions have support equal to the deterministic disturbance-reachable states, `B notin W_k` implies `V_k(b)<=1-min_{x in B}b(x)`. Support of a transition identifies **possible** adverse paths, not their probability. Counterexample: `B={s}`, `b(s)=1`, one action, disturbance outcomes `F(s,a,good)=s` and `F(s,a,bad)=unsafe`, with stochastic transition probabilities `99/100` and `1/100`, respectively (constant observation). Both outcomes are in its declared support; robust `W_1` excludes `B`, but the *defined stochastic* `V_1(b)=99/100`, contradicting the claimed bound `V_1<=0`. The phrase “disturbance read adversarially within its support” might intend a **different robust/minimax value**, but no such value is defined by the displayed POMDP recursion. Moreover the proof's claim that `B notin W_k` forces one-step exit under every action is false for multi-step obstructions even in deterministic models. The paper02 probabilistic-sufficiency v14 theorem instead explicitly restricts its min-mass statement to deterministic **kernels** and observation maps, with a recursive proof.

Remedy: distinguish (a) fixed stochastic kernel, where support failure only establishes a positive deficit dependent on path probabilities unless a uniform lower probability is supplied, from (b) an explicitly defined adversarial robust value; or restrict this proposition and its examples to deterministic single-successor kernels and replace the one-step proof by induction. This is present in source obstr v55 as well, not merge damage.

### 4. **Two different models conflated in the LP-instantiation example (§3.3)**

Main lines 896–940 first give a correct sum-of-branches LP threshold for `z[k+1]=0.9 z[k] ± u[k]`: over `U=[-1,1]`, `u=0` keeps both branches safe through horizon `K` iff `z0>=(10/9)^K`. But the same *decaying-model example* then says for `U_fin={±1}`, `sigma*=z0-1`, a value derived for the **different** non-decaying hidden-regime model `z[k+1]=z[k]±u[k]` under a whole-window hold. At `z0=2`, choosing either finite action in the displayed decaying model has worst next value `0.9*2-1=4/5<1`, disproving that comparison. The theorem's separate statement that `u=0` lasts forever explicitly concerns the *non-decaying* hidden-regime data; the example does **not** literally assert this for the decaying model, but its cross-reference wrongly presents the two computations as one instance. For the decaying model `0.9^7*2<1`, so it could not establish infinite relaxed survival. Supplementary S1 lines 898–915 correctly gives the hidden-regime gap and the decaying model's duality computation **separately**. Retain the sound LP bound; split/qualify the example and theorem clause (iv)'s cross-reference rather than merging the models.

### 5. **Supplementary proof mismatch; main Helly statement has a different, plausible proof**

Main lines 966–990 prove the `m+1` sparse instantaneous-action witness from tangent inequalities `nabla q_j(x)·f(x,u,d)>=0`, affine in `u`, plus compactness and Helly. Supplementary S1 lines 477–522 calls itself the “complete proof” of the same proposition but instead assumes **convex constraint functions** and reasons about discrete next-state inequalities `q_j(F(x,u,d))<=0`; that is not the continuous-time tangent correspondence used by the main and has the opposite sign convention for its displayed safe set. The main argument appears to supply the needed convexity under its own hypotheses; this is **not** a demonstrated counterexample to the main Helly proposition. It is an internally mismatched supplementary proof that should be replaced or expressly labeled a different result, not used to justify an added convex-`q` hypothesis in the main without analysis.
