\section{Distributive constraints and measurement-level social indicators}\label{si:distribution}

\subsection{Candidate constituency and observed measurement}

For the northern-cod case, a candidate worst-off constituency is registered inshore harvesters and licence holders. Available community-level data do not measure that population directly. The mismatch is retained rather than resolved by substituting a convenient scalar:

Statistics Canada Tables 38-10-0167-01 and 38-10-0168-01 report community-level series for 43 fishing-dependent census subdivisions; the mean share of employment income from fishing falls from 32.2\% to 25.6\% between 2016 and 2021. The statistic is all-resident employment income, not an inshore licence-holder panel. It includes aquaculture and processing categories and cannot by itself identify which households rely on the cod fishery or who bears a policy transition.

A measured floor is a pair \((I_k,c_k)\): an instrument and its cutoff. For groups \(G\) and times \(t\), define margin \(m_k(G,t)=I_k(G,t)-c_k\). A componentwise constraint is violated if any required margin is negative; it does not permit a favourable aggregate indicator to compensate for a household or group falling below a different floor. No such floor is operationalized in v53. Non-decline is a normative choice, not automatically an empirically justified threshold. A defensible instrument must state baseline, cohort, inflation and purchasing-power treatment, uncertainty, attrition, acceptable variation, authority, and structural diversification.

\subsection{External measurement frameworks and limits}

The global Multidimensional Poverty Index (Alkire and Foster, 2011) and the World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform are potential reference frameworks, but their release and vintage must be locked before use. Neither substitutes for a local distributional record of who pays for persistence, who may use remaining resource diversity, whose knowledge counts, or how future persons are represented. These issues are left open as measurement and governance questions, not filled by a composite score. No distributional causal effect is estimated in this manuscript.
