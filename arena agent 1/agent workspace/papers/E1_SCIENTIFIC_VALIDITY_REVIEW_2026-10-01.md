## 3. Live-specific claim/evidence problems

### A. The new wrapper makes a **false across-systems annual ranking**

The live lead abstract says **“On both systems, at the annual origin, last-value persistence is more accurate than the process-based alternatives scored.”** Its own aquifer Part II table and retention paragraph say that the univariate autonomous `M1` has one-year RMSE **`12.84 < 13.23`** ft for persistence; the climatological-flux affine `M2m` reads **`12.28 < 13.23`** ft (listed but declined under a protocol class clause). Even if `M1` is categorized as an *output-only statistical model* rather than a physically process-based one, **`M2m` is explicitly the water-balance-identified affine map** and is numerically more accurate at one year. The source study distinguishes **accuracy** from **retention**; a class exclusion or five-year loss cannot reverse the one-year accuracy ranking. The wrapper also truthfully notes M1 is retained at that horizon. The claim needs its comparator class specified; as printed, it conflicts with the supplied table. These counter-values are from the live text; no fresh Edwards campaign rerun was performed in the E1 review.

### B. The new “fully locked” protocol claim contradicts the original E1 disclosure

The live related-work subsection says the **retention rule** was **“fixed before any score was computed”** and **“nothing is selected from the scores at all.”** The preserved source's §2.3 explicitly says the **scoring core** was coded before the first scoring pass, **but the 5% tie band and comparator declarations were completions recorded after Section 3 scores were computed**; it also argues they did not change recorded E1 verdicts. This is a substantive chronology distinction, not a typo or a prior false theorem. The rule's actual pseudocode uses the scores to select retain/not-retain. A predetermined *decision predicate* can reduce researcher discretion without eliminating score-dependent selection or automatically furnishing familywise-error control. The live subsection's analogy to White and Romano–Wolf therefore overstates both the timing and the statistical inference. The published D1 power and specificity remain valid as simulation summaries conditional on the applied rule; they cannot establish that the entire rule was prespecified.

### C. New bibliography entry inserted **inside** the source's scientific explanation

A source-specific diff locates `Romano, J.P. and Wolf, M., 2005. ...` and its journal line **between** `Pella and` and `Tomlinson, 1969)` in Part I's depensation explanation. Source v60 says correctly “Pella and Tomlinson, 1969” there. The live interpolation severs the attribution and inserts unrelated multiple-testing bibliography prose into the model explanation. This is a merge-introduced scholarly-content corruption, not a disagreement over the underlying cubic factor or an excuse to remove the source argument. The original bibliography and original abstract must be preserved in a later source-specific repair.

### D. Retention and cross-system mechanism need narrower wording

The live related work says “on both systems [the rule] does not [retain a module],” and the preserved E1 conclusion says “the retained set is empty on both systems,” without restricting the set to **process/stock-flow additions**. The Edwards Part II explicitly **retains output-only AR(1) M1 at `h=1`**, so unrestricted “no module retained” is false in the composite; “no *causal stock-flow* addition retained” is a different and potentially supported claim. Likewise the wrapper's sweeping “near-white driver” diagnostic is an interpretation of these fitted forecasts, not a formal inference from the Lean finite-sum results or the 73 record checks. It should not erase E1's stated limitations: the main stock reconstruction embeds later information, and the low-power alternatives' non-retention is weak evidence. Do not claim that the economic/ecological mechanism is identified merely by an RMSE ordering.
