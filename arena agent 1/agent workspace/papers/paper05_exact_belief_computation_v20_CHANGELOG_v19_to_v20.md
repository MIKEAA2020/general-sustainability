## 2. New mathematical content

* **Theorem 3.1 (linear criterion).** `S` is blind-survivable from some finite slack iff `V(S) = max_y min_θ ⟨θ,y⟩ ≥ 5/2`, iff `min_q ‖Σ q_θ θ‖₁ ≥ 5/2`; the maximiser gives a product mixture and a periodic word, the minimiser a pooled slack decreasing by `5 − 2V` per step. v19 had no criterion: survivability was decided set by set (pairs by a lemma, larger sets by enumeration and case analysis).
* **Corollary 3.2 (finite horizon).** A non-survivable set loses a cell within `⌊L/(5 − 2V)⌋` steps; sharp for far pairs at even `m` and for the whole cube; at `m = 5` the exact horizon of far pairs and tetrahedra is `2⌊(L−1)/2⌋` (two-step alternation, last two tenths unspendable).
* **Corollaries 3.3–3.5.** The pair bound `d_H ≤ m − 5/2` (v19 Lemma 1 for `m = 4` only), the Hamming-ball census at the floor with `r*(m) = ⌊(2m−5)/4⌋`, and Kleitman's diameter bound `K(m) = 2, 6, 12, 29, 58` for `m = 4…8` — attained for every `m` by floor balls (odd `m`) and doubled balls (even `m`, threshold 1). The reviewer's suggestion to use Kleitman rather than Harper is adopted and extended to a construction.
* **Proposition 3.6 (incomparable instruments).** The tetrahedron of the five-cube: all pairs survivable, all triples inside balls, `Σ|y_i|` large, yet `V = 2`. v19 treated tetrahedra by an aggregate-drift bound without the general principle.
* **Section 4 (thresholds).** The slack game on `ℕ^S` (Def. 4.1), its winning region as an antichain of minimal winning vectors (Prop. 4.2; v19's "antichain census" reinterpreted as this object), the saturated game as a sound upper bound and forward reachability with dominance pruning as the lower bound (Prop. 4.3), the first-step bound (Prop. 4.4) and the pinned-slack inequality for boundary sets (Prop. 4.5) that explains why the ten-cell sets need slack 3 although every first step costs 1. All 5,373 representative thresholds are certified on both sides; the cap artefact (cap 6 returns 5 for the ten-sets, cap 12 returns 3) is documented rather than hidden.
* **Theorems 5.1–5.3 (classifications).** Four-cube (as v19, now a corollary of the criterion and machine-checked), five-cube (as v19, with the census corrected to the count of sets: 1,616 for `L ≤ 2` and 1,696 for `L ≥ 3` against 1,776 admissible — v19 counted 496 and 1,552 "stored" objects with multiplicity), and the **six-cube, new**: 10,752 maximal cliques in 7 orbits; 853,680 admissible sets; 774,960 survivable; thresholds `{0: 7,072; 1: 624,368; 3: 143,360; 5: 160}`; maximal families 192 cylinders (`L* = 1`), 1,280 boundary ten-sets (`V = 5/2`, `L* = 3`), 160 subcubes (`L* = 5`); 2,400 minimal obstructions (1,440 four-sets, 960 five-sets), all with `V = 2`.
* **Corollary 5.4 (uniform horizon).** Every non-survivable set of the three cubes dies within `L` steps, because every minimal obstruction has `V = 2`.
* **Section 6 (observation).** The blind value of a belief and the antichain formula (Prop. 6.1); the probe ladder as a computed table for `m = 4, 5, 6` at every level (v19: two ladders at `m = 4`, "doubling" stated as a pattern; the `j = 0` entry corrected to 1); the feedback game (Def. 6.2) solved exactly: `F*(Θ₄) = 23`, `F*(Θ₅) = 18`, subcubes and shells, with the first-split decomposition (Prop. 6.3); the deadline law `5T` with both directions proved and the Lean statement cited for what it is (Prop. 6.4; v19 proved only sufficiency on paper).
* **Proposition 7.1 (finite versus indefinite horizon).** Point-based alpha-sets are exact for finite-horizon survival and agree with indefinite survival precisely when `k` exceeds the longest transient (`L` at `m = 4, 6`; `2⌊(L−1)/2⌋` at `m = 5`); the `17⁴ → 857` count is a count of masks at `L = 12`, tabulated for all 16 levels and `k = 1…4` (v19 reported single values and a "period ≤ 3" claim that is withdrawn).
* **Section 8 (exact versus float).** The 143,360 knife-edge sets; the sweep over all 1,280 maximal boundary sets (1,120 false collapses under `(2k−5)/10`, none under `−0.5 + 0.2k`, first at step 3); the `float32` collapse at step 3; the floating-point LP returns `V = 2.5` with zero error, so the danger is in the simulation, not the LP. Runtimes are reported per stage (Table 6).
