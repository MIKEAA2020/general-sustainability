# Highlights — paper05 v20

* **A linear criterion for blind survival.** A set `S` of sign cells is kept above the floor for ever by one action sequence from *some* finite slack if and only if `V(S) = max_y min_{θ∈S} ⟨θ,y⟩ ≥ 5/2`, equivalently `min_{q∈Δ(S)} ‖Σ q_θ θ‖₁ ≥ 5/2` (Theorem 3.1). Both sides are exact rational certificates: a maximiser `y` gives a periodic word, a minimiser `q` gives a pooled slack that falls by `5 − 2V` per step, so a non-survivable set loses a cell within `⌊L/(5 − 2V)⌋` steps (Corollary 3.2). The criterion yields the pair bound `d_H ≤ m − 5/2`, the Hamming-ball census at the floor, and Kleitman's diameter bound for the largest survivable set (`2, 6, 12, 29, 58` cells for `m = 4…8`, attained for every `m`).
* **The instrument that looked relevant is not.** A tetrahedron of the five-cube has survivable pairs, triples inside balls and a large "instrument strength" `Σ|y_i|`, yet `V = 2`: it is never blind-survivable (Proposition 3.6). Survivability is a property of the pooled cell, not of pairwise distances.
* **Thresholds with two-sided certificates.** The least sufficient slack `L*(S)` is the least `L` with `(L,…,L)` in the winning region of a safety game on `ℕ^S`, computed as an antichain of minimal winning vectors. A saturated game gives a sound upper bound, exact forward reachability with dominance pruning the lower bound; the two met for all 5,373 representative sets. The first-step bound is the threshold for most, not all, maximal sets: the boundary ten-sets of the six-cube need slack 3 although every first step costs at most 1, because their pooled slack is pinned at `L` for ever (Propositions 4.4–4.5).
* **Complete classifications for `m = 4, 5, 6`.** Four-cube: 16 singletons at the floor, 32 adjacent pairs from slack 1. Five-cube: 32 radius-one balls at the floor, 80 squares from slack 3, 80 tetrahedra never. Six-cube: of 853,680 pairwise-admissible sets, 774,960 are survivable — 7,072 at the floor, 631,440 from slack 1, 774,800 from slack 3, all from slack 5 — with maximal families of 192 Kleitman-extremal twelve-cell cylinders, 1,280 ten-cell sets sitting exactly on the boundary `V = 5/2`, and 160 three-dimensional subcubes (Theorems 5.1–5.3). Every minimal obstruction has `V = 2`, so every non-survivable set dies within `L` steps (Corollary 5.4).
* **The price of observation, exactly.** With the stock observed the whole cube is kept from slack 23 at `m = 4` and 18 at `m = 5`, against infinity for blind play; the price decomposes along the first split (`23 = max(13, 9+14, 5+18, 1+14)`), the probe ladder is a computed table rather than a conjectured doubling, and total ignorance for `T` periods costs exactly `5T` tenths (Propositions 6.1–6.4).
* **Point-based evaluation is exact — for a different question.** The `k`-step alpha-sets (`17⁴ → 857` masks at `m = 4`, `L = 12`) evaluate finite-horizon survival exactly, and they agree with indefinite survival precisely when `k` exceeds the longest transient — `L` at `m = 4, 6`, `2⌊(L−1)/2⌋` at `m = 5` — not at a fixed horizon (Proposition 7.1).
* **Exactness is not the limitation.** The six-cube has 143,360 survivable sets on the knife edge `V = 5/2`; a `float64` simulation of their certified words declares a false collapse in 1,120 of the 1,280 maximal cases under one spelling of the drift and in none under the other. The exact classification takes two and a half minutes; 53 exact checks regenerate every number; the dynamics, pair laws, four-cube classification and deadline law are machine-checked in Lean 4 (80 theorems, no `sorry`), with the scope of the Lean layer stated precisely.
