**Title:** An Obstruction Calculus for Viability under Incomplete Observation

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, **"An Obstruction Calculus for Viability under Incomplete Observation,"** for consideration in *Set-Valued and Variational Analysis*.

**What the paper does.** Viability theory under *perfect* measurement is mature: the viability kernel exists as the largest viable subset, characterised by Nagumo-type tangency conditions. Under *incomplete* observation the theory has been asymmetric: the sufficiency side is carried by Veliov's output-feedback regulation condition and the estimation-tube programme, while instruments for the complementary direction — certifying that *no* observation-based policy is viable — have been scarce. The manuscript supplies such instruments: an **obstruction calculus** of sound, computationally interpretable certificates for the non-viability of *every* observation-based policy. It establishes five mechanisms — two finitely checkable (a polyhedral common-action form and a finite-fibre certification form), two closed-form conditional (exit and timing bounds), and one minimal construction — plus a sixth under a policy-class restriction. The central common-action obstruction shows that when the safe controls of compatible states intersect emptily, no observation-based policy is viable, even though every compatible state is individually viable under full information. The further mechanisms give a finite-time exit certificate under an Isaacs-type drift condition, an epistemic-emptiness construction, a delayed-information obstruction with an explicit timing bound, and a certification limit characterising when an exact observation-only certifier exists. The certificates are proven sound sufficient conditions for nonviability — equivalently, necessary conditions for observation-based viability — and, as the manuscript states explicitly, they do not exhaust the complement of the epistemic kernel. Each impossibility is converted into a concrete design specification (timing, coarseness, aggregation, and bias of observation).

**Why it matters.** Sustainability governance operates under incomplete observation: stocks are assessed at discrete review intervals, indicators are coarser than the state, some stocks are unobserved, and information may arrive late. This paper makes precise when failure is attributable to *information* rather than to *dynamics* — establishing infeasibility that no better weighting, no finer scalar aggregate, and no re-timing of a fixed policy class can remove. Where a certificate applies, its conclusions are directly actionable for monitoring and indicator design: the certificate identifies *what to change* (a faster indicator, a separating observation, observation that separates the sides of each floor, or correction of a known bias). This is a formal contribution to a problem that is both mathematically open and practically consequential.

**Why it is appropriate for *Set-Valued and Variational Analysis*.** The paper is set-valued and variational throughout. Its object is the *epistemic kernel* — the observation-based counterpart of the viability kernel — and the results are expressed as set-valued identities between compatible-state safe-action sets, intersections at a single information state, and fibre-level membership criteria. The machinery is viability and invariance theory, differential inclusions, tangency and drift (Isaacs-type) conditions, estimation tubes, and polyhedral/finite-fibre certification — all core topics of the journal. The certificates are positioned explicitly against barrier certificates (Prajna–Jadbabaie, Prajna–Rantzer, Maghenem–Sanfelice) and against the estimation-tube reduction (Cardaliaguet–Quincampoix–Saint-Pierre), delimiting rather than contradicting both. I have chosen the MSC codes (49J53, 93B03, 93C41) to reflect the set-valued/differential-inclusion and dynamic-control character of the results.

**Fit and originality.** To my knowledge, constructive, certificate-based instruments for the non-viability side of viability under incomplete observation have not previously been collected in this form. The paper is honest about its scope: it claims no complete characterisation of the epistemic kernel (it states the open middle ground explicitly), and it presents the certificates as sound sufficient conditions that do not exhaust the complement — the appropriate scope for a new instrument. This is, I believe, precisely the kind of rigorous, applicable result *Set-Valued and Variational Analysis* exists to publish.

The manuscript is self-contained, with a bounded-constructions appendix. No code or data were used; all constructions are symbolic. I confirm the work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and that I have no conflicts of interest.
