\section{Rolling-origin decomposition and baseline horizons of Section 5}\label{si:horizon}

Table~\ref{tab:si_horizon} gives the rolling-origin RMSE of last-value persistence and of the training mean at every horizon $h=1,\dots,5$ on the three scored series, computed from the archived data files on the origin sets of the scored ladders (first origin 1990 with an 8-year minimum training window on Specification A; 1965 with 12 years on Specification B; 1948 with 15 years on J-17), together with the lag-$h$ autocorrelation $\rho_h$ of the predictand over the full series and the stationary prediction $\sqrt{2(1-\rho_h)}$ of main-text Remark~2 for the ratio of the two RMSEs. The $h=1$ and $h=5$ values reproduce the archived summaries (main-text Tables~3 and 7) to the printed precision: 98.0/264.7 and 423.6/507.4\,kt on Specification A, 84.4/300.0 and 458.1/521.6\,kt on Specification B, 13.23/21.11 and 16.17/16.80\,ft on J-17. On J-17 the observed ratio tracks the prediction within 0.11 at every horizon, always from below, and crosses one between $h=1$ and $h=2$, where $\rho_h$ falls through one half. On both cod series the observed ratio stays far below the prediction at every horizon, the training mean of a collapsing series being a poor forecast of anything; the stationary formula would place a crossover between four and five years on Specification A and beyond five on Specification B, and neither occurs. The script is \texttt{work/horizon\_profile.py}; its JSON summary is archived beside it.

For the exact rolling-origin application of main-text Proposition~2, at origin $i$ let $(\alpha_i,\beta_i,\gamma_i,\delta_i)$ be the coefficients fitted for M2 using only the training sample. Before output clipping, its increment evaluated at the realized target-year flux is
\[
m_i=\alpha_i+\beta_iR_{t+1}+\gamma_iP_{t+1}+\delta_iH_t,
\qquad \hat m_i=\alpha_i+\beta_iR_t+\gamma_iP_t+\delta_iH_t,
\]
where $\hat m_i$ is the increment used by the forecast, with fluxes persisted from the origin. Define $u_i=\Delta H_{t+1}-m_i$ and $d_i=m_i-\hat m_i$. The identity is checked origin by origin as
\[
(\Delta H_{t+1}-\hat m_i)^2-(\Delta H_{t+1})^2
= (d_i^2-m_i^2)-2u_i\hat m_i.
\]
Across all 75 one-year origins, the mean substitution term $\overline{d^2-m^2}$ is $+76.028642$\,ft$^2$, the mean residual cross-term $-2\overline{u\hat m}$ is $-27.490666$\,ft$^2$, and their sum, the raw M2-minus-persistence MSE gap, is $+48.537976$\,ft$^2$. Recomputed origin-specific M2 and persistence forecasts match the archived forecasts. After the $[610,710]$-ft output clip, one upper clip occurs (origin 1992; raw M2 proposal 719.1027\,ft, emitted value 710\,ft); no lower clip occurs. The archived scored RMSEs, 14.70\,ft for M2 and 13.23\,ft for persistence, imply a clipped MSE gap of $+40.988957$\,ft$^2$. Thus the decomposition is exact for the un-clipped fitted increments, whereas the output cap changes the scored difference. The implementation and per-origin audit are in \texttt{work/edw/increment\_identity\_rolling.py}, \texttt{work/edw/results/increment\_identity\_rolling.csv} and \texttt{work/edw/results/increment\_identity\_rolling.json}.

Table~\ref{tab:si_autocorr} reports full-series lag-one autocorrelations, means and standard deviations; these are descriptive full-sample diagnostics, not the rolling attribution above. The J-17 flux component is $\hat\beta R_t+\hat\gamma P_t$ with the full-sample least-squares coefficients of the one-pool map, $(\hat\alpha,\hat\beta,\hat\gamma,\hat\delta)=(177.6,\,0.0173,\,-0.0260,\,-0.273)$; over the 90 annual flux observations its lag-one autocorrelation is 0.078, its mean is 3.52\,ft and its standard deviation is 9.20\,ft. The apparent net production of the cod series is $S_{t+1}-S_t+C_t$ with the catch series of the respective pass. Further correlations used in the main text: $r(\Delta H_t,R_t)=0.742$, $r(\Delta H_t,R_{t-1})=0.056$, $r(R_t,R_{t-1})=0.172$ as a Pearson correlation of successive pairs (0.170 as the autocovariance-based estimator in the table), and $r(H_t,H_{t-1})=0.644$ as a Pearson correlation of pairs (0.629 autocovariance-based; the single-system aquifer study quoted 0.64).
