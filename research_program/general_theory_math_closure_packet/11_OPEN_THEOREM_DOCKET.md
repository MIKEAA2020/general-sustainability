# Open Theorem Docket — Major Coherence Obstacles

## T1. Typed architecture transformation beyond standard reachability

**Current:** finite-review exact-tube backward recursion is proved but likely mathematically standard.

**Needed:** a genuinely nontrivial result involving typed state translation, identity/liability/obligation preservation, noncompensatory transition safety, or a theorem proving false positives of endpoint-only/aggregate transformation tests.

**Acceptance:** independent theorem or impossibility result; no novelty claim based only on renamed state coordinates.

## T2. Observation–assessment–implementation bridge

Given physical/history state, observation map, exact/outer information update, prescription policy, and set-valued implementation, prove sufficient conditions for robust institutional viability.

**Needed distinctions:** measured versus hidden disturbance; exact versus conservative filter; prescribed versus realized action; latency and held commands; all implementation branches versus existential implementation.

**Acceptance:** one closed-loop theorem with exact quantifiers and a common-action counterexample.

## T3. Viability–diagnostic bridge

Determine when a diagnostic margin or finite-horizon horizon estimate is:

- sound for nonviability;
- complete for viability;
- an inner/outer certificate;
- merely descriptive.

**Acceptance:** theorem relating diagnostic error and model/observation error to kernel erosion or obstruction; counterexample showing why local stock-to-rate or aggregate margins are not kernels.

## T4. Domain admission and projectability

Given a domain model and the canonical schema, state necessary and sufficient conditions for exact admission; otherwise provide an approximation theorem.

**Required:** type/unit map; phase-space map; projectability; policy/information map; error bound; horizon; safety erosion; artifact/version identity.

**Acceptance:** theorem/certificate that can classify groundwater, phosphorus, or fisheries models without verbal analogy.

## T5. Composition beyond the first restricted theorem

**Closed:** proximal-normal joint-feasibility theorem under regular velocity envelopes.

**Open:** useful assume–guarantee or nonlinear small-gain theorem allowing state-dependent interface contracts without hiding shared controls or nonconvex implementation.

**Acceptance:** true gain operator/fixed point, counterexample outside hypotheses, no nonlinear functions inserted into a numerical matrix without reduction.

## T6. Cross-scale aggregation and dynamic closure

Prove conditions under which coarse variables are dynamically closed, approximately closed, or necessarily memory-bearing.

**Needed:** relation among projectability, variance/covariance corrections, Mori–Zwanzig/memory alternatives, and safety erosion.

**Acceptance:** exact theorem plus obstruction; static expectation identity alone is insufficient.

## T7. Intergenerational continuation and transformation

Formalize generation-indexed safe sets, changing authorities/thresholds, continuation kernels, obligations, and architecture transitions.

**Acceptance:** recursive theorem separating fixed-specification viability from specification change; compactness/impossibility result retained; no universal ethical claim.

## T8. Robust–epistemic–chance hierarchy

State exact implications among robust, epistemic, institutional, and chance viability under support, filtration, information-state, and implementation assumptions.

**Acceptance:** typed mappings rather than literal inclusions across different phase spaces; counterexamples to converses.

## T9. General-theory boundary theorem

Produce a theorem or formal proposition stating exactly which claims are universal consequences of the typed axioms and which remain model-class dependent.

**Acceptance:** conservation conditional on closure; viability conditional on safe/policy/disturbance sets; noncompensation conditional on declared binding components; no universal delay, substitution, or causal-diagnostic law.
