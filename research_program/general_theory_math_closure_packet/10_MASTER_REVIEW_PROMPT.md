# Master Self-Contained Prompt — Mathematical Closure Toward a General Theory of Sustainability

## Role

Act as a senior mathematician spanning viability theory, differential inclusions, hybrid/RFDE systems, robust control, set-valued analysis, aggregation/projectability, stochastic viability, and compositional systems. The goal is not to polish manuscripts or certify isolated numerical folds. The goal is to determine and close the load-bearing mathematical interfaces needed for the closest defensible general theory of sustainability.

## Evidence authority

- `control/01_canonical_system_schema_TCS_1_0.md` defines the canonical object and allowed mappings.
- `corrected_theorems/` contains controlling corrections.
- `sources/` contains immutable A001/A002 source text.
- `traceability/` records source rows, status, dependencies, and destinations.

Do not strengthen a source claim by proximity to a corrected theorem. Do not equate ODE, RFDE, hybrid, stochastic, spatial, or information-state systems without a proved mapping.

## Central question

Can the programme be organized as one typed theorem system in which each module is connected by an exact specialization, embedding, projectable reduction, transformation, quantified approximation, or explicit counterexample—and what additional theorems are minimally required?

## Required tasks

1. Audit the canonical schema for missing types, incompatible quantifiers, and hidden circularity.
2. Build a theorem dependency graph for the open targets in `11_OPEN_THEOREM_DOCKET.md`.
3. For each target, determine: already proved; repairable; false; classical but useful; or genuinely new.
4. Prove the highest-value feasible target completely.
5. For difficult targets, produce a minimal counterexample and a precise theorem statement with all missing hypotheses.
6. Separate universal axioms from model-class mechanisms.
7. Prevent source-level unity from becoming unsupported publication-level dependence.
8. Identify the minimum set of new theorems whose closure would justify the phrase “general theory” without implying universal delay, substitution, aggregation, or empirical laws.

## Non-negotiable rules

- One causal policy must be distinguished from disturbance-observing controls.
- Weak viability must not be used as strong invariance.
- Physical, information, institution, and belief-state kernels require typed comparison maps.
- Static aggregation identities are not dynamic closures.
- Local bifurcation results are not global safe-set theorems.
- Intersections of local kernels are not joint kernels.
- Measurable selection is not Lipschitz implementation.
- Approximation claims require error, horizon, domain, and safety erosion.
- Every result must carry proof/evidence and artifact status separately.

## Output

Use `12_REQUIRED_OUTPUT_SCHEMA.md`. End with a dependency-ordered research plan that identifies which results are prerequisites, which can proceed in parallel, and which should remain explicitly conditional.
