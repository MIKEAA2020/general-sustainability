# ER013 Point Inventory and Mathematical Adjudication

## Overall disposition

**Do not accept the claimed proofs or publication block.** ER013 has the right high-level conditional targets, but neither proof is self-contained or valid as written. It does not close the concrete A021 obligations, and several defects are independently fatal. The expanded ER006–ER012 hierarchy remains controlling pending a formally reopened joint audit that incorporates ER013.

## A. Invariant-graph proof: useful content

1. Correctly replaces the history cube by a compact finite-dimensional embedded invariant manifold.
2. Explicitly requires complete base trajectories on the manifold.
3. Includes binding-transverse directions and slack directions in the stable bundle.
4. Recognizes the need for tangent/normal domination.
5. Separates persistence of an embedding from vertical projection and works over the perturbed projected base.
6. Attempts quantitative `C^1` control and attraction rather than merely asserting them.

## B. Invariant-graph proof: live defects

### B1. Invalid phase-space differential-coordinate reduction

An RFDE semiflow on `C([−tau,0],R^d)` is not generally a classical ODE generated by a smooth vector field on that Banach phase space; its generator is unbounded and solution histories are not differentiable at arbitrary initial histories. A tubular chart does not by itself produce the displayed equations `dot theta` and `dot w = L_s(theta) w_t + ...`. This reduction requires a solution-manifold/sun-star framework and proof that all coordinate and variation-of-constants operations are legitimate.

### B2. Unsupported tubular/bump construction

A compact finite-dimensional split submanifold can have suitable local bundle charts under additional hypotheses, but the asserted global “perpendicular” tubular chart is not automatic from the citation as written. More seriously, `X=C([−tau,0],R^m)` with the supremum norm does not generally admit the claimed `C^infty` radial bump `chi(||w||/delta)`; the supremum norm is not smooth, and many `C(K)` spaces lack smooth bump functions. The globalization step is therefore invalid.

### B3. False cutoff bound

Before cutoff the remainder is `O(delta^2+epsilon)`. Cutting it off on `||w||<=2delta` does not make its global supremum `O(epsilon)` for fixed `delta`; an `O(delta^2)` term remains. The bound used to derive `||sigma_epsilon||=O(epsilon)` is false without a different decomposition or an epsilon-dependent cutoff, which would create other uniformity issues.

### B4. Cocycle evaluated along the wrong trajectories

The unperturbed stable evolution `S(t,s)` is defined along unperturbed base orbits. ER013 evaluates it along the section-dependent perturbed trajectories `Theta_sigma`, which are generally not unperturbed orbits. No such cocycle, its base regularity, or its variation-of-constants identity is constructed. This invalidates the Lyapunov–Perron operator and the invariance calculation.

### B5. Contraction estimate is algebraically wrong

For two perturbed base equations sharing the endpoint at time zero, their backward separation generally grows like `exp(alpha |s|)`; it does not decay as ER013’s bound claims. `beta` does not enter the base-distance Gronwall equation and cannot appear in its denominator at that stage. Domination appears only after integrating backward base growth against forward stable decay. The stated contraction constant is therefore not derived. The proof also omits dependence of `S` and the nonlinearities on the base point.

### B6. No proof that the section class is invariant

Only a uniform `C^0` bound is attempted. ER013 does not prove that the graph operator preserves the prescribed Lipschitz constant `K`, even though contraction is claimed on that set. Continuity/Lipschitz dependence across a moving bundle also requires charts or a connection.

### B7. Circular/incomplete `C^1` argument

The differentiated operator omits derivatives of the stable cocycle, moving bundle, section-dependent base flow, and the occurrence of `D sigma` inside that base flow. It treats `S` as base-independent. Formal differentiation is not a valid application of the fiber-contraction theorem without first constructing the correct joint transform and proving its regularity.

### B8. Projection injectivity does not follow from immersion

Small derivative implies an immersion, not global injectivity. The sentence “compact manifold without boundary, hence injective immersion” is false because injectivity was never proved. A separate theorem that sufficiently small `C^1` perturbations of a compact embedding remain embeddings could repair this step, but it must be invoked and its closeness established in the actual chart.

### B9. Attraction is assumed, not proved

The proof chooses an asymptotic-phase base orbit without constructing it and then cites a Bates–Lu–Zeng result. This is neither self-contained nor shown to match the hypotheses. The bibliographic identification is also suspect. Attraction/stable foliation remains an exact-theorem obligation.

### B10. Hypotheses do not support the theorem as stated

The theorem omits the exact semiflow smoothness, perturbation topology, bounded geometry/tubular projections, base dependence of the stable cocycle, and theorem-matched conorm conditions. The scalar `beta>alpha` is not a substitute for the exact inverse-tangent/conorm bunching of the selected persistence theorem.

## C. Nonlinear-Hopf proof: useful content

1. Raises regularity to at least cubic order and explicitly introduces nonlinear nondegeneracy.
2. Uses a full-system equilibrium and does not depend on the invariant graph.
3. Correctly expects simple eigenvectors, center data, and the first Lyapunov coefficient to vary continuously under a properly smooth perturbation.
4. Correctly expects criticality to persist when a valid uncoupled first Lyapunov coefficient is bounded away from zero.

## D. Nonlinear-Hopf proof: live defects

### D1. Stability assumptions are missing

“No other imaginary spectrum” and “no slack imaginary spectrum” permit unstable roots. Yet the stability proof asserts that every noncenter root lies in a strictly stable half-plane and invokes `beta_x`, which is not defined in the Hopf hypotheses. Full-space orbital asymptotic stability is false without explicitly assuming all complementary spectrum is stable. An unstable complementary mode would make the bifurcating orbit unstable regardless of the cubic center coefficient.

### D2. Fixed-delay phase space is not addressed

If `mu` is the delay, the phase space changes with `mu`. Smooth parameter dependence and center-manifold calculations require rescaling to a fixed interval or another precise fixed-space formulation.

### D3. Adjoint/eigenvector perturbation is asserted too casually

The RFDE adjoint and Hale pairing require the proper dual/sun-star setting. Applying an unspecified Banach-space IFT to boundary-value problems does not establish the displayed `O(epsilon)` primal and dual eigenvector estimates, domains, normalization, or parameter smoothness. Spectral projections/resolvents should be used in an exact framework.

### D4. Center-manifold coefficient equations are not justified in `C`

The injection `X_0`, zero except at zero, is not an element of the continuous phase space. The equations involving `(I-Pi)X_0 Q` require the standard enlarged/sun-star formalism. ER013 writes them as ordinary equations in `B` without defining that framework.

### D5. The displayed first-Lyapunov formula is not established

Normalization conventions and combinatorial factors for RFDE Hopf coefficients matter. The formula is presented without deriving it from a matched RFDE normal-form theorem and may not equal the stated `c1` under the chosen `w20,w11` conventions. Calling its ingredients “rational expressions” is unjustified. The qualitative `c1=c1_x+O(epsilon)` is plausible only after a valid smooth normal-form construction.

### D6. Claimed remainders exceed `C^3` regularity

With only `k>=3`, ER013 cannot generally claim the displayed `O(|z|^4)` normal-form remainder, `O(a^4)` parameter/period expansions, or all stated differentiability orders. Cubic regularity supports the generic Hopf conclusion with weaker remainder statements; fourth-order expansions require stronger smoothness and theorem-specific bookkeeping.

### D7. “Unique family” requires qualifications

A Hopf family is unique only locally modulo phase/time translation and within the equivalence/parameterization supplied by the exact theorem. The response states uniqueness without these qualifications.

### D8. Slack-profile estimate is ambiguous/incomplete

The bound relative to the uncoupled slack equilibrium omits the `O(epsilon)` equilibrium shift. If `y_*` denotes the coupled equilibrium it must be written so; even then the `O(a^2+epsilon a)` estimate requires a valid parameter-dependent center-manifold calculation.

### D9. Reduction principle is invoked with unmet hypotheses

The proof calls the center manifold normally attracting and transfers stability, but the assumptions permit unstable complementary spectrum. It also cites a reduction theorem without matching phase-space, smoothness, or spectral hypotheses.

## Net claim status

- **Concrete A021 invariant graph:** still unproved.
- **Abstract conditional NHIM/graph persistence:** remains best stated through an exact Banach-semiflow theorem, not ER013’s LP proof.
- **Simple characteristic crossing:** retained.
- **Nonlinear Hopf branch:** conditionally available from an exact RFDE Hopf theorem after fixed-space parameterization, adequate smoothness, nonlinear nondegeneracy, and full complementary spectral assumptions.
- **Orbital asymptotic stability:** requires every noncenter mode stable plus the appropriate sign of the first Lyapunov coefficient and crossing direction.
- **Publication block:** reject as currently overstated.
