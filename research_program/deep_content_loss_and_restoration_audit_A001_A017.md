# 2. Overall judgment

## 2.1 No destructive loss

No legitimate content was destroyed. Immutable sources, corrected sources, inventories, and provenance remain available and tracked.

## 2.2 Real over-condensation exists

The manuscript integration is **architecturally sound but formally under-complete relative to the accepted source corpus**. The largest compression occurs in A001 and A002. Calling A002 the canonical restricted theorem spine while displaying only a small fraction of its theorem families creates a mismatch between the publication claim and visible mathematical support.

A second, smaller compression affects A013: its worked ledger was restored, but its accepted diagnostic applications were not.

A third compression affects A007: the corrected phase-space semantics were integrated, but its reusable failure taxonomy was not restored as a single operational table.

## 2.3 Correct publication solution

Restoring every proof to the main text would make the flagship unreadable and duplicate the corrected article sources. The non-loss and minimum-paper rules are best satisfied by three layers:

1. restore load-bearing bridges, counterexamples, and operational taxonomies in the flagship;
2. create one formal supplement containing the retained A001/A002 theorem library and selected A006/A007/A010 results;
3. keep A011 and A012 as their conditional/separate publication objects, with A004/A005 domain equations and A013 data documentation in domain/evidence supplements.

# 3. Immediate flagship restorations

## R1 — Recovery impossibility and emergency-envelope necessity

**Sources:** A001 Propositions/definitions 3.1–3.4.  
**Current coverage:** capture basins and the official assessment map are defined, but the decisive logical result is not stated.

Restore:

> If a state in the nominal safe set can reach the viability kernel while remaining in that same safe set, concatenation with a viable continuation makes the initial state viable. Therefore a state in `safe set \ kernel` cannot recover to the kernel without leaving the nominal safe set; nontrivial recovery requires an authorized emergency envelope or a changed specification.

This is a load-bearing bridge between Operator I recovery and transition-relaxation semantics.

## R2 — Observation-fibre impossibility and hidden-mode counterexample

**Sources:** A001 Theorems 4.2/4.7 and Example 4.1; A002 observation-fibre criterion.  
**Current coverage:** common-safe prescriptions are defined, but there is no concrete counterexample and no exact fibre criterion.

Restore:

- exact certification is impossible when one observation fibre contains both safe and unsafe latent states that require incompatible verdicts/actions;
- the two-mode boundary example `dot z = θu`, `θ∈{-1,+1}`, shows how every state can be individually viable while the belief is nonviable.

This would make epistemic failure non-vacuous and testable.

## R3 — Observer-error safety erosion

**Sources:** A001 Theorems 4.4/4.9 and eroded-kernel proposition; A006 observer margin.  
**Current coverage:** selector and information semantics are present, but the quantitative observer-to-safety bridge is absent.

Restore a conditional margin statement: a strict inward barrier margin `η_j` survives bounded estimation/implementation error `ē` when `L_j ē ≤ η_j`, yielding an eroded inner safe set under the declared solution assumptions.

## R4 — Substitution theorem pair

**Sources:** A001 dimensionally correct CES thresholds; A002 Farkas pathway-infeasibility certificate; A013 directional support gap.  
**Current coverage:** substitution checklist and support fraction exist, but the two principal formal certificates are absent.

Restore together:

- CES results distinguish finite feasible substitution from unbounded substitution and require dimensions/scales;
- Farkas dual multipliers certify infeasibility of a declared linear pathway system;
- neither proves an application pathway without domain data.

This is a central bridge between strong sustainability and operational substitution.

## R5 — Composition counterexamples required by the manuscript’s own theorem

**Sources:** A001 coupling-destruction and coupling-rescue examples; shared-resource infeasibility obligation.  
**Current coverage:** §12.8 requires three examples but does not provide them.

Restore at least one explicit example of:

1. coupling destroying factorwise viability;
2. coupling rescuing a subsystem nonviable in isolation;
3. shared source/sink or actuator capacity making individually feasible local controls jointly infeasible.

This is the clearest accidental incompleteness in the current main text because the text explicitly promises the examples.

## R6 — Projectability and model-map criterion

**Source:** A002 exact projectability theorem and fibre obstruction.  
**Current coverage:** the manuscript requires model maps but supplies no exact criterion.

Restore the condition that an exact autonomous reduced vector field exists only when projected derivatives agree on every fibre of the projection; otherwise the reduction requires memory, approximation, or a different state.

This directly protects the groundwater one-/two-pool comparison and sampled/continuous model bridges.

## R7 — Failure taxonomy

**Current coverage:** assessment statuses exist, but failure causes are dispersed.

Restore one table distinguishing:

- material inconsistency;

- authority infeasibility;
- implementation infeasibility;
- temporal infeasibility;
- recovery failure;

## R8 — Intergenerational impossibility and discounting counterexample

**Source:** A001 Theorems 14.2–14.3.  
**Current coverage:** horizon typing and liabilities are present, but the source’s strongest intergenerational results are absent.

Restore:

- nested closed generation-specific sets with empty intersection are incompatible with an indefinitely viable path confined to a compact set;
- discounted welfare can prefer a finite high-consumption pulse that exits over a positive viable path, showing that discounted welfare and viability are not equivalent.

## R9 — Dynamics-aware viability value and intervention cost

**Source:** A001 Definitions 15.1–15.2.  
**Current coverage:** geometric slack and disturbance robustness are present, but dynamics-aware finite-horizon value and intervention-cost objects are absent.

Restore as diagnostics with attainment caveats. In particular, a supremal value of zero does not imply a safe policy unless the supremum is attained or the viability closure is used.

## R10 — A013 accepted diagnostic applications

**Source:** A013 groundwater, phosphate, and fisheries examples.  
**Current coverage:** the accounting theorem core is present; accepted application values are omitted.

Worth restoring, with exact labels:

- G3P anomaly-persistence indices are statistical anomaly ratios, not aquifer exhaustion forecasts;
- `74,000 Mt / 240,000 kt yr⁻¹ ≈ 309 yr` is a reserve-life ratio, not a physical exhaustion prediction;
- `Θ_F=log(SSB/B_lim)/F` is a removals-only pressure time, not net biomass depletion.

These examples teach the diagnostic distinctions better than abstract warnings alone.
