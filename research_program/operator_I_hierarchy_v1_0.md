# Operator I Judgment Hierarchy — Version 1.0

## Status

This document fixes the aligned quantifiers and comparison rules for within-architecture sustainability judgments under `TCS-1.0`. It resolves conceptual hierarchy ambiguity; it does not certify every A001/A002 theorem that instantiates the hierarchy.

## Common signature

Fix

\[
\Xi=(\Omega,\mathfrak A_q,H,K,\mathbb P,\mathbb W,\mathfrak S),
\]

where `H` is the horizon, `K=K_{q,Omega}` the admissible set, `P` the causal policy class, `W` the disturbance/model class, and `S` the declared solution concept. Every comparison below requires the same signature unless a map is explicitly supplied.

Let `Sol(z,pi,w)` be the set of trajectories allowed by the solution concept. Empty solution sets never make a safety statement true: admissible policies must generate at least one solution for every declared disturbance.

## Judgments

### Controlled viability

\[
z\in\operatorname{CViab}(\Xi)
\iff
\exists\pi\in\mathbb P\;\exists w\in\mathbb W\;\exists\phi\in\operatorname{Sol}(z,\pi,w):
\phi([0,H])\subseteq K.
\]

When no disturbance exists, omit `w`. This is the existential trajectory question; it must not be confused with robust safety.

### Robust viability

\[
z\in\operatorname{RViab}(\Xi)
\iff
\exists\pi\in\mathbb P\;\forall w\in\mathbb W\;\forall\phi\in\operatorname{Sol}(z,\pi,w):
\phi([0,H])\subseteq K.
\]

The same causal policy acts against all declared disturbances. A disturbance-observing policy belongs to a different policy class.

### Fixed-policy robust safety

For a specified `pi_0`,

\[
z\in\operatorname{Safe}_{\pi_0}(\Xi)
\iff
\forall w\in\mathbb W\;\forall\phi\in\operatorname{Sol}(z,\pi_0,w):
\phi([0,H])\subseteq K.
\]

### Epistemic viability

Let `b` be an information/history state generated by the declared observation and filter. With observation-based policy class `P^obs`,

\[
b\in\operatorname{EViab}(\Xi_B)
\iff
\exists\pi\in\mathbb P^{obs}\;\forall z_0\in\operatorname{Compat}(b)
\;\forall w\;\forall\phi:
\phi([0,H])\subseteq K.
\]

The object lives on information/history space. Its physical projection is not automatically a physical-state kernel.

### Institutional viability

Let `I_q` map prescriptions and institutional states to a realized-action correspondence. Institutional viability is epistemic/robust viability with policies restricted to prescriptions whose every declared implementation branch remains admissible and safe:

\[
b\in\operatorname{IViab}(\Xi_I)
\iff
\exists\pi^{cmd}\;\forall u^{real}\in \mathsf I_q(\pi^{cmd},b)
\;\forall w\;\forall\phi:
\phi([0,H])\subseteq K.
\]

Authority, budget, latency, compliance, enforcement, and allocation restrictions are part of the signature, not narrative modifiers.

### Chance viability

Under an explicit filtered probability space and law,

\[
z\in\operatorname{PViab}_p(\Xi)
\iff
\exists\pi\in\mathbb P:
\Pr^\pi_z\{\phi([0,H])\subseteq K\}\ge p.
\]

Pointwise-in-time probability is not pathwise chance safety.

### Recoverability

For target `G⊂K` and emergency envelope `E⊃K`,

\[
z\in\operatorname{Capt}_{E,H}(G)
\]

means that one declared policy keeps trajectories in `E` and reaches `G` by `H`, robustly or existentially according to the recorded quantifiers. Recovery is not an inclusion level in the viability ladder; it is a reach-avoid-maintain judgment with its own target and envelope.

## Valid implication and monotonicity propositions

### Proposition 1 — robust implies controlled

If `W` is nonempty and all other signature fields are identical,

\[
\operatorname{RViab}(\Xi)\subseteq\operatorname{CViab}(\Xi).
\]

**Proof.** A robust witness policy is safe for every `w`; choose any declared `w` and any generated solution. ∎

### Proposition 2 — fixed-policy safety embeds in robust viability

If `pi_0∈P`,

\[
\operatorname{Safe}_{\pi_0}(\Xi)\subseteq\operatorname{RViab}(\Xi).
\]

**Proof.** Use `pi_0` as the existential policy witness. ∎

### Proposition 3 — policy/action expansion

For aligned signatures with `P_1⊂P_2`,

\[
\operatorname{RViab}(\Xi_1)\subseteq\operatorname{RViab}(\Xi_2),
\]

and similarly for controlled and chance viability. This is existential-policy monotonicity. It does not imply that every newly admitted action is safe.

### Proposition 4 — disturbance expansion

For `W_1⊂W_2` and otherwise aligned signatures,

\[
\operatorname{RViab}(\Xi_{W_2})\subseteq\operatorname{RViab}(\Xi_{W_1}).
\]

### Proposition 5 — safe-set expansion

For `K_1⊂K_2` and otherwise aligned signatures,

\[
\operatorname{RViab}(K_1)\subseteq\operatorname{RViab}(K_2).
\]

### Proposition 6 — information refinement

Suppose information structure `I_1` refines `I_2` through a causal forgetting map, every `I_2`-policy can be lifted to an `I_1`-policy by ignoring extra information, compatible-state updates commute with the map, and physical/policy/disturbance signatures align. Then the refined structure cannot reduce epistemic viability after comparison through the induced information-state map.

**Proof.** Lift any coarser-information witness policy and ignore the additional signal. Commuting compatible-state updates expose no additional physical branch beyond those already handled by the coarse witness. ∎

Without the policy-lifting and state-comparison maps, “more information enlarges viability” is not a well-typed inclusion statement.

### Proposition 7 — implementation restriction

If institution `I_1` permits a subset of the realized-action/policy branches permitted by `I_2`, with identical state, information, disturbance, and solution signatures, existential institutional viability is monotone under the availability of additional implementable safe policies. If the semantics instead require safety for **every** permitted implementation branch, enlarging the implementation correspondence can shrink safety. The quantifier must be recorded.

### Proposition 8 — robust-to-chance implication

If the stochastic law is supported on the robust disturbance class and a robust policy is measurable/admissible under that law, then

\[
\operatorname{RViab}(\Xi)\subseteq\operatorname{PViab}_1(\Xi).
\]

No converse holds in general, and no implication is available when the stochastic support exceeds the robust class.

## Non-implications and type errors

1. Physical viability does not imply epistemic viability: compatible states may require conflicting actions.
2. Epistemic viability cannot be intersected with a physical-state kernel without a projection or prior-domain map.
3. Chance safety at each time does not imply pathwise chance safety.
4. Larger raw action sets do not guarantee larger institutional viability if authority or implementation changes simultaneously.
5. Stability does not imply viability, and viability does not imply asymptotic stability.
6. Intersections of separate subsystem kernels do not prove joint viability under coupling/shared controls.
7. Recovery outside `K` does not contradict nonviability in `K`; the envelope and target differ.
8. Finite-horizon viability does not imply infinite-horizon viability without a closure/compactness argument.

## Canonical hierarchy graph

```text
fixed-policy robust safety --(policy admitted)--> robust viability --> controlled viability
                                             |
                                             +--(supported law)--> chance viability at p=1

physical robust viability --(observation/filter lift)--> epistemic viability
                           --(implementation restriction)--> institutional viability

recoverability = separate reach-avoid-maintain operator, not a ladder node
```

The arrows involving epistemic and institutional objects denote construction/mapping, not literal subset inclusions across different state spaces.

## Publication consequence

Paper 2 may present this hierarchy and the restricted theorems that instantiate it. Application papers must retain model-specific observation, implementation, and solution assumptions. No paper may cite the hierarchy as proof that its compatible-state filter, implementation map, or closed-loop dynamics exists.
