## Executive Summary

This manuscript presents an original, mathematically ambitious, and conceptually timely framework that formalises the **"productivity illusion"** in human–environment systems by separating stock liquidation from flow yield under dual delays. The distinction between the **surplus-production (gross-harvest)** paradigm and the **deficit-driven** formulation is a major theoretical contribution.

However, a thorough line-level audit reveals several **critical mathematical errors**, **dimensional inconsistencies**, **internal contradictions between sections**, **dangling draft artifacts**, and **unreconciled notation sets**. ## 1. Critical Mathematical & Dimensional Flaws

### 1.1 Dimensional Inconsistency in Debt $D$ and the Degradation Law (Table 2.1 & Eq. 7)
* **The Flaw:** In Table 2.1 and Section 2.1, the units of accumulated ecological debt $D$ are declared as $\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}$, and $\alpha$ is declared as $(\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr})^{-1}$. The text justifies this by stating:
  > *"The accumulated ecological debt $D$ is their time-integral over the deficit, $D = \int [E - B]_+ dt$, and therefore carries $\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}$ (global-hectare-years) — a stock of past overshoot, not a rate."*
* **Mathematical Inconsistency:** Eq. (7) is written as:
  $$\frac{dD}{dt} = [E - B]_+ - \eta D$$
  Section 2.1 explicitly states that $E$ and $B$ are annual rates with units $\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}^{-1}$. Integrating an annual flow $(\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}^{-1})$ with respect to time $(\text{yr})$ yields:
  $$[D] = \left[\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}^{-1}\right] \times [\text{yr}] = \mathbf{gha}$$
  Integrating a rate with units $\text{gha}/\text{yr}$ over years does **not** produce $\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}$; it produces $\text{gha}$. Furthermore, in Eq. (7), the left-hand side $\frac{dD}{dt}$ has units $[D]/\text{yr}$. If $[D] = \text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}$, then $\frac{dD}{dt}$ has units $\text{gha}$, which is incommensurable with $[E - B]_+$, whose units are $\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}^{-1}$.
* **Correction:**
  1. Fix the unit of $D$ in Table 2.1 and throughout the text to $\mathbf{gha}$ (global hectares of accumulated capital deficit).
  2. Fix the unit of $\alpha$ to $\mathbf{gha^{-1}}$ so that the exponent $\alpha D$ in $e^{-\alpha D}$ is dimensionless.
  3. Correct the text in Section 2.1 to read:
     > *"Because $E$ and $B$ are per-annum rates ($\text{gha}\cdot\text{yr}^{-1}$), their time integral $D = \int [E - B]_+ dt$ carries units of $\text{gha}$—a stock of cumulative net liquidation, while $\eta$ ($\text{yr}^{-1}$) governs its annual rate of natural remediation."*

### 1.2 Mathematical Contradiction in Section 4.3 ($\det = 0$ vs. $\det > 0$)
* **The Flaw:** In Section 4.3, two adjacent paragraphs directly contradict one another regarding the determinant of the Jacobian at the interior state:
  * **Paragraph 2 states:**
    $$\det = r\cdot\rho\cdot A^*/A_{max} > 0 \quad (\text{never a saddle}) \implies \text{zero-delay condition is } a_{11} < r \ (\text{NOT } a_{11} < 0)$$
  * **Paragraph 3 states:**
    $$-a_E a_4 = r\cdot S \implies D(0) = 0 \text{ on the whole family — the neutral continuum, so there is no isolated interior attractor.}$$
* **Mathematical Analysis:** The characteristic equation at zero delay is $\det(sI - J) = s^2 - \text{tr}(J)s + \det(J) = 0$. 
  Evaluating $D(s; 0, 0) = (s - S)(s + r) + rS = s^2 - (S - r)s = 0$.
  Evaluating this at $s = 0$ gives $D(0) = \det(-J) = \det(J) \equiv 0$.
  The determinant is identically zero along the neutral continuum $P^* = B(A^*)/e$. The formula $\det = r\rho A^*/A_{max} > 0$ belongs strictly to the **gross-harvest (Schaefer) model** where $K$ is fixed or independent of $A$. Leaving that formula in Section 4.3 is a copy-paste error from an earlier draft that confuses the deficit-driven model with the gross-harvest model.
* **Correction:** Delete `det = r·ρ·A*/A_max > 0 (never a saddle)` entirely from Section 4.3. State clearly:
  > *"Because the interior equilibria form a one-parameter neutral continuum $P^* = B(A^*)/e$, the Jacobian determinant is identically zero ($\det J = 0$), yielding a trivial root $\lambda_1 = 0$. The stability of the non-neutral transversal mode is governed entirely by the trace, yielding $\lambda_2 = S - r = G'(A^*) + b/b_G - r$. Hence, the zero-delay condition for stability of this transversal mode is $S < r$ (i.e., $a_{11} < r$)."*

### 1.5 Coupled Equilibrium Inconsistency Under Reservation $\sigma < 1$ (Sections 2.2, 4.3, & 9)
* **The Flaw:** Section 2.2 states:
  > *"Under a reservation $\sigma < 1$ the equilibrium locus shifts to $P = (\sigma b A + b_G G(A))/e < B/e$..."*
* **Mathematical Analysis:** Look at Eq. (5) and Eq. (6) in Section 2.2:
  $$\frac{dP}{dt} = r P \left(1 - \frac{P}{K(t-\tau_p)}\right), \quad K = \frac{B}{e} = \frac{bA + b_G G(A)}{e}$$
  If $P = \frac{\sigma b A + b_G G(A)}{e}$ and $\sigma < 1$, then $P < K$.
  Consequently, $\frac{dP}{dt} = r P (1 - P/K) > 0$. The population will not remain at $P$; it will grow until $P = K = B/e$. Once $P = B/e$, the footprint is $E = B = bA + b_G G(A)$.
  Substituting this $E$ into Eq. (4) gives:
  $$\frac{dA}{dt} = G(A) - \frac{[B - \sigma b A]_+}{b_G} = G(A) - \frac{(1-\sigma)bA + b_G G(A)}{b_G} = -\frac{(1-\sigma)bA}{b_G} < 0$$
  Therefore, $P = (\sigma b A + b_G G(A))/e$ is **not** an equilibrium of the coupled system $(A, P)$. It is merely the $A$-nullcline. The coupled system will suffer inevitable collapse unless $K$ itself is capped at $\frac{\sigma b A + b_G G(A)}{e}$ (as belatedly recognized in Section 9: "Corrected cap: $K = 0.5 B/e$").
* **Correction:** State explicitly in Section 2.2 and Section 4.3 that under $\sigma < 1$, this locus is the **stock-preservation nullcline** ($dA/dt = 0$), and that a stationary population requires an accompanying institutional cap on carrying capacity:
  $$K_\sigma = \frac{\sigma b A + b_G G(A)}{e}$$

### 2.4 Ambiguity in the Operating Trigger: $B$ vs. $\tilde{B}$

## 1. Critical inconsistencies

### 1.1 `det > 0` contradicts the neutral continuum

§4.3 states both:

- `det = r·ρ·A*/A_max > 0` (never a saddle)
- `D(0) = 0` on the whole family `P = B(A)/e` (no isolated interior attractor)

These cannot both be true. A one-parameter family of equilibria forces a singular Jacobian. Direct linearisation of (4)–(5) in the deficit region with `σ = 1` gives `det = 0`, eigenvalues `{0, a₁₁ − r}`. The condition `a₁₁ < r` is exactly the condition that the non-neutral eigenvalue be negative, which is consistent with `det = 0` and inconsistent with `det > 0`.

The `det > 0` line is leftover from the **gross-harvest** formulation (unique interior attractor) and was not removed when the paper switched to the deficit-driven family. It should be deleted.

### 1.2 `ψ ⋚ 1/2` is not equivalent to `b_G ρ ⋚ b`

§4.1: “the single testable condition `b_G ρ ⋚ b` (equivalently `ψ ⋚ 1/2`)”.

From the paper’s own closed form in §4.5,

\[
\psi^* = \frac{2}{1 + b_G\rho/b}.
\]

- `b_G ρ = b` ⇒ `ψ* = 1`, not `1/2`
- `ψ* = 1/2` ⇒ `b_G ρ/b = 3`, not `1`

The regime boundary that decides whether an interior MSY exists is `b_G ρ = b` (`ψ* = 1`, the critical point sliding off `[0, A_max]`). The value `ψ = 1/2` is a different, much more capital-heavy point. The word “equivalently” is false. The regime table’s shorthand `b_G ρ > b` (`ψ → 0`) is also misleading: just above the boundary `ψ*` is just below 1, and `ψ → 0` only as `b_G ρ/b → ∞`. Interior MSY and low flow-share are distinct conditions; the paper collapses them.

### 1.3 Recover fraction for `τ_g ∈ [20, 60]` is stated twice, incompatibly

§8 table: recover fraction `0.0529` for the whole bin `τ_g = 20–60`.

§12.2 (and the fine-grid table): deep minimum at `τ_g = 30` (`≈ 0.03`), then **re-opening** `≈ 0.21` (40), `≈ 0.30` (50), `≈ 0.32` (60), declared “real, not a grid artifact”.

If the re-opening is real, Prediction 6 (“collapse whenever the regeneration lag exceeds ≈ 20 yr”) is false as stated, and the claim that “the field-supported band lies entirely in the collapse regime” is false for the extended 5–60 yr band. If the re-opening is only at `τ_p = 0`, the §8 table must not bin 20–60 together and §12.2 must say so. This is a load-bearing numerical result; it cannot remain in both forms.

### 1.4 “Three delayed states” versus the delay structure already declared

§5: “The full system is three delayed states `(A, P, D)`.”

§2.2: delays enter **only** in `G(A(t − τ_g))` and `K(t − τ_p)`; “Debt formation (7) and harvest act on **current** values.”

`D` is a state, not a delayed state. The §5 bullet is wrong and undoes a distinction the model section was careful to make.

### 1.5 `b = B/A` contradicts equation (2)

§2.1: “`b = B/A` is definable and the decomposition `B = b·A + b_G·G(A)` is identifiable.”

From (2), `B/A = b + b_G G(A)/A ≠ b`. The identifiability point is right (physical hectares, not gha, are what make a yield coefficient available). The formula `b = B/A` is not. Write: a yield coefficient `b` is definable, hence the two-term split is identifiable.

### 1.6 Two different reservation policies are treated as one

| Location | What `σ` actually does |
|---|---|
| Eq. (4) | liquidation threshold `σ b A` (flow yield only) |
| §2.2 text | “reserves a share `(1−σ)B`”; equilibrium `P = (σ b A + b_G G(A))/e` |
| §9 | “Cap the human-available flow at `σ B`”; `K = 0.5 B/e` |

Capping population at `σ B/e` is not the same as changing the harvest switch in (4), and `σ B` is not `σ b A`. Half-Earth-as-area-set-aside would be closer to reducing `A_max` (or the harvested fraction of area) than either of these. Pick one operator, write it in (4)–(6), and stop referring to the others as “the reservation”.

## 2. Units and parameters: the headline instability may be an artefact of `b_G`

This is the most serious **scientific** problem, not just an editorial one.

- Units paragraph and §2.1: `b_G` has units `gha·ha⁻¹`.
- §4.1 and §4.3: `γ = 1/b_G = 1/V` with `V ≈ 20–100 yr`, and “`b_G` the standing-stock value (20–100 yr)”.
- Baseline: `b_G = 0.8`, `b = 0.5` ⇒ turnover `b_G/b = 1.6 yr`, not 20–100.
- The same baseline gives `b/b_G = 0.625 yr⁻¹`, which **is** the reported leading eigenvalue `Re λ ≈ +0.62`.

So the paper’s own physical story (`V = 20–100 yr`) would require `b_G ∼ b V ∼ 10–50` in these units, not `0.8`. Under that interpretation:

- `S = b/b_G + G'(A*)` would be `O(0.01–0.05)`, comparable to `r = 0.02`, and the sufficient condition `S > r` for a delay-independent positive real root can fail;
- `ν = b/(b_G ρ)` would be `O(0.1–2)` as the prose claims, whereas baseline `ν = 12.5`;
- liquidation of the entire scaled stock in `O(1)` year of overshoot would no longer be the default.

The structural “vicious cycle, no Hopf, `Re λ ≈ +0.62` for every delay” is therefore **not** shown to survive the paper’s own meaning of `b_G`. Either:

1. treat `A` as non-dimensional and `b_G = 0.8` as a scaled coefficient, and **drop** the 20–100 yr identification; or
2. set `b_G` from a turnover time and **recompute** the spectrum, the cliff, and the recover fractions.

Until that is done, Prediction 1, the comparison table in §8, and the claim that collapse “needs no oscillatory mechanism” are all conditional on an unphysical salvage value. §12.3’s `b_G` sweep (0.4–1.2) does not cover the physically motivated decade.

Related unit slip: `D = ∫[E−B]₊ dt` in §2.1 ignores `η` in (7). Say “modulo repayment” or “when `η = 0`”.

## 3. Two formulations mixed in one narrative

The comparison table in §8 is the most useful object in the paper; the rest of the text does not live up to it. Gross-harvest objects keep being quoted as if they applied to Eq. (4):

- `τ_g* = 85.4 yr`, `τ_p* ≈ 231 yr`, `χ = 1`, `π/(2r) = 78.5 yr`
- the `χ`/`Λ` “which-lag” classification and the “baseline sits at a knife-edge because `ρ = 3q`”
- Prediction 2 (period `≈ 4×` dominant lag) and Prediction 1’s “which lag destabilises”

All of these belong to a model the paper says it is **not** using. On S0 they are explicit that there is no imaginary-axis crossing. Keeping the knife-edge discussion while also saying “this Hopf classification is not realised” forces the reader to hold two papers in their head.

**Recommendation:** split every analytic statement with a one-letter tag (`G` vs `D`, or “gross-harvest” vs “deficit-driven”), and move the entire `χ` classification to an appendix / “what we are not claiming”. Predictions 1–2 should be rewritten as negative results on S0, not as Hopf predictions.

Regime mapping is tangled in the same way. §4.1 assigns “the stability classification of §4.3” to the **capital-dominated** regime, but the numerical `Re λ ≈ +0.62` is computed at **baseline flow-dominated** `A* = 0.8`. At interior MSY one has `dB/dA = 0` ⇒ `a₁₁ = 0` ⇒ `S = 0 < r`, so the sufficient condition for a delay-independent unstable real root **fails** exactly at MSY. The vicious-cycle eigenvalue is an off-MSY / high-`b/b_G` fact, not a capital-regime theorem.

## 4. `B` versus `B̃`: the monitoring thesis is stated more cleanly than the equations allow

Dynamics in deficit (`σ = 1`):

\[
\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{\tilde B - E}{b_G}, \quad \tilde B = bA + b_G G(A(t-\tau_g)).
\]

The observed ratio is `R_B = E/B` with `B = bA + b_G G(A(t))`. For `τ_g > 0`, `B ≠ B̃`, so “stock declines iff `R_B > 1`” is true only at zero delay. The paper knows this (§4.5 separator degradation, §12.1 silent collapse 36.5 %), but §4.5 still writes “`dA/dt < 0 ⟺ E > B̃`, so the stock declines iff footprint exceeds total biocapacity” and immediately calls `R_B = 1` the trigger.

Fix the language:

- zero delay: `R_B = 1` is the balance point and the separator;
- long `τ_g`: `R_B` is neither sufficient nor, for a large minority of collapsing ICs, even necessary.

That is already Prediction 8; it should govern §4.5 rather than being appended to it.

A deeper measurement issue, only half-facing the reader: GFN biocapacity is essentially yield × area (closer to `bA` than to `bA + b_G G(A)`), and §2.1 admits the NFA decomposition is not identifiable. Advising a policy-maker to “watch `R_B` not `R_A`” therefore needs an operational series for the surplus-production term. Without it the recommended ratio is a model object, not a monitor.

## 6. Conceptual slips in the orchard accounting

Three distinct things are repeatedly fused:

1. flow yield `bA` (fruit)
2. surplus production `b_G G(A)` (increment / MSY harvest)
3. standing stock `A` (the trees)

Eq. (2) puts (1)+(2) into `B`. Eq. (4) subtracts harvest above (1) from (3) **net of** (2). So:

- `bA < E < B` does **not** “leave capital growth untouched” (§4.5); it harvests part of the increment and the stock still rises;
- capital growth is **not** “removable only by liquidating the base” (intro / §3); that phrase describes (3), not (2);
- true liquidation (`dA/dt < 0`) is `E > B̃`, i.e. taking more than (1)+(2).

The intro’s fruit/trees story only matches the **flow-dominated** limit (`ψ → 1`, baseline). That is also the only regime in which most of the numerics live. The paper should say so up front: the orchard metaphor is the baseline; interior MSY/fold/Scheffer language is a different regime, largely un-numericked.

Minor but related: “conservation is a property of the incidence structure” (§2.2). `D` is a damage accumulator, `ηD` does not return mass to `A`, and `b = (b₀+T_b)e^{-αD}` is not a transfer. Do not claim compartmental conservation.

`K ≥ b A_ext/e > 0` fails once `b → 0` through (8). §8’s `K_min` clamp is necessary; it should appear in §2.2.

## 7. Over-claimed or underspecified results

- **“Unboundedly negative `d ln A/dt`”** (§6). `A ≥ A_ext` (or 0). The mask is transient because a bounded logistic `T_b` cannot offset a one-way stock loss of order `A_0 − A_ext`, not because the loss is unbounded. Also `d ln B/dt = d ln b/dt + d ln A/dt` assumes `B = bA` and drops `b_G G(A)`.
- **Prediction 4** (`τ_e` vs `f`) has no corresponding equation.
- **Prediction 3** (“computable `t_peak`”) is a numerical observation on one configuration, not a formula.
- **Prediction 5** (mask more visible at high `ψ`) is not evidenced in the provided text; the demo is baseline only.
- **“Theorem”** that debt compounds while technology saturates: true for multiplicative (8) plus bounded `T_b`; false as a model-independent theorem. One sentence of algebra is enough; the word does not help.
- **May (1973) “inversion”** is rhetoric. May’s result is about random high-dimensional Jacobians. Instability of a 2–3 D deficit model does not invert it.
- **§12.3 `Re λ` independent of `ρ`**. At `(τ_g, τ_p) = (30, 25)` this is the long-delay approximation: `e^{-s τ}` kills the `G'` terms and `λ ≈ a₃ = b/b_G`. It is not evidence that the instability is “structural in `ρ`” at short delay (zero-delay root is `S − r = b/b_G + G' − r`, which **does** move with `ρ`). State the delay at which the table is computed.
- **Rescue set “measure-zero”**. On the 208-point grid, 5.3 % is ~11 cells, all at `A_0 = A_max`. That is “grid-measure small,” not Lebesgue measure zero in the continuum. If 1.19 and 1.21 both collapse, the set may indeed be a thin tongue around `A_max`; show the boundary curve (as §12.2 itself requests) rather than the phrase.
- **Scenario B/C “inverse orchard”** and **Scenario E “no illusion”**: scenarios are never defined in this text.
- **Silent-collapse 36.5 % (72/197)** is useful and consistent with 94.7 % collapse. Keep it; make it a figure.

## 10. What is worth keeping (and how to protect it)

The paper has a real core:

1. Deficit accounting (`dA/dt = (B̃ − E)/b_G`) rather than gross harvest, with a family of balances instead of an interior attractor.
2. Two observable ratios, `R_B` (trigger at zero delay) vs `R_A` (leading, regime-dependent, not causal), with `ψ` as the gap.
3. A delay-driven basin crisis at `τ_g ∼ 18–20 yr` with **no** CSD, distinct from a capital-regime `E`-fold at MSY.
4. A narrow, deficit-bounded `B`-up/`A`-down window, closed by the same technology that raises `K`.
5. Honest NFA limitations and the need for an `A`-proxy decomposition.

Those survive only if the manuscript:

- stops quoting gross-harvest Hopf numbers as if they were Eq. (4);
- repairs `det`, `ψ ⋚ 1/2`, `b = B/A`, “three delayed states,” `σ`’s three meanings, and the 20–60 recover table;
- either re-fits `b_G` to a 20–100 yr turnover or abandons that interpretation and re-scopes every “structural instability” claim;
- defines every symbol in §2.1;
- says, once, that numerics are flow-dominated baseline unless labelled otherwise.

Without the `b_G` repair in particular, the central dynamical story is not yet shown to be about orchards, forests, or footprints; it is shown to be about a scaled liquidation gain `b/b_G ≈ 0.63 yr⁻¹ ≫ r`.
