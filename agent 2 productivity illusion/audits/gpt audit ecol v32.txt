The manuscript has a strong conceptual core, but the central mathematical object is not yet internally consistent. The largest problems are not stylistic: several headline claims depend on equations that imply a different equilibrium structure, and the `σ < 1`, delay, ratio, and stability interpretations currently cannot all be true simultaneously.

## Highest-priority blockers

### 1. The Jacobian determinant contradicts the claimed neutral continuum

The most important mathematical inconsistency appears in §4.3.

You state

```text
det = r·ρ·A*/A_max > 0
```

and immediately afterward state

```text
D(0) = 0 on the whole family — the neutral continuum
```

Those cannot both hold. At zero delay, `D(0)` is the Jacobian determinant. More directly, in the deficit region with `σ = 1`:

\[
\dot A=G(A)+\frac{b}{b_G}A-\frac{e}{b_G}P,
\]

\[
\dot P=rP\left(1-\frac{P}{B(A)/e}\right).
\]

At an equilibrium \(P^*=B(A^*)/e\), define

\[
S=G'(A^*)+\frac{b}{b_G}
=\frac{B'(A^*)}{b_G}.
\]

The Jacobian is

\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
S & -e/b_G\\
rB'(A^*)/e & -r
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\det J
=
-rS+\frac{rB'(A^*)}{b_G}
=0.
\]

The zero determinant is exactly what the equilibrium continuum requires. The zero-delay eigenvalues are

\[
0,\qquad S-r,
\]

not a determinant-positive pair.

The expression

\[
r\rho A^*/A_{\max}
\]

appears to belong to a different formulation, probably the gross-harvest model. It must not be retained in the deficit-driven derivation.

**Required repair:** separate the two Jacobians explicitly. For the deficit-driven model, state:

\[
\det J=0,\qquad
\lambda_1=0,\qquad
\lambda_2=S-r.
\]

Then explain that the neutral eigenvalue is tangent to the equilibrium family and that instability concerns the transverse eigenvalue or the delayed characteristic roots.

---

### 2. The `σ < 1` equilibrium claim is incompatible with equation (5)

You state that under reservation,

\[
P=\frac{\sigma bA+b_GG(A)}{e}<\frac{B(A)}e.
\]

But equation (5) still uses

\[
K=\frac{B}{e}.
\]

At a positive population equilibrium, equation (5) requires

\[
P=K=\frac{B}{e}.
\]

The stock equation under `σ < 1` requires

\[
eP=\sigma bA+b_GG(A).
\]

These conditions are incompatible at an interior state whenever \(\sigma<1\) and \(bA>0\):

\[
\frac{B}{e}
\ne
\frac{\sigma bA+b_GG(A)}e.
\]

Thus the claimed shifted equilibrium locus does not follow from the stated equations.

You need to choose one of the following formulations:

1. Replace the population carrying capacity with an allocated capacity,
   \[
   K_\sigma=\frac{\sigma bA+b_GG(A)}e;
   \]

2. Keep \(K=B/e\), but impose a separate population or effort cap and accept that the cap creates a nonsmooth constrained system;

3. Define a policy intervention that changes \(E\), not \(K\), and derive the resulting equilibrium conditions separately.

The current text treats these as interchangeable. They are not.

This also affects the claims that reservation “succeeds,” removes liquidation, shifts the neutral continuum, and produces \(\Omega\to0.500\).

---

### 3. Current `R_B=1` is not the trajectory-level stock-decline boundary

With delayed regeneration,

\[
\dot A
=
G(A(t-\tau_g))
-
\frac{[E(t)-\sigma b(t)A(t)]_+}{b_G}.
\]

In the deficit region and with \(\sigma=1\),

\[
\dot A
=
\frac{
bA(t)+b_GG(A(t-\tau_g))-E(t)
}{b_G}.
\]

The relevant quantity is therefore

\[
\widetilde B(t)
=
b(t)A(t)+b_GG(A(t-\tau_g)),
\]

not the current algebraic biocapacity

\[
B(t)=b(t)A(t)+b_GG(A(t)).
\]

Consequently,

\[
\dot A<0
\iff
E>\widetilde B,
\]

not generally \(E>B\).

The repeated statements that:

- “the operating boundary is \(R_B=1\)”;
- “\(R_B=1\) is the stock-decline trigger”;
- “the basin separator is \(R_B=1\)”;
- “the macro-ratio monitor fails silently”

must be scoped carefully.

At a constant equilibrium, delayed and current regeneration coincide, so \(E=B\) is valid as an equilibrium identity. Along a trajectory, it is not the instantaneous decline boundary.

This distinction is especially important because the manuscript claims collapse while both `R_B < 1` and `R_A < 1`. That is possible only because the delayed regeneration term differs from the current `B`, or because the system leaves the stated deficit regime. The mechanism needs to be shown explicitly.

### 4. The ratio statements use a variable `ψ` as though it were constant

You define

\[
R_A=\frac{E}{bA},\qquad
R_B=\frac{E}{B},\qquad
\psi=\frac{bA}{B},
\]

so indeed

\[
R_A=\frac{R_B}{\psi}.
\]

But \(\psi\) varies with \(A\), \(b\), \(D\), technology, delay, and whether \(A>A_{\max}\). It is not generally a constant conversion factor.

Therefore these statements are not valid without additional assumptions:

> “\(R_A=1\) crosses earlier than \(R_B\) by the factor \(1/\psi\).”

> “The gap between them is the flow share.”

> “The lead is governed by \(\psi\).”

The ratio identity gives a difference in threshold values at the same state. It does not imply a temporal lead by a multiplicative factor. To make a time-lead statement, you need a trajectory and assumptions under which \(\psi(t)\) is approximately constant.

Also, once delayed regeneration permits \(A>A_{\max}\), \(G(A)\) can be negative, so \(B\) can fall below \(bA\), and \(\psi\) can exceed one or become negative. The declared range

\[
0\le\psi\le1
\]

is not globally true for the simulated state space.

### 5. The regime condition is not equivalent to `ψ ⋚ 1/2`

You state:

> “The single testable condition \(b_G\rho \gtrless b\) (equivalently \(\psi\gtrless1/2\)) decides everything downstream.”

That equivalence is false.

At the interior maximum,

\[
\psi^*
=
\frac{bA^*}{B(A^*)}
=
\frac{2b}{b+b_G\rho}
=
\frac{2}{1+b_G\rho/b}.
\]

Then

\[
\psi^*=\frac12
\iff
b_G\rho=3b,
\]

not \(b_G\rho=b\).

The condition

\[
b_G\rho>b
\]

determines whether the maximum lies in the interior. It does not determine whether \(\psi^*\) is above or below one-half.

Replace the equivalence with:

> \(b_G\rho>b\) determines whether \(B(A)\) has an interior maximum. Within the interior-MSY regime, \(\psi^*\) varies continuously as \(2b/(b+b_G\rho)\); \(\psi^*=1/2\) occurs only at \(b_G\rho=3b\).

### 6. The `b_G`, `γ`, and `V` units are inconsistent

You correctly assign

\[
b_G:\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}.
\]

But then you write:

\[
\gamma=\frac1{b_G}=\frac1V,
\]

where \(V\) is “standing biomass ÷ annual production.”

If

\[
V=\frac{b_G}{b},
\]

then

\[
\frac1V=\frac{b}{b_G},
\]

not \(1/b_G\).

The stock-loss coefficient in equation (4) is \(1/b_G\), with units

\[
\frac{\mathrm{ha}}{\mathrm{gha}}.
\]

The linearised coefficient

\[
\frac{b}{b_G}
\]

has units \(\mathrm{yr^{-1}}\) and equals \(1/V\).

This distinction matters because §4.3 uses \(b/b_G\) as a dynamical rate while §2.2 calls \(1/b_G\) the harvest coefficient and identifies it with `1/V`.

**Required repair:** distinguish:

\[
\gamma_{\rm conversion}=\frac1{b_G},
\]

from

\[
\gamma_{\rm turnover}=\frac{b}{b_G}=\frac1V.
\]

Do not call \(1/b_G\) a rate unless it is multiplied by a flow-yield quantity.

## Model formulation

### 7. The model is not well posed on the simulated domain without a state-domain definition

The text permits delayed recovery overshoots such as

\[
A>A_{\max}.
\]

Then

\[
G(A)=\rho A(1-A/A_{\max})<0.
\]

Because

\[
B=bA+b_GG(A),
\]

`B` can become smaller than `bA`, zero, or negative for sufficiently large `A`. Consequently,

\[
K=B/e
\]

can become nonpositive, and equation (5) is no longer defined in the stated form.

The later numerical use of `K_min` is not part of the model equations. It changes the system and therefore invalidates analytic claims derived from the unclamped equations.

You need to define one of:

- the admissible domain \(0<A\le A_{\max}\);
- a bounded regeneration function that does not produce invalid `B`;
- an explicit policy/model clamp and a separate constrained system;
- a population equation defined for nonpositive or near-zero `K`.

At present, “overshoot above \(A_{\max}\)” and “\(K\ge K_{\min}\)” are treated as numerical details, but they alter the dynamics.

---

### 8. `A ≥ A_ext` is not merely a numerical clamp

The text says:

> “we impose an extinction floor \(A_{\rm ext}>0\) so that \(K\ge bA_{\rm ext}/e>0\) is bounded”

This does not establish a uniform positive lower bound because \(b\) can approach zero as \(D\) grows. More importantly, clamping `A` changes the vector field at the boundary. The resulting system is a projected or constrained differential equation, not equation (4).

State clearly whether the floor is:

- a biological state constraint;
- an absorbing boundary;
- a numerical stopping condition;
- a projection rule.

Do not call collapse “a model result, not a clamp” if the trajectory is explicitly clamped at the extinction floor.

---

### 9. The population-cap language is not implemented in the equations

The text says:

> “\(\sigma\) is then set together with the population cap so that \(E\le\sigma B\).”

No population cap appears in equation (5). The logistic carrying capacity remains \(B/e\), and `P` can exceed any intended allocated cap during delayed response.

Similarly, the policy section introduces

\[
K=0.5B/e,
\]

but does not rewrite equation (5) or explain whether this is a permanent replacement for `K`, an externally imposed cap, or a controller.

The reservation results need a separate policy operator.

---

### 10. Equation (7) does not support the claim that `η` decides whether an equilibrium exists

For a no-overshoot equilibrium,

\[
E\le B,
\]

equation (7) gives

\[
D=0
\]

regardless of \(\eta\), including \(\eta=0\).

The equilibrium continuum in the `σ=1` subsystem is precisely of this form. Thus the statement

> “The full overshoot model has an equilibrium only because of \(\eta\)”

is too broad.

What \(\eta>0\) provides is a finite steady debt for an equilibrium with persistent positive debt input:

\[
D^*=\frac{E^*-B^*}{\eta}.
\]

That is a narrower and correct statement.

---

### 11. The debt “compounding without bound” claim is not a theorem as written

You state that under multiplicative degradation,

> “debt compounds without bound while technology saturates.”

Equation (7) is

\[
\dot D=[E-B]_+-\eta D.
\]

With \(\eta>0\), debt grows without bound only if the positive deficit remains sufficiently large relative to \(\eta D\). If `P` falls as `K` and `b` fall, then `E` may also decline, and `D` may converge to a finite value.

The exponential factor

\[
b=(b_0+T_b)e^{-\alpha D}
\]

makes productivity decay with debt. It does not, by itself, imply unbounded debt.

Replace “theorem” with a conditional statement and prove it under explicit lower bounds on \(E-B\).

### 13. The fixed-liability root formula is incomplete and its stability interpretation is too broad

For

\[
B(A)=E,
\]

the roots are

\[
A_{\pm}
=
\frac{A_{\max}}{2b_G\rho}
\left[
b+b_G\rho
\pm
\sqrt{(b+b_G\rho)^2-\frac{4b_G\rho E}{A_{\max}}}
\right].
\]

You display only the minus root. In the capital-dominated regime, the second root is essential: it is the stable branch when it lies in the admissible deficit region.

You later say there are:

> “two fixed points — one unstable … and one stable…”

but the displayed formula does not show both.

Also, the fold is a fold of the steady-state equation under a fixed-liability reduction. It is not automatically a full delayed-system saddle-node bifurcation. You need a transversality and spectral statement before calling it a bifurcation of the DDE.

- whether the model permits \(A>A_{\max}\);
- whether the delayed system has a compatible equilibrium history.

The claim should be restricted to the stated fixed-liability, no-delay or steady-state problem.

### 21. The empirical anchoring of `τ_g` changes the meaning of the delay

The manuscript defines \(\tau_g\) as:

> “the delay between a change in the environment state and the regeneration response”

but then estimates it using time to approximately 50% recovery.

A pure delay in

\[
G(A(t-\tau_g))
\]

means no response to the delayed state before \(\tau_g\), followed immediately by the modelled response. A time-to-50%-recovery statistic is not equivalent to a pure delay. It includes growth rate, amplitude, disturbance severity, and observation definition.

The conversion

\[
\tau_g\approx1.44\,t_{50}
\]

is not self-evident and needs derivation from a specified response kernel. Otherwise call the literature values “scenario ranges” rather than empirical estimates of the model’s pure delay.

### 23. The productivity-illusion derivation ignores the capital-growth term

### 24. Technology does not automatically raise population and demand

The text repeatedly states:

> “yield technology raises \(K\), \(P\), and \(E\)”

and treats this as a model theorem.

Because population responds to delayed carrying capacity,

\[
K(t-\tau_p),
\]

and because technology also changes debt, `b`, and future capacity, the signs of the short-run changes in `P` and `E` are trajectory-dependent. Technology may raise eventual carrying capacity, but it does not imply monotonic increases in population and demand at every time.

State this as a conditional mechanism:

> In the tested trajectories, the technology pulse increased delayed carrying capacity and subsequently increased population and footprint before debt-driven degradation dominated.

### 25. The “no rescue-by-stock” conclusion is overstated

The manuscript reports a recovery strip at \(A_0=A_{\max}\) for long delays and recovery at shorter delays. Therefore the claim

> For the baseline long-delay scenario, increasing initial stock over the tested interior range did not restore recovery; the only surviving recovery strip was concentrated at the boundary \(A_0=A_{\max}\).

That is a grid- and parameter-conditional result, not a structural theorem.

## Discussion and interpretation

### 32. The “no hysteresis” row contradicts the existence of two fixed points and a fold

The table says:

> “Hysteresis / path dependence in the threshold: No”

But §4.2 says that in the capital-dominated regime there are two fixed points, one stable and one unstable, which merge at a saddle-node. That is the classical structure capable of path dependence and hysteresis under parameter cycling.

A single-valued threshold \(A_c(E)\) does not rule out hysteresis. It is the separatrix for a fixed parameter, not evidence against hysteresis.

Replace the row with:

> “The fixed-liability problem has a separatrix and, in the capital-dominated regime, a fold. Whether a full hysteresis loop occurs under slowly varying liability requires a continuation analysis and is not established here.”

---

### 33. The CSD claims require a precise return-time definition

You state that return time is flat at approximately 150 years and then disappears. But the manuscript does not define:

- perturbation amplitude;
- perturbation direction;
- observable;
- fitting window;
- whether the equilibrium is unstable;
- how return time is defined when the trajectory does not return.

Because the equilibrium family contains a zero eigenvalue, ordinary return-time analysis is also delicate. A neutral tangent direction can produce apparent slow recovery unrelated to a saddle-node.

The CSD table should distinguish:

- transverse return time;
- tangent-to-manifold drift;
- finite-amplitude recovery time;
- time to threshold crossing.

### 34. The phase-line argument is not directly applicable to the data

The cod-style phase-line logic is not present in this excerpt, but the same issue is relevant to the model’s claims: an assessment trajectory is not an exact trajectory of a scalar autonomous ODE, and the current model is neither scalar nor autonomous.

Avoid importing the strong conclusion “the lag is the controlling variable” from a stylised state-space result into empirical systems without an observation and intervention model.

### 35. The claim that a fixed scalar model has “no path memory” is too strong

The presence of a fixed scalar autonomous equation does not automatically eliminate path dependence in the broader system. Delays add history dependence, and a fold can generate distinct basins. Even in a one-dimensional ODE with a parameter varied externally, the path through parameter space matters.

Limit the statement to:

> “For the fixed-liability, one-dimensional autonomous reduction considered here, the equilibrium threshold is single-valued. Hysteresis under parameter cycling was not analysed.”

---

### 36. The May comparison is rhetorically unnecessary and potentially misleading

The claim that this “inverts the classical complexity–stability expectation” is too broad. May’s result concerns random community matrices and stability as a function of complexity, not a universal proposition that all instability is complexity-generated.

The comparison adds little and risks creating a straw version of May’s result. Remove it or state narrowly:

> “This model demonstrates that instability can arise without high-dimensional ecological complexity.”

## Introduction and framing

### 37. The orchard analogy changes the meaning of “flow” midway

The text first says fruit is gathered without harming trees, then defines biocapacity as:

\[
B=bA+b_GG(A).
\]

The term \(b_GG(A)\) is not ordinary annual fruit flow. It is the value assigned to stock growth and is only available by harvesting or liquidating capital. Calling the whole `B` “biocapacity” and then treating `R_B=1` as a sustainable operating boundary makes the accounting interpretation ambiguous.

You need to distinguish:

- renewable flow yield \(bA\);
- capital-growth contribution \(b_GG(A)\);
- total accounting capacity \(B\);
- physically harvestable flow;
- sustainable harvest under the stock constraint.

The manuscript currently uses “total biocapacity,” “sustainable yield,” “capital growth,” and “available supply” interchangeably.

---

### 38. “Taking fruit at or below the rate trees regrow” does not correspond to the stated flow term

The flow term \(bA\) is generated by the standing area and does not consume `A`. It is not a regeneration flow \(G(A)\). The orchard analogy implies that taking fruit at the annual regenerative rate preserves the trees, but equation (4) allows the entire `bA` flow to be harvested without reducing `A`, regardless of `G(A)`.

That may be a deliberate two-book accounting assumption, but it should be stated plainly. Otherwise readers will interpret `bA` as a stock-regeneration flow and find equation (4) inconsistent.

---

### 39. The statement that the sustainable balance is a “family of balances” depends on an unusual degeneracy

The equilibrium continuum occurs because:

- `P` sets `E`;
- the stock equation balances `E` against `B`;
- the population equation sets `P=K`;
- there is no separate stabilising demand or harvest rule.

This is not a generic sustainability result. It is a structural degeneracy of the chosen equations. Say so explicitly:

> “The continuum is a consequence of defining demand through a logistic population equation whose equilibrium is exactly the algebraic carrying capacity.”

Otherwise the manuscript risks presenting the continuum as a general property of stock-flow systems.

---

### 40. The “protective institutional stance” is not yet a modelled controller

The conclusion recommends:

> “adopt the protective, effort-reducing institutional stance.”

But the main model has no explicit effort-control or governance variable. It has a population state and footprint \(E=eP\). The reservation policy is an imposed cap, not a feedback controller.

Call this a policy extension or control interpretation, not a result of the baseline model.

---

## Predictions and falsifiability

### 41. Several “predictions” are restatements of assumptions or outputs

Examples:

- Prediction 3: a computable `t_peak` follows from any specified simulation.
- Prediction 4: reducing a policy lag matters comparably to reducing overshoot is not defined mathematically.
- Prediction 8: `R_B=1` is an algebraic identity at equilibrium, not yet an empirical prediction.
- Prediction 7: rate and lag are decoupled because they are independently placed in different equations.

To make these falsifiable, define:

- the estimand;
- the parameter intervention;
- the null model;
- the expected sign or magnitude;
- the acceptance criterion.

For prediction 4, for example:

\[
\frac{\partial J}{\partial \tau_e}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\frac{\partial J}{\partial f}
\]

for a declared outcome \(J\).

---

### 42. “The stabilising sign is protective” is not established by the present equations

The model has no explicit sign-switch experiment in which demand responds positively or negatively to perceived shortfall. It has a fixed population law responding to delayed capacity.

The phrase “extractive sign” appears to describe a different controller architecture. Add that controller explicitly or remove the sign claim from the main conclusions.

---

### 43. The empirical claims are stronger than the model can support

The manuscript says the empirical program will test whether the monitor forecasts collapse. But `R_B=1` is derived from the model’s accounting identity, while actual NFA biocapacity includes accounting conventions and does not directly observe `A`, `b`, or `G(A)`.

A test requires an observation model linking:

\[
(A,b,D)
\longrightarrow
(B,E).
\]

Without that, the empirical programme can test association, not the model’s causal threshold.

---

## Editorial and structural issues

### 44. The manuscript contains many internal drafting instructions

Examples include:

- “must be stated”;
- “report”;
- “state the interval scoped”;
- “the exact crossing-curve method should be applied”;
- “future work”;
- “verified numerically” without displayed derivation;
- “the paper carries…”;
- “presentation rule.”

These read like revision notes rather than manuscript prose. They should be converted into either:

- completed methods and results; or
- a limitations/future-work paragraph.

The current form makes it difficult to distinguish established results from planned corrections.

---

### 45. The paper repeats the same claims too many times

The following claims recur throughout §4, §5, §6, §8, §12, §13, and §14:

- `R_B=1`;
- the `τ_g≈20` cliff;
- no Hopf;
- the `+0.62` eigenvalue;
- the neutral continuum;
- the narrow masking window;
- no rescue-by-stock;
- the protective institutional sign.

This repetition becomes dangerous because the claims are not always stated under the same assumptions. Consolidate each into one theorem/result and refer back to it.

---

### 46. The paper mixes four different models without sufficiently visible boundaries

At minimum, the manuscript contains:

1. the full delayed debt model;
2. the constant-parameter `S0` subsystem;
3. the reduced masking model with a softplus;
4. the gross-harvest comparator;
5. the reservation-policy model.

Each needs its own notation, parameter table, equations, and results. The current text often moves between them inside the same paragraph.

Use explicit labels such as:

```text
Model D: deficit-driven full model
Model D0: constant-b deficit-driven subsystem
Model M: reduced masking model
Model G: gross-harvest comparator
Model R: reservation controller
```

Every numerical result should identify one.

---

## Minimum revision path

Before polishing prose, I would make these changes in order:

1. Correct the deficit-model Jacobian and remove the incorrect positive determinant.
2. Define the exact state domain and eliminate or formally model all clamps.
3. Resolve the `σ<1` policy equations.
4. Distinguish current `B` from delayed \(\widetilde B\).
5. Rewrite all `R_B=1` claims as equilibrium identities unless a trajectory-level condition is proved.
6. Correct the `b_G`, \(b\), and \(V\) units.
7. Separate the gross-harvest model from the deficit-driven model.
8. Reconcile the contradictory \(\tau_g\) recovery tables and remove the universal “collapse above 20 years” claim.
9. Recompute or relabel all eigenvalue claims, explicitly excluding the permanent zero root.
10. Reclassify “measure-zero,” “theorem,” “guaranteed,” “no rescue,” and “confirmed” claims as conditional unless analytically demonstrated.
11. Add a complete model/version/parameter table for every numerical result.
12. Only then shorten the introduction, predictions, discussion, and conclusion.

The manuscript’s strongest defensible thesis is narrower than the current version:

> A deficit-driven stock-flow model with delayed regeneration can produce a narrow transient in which an aggregate accounting measure rises while an underlying stock declines. In the stated full-harvest formulation, the equilibrium set is degenerate, and delay-dependent recovery outcomes are governed by basin geometry rather than a conventional Hopf onset. These conclusions are conditional on the accounting decomposition, delayed regeneration rule, and treatment of the population-cap constraint.

That thesis is coherent. The current broader claims about `R_B` as a universal operating boundary, a 20-year empirical collapse threshold, no rescue-by-stock, and the protective institutional sign require the mathematical and numerical corrections above.
