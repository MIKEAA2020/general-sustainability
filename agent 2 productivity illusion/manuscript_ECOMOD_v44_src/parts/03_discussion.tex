\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the two-land structure changes, and what it removes}

The results depart from the single-stock, single-aggregate reading of the environment that dominates the resource and sustainability literature \citep{Schaefer1954,May1973,Meadows1972,Meadows2004,WackernagelRees1996,BranderTaylor1998} in three respects, each of which is a negative result as much as a positive one. First, the conversion component can raise aggregate capacity while capital-land area $A_c$ falls, but this bounded composition effect does not by itself determine the net change in the aggregate: Proposition~\ref{prop:mask} gives the exact sign condition for the conversion contribution, and Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded} explains why the technology/debt mask cannot persist. Second, the drawdown of capital-land area $A_c$ is a demand-driven floor collision. The alternative-stable-states literature \citep{Scheffer2001} predicts that a system approaching a fold shows critical slowing down, hysteresis and a discontinuous jump; none of these is present here, and their absence is structural (Table~\ref{tab:neg}): the deficit-side dynamics are affine with constant real eigenvalues, the equilibrium leaves the admissible set through a face rather than through a bifurcation, and the drawdown depth is continuous in demand. Third, recovery is not the reverse of degradation; it requires an explicit, gated flow, and the gate sign is a design decision with phase-dependent consequences.

The negative results have a direct bearing on early-warning practice. Generic indicators of critical slowing down (rising autocorrelation and variance) are motivated by the approach to a fold \citep{Scheffer2001}. In a two-book system in which the drawdown is a boundary collision, such indicators would not be expected to fire, and their silence should not be read as safety. The informative signal is the capital book itself, which requires the two-book series.

Several quantities that circulate for models of this kind are deliberately not claimed here. The two-gain Hopf classification of which delay destabilizes, the associated critical delays, and the reference eigenvalue of order $+0.6\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ belong to a single-stock fast--slow reduction that we retain only as a comparator in SI~\S S7, where its equations, equilibrium family and figures are given; none of its features is carried into the two-land results. The ``18--20\,yr regeneration cliff'' of the one-stock comparator does not survive the two-land structure (Table~\ref{tab:neg}). What is structural in the two-land model is the composition logic and the identifiability limit; what is conditional on effective parameters is the sign and size of $\dconv$ and the location of the ceiling.

\subsection{Measurement implications}

The methodological result of the paper is the identifiability limit. Biocapacity in global hectares is a value-weighted scalar, not a count of the underlying assets. Given only that a portfolio's value rose by $20\%$, one cannot tell whether every holding grew a little or some made large gains while others quietly fell; the scalar gloss hides the composition. This is the generic limit of any value-weighted composite \citep{Nardo2008}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}(iii) states it for biocapacity: the aggregate identifies one linear combination of the areas, and the capital-book parameters enter only through identifiable combinations. The aggregate is therefore a weak-sustainability index in the precise sense of the companion assessment work \citep{Abaee2026b}: it is the linear, perfectly substitutable member of the aggregator family (its Lemma~A), and it can satisfy an aggregate floor while the typed floor on $A_c$ is violated by exactly the amount that the fast-land yield gains against the capital loss. In that family no positive-elasticity aggregator is uniformly safe and only the typed, non-compensating one is (its Theorem~S2(ii)). Under exact-tube semantics, in which a transition is safe only if every state along it satisfies the constraints \citep{Abaee2026b}, the passage during which $A_c$ falls is itself a violation even if the endpoint recovers -- a reason to report through-time trajectories and boundary curves, not endpoint attractors.

For the National Footprint Accounts the implication is concrete. The accounts are account-based and do not capture soil erosion, deforestation, groundwater depletion or other cumulative degradation, so estimated biocapacity likely overstates the real base and the footprint understates the overshoot \citep{Wackernagel2002,Borucke2013}; critics have made the point forcefully \citep{Blomqvist2013,GiampietroSaltelli2014,Galli2016,vdBerghGrazi2015}. The present results add a different and more specific point: even an internally consistent aggregate cannot be inverted into its components. A growth headline for total biocapacity carries no information about whether cropland growth is compensating for stagnation or offsetting declines elsewhere, and the measured accounts show that this is exactly what happened in 1961--2022 (Fig.~\ref{fig:composition}). The informative assessment reports components with the total -- at minimum component endpoint ratios, log contributions, attribution shares, data vintage and endpoint sensitivity -- at every level at which an aggregate is used. Parallel observations have been made for human appropriation of net primary production and for land-management data \citep{Haberl2007,Krausmann2013,Erb2017,Carvalhais2014}: the composition of an aggregate account value, not its level, carries the sustainability information.

The model's capital decline has a different evidential status from those account movements. In the frozen-demand baseline (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}a), modeled capital land $A_c$ falls from $1.500$ to $0.808$\,ha while $B$ rises from $0.594$ to $0.825$\,gha; because restoration is off in that run, the reduced land allocation persists after the shortfall closes. The quality state $q$ rises from $0.9$ to $1.0$, but since $q_0=0.9>q^*=q_{\max}/2=0.5$, this rise lies on the descending side of the concave per-hectare function $\Ups(q)$. The logistic regeneration term falls, so $\Ups(q)$ declines from $0.05576$ to $0.05000$\,gha\,ha$^{-1}$; the effective capital account, i.e.\ the modeled capital contribution $\Ups(q)A_c$ to $B$, consequently falls from $0.08364$ to $0.04038$\,gha (by $51.7\%$, about $52\%$), distinct from the $46.2\%$ decline in capital-land area. This is a conditional model trajectory, not an estimate of a global physical-stock trend. NFA's forest-products, grazing and fishing categories are annual account values built from land classes, yields and conversion factors. Their modest declines are relevant composition signals, but they do not identify $\Delta A_c$, biomass, soil condition or any other physical capital-stock change.

A defensible physical-stock scoreboard should therefore preserve a vector of component accounts rather than add unlike stocks into a single unweighted total. Report ecosystem extent (for example, forest or wetland area in ha) separately from condition or stock measures (for example, forest growing stock or biomass in m$^3$ or tonnes of carbon, soil organic carbon in tonnes of carbon for a stated depth, fish spawning-stock biomass in tonnes, and freshwater storage in m$^3$). Keep regenerative and appropriation flows separate from stocks: net annual increment, soil-carbon accumulation or loss, fish recruitment and catch, and water recharge and withdrawal are not interchangeable with the corresponding stock levels. For each component, provide the reference year, spatial boundary, physical unit, absolute change and baseline ratio, uncertainty, data vintage, and any relevant ecological floor or reference range. Do not double-count nested measures such as forest extent and biomass over the same area. The components can be interpreted jointly as a noncompensating dashboard; if a single index is required, its valuation, normalization, weights, and permitted substitution must be explicit rather than hidden inside the unit. This approach is consistent with the separate physical ecosystem-extent and condition accounts in SEEA Ecosystem Accounting, with services and monetary presentations kept distinct \citep{UN2021SEEA_EA}. Mapping such a scoreboard to the model's effective $A_c$ and quality $q$ still requires a declared observation operator and independent measurements; the NFA biocapacity total alone cannot supply it.

The empirical program that follows is a flux reconstruction, not a curve fit. The observation operator of Section~\ref{sec:data} is a hierarchy: NFA land-type biocapacity with physical area and yield factors where obtainable; FAOSTAT area and yields as independent external proxies (used here); remote-sensing land cover and historical reconstructions for validation and extension (SI~\S S5.4). Forest area is a sentinel proxy for $A_c$, not the whole capital book; capital-land regeneration is unobserved in the accounts and would require growing-stock or net-annual-increment data. The proxies do not solve global-hectare identifiability; they make the composition mechanism an independently anchored, falsifiable statement rather than an asserted one.

\subsection{Policy operators}

Three operators follow from the typed-floor structure and are developed in SI~\S S6. Freezing conversion -- capping $A_f$ or flooring $A_c$ -- is the single operator that guarantees $A_c\ge\Acmin$; a governor that observes only the composite $B$ cannot guarantee a capital-land floor, and the failure is informational rather than dynamical. Restoration is protective only when surplus-gated, and a deficit-gated flow can deepen the shortfall it is meant to relieve; this is the protective, as opposed to mobilizing, institutional signature of the delay companion \citep{Abaee2026a}. A Half-Earth reservation \citep{Wilson2016}, $E\le\sigma B$ with $\sigma<1$, succeeds without liquidation on the nominal path, but this is a nominal-path consequence until it is certified against a declared disturbance class under a preregistered retention rule, and we state it as such.

\subsection{Relation to companion work}

The accounting layer -- the closed typed-flux ledger, conservation and donor-limitation rules, depletion arithmetic and the no-non-negative-weighting aggregation obstruction -- is the object of the ledger companion \citep{Abaee2026c}, which owns the static layer, cedes delay dynamics to its companions and registers a two-pool dynamical model as an open gap; the present paper supplies that minimal dynamical realization for the global-hectare case. The aggregation theorem that places $B$ at the weak extreme of the aggregator family is from the assessment companion \citep{Abaee2026b}, and the institutional-delay regime is from the governance companion \citep{Abaee2026a}; the delays here are ecological (regeneration, demography), not institutional. We cite, and do not re-derive, those results.

\subsection{Limitations}

The capital land is an effective class, not a single land type; the single regeneration timescale, the composition diagnostic and the ceiling are effective-parameter statements, and the full vector decomposition is cited rather than re-derived. Most parameters are representative rather than estimated; $b_c$, $b_{G,c}$ and $\rho_c$ are not identifiable from a cross-section, and the regeneration anchors bound a band rather than calibrate $\rho_c$ (the anchors concern the regeneration rate, not the lag $\tau_g$). The per-capita footprint is held constant to isolate the stock--flow--demand feedbacks; endogenizing $e$ is an extension. The composition window is bounded and demand-driven; without a sustained shortfall or a technology/demand feedback it closes at the balance point. Stability at $S=0$ is one-sided because the positive part is exact. Drawdown thresholds are tolerance-dependent bands; a small margin next to that band is reported as such and not as a sharp boundary. The debt-repayment rate is set physical and non-singular ($\eta=0.05\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$); the $\eta\to0$ case removes the debt equilibrium and is excluded by a floor, and outcomes are insensitive to $\eta$ over a modest positive range. The accounts do not separate yield from area, which is the identifiability limitation the paper is about rather than an estimation error. Finally, the delayed-regeneration term is implemented at the current quality in the deposited solver (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}); we have verified that applying the delay changes no reported quantity by more than $1\%$, and the corrected variant is deposited alongside.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

A single aggregate of annual bioproductive supply can conceal the composition of what is growing, and the concealment is structural. In a minimal two-land model with conserved area, a separate quality state and demand-driven conversion, the conversion contribution to aggregate change is positive when the fast-land yield exceeds the per-hectare value of capital land, while the active area transfer reduces capital-land area $A_c$; other terms determine whether the net aggregate rises or falls (Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}). An aggregate-only observation does not locally identify the capital-land area loss, and independent fast-book proxies identify at most a measured fast book and an accounting residual. The drawdown of capital-land area $A_c$ under sustained demand is a floor collision at a demand ceiling, not a catastrophic shift: the deficit-side dynamics are affine, the leading eigenvalue is constant along the branch, there is no fold, no hysteresis and no critical slowing down, and the drawdown depth is a continuous function of demand (Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}). The mask is bounded because technology lifts the fast yield by at most a fixed factor whereas debt erosion is limited only by the sustained shortfall (Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded}), and recovery requires an explicit restoration flow whose gate sign matters. In the measured National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts for 1961--2022, aggregate biocapacity grew $23\%$ while per-capita biocapacity halved, the exact Divisia decomposition attributes the growth to cropland yield with a negative non-cropland residual, and the land-type accounts show cropland biocapacity growing $2.85\times$ against a flat non-cropland book that is itself the net of declining forest, grazing and fishing biocapacity and rising built-up land. These results establish an identifiability property of aggregate biocapacity and a measured composition pattern; they are not a forecast and do not show a measured decline in the model's effective capital-land area $A_c$, still less in any specific physical stock. The practical reporting rule has two parts: report NFA components alongside any aggregate, and track heterogeneous physical assets in a component-level scoreboard with explicit units, boundaries, condition measures, and separate flows. Do not collapse unlike stocks into an unweighted sum; if a single scalar is required, declare its valuation, weights, and permitted substitution. Neither the NFA aggregate nor the physical scoreboard identifies the model's effective $A_c$ without an observation operator. A modern economy can run for decades on a harvest that looks healthy while the orchard behind it quietly shrinks; the harvest number will not say so, and only the two-book series can.
