\begin{abstract}
\footnotesize
Total biocapacity aggregates heterogeneous land accounts whose yields and regeneration rates differ. We ask what this headline reveals about land composition. We formulate a two-land delay-differential model in which account shortfall triggers one-to-one conversion from ecological-capital to fast-provisioning area; capital quality evolves separately, and degradation acts through accumulated ecological debt. Three propositions are proved and checked numerically. (i) During active conversion, the conversion contribution to biocapacity is positive exactly when the fast-land yield exceeds the per-hectare value of capital land; area $A_c$ declines, while an aggregate-only observation cannot identify this composition. (ii) Under frozen demand, the balance point is unique. Above the demand ceiling $\Eceil\approx1.14$\,gha, capital-land area approaches its typed floor asymptotically. Affine deficit-side dynamics give a constant leading eigenvalue ($-0.054\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$), so the boundary collision has no fold, hysteresis, or critical slowing down. With endogenous demand, drawdown depth varies continuously with initial demand. (iii) Technology gain is parameter-bounded, whereas debt erosion is bounded by sustained shortfall. Applied to the National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts (1961--2022), an exact Divisia decomposition finds aggregate biocapacity up $23\%$, cropland biocapacity up $2.85$-fold, non-cropland biocapacity nearly flat, and per-capita biocapacity halved. NFA categories do not directly measure modeled area $A_c$ or quality $q$. These model results are conditional, not estimates of a global physical-stock trend; we propose a physical-stock scoreboard with explicit units and valuation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Carrying capacity is one of ecology's most enduring and most elusive ideas. In classical population biology it is a fixed ceiling; imported into global human ecology it is usually treated as a static boundary, the maximum number of people the Earth can support \citep[cf.][]{Cohen1995}. Treating the ceiling as fixed overlooks a feedback that matters: a population that overshoots its resource base degrades the base and lowers the future ceiling. This paper is concerned with a subtler failure than a fixed ceiling. It concerns the \emph{measurement} on which monitoring and policy depend.

The standard aggregate measure of annual regenerative supply is biocapacity, expressed in the National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts (NFA) in global hectares (gha) for each annual account. Under the Global Footprint Network (GFN) convention, annual Ecological Footprint and biocapacity values are reported in gha, not gha\,yr$^{-1}$ \citep{GFNStandards2009,GFNGuidebook2019}. A global hectare is a productivity-normalized hectare whose value incorporates yield and equivalence factors \citep{Borucke2013,Lin2018}. Because the weighting sits \emph{inside} the unit, an aggregate biocapacity series does not tell a monitor whether capacity rose because land became more productive, because more land was brought into provision, or because ecological capital was converted into provisioning land. The composite-indicator literature warns that additive aggregation turns weights into trade-offs, so that a deficit in one dimension can be offset by a surplus in another \citep{Nardo2008,Becker2017,Fischer2022}; biocapacity is exactly such a composite, and the footprint framework has been criticized on related grounds \citep{Blomqvist2013,GiampietroSaltelli2014,vdBerghGrazi2015,Galli2016}. The aggregate remains useful as a headline, but only if its components are reported alongside it. What has been missing is a dynamical account of \emph{how} a rising aggregate and a falling capital base can coexist, for how long, and what would be needed to tell them apart.

Coupled human--environment models have largely treated the environment as a single stock or a single aggregate balance: the World3 system \citep{Meadows1972,Meadows1974,Meadows2004}, the Ricardo--Malthus models of Easter Island \citep{BranderTaylor1998,Anderies2000}, and more recent bidirectionally coupled Earth-system frameworks \citep{Motesharrei2016,Donges2017,CalvinBondLamberty2018,HendersonLoreau2018}. Estimates of Earth's carrying capacity span several orders of magnitude \citep{Cohen1995,vdBerghRietveld2004}, some authors reject the notion for humans altogether \citep{Simon1996}, and others let the ceiling itself vary logistically \citep{Meyer1994,MeyerAusubel1999} or treat natural capital as a depreciating asset \citep{Dasgupta2021}. Demographic projections used in most global assessments assume that population unfolds independently of environmental constraints \citep{UN2022}. Within this literature the environment has one book; the question of what an aggregate over two books with different yields and regeneration times can conceal has not been posed as a modeling problem, although the land-use literature has documented the historical operator -- conversion of forest, grassland and wetland into cropland -- in detail \citep{Haberl2007,Krausmann2013,Erb2017}.

Consider an orchard with two kinds of ground. Fast provisioning land -- cropland and intensively managed systems -- yields much and is quickly restored, but has little standing regenerative value. Ecological-capital land -- forest, soil, wetland, long-rotation systems -- has a large standing value and a regenerative flow that is slowly rebuilt. Demand is evaluated against the human-available annual account total; any remaining account shortfall triggers conversion from capital land into fast provision. Because aggregate biocapacity is a value-weighted sum over the two land books, conversion can raise the account total while capital-land area $A_c$ shrinks. A harvest that looks stable, or a rising aggregate measure of capacity, can therefore mask a shrinking ecological-capital account. Neither the orchard nor its harvesters respond instantly -- forest loss, soil degradation and demographic adjustment unfold over decades -- which is why societies can prosper for long periods while quietly undermining their productive base, only to decline abruptly \citep{Tainter1988,Diamond2005}. The companion ledger work calls the general phenomenon the \emph{productivity illusion} \citep{Abaee2026c}; the specific form addressed here is the \emph{composition} illusion, in which the aggregate rises not because technology outpaces degradation but because the books are re-weighted.

We address three questions. \textbf{(Q1)} Under what conditions does conversion of ecological-capital land raise the aggregate while capital-land area $A_c$ falls, how long does the mask last, and can the composition be identified from the aggregate? \textbf{(Q2)} Is the resulting capital drawdown a catastrophic shift in the sense of the alternative-stable-states literature \citep{Scheffer2001}, with a fold, hysteresis and early-warning signals, or something structurally different? \textbf{(Q3)} Is the mask self-limiting, and what does recovery require?

The paper makes four contributions. First, it formulates a minimal two-land model (Section~\ref{sec:model}) in which area is conserved one-to-one across three land books, per-hectare capital quality is a separate state, conversion is a rate with an explicit time constant, and degradation acts on the surviving yield through an accumulated ecological debt; the model is a delay differential equation with ecological (regeneration) and demographic delays \citep{Kuang1993,Hutchinson1948}. Second, it proves three propositions (Section~\ref{sec:results}): a composition identity that gives the exact sign condition for the conversion contribution and shows that an aggregate-only observation does not locally identify the capital loss (Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}); the uniqueness of the frozen-demand balance point, the location of the demand ceiling, and the absence of a fold and of critical slowing down, derived from the affine structure of the deficit-side dynamics (Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}); and a boundedness asymmetry between the technology and debt channels that makes the mask transient (Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded}). Third, it applies the composition identity to the measured NFA series for 1961--2022 with an exact logarithmic-mean (Divisia) decomposition and the measured land-type accounts (Section~\ref{sec:empirical}). Fourth, it states the falsifiable content of the model as five predictions and draws the reporting implication for sustainability indicators (Sections~\ref{sec:predictions} and \ref{sec:discussion}). The model is deliberately simple and most of its parameters are representative rather than fitted; it is not a forecast. Its purpose is to make the composition logic of aggregate biocapacity explicit and to state which outcomes are structural and which are conditional on effective parameters that are not separately identified.
\section{Methods}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{A two-land model with conserved area and a separate quality state}\label{sec:equations}

The bioproductive area $A_{tot}$ is divided into three books: fast-provisioning land $A_f$ (cropland and intensively managed systems), ecological-capital land $A_c$ (forest, soil, wetland and long-rotation systems, consolidated into one effective class), and a reserve $A_r$ (fallow, retired or not yet allocated land), with $A_f+A_c+A_r=A_{tot}$ at all times. Capital land carries a per-hectare quality index $q\in[0,q_{\max}]$ (a capacity state, not an area), which regenerates logistically,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gc}
G_c(q)=\rho_c\,q\left(1-\frac{q}{q_{\max}}\right),
\end{equation}
so that the per-hectare value of capital land, in annual NFA-account-yield units, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ups}
\Ups(q)=b_c+b_{G,c}\,G_c(q),
\end{equation}
the sum of a direct per-hectare yield $b_c$ and the regeneration-value coefficient $b_{G,c}$ times the quality growth $G_c(q)$. Biocapacity is the additive two-book aggregate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
B=Y_f+Y_c=b_f A_f+\Ups(q)\,A_c ,
\end{equation}
in gha, the annual NFA account unit \citep{GFNStandards2009,GFNGuidebook2019}. Demand is the footprint $E=eP$ of a population $P$ with constant per-capita footprint $e$ (gha\,cap$^{-1}$), and the shortfall above the human-available amount is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S}
S=\pos{E-\sigma_f Y_f-\sigma_c Y_c},
\end{equation}
with $\pos{x}=\max(x,0)$ exact (never smoothed) and $\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1$ in the baseline. The shortfall is closed by converting capital land into fast land at a finite rate,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uc}
u_c=\frac{1}{\tconv}\min\!\left(\frac{S}{b_f},\;A_c-\Acmin\right)\qquad[\mathrm{ha\,yr^{-1}}],
\end{equation}
where $S/b_f$ is the area that would close the shortfall at the fast-land yield, $\Acmin$ is a typed viability floor, and $\tconv$ is the conversion time constant. The land books evolve by one-to-one area transfers only,
\begin{align}
\dot A_c&=-u_c+R_{rc},\label{eq:Ac}\\
\dot A_f&=u_c+\mu A_r-\eta_f A_f,\label{eq:Af}\\
\dot A_r&=-\mu A_r+\eta_f A_f-R_{rc},\label{eq:Ar}
\end{align}
where $\mu A_r$ is cropland maintenance (replanting from the reserve), $\eta_f A_f$ is fast-land retirement to the reserve, and $R_{rc}$ is an explicit, surplus-gated restoration flow from reserve to capital land,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rrc}
R_{rc}=\chi_r\,A_r\,\pos{B-E},
\end{equation}
which is the only route back into the capital book. Equations \eqref{eq:Ac}--\eqref{eq:Ar} sum to zero, so the reserve is a constrained balance rather than an independent state. Quality regenerates with a recruitment delay, population follows a delayed logistic law \citep{Pearl1925,Hutchinson1948} on the carrying capacity $K=B/e$, and degradation acts on the surviving fast-land yield through an accumulated ecological debt $D$:
\begin{align}
\dot q&=G_c\big(q(t-\tau_g)\big),\label{eq:q}\\
\dot P&=rP\left(1-\frac{P}{K(t-\tau_p)}\right),\qquad K=\max(B/e,\,K_{\min}),\label{eq:P}\\
b_f&=\big(b_{f0}+T_b(t)\big)\,e^{-\alpha D},\label{eq:bf}\\
\dot D&=\pos{E-B}-\eta D,\label{eq:D}\\
T_b(t)&=\omega_{Tb}\,\frac{\Delta b}{1+e^{-\kappa_w(t-t_{wave})}},\qquad \omega_{Tb}\in\{0,1\}.\label{eq:Tb}
\end{align}
Because model time $t$ is measured in years and $E-B$ is an annual account amount in gha, the leaky shortfall accumulator $D$ has units gha\,yr: both $\dot D$ and $\eta D$ have units gha. This is a model-internal time-integrated liability, not an NFA-reported footprint or biocapacity item.

The technology wave $T_b$ is a bounded logistic yield step controlled by $\omega_{Tb}$: $\omega_{Tb}=0$ in the baseline, main figures, and all tables except the separately identified matched experiment in SI~\S S8, where $\omega_{Tb}=1$. The state is $(A_f,A_c,q,P,D)$ with one delay each in regeneration and demography; a timber-liquidation channel and a direct fast-to-capital restoration flow are stated extensions that remain off in every reported run (Supplementary Information, SI~\S S1).

Five conventions are essential. (i) \emph{The positive part is exact.} A smoothed ramp would leak conversion when $E<B$; the one-sided deficit mechanism is the model's content, so $\pos{\cdot}$ is never regularized and stability at $S=0$ is one-sided. (ii) \emph{Conversion is a rate.} $S/b_f$ is a stock (ha); it becomes a rate only through $\tconv$, so $u_c$ is dimensionally ha\,yr$^{-1}$ and conversion is never instantaneous. (iii) \emph{Area is conserved one-to-one and capacity is a separate book.} Every area flow in \eqref{eq:Ac}--\eqref{eq:Ar} moves hectares between books; regeneration $G_c$ is a flow of capacity into $q$ and never transfers land, so conservation is a property of the equations, not an identity imposed afterwards. (iv) \emph{Annual account amounts and their time-integral are never interchanged.} $B$, $E$, $Y_f$, $Y_c$ and $S$ are annual NFA account amounts in gha; $D$ is a leaky time-integral of shortfall in gha\,yr, repaid at rate $\eta$; $b_f,b_c$ are in gha\,ha$^{-1}$ and $b_{G,c}$ is a regeneration-value coefficient in gha\,yr\,ha$^{-1}$. (v) \emph{A sustainable cropland equilibrium exists.} Because \eqref{eq:Af} contains the maintenance term $\mu A_r$, a positive fast book $A_f^*$ is sustained without conversion; without it the fast book would decay to zero at every equilibrium.

Two positive feedback loops act on different books. The \emph{conversion loop} reallocates area: $A_c\downarrow\to A_f\uparrow\to$ a positive conversion contribution to $B$ (if $b_f>\Ups$) $\to K\uparrow\to P\uparrow\to S\uparrow\to A_c\downarrow$; other terms can alter the net change in $B$, but this loop is the composition mechanism of the paper. The \emph{debt loop} erodes the surviving yield: $D\uparrow\to b_f\downarrow\to B\downarrow\to S\uparrow\to D\uparrow$; it later turns against the aggregate and is one reason the mask is bounded (Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded}).

\subsection{Data and the observation operator}\label{sec:data}

The empirical analysis uses the world series of the National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts (2025 edition): aggregate biocapacity and footprint account values ($B,E$ in gha) for 1961--2022, the land-type biocapacity matrix (cropland, grazing land, forest products, fishing grounds, built-up land), and population (cap). The normalized indices, ratios and log changes used below are dimensionless. The land-type vintage incorporates a retroactive re-basing and gives a slightly higher aggregate growth ($+24.5\%$, $\Delta\ln B=+0.219$) than the aggregate series ($+23.0\%$, $\Delta\ln B=+0.207$); rather than blend the vintages we report the attribution across both (SI~\S S5.2). The independent proxies that advance past the aggregate are FAOSTAT cropland area (arable plus permanent crops; $A_f$ in ha) and a normalized FAO crop-yield index calibrated to the model account yield $b_f$ (gha\,ha$^{-1}$) at the base year either by a nominal, dimensionless cropland share $\phi_0$ of 1961 biocapacity or directly against measured NFA cropland biocapacity \citep{FAO2023}; forest area \citep{FAO2020}, historical land-use reconstructions \citep{Hurtt2011,KleinGoldewijk2017}, remote-sensing land cover \citep{ESA2017,Copernicus2019} and cropland-productivity maps \citep{Monfreda2008} form the hierarchy of extensions described in SI~\S S5.4.

The decomposition instrument is the exact logarithmic-mean (Divisia) identity. Writing $B_t=X_t+C_t$ with a proxy fast-book component $X_t=\hat b_{f,t}A_{f,t}$ and residual $C_t=B_t-X_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divisia}
\begin{split}
\Delta\ln B&=w_X\big(\Delta\ln b_f+\Delta\ln A_f\big)+w_C\,\Delta\ln C,\\
w_X&=\frac{L(X_T,X_0)}{L(B_T,B_0)},\qquad w_C=\frac{L(C_T,C_0)}{L(B_T,B_0)},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
with the logarithmic mean $L(a,b)=(a-b)/(\ln a-\ln b)$ for $a\neq b$ and $L(a,a)=a$. The identity holds exactly whenever $X$ and $C$ are positive at both endpoints; average-share weights do not (they overshoot by $\approx11\%$ here), and the level decomposition $\Delta B=\Delta X+\Delta C$ is the exact fallback when the residual is not positive (SI~\S S5.2). The extensive/intensive split uses the exact identity $d\ln B=d\ln(B/P)+d\ln P$.

\subsection{Numerical solution and verification}\label{sec:numerics}

The delay system is integrated by the method of steps with an explicit first-order Euler scheme ($\Delta t=0.05$\,yr for the demonstration run, $0.1$ and $0.05$\,yr for the drawdown sweeps). The exact positive part is used; state bounds enforce $A_c\ge\Acmin$, $A_f\ge0$, $q\in[0,q_{\max}]$, $P,D\ge0$, and $K\ge K_{\min}$. The constant pre-run histories of $B$ and $q$ cover the maximum active delay; the initial $B$ sample is computed from the initial state, not set to zero. In the SI wave experiment, $T_b(t)$ is applied consistently to the current fast-land yield, returned $B(t)$, and the delayed $B$ history used in $K(t-\tau_p)$; the base-model wave remains off. These v44 corrections remove an artificial time-zero capacity dip and the inconsistent wave bookkeeping identified in the earlier solver. Area conservation is checked at every step ($\max|A_f+A_c+A_r-A_{tot}|=2.2\times10^{-16}$\,ha). Every analytic quantity below (balance points, ceiling, eigenvalues, composition diagnostic) is recomputed and asserted before the figure is written. Drawdown thresholds are reported as bands because the operational floor-proximity criterion depends on tolerance and step size (Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}). The deposited current-quality solver evaluates the regeneration term of \eqref{eq:q} at current rather than delayed quality; because $q$ is monotone and the regenerative term is small at baseline ($b_{G,c}G_c\le0.016$\,gha\,ha$^{-1}$ versus $b_c=0.05$\,gha\,ha$^{-1}$), applying the delay changes the reported quantities by less than $1\%$ (window $149$ versus $150$\,yr; initial-population thresholds within $0.001$\,cap), and both variants are reported in SI~\S S3. The new controlled wave run and step-halving checks are in SI~\S S8. The prior v43 code/data deposit is Zenodo record 22554480; the corrected v44 source and reproduction package is available as described in the Data availability statement.
\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The composition mask and what the aggregate cannot identify}\label{sec:mask}

The per-hectare value of capital land $\Ups(q)$ is a concave quadratic in $q$ with an interior maximum at $q^*=q_{\max}/2$, where $\Ups(q^*)=b_c+b_{G,c}\rho_c q_{\max}/4=0.066\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$ at the baseline; the regenerative term there ($0.016\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$) is about one third of the direct yield ($0.05\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$; ratio $0.32$), so capital value is only modestly enhanced by regeneration. The quantity that governs what conversion does to the aggregate is the \emph{composition diagnostic}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dconv}
\dconv(q,D)=b_f(D)-\Ups(q),
\end{equation}
the difference between what a hectare yields in the fast book and what it is worth in the capital book. At the baseline $\dconv=+0.787,\ +0.784,\ +0.794\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$ at $q=0.3,\,0.6,\,0.9$: the conversion contribution is robustly positive, although other terms still determine the net account change. The three regimes $\dconv>0$ (premium: conversion contributes positively to $B$, so a conversion-driven mask is possible), $\dconv=0$ (first-order neutrality, non-generic) and $\dconv<0$ (discount: conversion contributes negatively to $B$, so conversion alone cannot produce that mask) are the only shape distinction the quality-based model admits; because $Y_c$ is linear in $A_c$ at fixed $q$, there is no area-based capacity maximum and no regime split of the kind an area-based regeneration law would produce.

\begin{proposition}[Composition identity, mask and non-identifiability]\label{prop:mask}
Let $T_b\equiv0$, $R_{rc}\equiv0$ and hold $D$ fixed, so that $b_f$ is constant. Along any solution of \eqref{eq:B}--\eqref{eq:q},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
\dot B=\dconv(q)\,u_c+b_f\big(\mu A_r-\eta_f A_f\big)+b_{G,c}\,G_c'(q)\,A_c\,\dot q .
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Whenever $u_c>0$, the conversion contribution to $\dot B$ is $\dconv(q)u_c$ and is positive if and only if $\dconv(q)>0$; with $R_{rc}=0$, the same transfer gives $\dot A_c=-u_c<0$. At any state where the maintenance--retirement imbalance and quality-growth terms vanish ($\mu A_r=\eta_f A_f$ and $\dot q=0$), the net derivative is $\dot B=\dconv(q)u_c$, so its sign has this same condition.
\item In the reduced frozen-demand, fixed-$D$ case in which $\mu A_r=\eta_f A_f$ and $\dot q=0$ throughout, and with $\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1$, $\dconv>0$ implies $\dot S=-\dot B=-\dconv u_c<0$ whenever $S>0$. Thus the shortfall decreases to zero if the balance is reached first; if the floor constraint becomes active first, $A_c$ approaches $\Acmin$ asymptotically and the shortfall can persist. This monotonicity is not asserted when the other terms in the full model are nonzero.
\item At fixed $b_f>0$ and fixed $q$ (so that $\Ups(q)>0$), the observation $B=b_fA_f+\Ups(q)A_c$ is one scalar constraint on two land areas. At an interior feasible state it leaves a local continuum of decompositions: a sufficiently small $\delta A_c$ can be offset by $\delta A_f=-(\Ups/b_f)\delta A_c$, with $\delta A_r=-\delta A_f-\delta A_c$, while preserving $B$ and the area total, provided the area bounds remain satisfied. Thus an aggregate-only observation does not locally identify $A_c$. For a finite observed change $\Delta B$, the algebraic offset $\Delta A_f=(\Delta B-\Ups\Delta A_c)/b_f$ is feasible only when the resulting land areas respect their bounds; the admissible range depends on the state and need not be the whole interval from $\Acmin-A_c(0)$ to zero. If $b_f$ and $A_f$ are independently observed, the residual $C=B-b_fA_f=\Ups(q)A_c$ identifies that product, not $\Ups$ and $A_c$ separately. An independent observation of $A_c$ would identify the effective value $\Ups(q)=C/A_c$; even then, $b_c$, $b_{G,c}$, $\rho_c$, and $q$ are not separately identified from a single value. With $q$ independently observed over a sufficiently informative range, $\Ups(q)$ can at most identify $b_c$ and the product $b_{G,c}\rho_c$, not $b_{G,c}$ and $\rho_c$ separately.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:B} with $b_f$ constant and substitute \eqref{eq:Ac}--\eqref{eq:Af} with $R_{rc}=0$: $\dot B=b_f(u_c+\mu A_r-\eta_fA_f)-\Ups u_c+b_{G,c}G_c'(q)A_c\dot q$, which is \eqref{eq:identity}. (i) Follows from \eqref{eq:identity} with $R_{rc}=0$; when the maintenance--retirement imbalance and quality-growth terms vanish, only the conversion term remains. (ii) Under the reduced-case conditions in the statement, $S=E-B$ and $\dot S=-\dot B=-\dconv u_c<0$ on $S>0$. Since $S$ is bounded below by zero and $A_c$ is bounded below by $\Acmin$, conversion either closes the shortfall or is limited by the floor. (iii) At fixed $b_f$ and $q$, the observation map is linear; an interior feasible point admits the stated small perturbations along a level set of $B$, while finite changes must also obey the area bounds. The residual $C$ identifies only $\Ups(q)A_c$ unless $A_c$ is independently observed; the parameter combination follows from the definition of $\Ups(q)$.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:model}a shows the mask at the baseline under frozen demand $E=0.825$\,gha (restoration off, $A_{f0}=0.6$\,ha, $A_{c0}=1.5$\,ha, dimensionless $q_0=0.9$, $A_r(0)=0.4$\,ha). Biocapacity rises from $0.594$ to $0.825$\,gha ($+39\%$) while capital land area $A_c$ falls from $1.500$ to $0.808$\,ha ($-46\%$) and fast land $A_f$ rises from $0.600$ to $0.923$\,ha. The dimensionless quality state $q$ rises from $0.9$ to $1.0$, but because $q_0=0.9>q^*=q_{\max}/2=0.5$, this rise lies on the descending side of the concave per-hectare function $\Ups(q)$: its value falls from $0.05576$ to $0.05000$\,gha\,ha$^{-1}$. Accordingly, the modeled capital contribution $\Ups(q)A_c$ to $B$ falls from $0.08364$ to $0.04038$\,gha (by $51.7\%$, about $52\%$), distinct from the $46\%$ decline in capital-land area. The area changes are one-to-one ($\Delta A_f+\Delta A_c+\Delta A_r=0$ to machine precision). The dynamics are fast at first and then asymptotic: because the deficit-side land subsystem is affine (Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}(ii)), the approach to the balance point is exponential at the slow rate $|\lambda_1|=0.054\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ (e-folding $\approx19$\,yr), so within $25$\,yr $98\%$ of the rise in $B$ and $85\%$ of the fall in $A_c$ have occurred ($B=0.820$\,gha, $A_c=0.913$\,ha; $97\%$ of the fall by $50$\,yr), while the strict inequalities $\dot B>0$ and $\dot A_c<0$ persist for $\approx150$\,yr at numerical resolution. The slow tail is reserve equilibration: retirement of fast land to the reserve lowers the fast flow, re-opens a small shortfall, and converts a little more capital, until the reserve reaches its equilibrium share $A_r/A_f=\eta_f/\mu$. The window is therefore not a fixed horizon: its scale is set by $\lambda_1$, which depends on $\tconv$, $\mu$, $\eta_f$ and the yield ratio $b_c/b_f$ (SI~\S S2.2), and its amplitude by $\dconv$ and the initial deficit.

Two consequences of Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}(iii) frame the empirical analysis of Section~\ref{sec:empirical}. First, the composition signature ($B$ rises while $A_c$ falls) is contemporaneously observable only in the two-book series; a monitor who sees the aggregate alone cannot distinguish a composition mask from broad growth. Second, independent proxies for the fast book ($A_f$, $b_f$) convert the aggregate into a measured fast book and a residual, which is the most that can be identified without independent ecological valuation of the capital book. We call the net two-book signature $B$ rising while $A_c$ falls the \emph{composition mask}; its conversion component is positive when $\dconv>0$, but the net sign of $\dot B$ also depends on the other terms in \eqref{eq:identity}. The alternative \emph{liquidation mask} is $E$ steady while $A_c$ falls, which does not require a positive conversion contribution. The two-book series makes these readings distinguishable.

\subsection{The drawdown is a demand-driven floor collision, not a fold}\label{sec:ceiling}

With endogenous population there is no interior steady state with active conversion: at a demographic equilibrium $P=K=B/e$, so $E=B$ and $S=0$. Conversion is therefore a transient regime, and its steady states exist only under a frozen exogenous demand pinned above the flow. The frozen-demand system is where the question of a catastrophic shift must be posed, and it has a complete answer.

\begin{proposition}[Balance point, demand ceiling and affine deficit-side dynamics]\label{prop:ceiling}
Assume frozen demand $E$, $T_b\equiv0$, $R_{rc}\equiv0$, $q=q_{\max}$ (the attractor of \eqref{eq:q} for $q(0)>0$) and $D=0$. Let $k=\mu/(\mu+\eta_f)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Beq}
B_{eq}(A_c)=b_{f0}\,k\,(A_{tot}-A_c)+b_cA_c=b_{f0}kA_{tot}-(b_{f0}k-b_c)A_c .
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Equilibria of the land subsystem on the no-conversion face $S=0$ satisfy $A_f=k(A_{tot}-A_c)$, $A_r=(1-k)(A_{tot}-A_c)$ and $B_{eq}(A_c)\ge E$. If $b_{f0}k>b_c$, $B_{eq}$ is strictly decreasing, so the balance point $B_{eq}(A_c^*)=E$ is unique,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Acstar}
A_c^*(E)=\frac{b_{f0}kA_{tot}-E}{b_{f0}k-b_c},
\end{equation}
The algebraic root lies in the feasible land interval $A_c\in[\Acmin,A_{tot}]$ exactly when $B_{eq}(A_{tot})\le E\le\Eceil:=B_{eq}(\Acmin)$; it lies strictly above $\Acmin$ iff $E<\Eceil$. For $E<B_{eq}(A_{tot})$, the root lies above $A_{tot}$ and feasible no-conversion equilibria already satisfy $B_{eq}\ge E$.
\item On the deficit side ($S>0$, $S/b_f<A_c-\Acmin$) the land subsystem is affine in $z=(A_f,A_c)$, $\dot z=Jz+c$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
J=\begin{pmatrix}-\tconv^{-1}-\mu-\eta_f & -\mu-\dfrac{b_c}{b_f\tconv}\\[8pt] \tconv^{-1} & \dfrac{b_c}{b_f\tconv}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det J=\frac{\mu+\eta_f}{\tconv}\Big(k-\frac{b_c}{b_f}\Big),
\end{equation}
and $\operatorname{tr}J<0$.
$J$ does not depend on $E$ or on the state, $\det J>0$ exactly when $B_{eq}$ is decreasing, and the fixed point of the affine system is the balance point of (i). For $B_{eq}(A_{tot})\le E<\Eceil$ the balance point lies above the typed floor and every trajectory that remains in this regime converges to it at the constant rates given by the eigenvalues of $J$; for $E>\Eceil$ the affine fixed point lies below the floor, so the trajectory approaches the face $A_c=\Acmin$ asymptotically, with a limiting persistent shortfall $S^*=E-\Eceil>0$. The ceiling is a collision of the equilibrium with a face of the admissible set, not a bifurcation of the vector field: there is no second branch, no fold, no hysteresis, and the leading eigenvalue does not tend to zero as $E\to\Eceil$.
\item Beyond the ceiling, with degradation active, the debt converges to the unique root $D^*(E)$ of $\eta D=E-B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)$, where $B_{eq}(\cdot\,;D)$ uses $b_{f0}e^{-\alpha D}$, provided $\eta>\alpha b_{f0}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)$; $D^*$ is increasing in $E$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) With $S=0$ and $R_{rc}=0$, \eqref{eq:Af} gives $\mu A_r=\eta_fA_f$, hence $A_r/A_f=\eta_f/\mu$ and, with $A_r=A_{tot}-A_f-A_c$, $A_f=k(A_{tot}-A_c)$; substituting into \eqref{eq:B} with $q=q_{\max}$ ($\Ups=b_c$) and $D=0$ gives $B=B_{eq}(A_c)$, and $S=0$ requires $B\ge E$. Strict monotonicity and \eqref{eq:Acstar} follow from $b_{f0}k>b_c$. (ii) On the deficit side $u_c=(E-b_fA_f-b_cA_c)/(b_f\tconv)$ is affine in $z$; substituting into \eqref{eq:Ac}--\eqref{eq:Af} with $A_r=A_{tot}-A_f-A_c$ gives $J$ and a constant vector $c$ by direct differentiation; $\det J$ follows by expansion, and $\operatorname{tr}J=-\tconv^{-1}(1-b_c/b_f)-\mu-\eta_f<0$ for $b_c<b_f$. The fixed point solves $u_c=0$ and $\mu A_r=\eta_fA_f$, i.e.\ it is the balance point. Because $J$ is constant, convergence rates are constant along the branch. (iii) The map $D\mapsto\eta D+B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)-E$ is continuous, negative at $D=0$ when $E>\Eceil$, tends to $+\infty$, and is strictly increasing when $\eta>\alpha b_{f0}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)\ge-\partial_DB_{eq}$; hence it has a unique root, which increases with $E$.
\end{proof}

At the baseline $k=0.5455$, the slope $b_{f0}k-b_c=0.414\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$, and $\Eceil=1.138\,\mathrm{gha}$; the balance points $A_c^*(E)=1.110,\,0.626,\,0.385,\,0.143,\,0.070\,\mathrm{ha}$ at $E=0.70,\,0.90,\,1.00,\,1.10,\,1.13\,\mathrm{gha}$ agree with the integrated equilibria (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}b). The eigenvalues of $J$ are real and negative, $\lambda_1=-0.0537\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ and $\lambda_2=-0.998\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$; $\lambda_1$ varies only weakly with the fast-land yield, from $-0.0522\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ at $b_f=0.60$ to $-0.0556\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ at $b_f=2.00$ (Table~\ref{tab:model}a), and never changes sign. There is no universal scalar stability index: the conversion-loop gain is the state- and parameter-dependent $\dconv$, of order $1\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}}$, and the return rate is $|\lambda_1|$ of order $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$. Beyond the ceiling the debt fixed points are $D^*=1.19$ and $5.38$\,gha\,yr at $E=1.158$ and $1.238$\,gha (integrated values $1.21$ and $5.39$\,gha\,yr); the monotonicity condition of (iii) holds with margin ($\alpha b_{f0}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)=0.034\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}<\eta=0.05\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$), so under frozen demand the debt saturates rather than running away.

The statement ``no critical slowing down'' is precise here. By critical slowing down we mean that the least-negative eigenvalue of the relevant linearization tends to $0^-$ as a parameter approaches a fold, so that the return time $T_{ret}\approx-1/\lambda_1$ diverges. In Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling} the linearization is constant along the entire branch, so neither $\lambda_1$ nor $T_{ret}\approx19$\,yr changes as $E\to\Eceil$. The only early-warning signal the model offers is a \emph{boundary} signal, the capital book approaching its typed floor, which is observable only in the two-book series.

With endogenous demand the same structure appears as a continuous drawdown. Figure~\ref{fig:model}c shows the minimum capital land reached during a run as a function of initial population $P_0$ for four initial capital-land areas $A_{c0}$: the curves are continuous and monotone, the floor is approached asymptotically (because $u_c\le(A_c-\Acmin)/\tconv$, the distance to the floor decays exponentially during a sustained deficit and is never crossed), and higher initial capital-land area supports higher demand before the floor is approached. The ``collapse thresholds'' of the earlier literature on this model are therefore operational quantities: Table~\ref{tab:model}b reports the initial demand at which the floor is approached to within $10^{-2}$, $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-6}$\,ha of its value. The $10^{-6}$\,ha floor-proximity thresholds ($P_0\approx2.41,\,2.70,\,2.98,\,3.14$\,cap for $A_{c0}=0.40,\,0.70,\,1.00,\,1.18$\,ha) are obtained by bisection on that tolerance criterion; they rise monotonically with initial capital, as do the looser criteria, and no criterion exhibits a jump. Two robustness checks bear on the predictions of Section~\ref{sec:predictions}: the initial-population floor-proximity threshold at $A_{c0}=1.0$\,ha is identical to three decimals for $\tau_g=5$, $20$ and $40$\,yr, and identical to three decimals with the restoration coefficient $\chi_r=0.10$\,gha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ and $\chi_r=0$\,gha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ (SI~\S S4). The first reflects the small leverage of the regenerative term at the baseline and the monotone quality dynamics; the second reflects the fact that a surplus-gated flow is inert during the shortfall that produces the drawdown.

\subsection{The mask is bounded: technology gain is parameter-bounded, debt erosion is shortfall-bounded}\label{sec:bounded}

Both the technology wave and the degradation act on the same coefficient, the fast-land yield $b_f$ of \eqref{eq:bf}, and they do so multiplicatively. This yields a structural asymmetry.

\begin{proposition}[Boundedness asymmetry]\label{prop:bounded}
Under \eqref{eq:bf}--\eqref{eq:Tb}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the technology factor satisfies $1\le(b_{f0}+T_b(t))/b_{f0}\le1+\Delta b/b_{f0}$ for all $t$, so the logarithmic gain of the yield channel is bounded by $\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})$ uniformly in time ($0.78$ at the baseline);
\item the debt satisfies $D(t)\le\max\{D(0),\ \sup_{s\le t}\pos{E-B}(s)/\eta\}$, and $D\to S^*/\eta$ whenever the shortfall settles at $S^*$; the degradation factor $e^{-\alpha D}$ is therefore bounded below only through the sustained shortfall;
\item the degradation loss exceeds the maximal technology gain once the sustained shortfall exceeds $S^\dagger=(\eta/\alpha)\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})$, equal to $1.30\,\mathrm{gha}$ at the baseline.
\end{enumerate}
Under an additive degradation law $b_f=b_{f0}+T_b-\alpha D$ the two channels enter symmetrically and (iii) reduces to a comparison of $\Delta b$ with $\alpha D^*$; the asymmetry is a property of the multiplicative form.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) is $0\le T_b\le\Delta b$. (ii) From \eqref{eq:D}, $\dot D\le\bar S-\eta D$ with $\bar S=\sup_{s\le t}\pos{E-B}$, so $D(t)\le\max\{D(0),\bar S/\eta\}$ by comparison; if $\pos{E-B}\to S^*$ then $D\to S^*/\eta$. (iii) compares $\alpha S^*/\eta$ with $\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})$.
\end{proof}

The proposition corrects a tempting overstatement: debt does not compound without bound under a fixed shortfall -- it saturates at $S^*/\eta$ -- but its ceiling is set by the shortfall rather than by a parameter, and the shortfall is itself raised by demand growth. At the baseline the crossover shortfall $S^\dagger\approx1.3\,\mathrm{gha}$ exceeds the baseline biocapacity, so at these parameters the debt channel is a slow erosion rather than a reckoning; what bounds the mask at the baseline is the balance-point mechanism of Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}(ii), with debt erosion as the second, slower limit. A second coupling is possible: because $K=B/e$, a technology-driven rise in $B$ can raise carrying capacity and, after the demographic delay, demand. Whether this produces a larger all-time debt peak depends on the initial state and the timing of the response; it is not a sign-general theorem. In the controlled matched experiment in SI~\S S8, with $P_0=1.0$\,cap, the active $T_b$ run reaches a global $D$ maximum of $0.02020$\,gha\,yr versus $0.00813$\,gha\,yr without the wave (ratio $2.49$). At the reference $P_0=1.2$\,cap, the all-time peak is instead lower with the wave ($0.05104$ versus $0.06122$\,gha\,yr), while the maximum over $t\ge t_{wave}=40$\,yr is higher ($0.02518$ versus $0.01085$\,gha\,yr; ratio $2.32$). This delayed-rebound response is conditional on the declared parameters and initial demand, and the global maximum must not be conflated with the post-wave-center maximum. The active-wave experiment is a diagnostic extension, not a forecast or an empirical estimate.

\subsection{Recovery requires an explicit, gated restoration flow}\label{sec:recovery}

The model is structurally asymmetric: $A_c\to A_f$ proceeds through conversion, but no term in \eqref{eq:Ac}--\eqref{eq:Ar} returns land to the capital book except the restoration flow $R_{rc}$. Fast-land retirement sends land to the reserve, not to capital. Reducing population, footprint or shortfall therefore does not reverse prior conversion: with $\chi_r=0$ the capital book stays where the shortfall left it. In the endogenous-demand run with $A_{c0}=1.0$\,ha and $P_0=2.0$\,cap, capital land falls to $0.398$\,ha within four years and remains there without restoration, whereas with the surplus-gated flow ($\chi_r=0.10$\,gha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$) it recovers to $0.828$\,ha, a dimensionless recovery fraction $\mathcal R_c=(A_c(T)-A_c^{\mathrm{deg}})/(A_c^{\mathrm{init}}-A_c^{\mathrm{deg}})=0.71$ (SI~\S S4.3). The sign of the gate matters. A surplus-gated flow, active when $B>E$, is the ecologically sign-correct rewilding: it retires land only when the system has spare capacity, but it is inert during the shortfall it must eventually address -- which is why the drawdown thresholds of Table~\ref{tab:model}b do not depend on $\chi_r$ -- and once a surplus opens it can over-restore while the supported population re-equilibrates downward, so a target-level stopping rule is needed for a clean $\mathcal R_c=1$ (SI~\S S4.4). A deficit-gated flow, active when $E>B$, is the one gate that acts during the shortfall, but it retires productive land exactly when provision is short and can deepen the near-term deficit; at the tested rates conversion outpaces it, so it is necessary but insufficient and must be sized or capped. Neither gate is an unqualified good, and the gate-sign asymmetry is phase-dependent rather than a universal ranking.

\subsection{Measured composition of aggregate biocapacity, 1961--2022}\label{sec:empirical}

The NFA world series records annual account values $B,E$ in gha and population $P$ in cap; the per-capita levels $B/P,E/P$ are in gha\,cap$^{-1}$. Indices, ratios and logarithmic changes below are dimensionless. The identity $d\ln B=d\ln(B/P)+d\ln P$ applied to the NFA world series gives the aggregation face of the result (Fig.~\ref{fig:aggregation}). Aggregate biocapacity rose $+23.0\%$ ($\Delta\ln B=+0.207$, $+0.339\%\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$); per-capita biocapacity fell $-52.9\%$ ($\Delta\ln(B/P)=-0.752$, $-1.23\%\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$); the per-capita footprint rose $+16.9\%$; and the biocapacity ratio $R_B=E/B$ rose from $0.718$ to $1.782$, crossing $1$ in 1971. Because the identity is exact (dimensionless residual $<10^{-16}$) the split is unambiguous: population contributed $+0.959$ ($+464\%$ of $\Delta\ln B$) and the per-capita term $-0.752$ ($-364\%$). This is the extensive/intensive split, not a causal claim (biocapacity is computed from area and yield independently of $P$), and it is not yet the two-land composition mask: the same aggregate that hides a per-capita halving is the one that would hide a capital-land drawdown.

\subsection{Falsifiable predictions}\label{sec:predictions}

The model's testable content, framed against two-book series ($B$, $A_c$, $A_f$ and land-cover/yield proxies) rather than the aggregate, is the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=P\arabic*.,leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Mask signature.} In the baseline frozen-demand run, the model predicts a finite interval with $\dot A_c<0$ and $\dot B>0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}a). The conversion contribution is positive exactly when $\dconv>0$, but the net sign of $\dot B$ also depends on maintenance/retirement, quality growth, debt, technology, and restoration. With baseline shares $\sigma_f=\sigma_c=1$, active conversion requires $R_B=E/B>1$; in the reserved-flow extension with $\sigma_f,\sigma_c<1$, conversion can occur even when $R_B\le1$, which is not a baseline result. A two-book series distinguishes the net composition signature ($B$ rises while $A_c$ falls) from a liquidation mask ($E$ steady while $A_c$ falls) (Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}).
\item \textbf{Window scale.} The duration of the mask is set by the slow eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ (through $\tconv$, $\mu$, $\eta_f$, $b_c/b_f$) and its amplitude by $\dconv$ and the deficit, not by a fixed horizon; the bulk of conversion precedes the closing of the deficit by one to two e-folding times.
\item \textbf{No critical-slowing precursor.} The return time does not grow as demand approaches the ceiling, and the drawdown depth is continuous in demand (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}c); the observable warning is the capital book approaching its typed floor (Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}). This is a negative prediction: a catastrophic-shift early-warning indicator is not expected to precede the two-land drawdown.
\item \textbf{Demand threshold, not delay threshold.} The operational initial-demand threshold for approaching the capital floor rises with initial capital (Table~\ref{tab:model}b) and is insensitive to the regeneration delay (identical to $10^{-3}$ for $\tau_g=5$--$40$\,yr), because the regenerative share of capital value is small and quality is monotone; a one-stock regeneration cliff is not expected.
\item \textbf{Recovery requires a gated flow.} Reducing demand does not restore the capital book; recovery requires an explicit restoration flow whose gate sign determines whether it is inert during the shortfall (surplus-gated; thresholds unchanged by $\chi_r$) or deepens it (deficit-gated).
\end{enumerate}
The identifiability statement of Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}(iii) is not a prediction but a constraint on testing: P1--P5 can be tested only with independent $A_c$ and $b_f$ proxies, never with the aggregate alone.
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the two-land structure changes, and what it removes}

The results depart from the single-stock, single-aggregate reading of the environment that dominates the resource and sustainability literature \citep{Schaefer1954,May1973,Meadows1972,Meadows2004,WackernagelRees1996,BranderTaylor1998} in three respects, each of which is a negative result as much as a positive one. First, the conversion component can raise aggregate capacity while capital-land area $A_c$ falls, but this bounded composition effect does not by itself determine the net change in the aggregate: Proposition~\ref{prop:mask} gives the exact sign condition for the conversion contribution, and Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded} explains why the technology/debt mask cannot persist. Second, the drawdown of capital-land area $A_c$ is a demand-driven floor collision. The alternative-stable-states literature \citep{Scheffer2001} predicts that a system approaching a fold shows critical slowing down, hysteresis and a discontinuous jump; none of these is present here, and their absence is structural (Table~\ref{tab:neg}): the deficit-side dynamics are affine with constant real eigenvalues, the equilibrium leaves the admissible set through a face rather than through a bifurcation, and the drawdown depth is continuous in demand. Third, recovery is not the reverse of degradation; it requires an explicit, gated flow, and the gate sign is a design decision with phase-dependent consequences.

The negative results have a direct bearing on early-warning practice. Generic indicators of critical slowing down (rising autocorrelation and variance) are motivated by the approach to a fold \citep{Scheffer2001}. In a two-book system in which the drawdown is a boundary collision, such indicators would not be expected to fire, and their silence should not be read as safety. The informative signal is the capital book itself, which requires the two-book series.

Several quantities that circulate for models of this kind are deliberately not claimed here. The two-gain Hopf classification of which delay destabilizes, the associated critical delays, and the reference eigenvalue of order $+0.6\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ belong to a single-stock fast--slow reduction that we retain only as a comparator in SI~\S S7, where its equations, equilibrium family and figures are given; none of its features is carried into the two-land results. The ``18--20\,yr regeneration cliff'' of the one-stock comparator does not survive the two-land structure (Table~\ref{tab:neg}). What is structural in the two-land model is the composition logic and the identifiability limit; what is conditional on effective parameters is the sign and size of $\dconv$ and the location of the ceiling.

\subsection{Measurement implications}

The methodological result of the paper is the identifiability limit. Biocapacity in global hectares is a value-weighted scalar, not a count of the underlying assets. Given only that a portfolio's value rose by $20\%$, one cannot tell whether every holding grew a little or some made large gains while others quietly fell; the scalar gloss hides the composition. This is the generic limit of any value-weighted composite \citep{Nardo2008}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}(iii) states it for biocapacity: the aggregate identifies one linear combination of the areas, and the capital-book parameters enter only through identifiable combinations. The aggregate is therefore a weak-sustainability index in the precise sense of the companion assessment work \citep{Abaee2026b}: it is the linear, perfectly substitutable member of the aggregator family (its Lemma~A), and it can satisfy an aggregate floor while the typed floor on $A_c$ is violated by exactly the amount that the fast-land yield gains against the capital loss. In that family no positive-elasticity aggregator is uniformly safe and only the typed, non-compensating one is (its Theorem~S2(ii)). Under exact-tube semantics, in which a transition is safe only if every state along it satisfies the constraints \citep{Abaee2026b}, the passage during which $A_c$ falls is itself a violation even if the endpoint recovers -- a reason to report through-time trajectories and boundary curves, not endpoint attractors.

For the National Footprint Accounts the implication is concrete. The accounts are account-based and do not capture soil erosion, deforestation, groundwater depletion or other cumulative degradation, so estimated biocapacity likely overstates the real base and the footprint understates the overshoot \citep{Wackernagel2002,Borucke2013}; critics have made the point forcefully \citep{Blomqvist2013,GiampietroSaltelli2014,Galli2016,vdBerghGrazi2015}. The present results add a different and more specific point: even an internally consistent aggregate cannot be inverted into its components. A growth headline for total biocapacity carries no information about whether cropland growth is compensating for stagnation or offsetting declines elsewhere, and the measured accounts show that this is exactly what happened in 1961--2022 (Fig.~\ref{fig:composition}). The informative assessment reports components with the total -- at minimum component endpoint ratios, log contributions, attribution shares, data vintage and endpoint sensitivity -- at every level at which an aggregate is used. Parallel observations have been made for human appropriation of net primary production and for land-management data \citep{Haberl2007,Krausmann2013,Erb2017,Carvalhais2014}: the composition of an aggregate account value, not its level, carries the sustainability information.

The model's capital decline has a different evidential status from those account movements. In the frozen-demand baseline (Fig.~\ref{fig:model}a), modeled capital land $A_c$ falls from $1.500$ to $0.808$\,ha while $B$ rises from $0.594$ to $0.825$\,gha; because restoration is off in that run, the reduced land allocation persists after the shortfall closes. The quality state $q$ rises from $0.9$ to $1.0$, but since $q_0=0.9>q^*=q_{\max}/2=0.5$, this rise lies on the descending side of the concave per-hectare function $\Ups(q)$. The logistic regeneration term falls, so $\Ups(q)$ declines from $0.05576$ to $0.05000$\,gha\,ha$^{-1}$; the effective capital account, i.e.\ the modeled capital contribution $\Ups(q)A_c$ to $B$, consequently falls from $0.08364$ to $0.04038$\,gha (by $51.7\%$, about $52\%$), distinct from the $46.2\%$ decline in capital-land area. This is a conditional model trajectory, not an estimate of a global physical-stock trend. NFA's forest-products, grazing and fishing categories are annual account values built from land classes, yields and conversion factors. Their modest declines are relevant composition signals, but they do not identify $\Delta A_c$, biomass, soil condition or any other physical capital-stock change.

A defensible physical-stock scoreboard should therefore preserve a vector of component accounts rather than add unlike stocks into a single unweighted total. Report ecosystem extent (for example, forest or wetland area in ha) separately from condition or stock measures (for example, forest growing stock or biomass in m$^3$ or tonnes of carbon, soil organic carbon in tonnes of carbon for a stated depth, fish spawning-stock biomass in tonnes, and freshwater storage in m$^3$). Keep regenerative and appropriation flows separate from stocks: net annual increment, soil-carbon accumulation or loss, fish recruitment and catch, and water recharge and withdrawal are not interchangeable with the corresponding stock levels. For each component, provide the reference year, spatial boundary, physical unit, absolute change and baseline ratio, uncertainty, data vintage, and any relevant ecological floor or reference range. Do not double-count nested measures such as forest extent and biomass over the same area. The components can be interpreted jointly as a noncompensating dashboard; if a single index is required, its valuation, normalization, weights, and permitted substitution must be explicit rather than hidden inside the unit. This approach is consistent with the separate physical ecosystem-extent and condition accounts in SEEA Ecosystem Accounting, with services and monetary presentations kept distinct \citep{UN2021SEEA_EA}. Mapping such a scoreboard to the model's effective $A_c$ and quality $q$ still requires a declared observation operator and independent measurements; the NFA biocapacity total alone cannot supply it.

The empirical program that follows is a flux reconstruction, not a curve fit. The observation operator of Section~\ref{sec:data} is a hierarchy: NFA land-type biocapacity with physical area and yield factors where obtainable; FAOSTAT area and yields as independent external proxies (used here); remote-sensing land cover and historical reconstructions for validation and extension (SI~\S S5.4). Forest area is a sentinel proxy for $A_c$, not the whole capital book; capital-land regeneration is unobserved in the accounts and would require growing-stock or net-annual-increment data. The proxies do not solve global-hectare identifiability; they make the composition mechanism an independently anchored, falsifiable statement rather than an asserted one.

\subsection{Policy operators}

Three operators follow from the typed-floor structure and are developed in SI~\S S6. Freezing conversion -- capping $A_f$ or flooring $A_c$ -- is the single operator that guarantees $A_c\ge\Acmin$; a governor that observes only the composite $B$ cannot guarantee a capital-land floor, and the failure is informational rather than dynamical. Restoration is protective only when surplus-gated, and a deficit-gated flow can deepen the shortfall it is meant to relieve; this is the protective, as opposed to mobilizing, institutional signature of the delay companion \citep{Abaee2026a}. A Half-Earth reservation \citep{Wilson2016}, $E\le\sigma B$ with $\sigma<1$, succeeds without liquidation on the nominal path, but this is a nominal-path consequence until it is certified against a declared disturbance class under a preregistered retention rule, and we state it as such.

\subsection{Relation to companion work}

The accounting layer -- the closed typed-flux ledger, conservation and donor-limitation rules, depletion arithmetic and the no-non-negative-weighting aggregation obstruction -- is the object of the ledger companion \citep{Abaee2026c}, which owns the static layer, cedes delay dynamics to its companions and registers a two-pool dynamical model as an open gap; the present paper supplies that minimal dynamical realization for the global-hectare case. The aggregation theorem that places $B$ at the weak extreme of the aggregator family is from the assessment companion \citep{Abaee2026b}, and the institutional-delay regime is from the governance companion \citep{Abaee2026a}; the delays here are ecological (regeneration, demography), not institutional. We cite, and do not re-derive, those results.

\subsection{Limitations}

The capital land is an effective class, not a single land type; the single regeneration timescale, the composition diagnostic and the ceiling are effective-parameter statements, and the full vector decomposition is cited rather than re-derived. Most parameters are representative rather than estimated; $b_c$, $b_{G,c}$ and $\rho_c$ are not identifiable from a cross-section, and the regeneration anchors bound a band rather than calibrate $\rho_c$ (the anchors concern the regeneration rate, not the lag $\tau_g$). The per-capita footprint is held constant to isolate the stock--flow--demand feedbacks; endogenizing $e$ is an extension. The composition window is bounded and demand-driven; without a sustained shortfall or a technology/demand feedback it closes at the balance point. Stability at $S=0$ is one-sided because the positive part is exact. Drawdown thresholds are tolerance-dependent bands; a small margin next to that band is reported as such and not as a sharp boundary. The debt-repayment rate is set physical and non-singular ($\eta=0.05\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$); the $\eta\to0$ case removes the debt equilibrium and is excluded by a floor, and outcomes are insensitive to $\eta$ over a modest positive range. The accounts do not separate yield from area, which is the identifiability limitation the paper is about rather than an estimation error. Finally, the delayed-regeneration term is implemented at the current quality in the deposited solver (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}); we have verified that applying the delay changes no reported quantity by more than $1\%$, and the corrected variant is deposited alongside.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

A single aggregate of annual bioproductive supply can conceal the composition of what is growing, and the concealment is structural. In a minimal two-land model with conserved area, a separate quality state and demand-driven conversion, the conversion contribution to aggregate change is positive when the fast-land yield exceeds the per-hectare value of capital land, while the active area transfer reduces capital-land area $A_c$; other terms determine whether the net aggregate rises or falls (Proposition~\ref{prop:mask}). An aggregate-only observation does not locally identify the capital-land area loss, and independent fast-book proxies identify at most a measured fast book and an accounting residual. The drawdown of capital-land area $A_c$ under sustained demand is a floor collision at a demand ceiling, not a catastrophic shift: the deficit-side dynamics are affine, the leading eigenvalue is constant along the branch, there is no fold, no hysteresis and no critical slowing down, and the drawdown depth is a continuous function of demand (Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}). The mask is bounded because technology lifts the fast yield by at most a fixed factor whereas debt erosion is limited only by the sustained shortfall (Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded}), and recovery requires an explicit restoration flow whose gate sign matters. In the measured National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts for 1961--2022, aggregate biocapacity grew $23\%$ while per-capita biocapacity halved, the exact Divisia decomposition attributes the growth to cropland yield with a negative non-cropland residual, and the land-type accounts show cropland biocapacity growing $2.85\times$ against a flat non-cropland book that is itself the net of declining forest, grazing and fishing biocapacity and rising built-up land. These results establish an identifiability property of aggregate biocapacity and a measured composition pattern; they are not a forecast and do not show a measured decline in the model's effective capital-land area $A_c$, still less in any specific physical stock. The practical reporting rule has two parts: report NFA components alongside any aggregate, and track heterogeneous physical assets in a component-level scoreboard with explicit units, boundaries, condition measures, and separate flows. Do not collapse unlike stocks into an unweighted sum; if a single scalar is required, declare its valuation, weights, and permitted substitution. Neither the NFA aggregate nor the physical scoreboard identifies the model's effective $A_c$ without an observation operator. A modern economy can run for decades on a harvest that looks healthy while the orchard behind it quietly shrinks; the harvest number will not say so, and only the two-book series can.
