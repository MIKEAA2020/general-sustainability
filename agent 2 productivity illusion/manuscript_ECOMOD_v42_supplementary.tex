\paragraph{Stated extensions (off in every reported run).} (i) A timber-liquidation channel $L_c=\pos{H_c-\sigma_cY_c}/b_{G,c}$ removes capital area when a timber flow target $H_c$ exceeds the harvestable capital flow; $\gamma=1/b_{G,c}$ (ha\,gha$^{-1}$) applies only to this channel. (ii) A direct fast-to-capital restoration $R_{fc}=\chi A_f\pos{B-E}$ (surplus-gated) can replace or supplement $R_{rc}$. (iii) A reservation $\sigma_f,\sigma_c<1$ withholds a share of each flow from demand (Section~\ref{si:policy}). (iv) The technology wave $T_b$ of Eq.~(14) is available but is off in all reported runs; applying it non-contingently in the recovery experiments produces artefacts (Section~\ref{si:gates}). (v) An endogenous per-capita footprint $e(t)$ is an offered extension not implemented here.

\paragraph{Conventions.} The positive part is exact; conversion is a rate through $\tconv$; land is conserved one-to-one across $A_f,A_c,A_r$ while $q$ is a separate capacity book; flows (gha\,yr$^{-1}$) and accumulations (gha) are never interchanged; a sustainable fast book exists because of the maintenance term $\mu A_r$. The reserve floor $A_r^{\min}$ and arable ceiling $A_f^{\max}$ are declared domain constraints that are checked after each run (never violated in any reported run: the minimum reserve in the drawdown sweeps is $0.80$\,ha) rather than enforced by clamps; $\Acmin$, $A_f\ge0$, $q\in[0,q_{\max}]$ and $K_{\min}$ are enforced.

\section{Derivations and proofs}\label{si:proofs}

\subsection{The composition identity with varying debt and quality}\label{si:identity}

Differentiating $B=b_fA_f+\Ups(q)A_c$ with $b_f=(b_{f0}+T_b)e^{-\alpha D}$ and substituting Eqs.~(6)--(7) gives the general identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:si_identity}
\dot B=\dconv\,u_c+b_f\big(\mu A_r-\eta_fA_f\big)+b_{G,c}G_c'(q)A_c\,\dot q+\Ups R_{rc}-\alpha\dot D\,b_fA_f+\frac{\dot T_b}{b_{f0}+T_b}\,b_fA_f .
\end{equation}
The conversion term has the sign of $\dconv$; the quality term has the sign of $G_c'(q)$ (non-negative for $q\le q_{\max}/2$, non-positive above it, since $\dot q\ge0$) and is bounded in magnitude by $b_{G,c}\rho_c\dot qA_c$, which is small at the baseline; the debt term is non-positive while debt accrues; the technology term is non-negative while the wave rises; the restoration term is non-negative. Proposition~1 of the paper is the case $T_b\equiv0$, $R_{rc}\equiv0$, $D$ fixed. In the demonstration run $D$ is not fixed (it rises to $0.20$ and decays), so the debt term is present, and the mask holds nevertheless: the conversion term dominates by two orders of magnitude at the start ($\dconv u_c\approx0.78\times0.27=0.21$ against $b_f(\mu A_r-\eta_fA_f)=-0.005$ and $\alpha\dot Db_fA_f\approx0.004$).

\subsection{The deficit-side affine system and its spectrum}\label{si:affine}

On the deficit side with $q=q_{\max}$, $D$ frozen, $R_{rc}=0$ and $S/b_f<A_c-\Acmin$, Eq.~(5) gives $u_c=(E-b_fA_f-b_cA_c)/(b_f\tconv)$, and with $A_r=A_{tot}-A_f-A_c$ Eqs.~(6)--(7) become
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}A_f\\A_c\end{pmatrix}=
\begin{pmatrix}-\tconv^{-1}-\mu-\eta_f & -\mu-\dfrac{b_c}{b_f\tconv}\\[8pt]\tconv^{-1} & \dfrac{b_c}{b_f\tconv}\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}A_f\\A_c\end{pmatrix}+
\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{E}{b_f\tconv}+\mu A_{tot}\\[8pt]-\dfrac{E}{b_f\tconv}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Hence $\operatorname{tr}J=-\tconv^{-1}(1-b_c/b_f)-\mu-\eta_f$ and $\det J=(\mu+\eta_f)(k-b_c/b_f)/\tconv$ with $k=\mu/(\mu+\eta_f)$. At the baseline $\operatorname{tr}J=-1.0512$, $\det J=0.05353$, and the eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{1,2}=\tfrac12\Big(\operatorname{tr}J\pm\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}^2J-4\det J}\Big)=-0.0537,\ -0.9975\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Because $J$ contains neither $E$ nor the state, the spectrum is constant along the frozen-demand branch (verified at $E/\Eceil=0.50,\,0.90,\,0.99,\,0.999$: $\lambda_1=-0.05366$ in every case). $J$ contains no $\rho_c$, so $\lambda_1$ is exactly independent of the regeneration rate ($-0.0537$ at $\rho_c=0.04,\,0.08,\,0.16$). It depends mildly on $\tconv$ ($-0.0553,\,-0.0537,\,-0.0505,\,-0.0417$ at $\tconv=0.5,\,1,\,2,\,5$\,yr) and more strongly on the maintenance rate ($-0.0254,\,-0.0537,\,-0.1099$ at $\mu=0.03,\,0.06,\,0.12\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$); its value should therefore be reported as an order-of-magnitude negative number that never crosses zero, while the qualitative statements (no positive eigenvalue, no fold, no critical slowing) hold across all sweeps. The fixed point of the affine system is $A_c^*(E)$ of Eq.~(19) with $A_f^*=k(A_{tot}-A_c^*)$; for $E>\Eceil$ it lies below $\Acmin$ and the trajectory is captured by the face $A_c=\Acmin$, on which $u_c=0$ and the shortfall $S^*=E-\Eceil$ persists. Two verified members of the no-conversion family at the baseline are $(A_f^*,A_c^*,A_r^*,P^*)=(0.914,0.824,0.762,1.488)$ and $(1.091,0.500,0.909,1.731)$, both with $A_r^*/A_f^*=\eta_f/\mu=0.8333$ exactly; the second is a sustainable cropland equilibrium supported by the maintenance term. The slow mode is the equilibration of the reserve share $A_r/A_f\to\eta_f/\mu$, which is why the strict-inequality window of the demonstration run extends to $\approx150$\,yr although most of the conversion is complete within $25$\,yr.

In the other branch of the minimum ($S/b_f\ge A_c-\Acmin$), $u_c=(A_c-\Acmin)/\tconv$ and the distance to the floor obeys $\frac{d}{dt}(A_c-\Acmin)=-(A_c-\Acmin)/\tconv$: it decays exponentially and never reaches zero in finite time. This is the reason the floor is approached asymptotically in Fig.~1c of the paper and the ``floor-contact'' thresholds depend on the tolerance used to declare contact (Section~\ref{si:ladder}).

\subsection{Characteristic structure of the full delay system}\label{si:char}

With state $z=(A_f,A_c,q,P,D)$ the linearised system about any point of a given active set is $\dot z(t)=J_0z(t)+J_gz(t-\tau_g)+J_pz(t-\tau_p)$ with characteristic equation $\det\Delta(\lambda)=0$, $\Delta(\lambda)=\lambda I-J_0-J_ge^{-\lambda\tau_g}-J_pe^{-\lambda\tau_p}$. $J_0$ carries the conversion and debt feedbacks on the current state (the $2\times2$ block above, plus the $D$ row $\partial\dot D/\partial z=(-b_f,-\Ups,-b_{G,c}G_c'A_c,e,-\eta)$ on the deficit side and the $\partial b_f/\partial D=-\alpha b_f$ couplings); $J_g$ has the single entry $G_c'(q^*)$ in the $q$ row; $J_p$ has the row $(rP^{*2}/K^{*2})\,e^{-1}\,\partial B/\partial z$ in the $P$ position (from $\partial\dot P/\partial K=rP^2/K^2$ and $K=B/e$). Two scalar delayed modes decouple at the baseline. The quality mode linearised at $q_{\max}$ is $\dot\xi=-\rho_c\xi(t-\tau_g)$ (rate $-0.08\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ without the delay); its characteristic root satisfies $\lambda=-\rho_ce^{-\lambda\tau_g}$, and since $\rho_c\tau_g=1.6>\pi/2$ the linear delayed mode would be oscillatory. In the nonlinear model this cannot occur because $\dot q=G_c(q(t-\tau_g))\ge0$ for $q(t-\tau_g)\in[0,q_{\max}]$ and $q$ is clamped at $q_{\max}$, so quality is monotone non-decreasing and converges to $q_{\max}$; the linear oscillation is an artefact of linearising a one-sided law at its boundary. The population mode on the no-conversion face is the delayed logistic law with $r\tau_p=0.5<\pi/2$, below the Hutchinson threshold, so it is non-oscillatory at the baseline. No entry, determinant sign or eigenvalue is inherited from the one-stock characteristic equation of Section~\ref{si:onestock}; each is derived per active set (no conversion; active conversion; floor face $A_c=\Acmin$; $K=K_{\min}$).

\subsection{Debt fixed points beyond the ceiling}\label{si:debt}

For $E>\Eceil$ the capital book sits on the floor and the shortfall feeds the debt. With $B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)=b_{f0}e^{-\alpha D}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)+b_c\Acmin$, the debt equation $\dot D=E-B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)-\eta D$ has the fixed point $D^*(E)$ solving $\eta D=E-B_{eq}(\Acmin;D)$; the right-hand side is increasing in $D$, so the fixed point is unique and attracting whenever $\eta>\alpha b_{f0}k(A_{tot}-\Acmin)$ ($0.034<0.05$ at the baseline). Solving numerically, $D^*=1.186$ at $E=1.158$ and $D^*=5.375$ at $E=1.238$, with $B(D^*)=1.099$ and $0.969$ and persistent shortfalls $S^*=0.059$ and $0.269$; the integrated values quoted in earlier versions ($1.21$ and $5.39$) agree to within $2\%$ (finite horizon). Under frozen demand the debt therefore saturates; it is unbounded only if the shortfall is.

\subsection{Boundedness asymmetry and the crossover shortfall}\label{si:bounded}

From Eq.~(12), $\ln b_f=\ln(b_{f0}+T_b)-\alpha D$. The first term lies in $[\ln b_{f0},\ \ln(b_{f0}+\Delta b)]$, so the technology channel contributes at most $\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})=\ln(1.85/0.85)=0.778$ to $\ln b_f$ at the baseline. From Eq.~(13), $\dot D\le\bar S-\eta D$ with $\bar S$ the running supremum of the shortfall, so $D(t)\le\max\{D(0),\bar S/\eta\}$ and the degradation channel contributes at least $-\alpha\bar S/\eta$. The two are equal when $\bar S=S^\dagger=(\eta/\alpha)\ln(1+\Delta b/b_{f0})=1.296\,\mathrm{gha\,yr^{-1}}$ at the baseline. For the fast-book yield itself, $Y_f=b_fA_f$ shares this structure; the capital term $\Ups A_c$ is unaffected by either channel. Under the additive law $b_f=b_{f0}+T_b-\alpha D$, both channels are bounded quantities of the same kind ($\Delta b$ against $\alpha D^*$) and the logarithmic asymmetry disappears, which is why the paper states the result for the multiplicative form.

\subsection{Macro-ratio identities}\label{si:ratios}

Define $R_B=E/B$, $R_A=E/Y_f$ and the fast-land share $\psi_f=Y_f/B$; then $R_A=R_B/\psi_f$ is an identity. The direct-yield share inside the capital book, $\psi_c=b_cA_c/Y_c$, is a different quantity and $R_A=R_B/\psi_c$ is false. $R_B=1$ is aggregate balance; $R_A=1$ (demand equals the fast flow) is necessary but not sufficient for capital decline because the capital direct yield and regeneration flow are untouched. The capital book declines iff $u_c>R_{rc}$, with $u_c$ given by Eq.~(5), which is a statement about the shortfall $S$ and not about $R_B$: the capital book can fall while $R_B\le1$ (the composition mask does not require overshoot), and ``$R_B=1$ is the fold'' is a statement about the one-stock frozen-demand reducer (Section~\ref{si:onestock}), not a two-land property. At the regeneration maximum $q^*=q_{\max}/2$ the direct-yield share of capital value is $b_c/(b_c+b_{G,c}\rho_cq_{\max}/4)=0.758$ at the baseline; this is a property of that point, not a structural constant.

\section{Numerical protocol and verification record}\label{si:numerics}

\paragraph{Solver.} Method of steps with an explicit first-order (Euler) update; exact positive part (a smooth ramp leaks conversion when $E<B$); clamps $A_c\ge\Acmin$, $A_f\ge0$, $q\in[0,q_{\max}]$, $P\ge0$, $D\ge0$, $K\ge K_{\min}$; the reserve is computed as $A_r=A_{tot}-A_f-A_c$ after each update and the conservation residual $|A_f+A_c+A_r-A_{tot}|$ is recorded at every step (maximum $2.2\times10^{-16}$, i.e.\ machine precision, at every step size used). Delay histories of $B$ (for $K(t-\tau_p)$) and of $q$ (for $G_c(q(t-\tau_g))$) are populated over $[-\max(\tau_g,\tau_p),0]$ with the initial state, not with zero; a zero history produced spurious early collapses at $\tau_p=0$ in an earlier version and was corrected. Step sizes: $\Delta t=0.05$\,yr for the demonstration run and the bisection thresholds; $\Delta t=0.1$\,yr for the drawdown-depth sweeps (checked against $0.05$ at selected points; the floor-contact threshold at $A_{c0}=1.0$ moves from $2.955$ to $2.98$, which is why thresholds are reported as bands).

\paragraph{Delayed versus current quality in the regeneration term.} The deposited solver (\texttt{model\_sims/twoland\_fixed.py}) computes the delayed quality $q(t-\tau_g)$ but evaluates the update of $q$ at the current quality. Table~\ref{tab:si_delay} compares the two variants at the baseline; because $q$ is monotone and the regenerative term is small ($b_{G,c}G_c\le0.016$ against $b_c=0.05$), the differences are below $1\%$ in every reported quantity. The corrected variant (\texttt{twoland\_fixed\_delayed.py}, a one-line change) is deposited alongside; all quantities in the paper are quoted to a precision at which the two agree.

\paragraph{Well-posedness.} The state space $\Omega=\{(A_f,A_c,A_r,q,P,D):A_i\ge0,\ q\in[0,q_{\max}],\ A_f+A_c+A_r=A_{tot},\ K\ge K_{\min}\}$ is forward invariant under the clamped scheme; the only non-smoothness is the exact positive part at $S=0$ and the minimum in Eq.~(5), both of which are Lipschitz, so solutions exist and are unique for each history. Stability statements at $S=0$ are one-sided (deficit side).

\subsection{Drawdown-depth ladder}\label{si:ladder}

\section{Empirical details}\label{si:empirical}

\subsection{Data and vintages}\label{si:vintages}

The aggregate growth of $+23.0\%$ ($\Delta\ln B=+0.207$) is anchored to the NFA aggregate world series (2025 edition). The land-type matrix (cropland, grazing land, forest products, fishing grounds, built-up land) incorporates a retroactive re-basing and gives $+24.5\%$ ($\Delta\ln B=+0.219$); the $1.5$-point difference is an edition move, not a change in the underlying land facts. The attribution is robust across both: cropland accounts for $\approx103\%$ of net growth against the aggregate series and $\approx97\%$ against the land-type total, the residual band reflecting the edition move; the non-cropland book is flat as an endpoint change ($1.008\times$; intra-period index up to $1.024\times$) in both. Population is from the same accounts. The series are account-based (measured yields and conversion factors) and do not capture cumulative degradation; estimated biocapacity likely overstates the real base.

\subsection{Divisia decomposition, $\alpha$-robustness and the level fallback}\label{si:divisia}

With $X_t=\hat b_{f,t}A_{f,t}$ and $C_t=B_t-X_t$, the log-mean identity $\Delta\ln B=w_X(\Delta\ln b_f+\Delta\ln A_f)+w_C\Delta\ln C$ with $w_X=L(X_T,X_0)/L(B_T,B_0)$, $w_C=L(C_T,C_0)/L(B_T,B_0)$ holds exactly whenever $X$ and $C$ are positive at both endpoints. The base-year calibration $\alpha=b_f(1961)A_f(1961)/B(1961)$ (the cropland share of 1961 biocapacity) is unidentified; Table~\ref{tab:si_alpha} recomputes the decomposition over $\alpha\in[0.10,0.34]$. The identity sums to $+0.207$ exactly in every row and the yield channel is the dominant positive term in every row, but the split is not pinned: $w_X$ ranges $0.183$--$0.622$, the 2022 fast-book share ranges $\approx0.30$--$1.00$, and the raw residual log-change ranges $\approx-0.04$ to $\approx-6.8$. Only the sign ordering is carried forward.

The log-mean weighting fails once $C_{2022}\le0$, which happens for
\begin{equation}
\alpha\ \ge\ \frac{B_{2022}}{B_{1961}\,e^{\Delta\ln A_f+\Delta\ln b_f}}\ \approx\ 0.339 .
\end{equation}
In that regime the exact decomposition is the level one, $\Delta B=\Delta X+\Delta C$ with $\Delta X=\alpha B_{1961}(e^{\Delta\ln A_f+\Delta\ln b_f}-1)$ and $\Delta C=\Delta B-\Delta X$, which holds for every $\alpha$, reproduces $\Delta B=+2.242\times10^{9}$\,gha, and preserves the sign statement ($\Delta X>0$, $\Delta C<0$). Average-share weights ($\bar s_X=0.375$, $\bar s_C=0.625$) give $+0.231$, an $\approx11\%$ overshoot, and are not used. The residual $C$ is an accounting residual (grazing, forest products, fishing, built-up land, equivalence-factor and calibration effects), not an observed capital stock; the instantaneous fast-book share $s_X$ rises from $0.19$ to $0.56$ because the crop-yield index is applied to the whole fast book.

\subsection{Cropland-book calibration}\label{si:cropland}

The base year can be pinned directly from the measured NFA cropland biocapacity: $b_f(1961)=B_{\mathrm{cropland,NFA}}(1961)/A_f(1961)\approx0.93\,\mathrm{gha\,ha^{-1}\,yr^{-1}}$, corresponding to a cropland share of $B$ of $\approx0.128$ in 1961, inside the robustness band of Table~\ref{tab:si_alpha}. Over 1961--2022 cropland biocapacity grew $2.85\times$, cropland area $1.17\times$ and cropland biocapacity per hectare $2.43\times$; the model's representative $b_{f0}=0.85$ is of the same order as the measured 1961 value and is not a fit.

\subsection{Observation-operator hierarchy}\label{si:operator}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Fast provisioning land.} $A_f(t)$ = FAOSTAT arable plus permanent crops, element Area, world, 1961--2022 \citep{FAO2023}.
\item \emph{Fast-book yield.} $\tilde b_f(t)$ = FAOSTAT crop production per hectare of $A_f$, normalised to the base year and calibrated there against NFA cropland biocapacity, $b_f(t)=b_f(t_0)\tilde b_f(t)/\tilde b_f(t_0)$. FAOSTAT yields are physical output per hectare, not gha\,ha$^{-1}$, and must be normalised or calibrated before any absolute statement; a cleaner proxy is aggregate crop production per hectare on a calorie or dry-matter basis.
\item \emph{Ecological-capital land.} $A_c(t)$ = FAO/FRA forest area \citep{FAO2020}, with natural grassland, shrub and wetland added where available; before 1990, LUH2 \citep{Hurtt2011} or HYDE~3.2 \citep{KleinGoldewijk2017} scaled to FRA. Forest area is a sentinel proxy, not the whole capital book (no soil carbon, wetland, fishery or non-forest regenerative capital). Where they are not separately measured the capital book is the residual $B_{\mathrm{NFA}}-b_fA_f$.
\item \emph{Capital yield and regeneration.} $b_{G,c}G_c(q)$ is not observed in the NFA; proxies would need FRA growing stock or net annual increment, GAEZ potential productivity, or EarthStat/MapSPAM \citep{Monfreda2008}. This is why $b_c$, $b_{G,c}$, $\rho_c$ are not individually identified: the observable combinations are $b_fA_f$, $\Ups A_c$ and $b_{G,c}G_c(q)A_c$.
\item \emph{Hierarchy.} (1) NFA land-type biocapacity with physical area and yield factors (best); (2) FAOSTAT area and yields as independent external proxies (used here); (3) remote-sensing land cover \citep{ESA2017,Copernicus2019} or the reconstructions above with EarthStat/MapSPAM/GAEZ, for validation or extension.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Regeneration anchors}\label{si:anchors}

The capital-land regeneration timescale is a lumped recruitment-response timescale; the anchors bound a band and do not calibrate $\rho_c$. A lagged saturating recovery model fitted to plot-level age--above-ground-biomass records of Neotropical secondary forests \citep[2ndFOR;][1{,}334 plots, 41 chronosequences, 35 good fits]{Poorter2016} gives a median $t_{50}\approx30$\,yr and a recovery timescale $\tau_r\approx38$\,yr; the same model fitted to deplete-then-rebuild trajectories of collapsed fish stocks (RAM Legacy $B/B_{MSY}$, 33 episodes) gives $t_{50}\approx5.6$\,yr and $\tau_r\approx3.4$\,yr \citep[cf.][]{HutchingsReynolds2004,Neubauer2013}; soil organic carbon re-equilibrates after $\approx17$--$23$\,yr following land-use change \citep{Poeplau2011}. Forest biomass recovers to about half of old-growth values by $\approx20$\,yr and to $90\%$ at a median $\approx66$\,yr \citep{Poorter2016} (the 66--95\,yr tail lies beyond the core band); only $\approx29\%$ of collapsed fish stocks recovered to $50\%$ within 5--15\,yr \citep{HutchingsReynolds2004}. The two fitted legs bracket the effective timescale (forest slow, fishery fast) with soil between them; the band is $\approx25$--$33$\,yr (representative $\approx29$\,yr; order 10--40\,yr). These are statements about the regeneration rate, not the delay $\tau_g$. In the one-stock comparator the forest value lies at or above that model's $\approx18$--$20$\,yr cliff; in the two-land model the drawdown boundary is a demand threshold and the anchors bound a band rather than locate a cliff.

\subsection{Recursive identifiability in the land-type accounts}\label{si:recursive}

In the land-type vintage the non-cropland total is flat at the endpoint ($1.008\times$) but is the net of declining forest-products ($0.90\times$), grazing ($0.98\times$) and fishing ($0.96\times$) biocapacity -- together $\approx-6.4\times10^{8}$\,gha -- and an $8.63\times$ rise in built-up land; contributions to the $+24.5$-point aggregate growth are cropland $+23.8$, built-up $+7.3$, forest products, grazing and fishing $-6.6$ (gross positive $+31.1$). These categories are accounting flows and land classes rather than the model's capital stock, so they do not demonstrate a measured decline in $A_c$; they demonstrate that an aggregate conceals composition at every level at which it is used.

\section{Policy extensions}\label{si:policy}

\paragraph{Half-Earth and reservation.} The Half-Earth proposal \citep{Wilson2016} caps human-usable biocapacity at half of the total. In the two-land setting this is a reservation $E\le\sigma B$ with $\sigma$ the human-available share, enforced either as a cap on the fast book or on the carrying capacity; with $\sigma_f,\sigma_c<1$ in Eq.~(4) the shortfall is computed against the reserved flows, so conversion begins earlier for a given demand and the ceiling of Proposition~2 is lowered to $\sigma$-weighted values. On the nominal path a reservation that keeps $E\le\sigma B$ succeeds with no liquidation. This is a nominal-path consequence until it is certified against a declared disturbance class (persistent productivity, $b_f$ or $\rho_c$ shocks) under a preregistered retention rule (a governance module is retained only if it improves a declared protection-and-supply score), with the model's declared fit defects converted into an erosion margin on the closed loop's contraction rate; until then it is a property of the nominal calibration, not a robustness claim.

\paragraph{Typed floors and freeze conversion.} Reserve $A_r\ge A_r^{\min}$, capital viability $A_c\ge\Acmin$, arable ceiling $A_f\le A_f^{\max}$ and $K\ge K_{\min}$ are separate constraints. Freezing conversion (capping $A_f$ at $A_f^{\max}$ or flooring $A_c$ at $\Acmin$) is the single operator that guarantees $A_c\ge\Acmin$; the conversion rate saturates as $A_c\to\Acmin$ by construction of Eq.~(5). A policy that observes only the aggregate $B$ cannot guarantee capital-land integrity: the failure is informational (aggregation relative to the typed floor), not dynamical.

\paragraph{Institutional signature.} In the mobilising/protective taxonomy of the delay companion \citep{Abaee2026a}, freeze-conversion and surplus-gated restoration are protective; the mobilising sign (demand responding to a perceived shortfall by converting more) is the destabilising one; deficit-gated restoration is not unambiguously protective because it can deepen the short-run shortfall (Section~\ref{si:gates}).

\paragraph{Weak and strong sustainability reading.} In the material-cycle reading of the ledger companion \citep{Abaee2026c}, weak sustainability is the idealised regime in which substitution and regeneration jointly redistribute matter so that it is used as it arises -- substitution admissible only through an identified physical pathway, never merely because a weighted aggregate permits it -- and strong sustainability is ``the regime in which that closure fails -- either because no identified physical pathway re-routes the extracted matter, or because depletion outruns the rate at which such a pathway could be deployed'', so that a separately binding floor on a critical stock binds. The composite $B$ is the weak-sustainability index: the linear, perfectly substitutable member of the aggregator family of the assessment companion \citep[Lemma~A of][]{Abaee2026b}; it can satisfy its aggregate floor while the typed floor on $A_c$ is violated by exactly the amount that the fast-land yield gains against the capital loss, and in that family no positive-elasticity aggregator is uniformly safe (its Theorem~S2(ii)). Under exact-tube semantics the intermediate passage during which $A_c$ falls is itself a violation even if the endpoint recovers.

\paragraph{Reporting rule.} A growth headline for total biocapacity carries no information about whether cropland growth is compensating for stagnation or offsetting changes in the other components. The informative assessment states, at every level at which an aggregate is used: component endpoint ratios, log contributions, attribution shares, data vintage and endpoint sensitivity.

\section{The one-stock comparator}\label{si:onestock}

Earlier versions of this work developed a single-stock model that the two-land model supersedes. It is retained here as a comparator so that quantities that circulate for it are not mistaken for two-land results. The comparator has three states ($A$, $P$, $D$) and one algebraic relation:
\begin{align}
G(A)&=\rho A(1-A/A_{\max}),\qquad B=bA+b_GG(A),\qquad E=eP,\\
\dot A&=G\big(A(t-\tau_g)\big)-\pos{E-\sigma bA}/b_G,\\
\dot P&=rP\big(1-P/K(t-\tau_p)\big),\qquad K=B/e,\\
\dot D&=\pos{E-B}-\eta D,\qquad b=(b_0+T_b)e^{-\alpha D},
\end{align}
with demand met first from the flow $bA$ and the shortfall liquidating the standing stock at rate $1/b_G$. Its properties, each of which was re-derived under the two-land structure and found not to carry over, are the following.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{MSY and fold.} $B(A)$ has an interior maximum (the MSY of Schaefer, 1954) only in the capital-dominated regime $b_G\rho>b$; a fixed liability above $B_{\max}$ produces a saddle-node, the catastrophic-shift structure of \citet{Scheffer2001}. At the comparator baseline ($b_G\rho=0.04<b=0.5$) the fold is absent. In the two-land model $Y_c$ is linear in $A_c$ and $B_{eq}(A_c)$ is monotone; there is no fold in any regime (Proposition~2).
\item \emph{The equilibrium family $P=B(A)/e$ and $\det J\equiv0$.} Under full harvest ($\sigma=1$) the comparator has a neutral continuum of equilibria on which the Jacobian is exactly singular. The two-land model has a one-parameter family in the \emph{land allocation} (indexed by $A_c$ on the no-conversion face, with $A_r/A_f=\eta_f/\mu$), not a free $P$, and no identically singular Jacobian.
\item \emph{The $+0.6$ eigenvalue and the two-gain Hopf classification.} The comparator's deficit-region linearisation has a positive real root ($a_{11}=G'(A^*)+b/b_G\approx0.608$--$0.625$ at its baseline) and a two-gain rule $\chi=q/(\rho-2q)$ selecting which delay destabilises ($\tau_g$-only for $\chi>1$, $\tau_p$-only for $\chi<1$, with critical delays $\tau_m\approx85.4$ and $\tau_p\approx231$\,yr). None of this is a two-land quantity: the two-land deficit-side spectrum is real and negative ($-0.054$, $-1.0\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$) and constant along the branch.
\item \emph{The regeneration cliff and the masking window.} The comparator shows a recover/collapse boundary at $\tau_g\approx18$--$20$\,yr and a productivity-illusion window of $\approx5.4$\,yr ($A:1.00\to0.86$ while $B:0.578\to0.647$; the mask vanishes for deficits $\gtrsim0.075$). In the two-land model the boundary is a demand threshold insensitive to $\tau_g$ (Table~\ref{tab:si_delay}) and the composition window is set by $\lambda_1$ and $\dconv$ (paper, Section~3.1). The recover fractions $0.54\to0.52\to0.50$ for $\tau_g=5\to25\to40$\,yr quoted in earlier versions were computed with an area-based intermediate formulation and are superseded by the identical-to-$10^{-3}$ result of Table~\ref{tab:si_delay}.
\end{itemize}
