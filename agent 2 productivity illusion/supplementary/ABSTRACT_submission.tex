\begin{abstract}
Coupled human--environment models typically treat carrying capacity as an imposed, static ceiling. We show it is instead emergent, and that aggregate biocapacity can conceal a decline in the ecological capital that sustains it. We develop a minimal deterministic two-land model: biocapacity sums two distinct books --- fast provisioning land and ecological capital land --- linked by land-use conversion. Population responds through a delayed carrying capacity, regeneration of capital-land \emph{quality} through a delayed flow, and overshoot accrues as debt that erodes the flow yield. Land area is conserved one-to-one; capacity is a separate quality state, and conversion is a finite rate with an explicit time constant. First, the no-conversion face is a one-parameter family in the land allocation, with no identically singular Jacobian, and a sustainable cropland equilibrium exists. Second, aggregate biocapacity can rise while ecological capital falls --- the \emph{aggregation} face of the composition effect appears in the real series: in the National Footprint and Biocapacity Accounts (world, 2025) biocapacity rose $\approx$23\,\% (1961--2022), and the exact identity $d\ln B=d\ln(B/P)+d\ln P$ gives per-capita $-364$\,\% and population $+464$\,\% of $d\ln B$, so capacity rose only because population grew while per-capita capacity halved; overshoot began in 1971. Third, the collapse of the capital book is a demand-driven drawdown: above $E_{ceil}\approx1.138$ it is driven to its typed floor and debt accrues. Because $B_{eq}(A_c)$ is monotone this is a \emph{floor collision}, not an interior saddle-node --- the equilibrium is unique and the leading eigenvalue is constant ($\approx-0.0537\,\text{yr}^{-1}$), so there is \emph{no} critical slowing down; the composition-loop gain is a state-dependent contrast (of order $10^{-2}\,$yr$^{-1}$), not a universal eigenvalue. Recovery is not the inverse of conversion: it needs an explicit restoration flow whose \emph{gate sign} matters. Because biocapacity in global hectares embeds yield and equivalence factors, the composition step is \emph{not} identifiable from the aggregate alone and needs independent land-cover and yield proxies. The illusion is a robust, bounded, demand-driven composition effect, separate from a non-identifiable single-stock reading.
\end{abstract}
